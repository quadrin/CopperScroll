## Verdict
**No unit changes.** Bar-Adon 1972 was run 25's top record (6 EVIDENCE units). Read at full resolution, it gives no fissure, crack, cairn, conduit head or pool that the six criteria need. Every unit stays where it was. Details per unit and place: [out/units_scored.csv](out/units_scored.csv).

The reason is the survey's format. Each site entry lists the main features and the periods. Bar-Adon does not describe the ground around a cistern or a pool, and he does not date single features. The criteria need exactly those things, so this record could fill them only by luck.

## What was read (EVIDENCE, paraphrased; Hebrew text, old Palestine grid as printed)
- **Site 102, Karm es-Samra (el-ʿAjaz), 1872 1249, p. 123.** An Iron Age casemate fort, 69 × 39.5 m, with buildings against its south and east sides. Several water-catchment dams lie near it. Periods: Iron II (most of the finds), Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine. Cites Cross & Milik, *BASOR* 142 pp. 9–12. **No cistern is mentioned.**
- **Site 104, Maghayir et-Tabaqa / el-Makari / Kurm el-ʿAjaz, 1862 1231, p. 123.** A casemate fort, 33 × 32 m, with outer walls 1.1 m and inner walls 0.7 m. **East of the fort** is a pool with an earth bank, 25 m across and 2 m deep. Periods: Iron I–II, Hellenistic, Roman, Arab. Cites Cross & Milik pp. 12–14.
- **Site 92, Kh. Karm ʿAṭrad / Magharat Abu Ṭabaq, 1886 1277, pp. 120–121.** Six features, each with its own grid and periods:
  - (a) a rectangular Iron fort with a water cistern north of it (18855 12767; Iron II, 8th c.);
  - (b) an elliptical building in a Bedouin cemetery (Iron II, Roman);
  - (c) a round building 19 m across, 18893 12758, about 380 m east of the fort (Iron I, Roman);
  - (d) remains of three buildings (Roman);
  - (e) a wall about 1,200 m long, with a semicircular building attached (Iron, Hellenistic);
  - (f) two buildings.
- **Site 96, Roman camp, 1873 1260, p. 122.** The camp is 106 × 76 m, with its opening on the west. South of it is a rock-cut cistern with a round mouth and troughs. Stone-paved water channels reach the mouth. One is visible for about 500 m, from 1868 1260 to 1873 1260. Built pools lie in the wadi to the south.
- **Sites 93, 94, Nukb Kumran, and 98, pp. 121–122.** On the north bank of Wadi Qumran: buildings and walls, and a possible watchtower. Site 98 is the building group about 1 km south of Kh. Qumran. No cairn, dam or conduit is described.
- **Site 13, T. esh-Sheikh Dhiab, 1908 1615, p. 105.** The tell's top is 15 × 25 m, and its whole extent about 50 × 50 m. Building remains and foundations are on the tell. A new Arab cemetery is on a hillock near it. At the south-west foot is a building of large dressed stones, 45 × 45 m, with water-channel remains about 20 m west of it. Periods: Chalcolithic to Arab, including Roman. No pool is described.

## Observations worth keeping (not scored)
1. **The Maqari pool's bearing.** Bar-Adon puts the pool **east** of the fort. Run 1 placed the birkeh about 198 m **north**, from Patrich Site 101's point. This bears on run 1's J5 geometry at Maqari. Check it against Cross & Milik p. 13 and Patrich's plan.
2. **Site 96 is probably Patrich Site 52**, with a printed channel head (1868 1260) and length (about 500 m). It is a Roman-labelled camp with a paved feeder system, 1.1 km north of Samra. It is the nearest Roman water installation to Samra in this survey.
3. **Abu Ṭabaq features with their own grids and periods.** The round building (c), about 380 m east of the fort, has Roman sherds of its own. Run 1's J6 note ("stone heap among the round courtyards east of the fort") had no positions. J6 is not in this addendum's scope.
4. **Correction to the 29 Oct held-area screen** (`research/sources/books_usc_2026-10-30/held_asi1_deirhajleh_2026-10-30.md`, site 13). The Arab cemetery is on a hillock near the tell, not on the tell. This is an owner decision and has not been applied.
