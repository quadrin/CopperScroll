## Verdict
**Not discriminating.** No place fits all of entries 20–24 jointly, and none is excluded. The favoured candidate, Kh. Qumran, does no better than the control Hyrcania, and under the lenient reading both Hyrcania and Kh. es-Samra do better than Qumran.

## What the run shows
1. **J5 is the bottleneck everywhere.** Every place has a large reservoir; no source records a fissure, crack or cleft east of any of them, except Qumran, where a crack runs ~1.5–2.2 m east of L71 through dump fill, of disputed date (Magen & Peleg 2018 pp. 51, 67, Fig. 71). Entry 22 ("the fissure in Sekakah, east of the Reservoir of Solomon") is the one relation that could separate the places.
2. **The joint fit does not favour Qumran.** The Qumran preference in the editions rests on order and on "from Jericho to Sekakah". This run gives no extra support to Qumran over a Herodian fortress control, or over Milik's biblical Secacah at Kh. es-Samra.
3. **Kh. es-Samra and Hyrcania share a valley.** Hyrcania's dam and aqueduct head lie on Naḥal Sekhakha (Wadi Abu Shuʿla), and Samra lies on the same wadi 4.5 km downstream (outside the frozen 3 km bound). See the follow-up below.

## Decisive next observations (Richard III step: the record that most changes the result)
1. **A dated section, or de Vaux's 1956 plotted crack line, for the crack east of Qumran L71** → J5 at Qumran.
2. **A measured plan of the ground east of Hyrcania's double pool** (Feldman 1974 plan p. 330; Patrich 2002 Fig. 22) → J5 at Hyrcania; a fissure there makes the cluster non-discriminating.
3. **A plan of Kh. es-Samra's 18×22 m cistern and its east side** (Stager 1976, *BASOR* 221; Bar-Adon 1972) → J5 at Samra.
4. **A primary record of an ancient Jericho–Qumran road** → J1 at Qumran.

## Exploratory follow-up (new model, not a rescue of this test)
**"Valley of Secacah" = Naḥal Sekhakha / Wadi Abu Shuʿla**, with Sekakah at Kh. es-Samra (Milik 1962's biblical town) and entry 21's "head of the water conduit" = Hyrcania's northern-aqueduct intake dam in the same valley (Hasmonean phase, Patrich 1989 pp. 244, 251). This needs its own rule: the 3 km bound was frozen before the shared valley was known, and the modern Hebrew name "Sekhakha" is not evidence (it was given after the Samra identification).
