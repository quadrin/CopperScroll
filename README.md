# Copper Scroll (3Q15)

The project aims to identify and locate real places described in the Copper Scroll. We combine manuscript readings, ancient geography, archaeological reports, plans and imagery to ask where its directions lead and whether a proposed place fits better than competing possibilities.

The central questions connect to each other: what do the directions mean; which ancient landscape and period do they describe; which sites and particular structures fit; and what observation could distinguish a proposed identification? Groups of entries may constrain one another, so synthesis across the scroll matters alongside individual site studies.

A useful result gives a defensible location or bounded area, the evidence connecting it to the text, surviving alternatives and a checkable prediction. Establishing a landmark is separate from establishing that a listed deposit existed or survives. Research can also advance by excluding a candidate or showing which broader geographic interpretation the evidence supports.

## Start here

For a new session, read [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md), then [Current research direction](research/ACTIVE_TEST.md). An inherited priority label, unfinished task or incoming source does not determine the next work.

## What the research supports

Within the formal audited scope, no landmark identification has independently distinguished its candidate from the registered alternatives, and no deposit has been confirmed. Three formal candidate assessments remain inconclusive. The [research outcome record](research/PROGRESS_METRICS.md) defines the scope; these counts do not cover every historical proposal or atlas marker.

The [first method tests, 23–24 October](research/first_tests_2026-10-24/README.md) establish limited results: hoard sizes are compatible with the smaller tested weight unit; one historical satellite frame resolves large open pools; text extraction has unmeasured field accuracy; and matching early manuscript photographs stopped after failure. These results identify no hiding place. The [source findings, 29–30 October](research/sources/books_usc_2026-10-30/README.md) record newly received colour photographs, preliminary readings and archaeological corrections.

The [research methods and results](research/experimental_approaches/README.md) distinguish completed tests, partial results and conditional techniques. The [Drive source index](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1A08WOW_9PIkryXauw6WS1mSgT11NREpK/view) locates the underlying sources. Select methods for a research question; a method’s existence gives it no priority.

## Provenance and reuse

This material was migrated from the [`copper_scroll/` section of AncientHebrewTexts](https://github.com/quadrin/AncientHebrewTexts/tree/176b4c09340c4402d06bb612c493e9f97b049b53/copper_scroll) at source commit `176b4c0`. The source repository also holds research copies of modern Copper Scroll editions and other PDFs; this repository carries the analyses and citations, while those edition files remain there. The [source inventory](research/sources/sources.md) records what was actually checked and what remained inaccessible.

The Hebrew reader uses Martin G. Abegg Jr.'s transcription from ETCBC *dss* 2.0.1, credited under CC BY-NC 4.0. The English translation, glosses, notes, and research assessments were written for this project. Maps and photographs have their own source credits. See the [research instructions](AGENTS.md) before adding textual or geographic claims.
