# Siloam: channels, pools and the stepped street (entry 49, X 15–16)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = the authors state it. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## Reich, Shukron and Lernau 2007 (pp. 163–168)

- **Evidence (pp. 163–164).** The stepped pool "at the southern edge of the Tyropoeon Valley" has two phases: "In the earlier phase, the steps were made of grey cement ... In the later stage, the cement steps were faced with stone steps set directly upon the earlier steps. Coins embedded in the cement of the earlier phase date the construction of the pool to the middle of the first century BCE." Coins on the esplanade and in the drains show the area "went out of use ... with the sacking of Jerusalem in 70 CE". They identify it as "the 'Pool of Siloam' of the Second Temple period" (p. 164).
- **Evidence (p. 164).** "The system of channels crossing under the stone-paved areas (fig. 13) was cleared", partly known from Schick and Guthe (1882). "The active channel is not, in fact, an 'overflow channel', but the actual feeding channel of the Pool of Siloam in the Second Temple period."
- **Evidence (p. 166).** At the tunnel's southern outlet, "a shallow channel cut in the bottom of the Byzantine pool, continuing southwards in a straight course"; another segment of it lies "under the stone-paved esplanade, approximately in the centre of the valley", which "may have flowed to the 'Pool of the Shelah' (Neh. 3:15) or its predecessor in the late Iron Age II".
- **Evidence (p. 166).** "Under this stone paving, the active channel branches off to the east. It is this channel ... that emerges at the eastern outskirts of the stone-lined pool, at the very southern tip of the hill, and feeds it."
- **Evidence (p. 166).** A third channel, a rain-water drain along the western cliff of the City of David, runs north; it was cleared for c. 40 m and held late Second Temple pottery, several hundred coins "mostly minted under the Jewish Revolt", and intact cooking pots. It runs under the stepped street (pp. 166–168).
- **Evidence.** No plan of the channels, no levels and no locus numbers are printed; fig. 13 is a photograph of the esplanade.
- **Inference.** This is the English report behind the 2011 chapter already extracted ([galor_avni2011](galor_avni2011_unearthing_extracted.md), p. 247). It adds a second channel junction: under the esplanade the line from the tunnel outlet splits, one arm south down the valley, one east to the stone-lined pool, which it enters at its eastern edge. A junction is reported, but it is not surveyed, not drawn and not separately dated. It is a channel branch, not a trough (השקת).

## Adan 1979 (pp. 92–100)

- **Evidence (pp. 94–96).** In 1977 a municipal sewer trench at the Pointe Sud, south-east of the City of David, cut "a wall 2.5 m. thick" plastered on its north face; the opposite face lay "18.2 m. to the north". The plaster of side and floor "was spread on bedrock". He reconstructs the pool up to the City of David scarp 5.5 m to the west (p. 96).
- **Evidence (pp. 96–98).** "The pottery from the pool floor is completely homogeneous and dates from the first century C.E." Out of several hundred sherds only one was of another period.
- **Evidence (p. 98).** "The pool seems to have been supplied by the waters of the Gihon, which generally overflowed the pool of Siloam and were channelled to our pool", through Weill's Canal IV: "According to our reconstruction, this channel continued from the last point excavated by Weill to our pool". Note 29: "this proposal awaits verification".
- **Evidence (p. 98 n. 30).** Levels: Siloam pool 632.59 m; floor of his pool by the north wall 631.41 m, the upward curve on the south side 630.65 m; the interpolated end of Canal IV 631.73 m.
- **Evidence (p. 100).** He identifies the pool with Josephus's "Solomon's pool" (*J.W.* 5.145), between the pool of Siloam and Ophlas, outside the wall; the "fountain of Siloam" lay inside.
- **Inference.** A third, downstream pool of the window, with a supply channel that is reconstructed, not observed. It gives levels the repo did not have. It records no trough and no outlet at the Siloam pool. Adan's "Solomon's pool" is not the one the repo uses: Szanton 2023 (findings log F5.2 note) makes the stone-lined stepped pool Solomon's Pool and the small pool at the tunnel outlet Siloam; Adan, writing before the stepped pool was found (2004), puts Solomon's pool at the Pointe Sud. Both keep "Siloam" at the tunnel-outlet pool.

## Grossberg 2013 (pp. 205–216)

- **Evidence.** The southern part of Channel II was cut as a tunnel by the shaft method; at opening 106-5 the chisel marks of two teams meet (pp. 205–208). He dates it to Hezekiah and sends it to the "Lower Pool" (Isa. 22:9), "today Birket el-Hamra" (p. 214), later "the Pool of Shelah" (Neh. 3:15) and "the pool of Siloam" (John 9:7) (p. 215).
- **Inference.** Iron Age; nothing for the window or the trough. No effect on entry 49.

## Hagbi, Szanton, Uziel and Levy 2022 (HA-ESI 134)

- **Evidence.** The excavation (2015–2018) had three areas near the Siloam Pool (Siloam, S11, S14, S15). The Stepped Street "ascends from the Siloam Pool to the Temple Mount"; in Area S12 a bifurcation, "the Large Staircase", "leads down to the Siloam Pool"; its steps "cross the route of the main drainage channel". Most paving stones there were robbed.
- **Evidence.** In Area S1, by Podium 1001 (about 220 m north of the pool), plastered installation L1002 has two steps in its east wall, and "a patch of dark brown calcareous plaster seems to have cut off a feeder pipe from the north. The installation may have been a miqveh."
- **Evidence.** Building 80110 (70 CE to the founding of Aelia) was "probably a public building or a fortified structure designed to oversee the Siloam Pool". In S11 they re-exposed the Siloam Church (narthex, 16-step staircase).
- **Inference.** No trough, basin or outlet at the pool is reported. The pool's access from the street is a staircase; the drain passes under it.

## Szanton and Uziel 2016 (HA-ESI 128)

- **Evidence.** The street section about 220 m north of the Siloam Pool is dated by coins under the paving, the latest of 30–31 CE (Pilate), so "the fourth decade of the first century CE, at the earliest". Manholes connect the street to the drain beneath it; a round plastered installation (L1002) stood in the building east of the podium.
- **Inference.** Background date for the street. No effect on entry 49.

## Effect on entry 49 (applied 16 October 2026)

- **No rating change.** R 1, P 3, F 1, D as before. No article reports a trough (השקת), a hollowed stone or a surveyed, phase-dated junction at the pools.
- **What is new.** (1) The branch under the esplanade, where the tunnel-outlet line splits south and east, and the eastern inlet of the stone-lined pool (Reich, Shukron and Lernau 2007 p. 166). (2) Adan's pool at the Pointe Sud, dated 1st century CE, with levels, fed by a reconstructed overflow channel.
- **Next record.** Unchanged: a surveyed, phase-dated trough or conduit junction at the pools. The two named junctions (the sluice branch, p. 247 of the 2011 chapter, and the esplanade branch, p. 166 here) are the places to ask for; Reich and Shukron 2008 and Shukron and Reich 2010 (*City of David Studies* 3 and 6, Hebrew) are the channel reports cited for them.
