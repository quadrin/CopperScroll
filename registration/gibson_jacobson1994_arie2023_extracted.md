# Gibson & Jacobson 1994 (entries 3, 5, 8, 9, 10) and Arie 2023 (entry 57)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = the authors state it, or it is drawn on their figure. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## A. Two Haram cisterns

- **Evidence (pp. 150–153).** About 45 subterranean chambers were recorded by Wilson, Warren, Conder and Schick. The article treats two: Schick nos. **15** and **29** (Wilson/Warren nos. 22 and 34), with nos. 16 (W/W 18) and 30 (Warren 2) in passing. Haram plan on p. 152.
- **Evidence, no. 15 (pp. 153–154).** Near the north-west corner, under the Sabil Basiri. A kidney-shaped double chamber (about 9 × 10.5 m and 7 × 13 m, joined by a 3 m doorway), each chamber with a round shaft in its ceiling, and an opening near the north wall read as a conduit inlet. A round southern chamber (11 m) under the 15th-century Sabil "may have a medieval origin". The original entrance is a rectangular opening at the Haram surface; from it a narrow flight of steps (0.60 m) runs anti-clockwise down along the curved wall of the eastern chamber (p. 154). No sill, threshold or structure is recorded at the head of the stair.
- **Evidence, no. 29 (pp. 154–155).** "Just inside the eastern wall" of the Dome of the Rock platform, at its north-east angle, beside no. 30. Two oval chambers (9.5 × 7.6 m and 8.2 × 8.0 m) joined by a doorway, each with a round shaft through its roof; about 11.5 m high, floor about 14 m below the Haram (Schick's plans, PEF Schick 239:2–4). No stair; Warren notes only a short rock-cut passage at the north-east angle.
- **Inference (p. 152 plan, scale bar).** No. 29 is about 90 m west of the Golden Gate. The text never mentions the Golden Gate; unnumbered features between the platform and the gate are not discussed.
- **Evidence (pp. 155–159).** Both cisterns are dated to the 3rd–2nd century BCE **by typology**: the twin bell-shaped stepped "Sandahannah" cisterns of Maresha, where the type ends by 113–112 BCE. No. 15's feeder conduit was cut by the Herodian western wall, so it is pre-Herodian (p. 157). No. 29 is tied to the pre-Herodian sacred precinct (Jacobson's view of the platform) and to the cisterns of the *Epistle of Aristeas* (pp. 158–159). Neither cistern is dated by stratified finds.

## B. Arie on the Mount Gerizim precinct

- **Evidence (pp. 141–150).** Arie re-dates the founding of the earliest precinct to the late Iron Age (ca. 650–550 BCE; at earliest ca. 670–650), from the six-chamber gate, the Proto-Ionic capitals and 15 of 89 sherds (which he concedes may be residual). Use continued to the early 2nd century BCE. He does not re-date Magen's later phase (destroyed ca. 110 BCE).
- **Evidence (p. 144).** The capitals were found on "the eastern staircase" of the precinct; its date and context are not given.
- **Evidence.** No 1st-century BCE or 1st-century CE finds, no pits, and no Tell er-Ras or Roman temple are mentioned.
