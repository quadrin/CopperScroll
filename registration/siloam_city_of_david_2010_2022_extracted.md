# Siloam pools: troughs, conduit junctions, dam, baths, south wall and gates

Extract for Copper Scroll 3Q15 X 15–16 (entry 49: deposit at השלוח, "under the trough (השקת)").

Method. I read the text layers of the four Hebrew PDFs (they are in logical order; in some files the letter tav comes out as a replacement glyph, so I checked passages against the rendered pages). I rendered every page at 150 dpi, and the plans at 400–600 dpi, and looked at them. I read the three HA-ESI exports in full. Page numbers are the printed journal pages. In the Hebrew PDFs, PDF page n is printed page 25+n (2010 paper), 52+n (Reich and Shukron 2011), 156+n (Shukron and Reich 2011) and 69+n (Grossberg). For the HA-ESI exports I cite the export page ("Online-source export | n"). Quotes are translated unless they are given in Hebrew.

Labels: **Evidence** means the source states it (page or figure given). **Inference** means my own reading.

Negative search: none of the seven sources uses שוקת or שקת (trough), אבוס (manger), "trough" or "hollowed stone". I searched every text layer and checked the plans. **Evidence (by absence)**: no trough is recorded in any of these sources.

## 1. Reich and Shukron 2010, *City of David Studies* 5: 27–45 (Hebrew). The excavation between the stepped pool and the inner face of the dam wall

### Setting
- **Evidence** (p. 28, Fig. 1 plan): in 2005 they exposed the east side and SE corner of the stepped Second Temple pool. Bliss and Dickie's test pit E3 had already hit its southern stepped side (B&D 1898: 176–177, Pl. XVII, Plan II). Fn. 1: B&D's "Pool of Siloam" is the Byzantine pool at the outlet of the Siloam Tunnel.
- **Evidence** (pp. 28–29): they dug a 5 × 4 m test pit from the surface (635.50 m) down to bedrock (629.50 m). It lies against the north face of the inner of B&D's two parallel walls (the wall drawn black on B&D Plan II = Fig. 6), where the pool's south stair is destroyed.
- **Evidence** (p. 29, Fig. 5): the pool had two building phases. First the steps were cast in grey mortar. Later they were faced with elongated ashlars. Landings lie at 632.08, 633.54 and 635.07 m.

### Pool retaining wall
- **Evidence** (pp. 30, 32; Figs. 3–5, 8): a sloping retaining wall (c. 60°) supports the south stair, which slopes at c. 38°. The wall thickens downward. It is cast of grey lime mortar containing ash, "of the type customary at the end of the Second Temple period", with stones, and it is founded on bedrock (p. 32).
- **Evidence** (p. 30): the authors suggest a horizontal "console" running SE onto the vertical face of W1020 to carry the upper steps. They know of no parallel for this retaining-wall technique.

### Conduit junction at the main stepped pool (feed channel)
- **Evidence** (p. 30): remains of five stone steps and a small part of the pool's SE corner were exposed "against the southern rock cliff of the City of David hill, opposite the pool's feed channel". Quote: "מול תעלת ההזנה של בריכת השילוח (שכונתה בעבר בשם 'תעלת העודפים')" ("opposite the Siloam Pool's feed channel, formerly called the 'overflow channel'").
- **Inference**: this is a conduit that joins the *main* stepped pool at its SE/east corner, under the City of David cliff. It is not L103 at the small Byzantine receiving pool. Grossberg (p. 76 n. 4, below) identifies it as Channel IV, cut in the Second Temple period. The paper gives no locus, construction details or finds for it.

### Dam wall W1020 (its own description and date)
- **Evidence** (p. 32): W1020 closes the test pit on the SE. It is the inner face, toward the city. It runs SW–NE, aimed at the SE rock corner of the City of David hill.
- **Evidence** (pp. 32–33, Fig. 6): it is drawn as a black line on B&D Plan II, so B&D did not excavate it. Its head was probably exposed at the surface.
- **Evidence** (pp. 33–34, Figs. 3–5, 9): its lower part projects c. 0.5 m inward. The thinner upper part was exposed for c. 2.70 m: six courses of medium dressed stones. The wall is founded on bedrock. The lower part is 1.5 m high in the NE and c. 3.0 m in the SW. It is coated with thick clay plaster (טיח טיני), and the same clay serves as mortar, "completely different" from the grey lime of the pool. The upper thickness is c. 3 m or a little less, estimated by eye against Greenhut and Mazor's 2009 exposure of the SE face (p. 34).
- **Evidence** (p. 34, Figs. 4, 10): five rock-cut steps descend NE to SW along W1020 (one tread 2.4 m; rises 40–70 cm). The clay plaster of W1020 continues over the treads. They are "a segment of an early stairway of an early water pool", a local stair c. 1 m wide.
- **Evidence** (pp. 34–35): the fill, Loci 164 (upper) and 171 (lower), was loose grey soil with a few medieval and later sherds. It is a backfill of earlier diggers (Guthe? B&D?) and is unrelated to the structures.
- **Evidence (date)** (p. 35, Fig. 7): (1) clay plaster is characteristic of early Jerusalem: Channel II (MB II) and the Siloam Tunnel (late Iron II); (2) a thickened krater rim (diam. c. 31 cm; basket 2157) was taken out of the clay plaster. The text says the plaster on the wall's lower part; the Fig. 7 caption says the plaster on the steps against W1020. The rim is "typical of late Iron Age II". Conclusion: the lower part of the wall was built across the valley in late Iron II.
- **Evidence** (p. 44, summary): two thick parallel walls crossed the Tyropoeon. The inner one (Kenyon's thick wall plus W1020) dates to the end of the Iron Age. It was "built in connection with the cutting of the Siloam Tunnel", dammed the valley to collect the tunnel water in a storage pool, and was part of the new fortification line. The outer buttressed wall of B&D is probably correctly dated by them to the Second Temple period, on the basis of its ashlar buttresses.

### Earlier excavations around Birket el-Hamra (as summarized here)
- **Evidence** (p. 37): B&D found a gate at the south end of the cross-valley wall, with a tower east of it (Fig. 12, p. 39). "Under the threshold of the gate" lay the southern outlet of the great drainage channel that ran down the Tyropoeon under the paved street.
- **Evidence: bath-house** (p. 37): "במרחק קטן מדרום לשער, מחוץ לתחומי העיר, נחשפו חלקים של בית מרחץ" ("a short distance south of the gate, outside the city, parts of a bath-house were exposed"), surely built there because water was available. The plan (Fig. 6, p. 33) labels it "BATHS", south of the tower at D2, beside a "scarp" (a second label there is illegible at this resolution). No date is given.
- **Evidence** (p. 37): the gate preserves the inner angle of the wall, the left jamb, thresholds of several periods and steps up to the gate. There are three building periods.
- **Evidence** (p. 38): a rectangular tower c. 4.6 × 14.3 m guards the gate at the SE corner of the fortifications. Its ashlar superstructure, in two styles, sits on rough masonry.
- **Evidence** (pp. 38–40, Figs. 6, 11): B&D's cross-valley wall N2–G2–F2 had a 6 m base, a 2.4–2.7 m superstructure and six buttresses, plus a corner tower L2–M2 (period 1). In period 2 the gaps between buttresses were filled, "thus giving the dam a thickness of c. 6 m along its whole length" (p. 39). Period 3 was a rough buttressing wall, made necessary, in B&D's view, by the wall's forward lean under the water pressure of the pool behind it (p. 39). Shaft K2 reached c. 14.15 m below the Kidron road; the wall is preserved 13.4 m high; no lime was used; it is 5.8 m thick at L2 (p. 40).
- **Evidence: basin** (p. 40): at Q2, at 7.9 m depth, B&D hit "the cement floor of a small water pool" already found by Guthe (wall 1.8 m thick). Fn. 14: Greenhut and Mazor recently re-exposed part of it. Wall Q2 (NE–SW) is the inner dam/wall and the southern continuation of B&D's black wall, perhaps the same as Kenyon Area F (pp. 40–41).
- **Inference**: Guthe's small pool at Q2 is very probably the plastered stepped pool L124/W50 published by Weksler-Bdolah (2014, 2022) and Greenhut and Mazor (2020): it is inside the inner wall, at its SW end. It is a basin, not a trough.
- **Evidence** (p. 40): B&D's rock scarp N2–V2 west of the pool (2.4–3 m thick where measured) may be a city wall. Fn. 15: it bounds the western stair and the paved plaza.
- **Evidence: Kenyon Area F** (p. 41): an 8.80 m thick wall of undressed stones crosses the valley end on the line of the present south end of Birket el-Hamra. Kenyon called it a dam wall. Its foundations cut fills with late Iron II (7th c. BCE) sherds, so she dated it to the 1st c. CE. A later ashlar wall with heavy bosses was built on it (p. 42). Foundation-trench pottery was mid- to late 1st c. CE, which she attributed to Agrippa I (p. 42).
- **Evidence: candidate sluice or overflow conduit** (p. 42): Kenyon also reached "a tunnel, or rather a built channel covered with stone slabs". It lay under soil with Iron II sherds, so she dated it to the Iron Age and took it for an overflow channel leaving Birket el-Hamra toward the SE. Reich and Shukron: "it cannot be known for now" whether this is a new channel or a re-excavated part of the late Second Temple great drainage channel.
- **Evidence** (p. 43): Kenyon's Area X, against the SE face of the buttressed dam, was apparently never dug. Avigad joined Kenyon's thick wall to the Iron II broad wall of the Western Hill (p. 43).

## 2. Reich and Shukron 2011, *City of David Studies* 6: 53–69 (Hebrew). The date of the Siloam Tunnel

- **Evidence** (pp. 56–58, Figs. 3–5): the tunnel was cut by two teams. The northern team did not start at the spring but in the "Round Chamber", the deep NE corner of the Rock-cut Pool, through Tunnel IV, while Channel II kept flowing. Tunnel VI was cut last, from west to east, to connect to the spring (p. 58).
- **Evidence** (pp. 60–62, Figs. 7–8): after the tunnel was finished, Channel II and the Rock-cut Pool went out of use. A dwelling was built inside the pool on earth fills, and those fills sealed the entrance to Tunnel IV. Fn. 2 (p. 60): the disturbed square above the Round Chamber held mixed Herodian and Iron II sherds.
- **Evidence (date)** (p. 63): the latest pottery under the floor predates Lachish Stratum III, "dated to the first part of the 8th century BCE" (De Groot and Fadida 2010). So Tunnel IV, the entrance to the northern branch, was cut then or slightly earlier, and the tunnel cannot be attributed to Hezekiah. The cutting took several years (p. 63).
- **Evidence** (p. 65 n. 3): Iron II loom weights in Warren's Shaft tunnel; a dwelling built in the Rock-cut Pool in the 8th c. BCE.
- **Inference**: this paper deals only with the north (Gihon) end. It records no trough, conduit junction, dam or bath at the Siloam pools. It matters here only as the *terminus* for the tunnel outlet: early 8th c. BCE or a little before.

## 3. Shukron and Reich 2011, *City of David Studies* 6: 157–185 (Hebrew). The central drainage channel

### Bliss and Dickie segment south of the pool
- **Evidence** (p. 165; plan Fig. 9, p. 166): south of the pool B&D followed only the channel. It has a gap of c. 52 m to point A3. At W2 it turns SW, and after c. 50 m more it passes "under the gate (point D2), out of the city" (B&D 1898: 88–96). It probably did not empty into the Kidron but ran on south (cf. Warren's channels near Bir Ayyub). Its runoff was perhaps meant for a large reservoir down the Kidron, to irrigate fields.

### Kenyon Area F channel (candidate overflow conduit near the dam)
- **Evidence** (p. 170): comparing Kenyon 1965 Fig. 2 with B&D Plan II (R2, P2, O2) shows that Kenyon did not expose the central channel "but another channel that perhaps served as an overflow channel of the Second Temple Siloam Pool". It may connect with B&D's southern segment "that exits through the city's south gate".
- **Inference**: Reich and Shukron 2010 (p. 42) report Kenyon's own Iron Age date for this channel. Here the same authors lean to a Second Temple overflow function. The date is unresolved in these sources.

### Great (western) channel at the pool
- **Evidence** (p. 174, Fig. 17): c. 70 m north of the pool the stepped street forks. Its western branch passes west of and above the pool, and under it the great channel continues out of the city. The eastern branch descends more steeply "and joins the paved plaza and the Siloam Pool".
- **Evidence (construction)** (pp. 175–176): two walls of large ashlars set mostly as headers, on bedrock, each c. 1 m thick. The mortar is dark grey to black with white grits and no crushed sherds, "the type found in 1st-c. CE Jerusalem" for water installations. Width c. 0.85 m; height c. 2 m; flat cover slabs c. 0.4 m thick and 0.5–0.6 m wide. The street paving lay directly on the covers. Walls and floor are unplastered.
- **Evidence (date)** (pp. 179–181): contents date only the last use. Fifteen coins in the fill above the northern vault, the latest of Pontius Pilate (26–36 CE) (p. 172), give only a general late Second Temple date for the vaulting (p. 180). Sometime in the 1st c. CE the channel was extended south with built walls and flat slabs "up to near the Siloam Pool, and beyond it to the Kidron" (p. 181).
- **Evidence** (p. 177): the western channel "bypassed the Siloam Pool on the west and discharged its water to the Kidron".

### Eastern channel at the paved plaza (candidate esplanade junction)
- **Evidence** (p. 177): Reich and Shukron found a drainage channel near the east end of the paved plaza north of the Second Temple pool. It runs north. "As found it did not open into the Siloam Pool because of a blockage at its south, but originally it did." A small segment may appear on Guthe 1882 Plan II near point D, as "the outlet of the channel arriving from the north", drawn while Guthe was tracing the overflow channel of the Byzantine pool.
- **Evidence (date)** (p. 178): debris on the street and the channel fill were both dated by pottery and coins to the 70 CE destruction. The channel's last use was during the siege (Josephus).
- **Evidence** (p. 157): the channel under the eastern street segment is low: "one can barely crawl in it on all fours".
- **Inference**: this is the clearest recorded *conduit junction with the main stepped pool* other than L103. It entered the pool from the north, from the plaza side, and was blocked at its south end before or at 70 CE. The paper gives no locus number, no dimensions at the junction and no construction date beyond use up to 70 CE.

## 4. Grossberg 2011, *City of David Studies* 6: 71–125 (Hebrew). Channel II and the Siloam Tunnel

- **Evidence** (p. 72): the northern Channel II dates to MB II (c. 1800 BCE). Its southern part was cut as a tunnel in Iron II, about a thousand years later, and "ended in the rock cliff above the pool at the end of the central valley" (Figs. 3, 28).
- **Evidence** (p. 72; Fig. 5, p. 74): the Siloam Tunnel ends "in a small pool at the south end of the tunnel", part of the Byzantine Siloam Pool (Guthe 1882; B&D 1898: 152–157, Pl. XVI). "From there the water flowed south to the pool at the end of the central valley."
- **Evidence (plan)** (p. 73, Fig. 1): Channel II (orange) runs down the east slope and ends at the NE/upper edge of the stepped pool, labelled "בריכת השילוח / הבריכה התחתונה" (Siloam Pool / Lower Pool). The tunnel (blue) loops west to the small pool at the NW. The Canaanite-Israelite wall (red) runs along the pool's SE side. Fig. 3 (p. 73): the rock-cut south end of Channel II.
- **Evidence (photo)** (p. 104, Fig. 28): caption: Second Temple Siloam Pool, looking NW, "and the south end of Channel II (on the right, marked by an arrow)". The opening is high in the rock face above the NE steps.
- **Inference**: Channel II's terminus is a conduit junction at the main stepped pool, at its NE corner under the City of David cliff. It is Iron II (8th c. BCE: Reich and Shukron; c. 701 BCE in Grossberg's thesis) and went dry once the Siloam Tunnel worked. It is not the outlet of the Siloam Tunnel.
- **Evidence** (p. 76 n. 4): Channel IV ("the overflow channel"), which carries Gihon water from the end of the Siloam Tunnel to the Kidron, "was not cut as part of the Siloam Tunnel but only in the Second Temple period". It "served as the feed channel of the Siloam Pool". In the First Temple period the water flowed straight south down the Tyropoeon to the pool at the valley's end (Reich and Shukron 2006). Same point on p. 85.
- **Inference**: this links Reich and Shukron 2010 p. 30 ("feed channel, formerly overflow channel") to Channel IV. It is a Second Temple conduit linking the tunnel outlet with the main stepped pool, distinct from L103.
- **Evidence** (p. 77 n. 7): Reich and Shukron reject the theory that the "windows" were irrigation outlets. In the southern part "no sign was found of water channels leading from Channel II to the Kidron". Opening 106-5 is unplanned (pp. 97–99, Figs. 20–24).
- **Evidence** (pp. 95–96): the southern Channel II was dated by Reich and Shukron to the 8th c. BCE. In their Area A (290–325 m south of the Gihon; Fig. 1 point 3) a shaft was cut in both directions.
- **Evidence** (p. 103): Grossberg identifies the "Lower Pool" with Birket el-Hamra. It was probably smaller than the Second Temple pool and lay in its low central part. He rejects Avi-Yonah's identification with the pool at the tunnel end (Byzantine), but if an earlier pool is found there it might be the "pool" of 2 Kgs 20:20 (p. 103; also p. 111).
- **Evidence** (p. 105): the late Iron Age wall crossing the Tyropoeon borders the SE side of the Second Temple pool (citing Reich and Shukron 2010). The pool belongs to the mid-1st c. BCE to the end of the Second Temple period.
- **Inference**: Grossberg records no trough, bath or junction other than Channel II's terminus and Channel IV.

## 5. Weksler-Bdolah and Lavi 2013, HA-ESI 125 (Area A, c. 50 m south of the pool)

- **Evidence** (export p. 1): a 17 m trench on the SE slope of the Mount Zion spur, dug to bedrock (632.5 m in the middle). It has Strata V–I: late Iron Age, Persian, Early Roman to Early Islamic.
- **Evidence: basin** (pp. 1–2, Figs. 2–3): Stratum IVc is a plastered installation (diam. c. 1.75 m) with two superposed plaster floors (L141 below, L138 above), on Stratum V fill. Pottery on the floors and in the sealed fill dates it to Herod's reign (second half of the 1st c. BCE).
- **Inference**: this is a small round plastered basin c. 50 m south of the pool, outside the line of the dam. There is no evidence that it is a trough or that a conduit fed it.
- **Evidence** (p. 2): the IVa ashlar collapse (Hasmonean dressing, signs of fire, 1st c. CE finds) is linked to 70 CE and probably comes from the First Wall. Enclosure wall W8 (IIIb) is Late Roman (2nd–4th c. CE or a little later).
- **Evidence: city wall and gate** (p. 3, Figs. 10–12): the SE corner of a tower (W1, W2), 4 m preserved, of hard limestone ashlars. Bliss and Dickie had recorded it (1897: 94–96, Pl. XI). It postdates W8/W6, "was apparently built after the fourth century CE", and "can be identified with the southeastern gate tower in the Byzantine-period city wall".

## 6. Weksler-Bdolah and Szanton 2014, HA-ESI 126 (Areas B, C, just south of the pool junction)

- **Evidence: general plan** (Fig. 2, export p. 8): the dam (hatched green, "Iron Age II (8th Century BCE)") runs along the SE side of the Shiloah Pool. South of its SW end is a "Pool" (Greenhut and Mazor 2009, A-5653), outlined in the yellow of the "Early Roman (1st Century CE)" legend. Red Hellenistic/Early Roman walls run south.
- **Evidence: channel L133** (p. 2; Fig. 3, p. 9, coloured blue "Iron Age"): a rock-hewn drainage channel curving from north to SE. Exposed 2 m, c. 0.4 m wide, dug to 0.5 m, covered with large slabs. It "may have also belonged to" Phase 4a (8th c. BCE). It "was deliberately blocked and canceled when the gateway was installed in the 'First Wall'". It is "very similar to a channel of the First Temple period" in Kenyon's Area F (1965: Pl. 7B, Fig. 2).
- **Inference**: L133 sits at the inner side of gate L127, south of the pool and outside the dam. It is a drain, not a pool junction. The "similar" Kenyon F channel is the same feature that Shukron and Reich 2011 p. 170 call a possible Second Temple overflow of the pool.
- **Evidence: First Wall W11 and gate L127** (p. 3, Figs. 3, 7, 10): W11 is c. 20 m long and 3.0–3.3 m wide, built over quarries. A previously unknown gate L127 (c. 3 m wide) has a nari jamb and two threshold stones in situ (634 m asl). South wall W21 is Hasmonean-dressed. Pavement L115 (635 m) lies c. 3 m NW, inside. Fill L206 holds late 2nd to mid-1st c. BCE pottery. "The wall (W11) and the gate … were built in the Hasmonean period", identified as the First Wall.
- **Evidence: tower L119 with reservoir** (pp. 3–4, Figs. 11–12): the tower is c. 5 × 7 m and juts east, south of the gate (B&D called it a "small, open cistern", 4.20 × 6.53 m). Its south wall W10 has Hasmonean drafted ashlars. Inside is a reservoir with grey hydraulic plaster, seven steps in W12 and a central pillar base. The reservoir was probably inserted in Phase 3d.
- **Evidence** (p. 4, Figs. 13–14): W30, a Hasmonean-dressed wall with delta mason's marks, bounded a street or square inside the gate. An opening (2.75 m) between W11 and W30 to the tower was later blocked.
- **Evidence: Phase 3d–3c** (p. 4): the First Wall was repaired and "the gateway was probably blocked". The reservoir was installed. A "stepped plastered pool" L124 (3 × 2.5 m, c. 2.5 m deep, six steps) "was added north of the gateway"; its south wall overlies Pavement 115. Its northern part was exposed by Greenhut and Mazor in 2009. Terrace walls W18, W20 and W25 hold 1st c. CE pottery.
- **Evidence** (p. 5): ash L223 (3b) and ashlar collapse L122 (3a) mark the 70 CE destruction. Post-70 Tenth Legion pottery and a possible milestone base lead the authors to suggest "Legion soldiers probably guarded the pool".
- **Inference**: L124 is a stepped plastered pool (an immersion pool or cistern type), not a trough. It lies outside or south of the dam, by the gate.

## 7. Weksler-Bdolah and Hagbi 2022, HA-ESI 134 (Areas D, E, 2016)

- **Evidence: the dam** (pp. 1–2, Figs. 3, 6): broad wall W40 + W51 (total length ≥7.5 m, width ≥7 m) at the Tyropoeon outlet. It is built of large partly dressed boulders (0.4–0.5 × 0.5 × 0.7–0.8 m) in gray mortar with charcoal; each course was capped with a cast leveling layer 0.20–0.25 m thick. The SE face of W40 is B&D's R2. Four courses of W51 sit on alluvium; bedrock was not reached. It "would appear to be the southernmost part of a broad wall that was built across the outlet of the Tyropoeon Valley and probably served as a dam".
- **Evidence (date)** (p. 2): "The excavation yielded no clearly dated, diagnostic finds." Tiny Herodian body sherds (1st c. BCE–1st c. CE) among the upper-course stones may be intrusive. Mortar samples were taken for radiocarbon dating. The Fig. 2 legend (export p. 6) now reads "Iron Age II or later" for the hatched dam.
- **Inference**: the dam date has been softened from "8th c. BCE" (2014 plan) to "Iron Age II or later" (2022). Reich and Shukron 2010 date its inner face (W1020) to late Iron II from the clay plaster and one rim.
- **Evidence** (p. 2): W41 is a rock-hewn wall on the line of B&D's Hasmonean "wall to the west of the pool", continuing W30 of 2014.
- **Evidence: stepped pool wall W50** (p. 3; Area E2 on Fig. 3): a plastered wall (exposed 3 m × 0.6 m, 1.2 m high), "the southwestern delimiting wall of a stepped pool" (L124) "immediately south of the Pool of Siloam", dated to the late Second Temple period (Guthe 1882 Pl. III; Weksler-Bdolah 2013: 181–182; Greenhut and Mazor 2020: 137). Its northern face has grey hydraulic plaster.
- **Evidence: channel L408** (p. 3, Figs. 3, 7): a rock-hewn channel (exposed 3 m, 0.80–0.85 m wide) running NW→SE "toward the city wall", with NE wall W42. It is "probably part of a previously documented municipal drainage channel" and "led to a gate in the city wall". "The excavation yielded no finds that could date the channel."
- **Inference**: L408 matches the great drainage channel's course west of and below the pool toward B&D's gate D2 (Shukron and Reich 2011 p. 165). It bypasses the pools and does not join them.
