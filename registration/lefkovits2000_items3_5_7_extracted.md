# Lefkovits 2000: his readings at I 6–8, I 13–15 and II 3–4

**Labels:** **Evidence** = Lefkovits prints or states it. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## Item 3 (I 6b–8), p. 64 — entry 3

- **Evidence — text.** "בבור הגדול שבחצר / הפרסטלון בירכ קרקעו סתום בחליא / נגד הפתח העליון ככרין תשע מאת".
- **Evidence — translation.** "In the great cistern which is in the Court of the Peristyle, at the side of its bottom concealed in the circular-wall, opposite to the upper entrance, (there are) nine hundred (unspecified) talents."
- **Inference.** He reads הפרסטלון, Puech's spelling; Milik's הפרסטלין differs only in the mater. All three editions therefore read the same anchor words: the court of the peristyle (חצר הפרסטלון), the cistern (בור) and the upper entrance (פתח העליון). His "concealed in the circular-wall" is a sense for סתום בחליא, which is not an anchor word.

## Item 5 (I 13–15), pp. 90–96 — entry 5

- **Evidence — text (p. 90).** "בשיא המסבא של מנס בירדא לסמל / גבה מן הקרקע אמות שלוש [כ]סף ארבעין / [כ]כר".
- **Evidence — translation (p. 90).** "In the tower of the staircase of Manos, in the descent to the left, three cubits above the ground, (there are) forty [ta]lents of [si]lver."
- **Evidence — the drawings (p. 91).** For the first two words, Baker's drawing has "a dotted curve that may be the right part of an ʿayin or samekh"; Milik's has "ʿayin (a dotted curve plus a solid right hand)"; Allegro's has samekh. "Milik's and Allegro's third letter is absent in Baker's drawing."
- **Evidence — the word (p. 93).** "The second word is הַמְּסִבָּא or מְסִבָּא (depends whether the he/ḥet/taw belongs to this word or the previous one)", meaning "staircase", from סבב. He cites the Temple Scroll, the Mishnah (m. Mid. 1:9) and Targum, and Yadin's reading of a second occurrence at XI 6–7 (#52).
- **Evidence — alternatives (pp. 90, 96).** He lists Milik's "In a hole of the mine which is at Manos" (בשוא המעבא) and Beyer's "In the pit of the ground-foundry of Mannos", and gives a table of further combinations, among them "In/at/by a/the pit of the Maʿaveh/foundry/pit" (p. 96).
- **Inference.** Two of the three editions (Puech and Lefkovits) read samekh, the staircase; Milik reads ʿayin. The drawings divide the same way. No plate check in the repo covers I 13.

## Item 7 (II 3–4), pp. 106–110 — entry 7

- **Evidence — text (p. 106).** "במערת בית חמדה הישן ברובד / השלשי {ל} עשתות זהב ששין וחמש ΘΕ".
- **Evidence — translation (p. 106).** "In the cave of Old Beth Ḥemdah, in the third row of stones, (there are) sixty five masses of unused gold. (Code:) ΘΕ."
- **Evidence — the letters (p. 110).** "The problem is with the third word that modifies בית. It consists of four letters, each can be read in two or three different ways: he/ḥet/taw, mem/bet, dalet/reš, and he/ḥet/taw. Thus, there are 36 possible letter combinations for this word." Note 22 lists them.
- **Evidence — others (p. 109).** Allegro's "the Old House of Tribute" (המדה); Milik's בית המרה הישן; Wolters's בית המדח הישן, "the old Washer's Chamber"; he notes the Zuckerman photographs show the last letter of הישן "much longer than a usual waw".
- **Evidence — a list of בית forms (p. 107).** Among them "בית המים, 'the water-closet, laundry,' or בית חמים, 'the hot-house, hot-bath-house, hot-spring-house' (see #49, 10:15)" and "בתי העצין, 'the wood-sheds' (alt., see #8, 2:5)".
- **Inference.** Lefkovits prints חמדה but treats every letter of the word as uncertain. The four editions' readings (המדה, המרה, המדח, חמדה) remain four letter sets; the reading limit for entry 7 is unchanged. The p. 107 list confirms his 'water-closet' sense at entry 49 and shows a 'wood-sheds' alternative at II 5 (entry 8).
