# Bull 1965 and 1968: the Tell er-Ras fosse, stairs and cisterns (entry 57)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = the authors state it. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## The fosse

- **Evidence (1965 p. 236, page image).** "A great fosse had been dug along the southern side to separate it completely from the slope that rose gradually to the southern peak. Is this the 'fosse' that is mentioned on Mt. Gerizim in the Copper Scroll from Qumran Cave 3?" Note 7 quotes Milik's translation of col. XII, "Au mont Garizim, sous les marches de la fosse supérieure", and his discussion of שיח, rendered "fosse" (DJD III pp. 241, 298).
- **Evidence (1965 p. 236).** "It seems doubtful that the 'preparation' of this tell was done entirely by Hadrian. There is surely something older there." Ancient terraces are evenly spaced around the mound; "a two-meter wide ramp makes ascent to the top very easy".
- **Evidence (1968 p. 59).** Tell er-Ras is a conical mound "not more than 400 feet long and 265 feet wide" on the peak at the end of Gerizim's northern ridge. "The southern limit of the mound is marked by a wide east-west fosse or trench which was dug in such a manner as to isolate the mound from the rest of the ridge. Whether or not this is to be identified with the 'upper fosse' on Mt. Gerizim mentioned in the Copper Scroll ... will require further examination."
- **Evidence.** Neither article reports excavating, sectioning or dating the fosse.

## Steps and stairs

- **Evidence (1965 p. 235).** Wilson (SWP *Memoirs* II p. 192) saw "traces of steps on the four sides leading to the summit of the mound".
- **Evidence (1968 pp. 68, 72).** On the northern slope, Kee found terraces "each connected by a series of five or more steps", and rock-hewn steps in six areas on a line from the north end of the enclosure toward Nablus. "The line of steps up the side of Mt. Gerizim are certainly to be identified with those noted by the Bordeaux Pilgrim and Epiphanius and depicted on the Neapolis mint coins" (coins of 138–253 CE). The stairs led to the north gate of the temenos (1965 p. 237).

## Buildings and cisterns

- **Evidence (1965 p. 236; 1968 pp. 62–63, 70–71).** Building A, the temple of Zeus, 14 × 22.50 m on a three-stepped stylobate, is Hadrianic (2nd century CE), with 4th- and probably 6th-century use. Below it, Building B (20.93 × 20.14 m, on bedrock) has Hellenistic pottery (3rd–2nd centuries BCE); Bull offers it as the Samaritan temple, "certainly nothing more than a hypothesis so far" (1968 p. 71).
- **Evidence (1968 pp. 68–70).** Six plastered, barrel-vaulted cisterns (about 4.2 × 3.6–4.2 m) were built against the north face of the enclosure wall, three on each side of the stone ramp. The silt in their bottoms held some fifty coins that "all fall between A.D. 285 and 363, and are for the most part from the time of Constantine" (p. 70), and two dedications to Zeus Olympius.
- **Image (1968 Fig. 18, p. 71).** The plan, with north to the right, shows Building A on Building B inside the temenos, and the cisterns (I–III and their pair) at the north wall by the ramp. The fosse and the stairs are not on the plan.

## Inference for entry 57 (XII 4–5)

- The repo's candidate is Magen's sacred precinct on the main summit. Tell er-Ras is a different place on Gerizim, the northern peak. Bull's proposal gives a specific feature, a fosse, matching Milik's sense of שיח, but not the relation the entry needs: the fosse is on the south side, the stairs come up the north side, and no step is recorded at the fosse.
- Nothing dates the fosse. The stairs and the temple are Hadrianic and later (138 CE onward on the coins). Building B is Hellenistic; the window (50 BCE–70 CE) is not represented in what Bull reports.
- The ratings for the summit candidate do not change (R 3, P 3, F 1, D 1). A Tell er-Ras branch would start with F 1 (no step relation) and an undated fosse. Bull's AJA 1967 report (not read) and any later section of the fosse are the next records.
