# Avigad 1962: the Karm esh-Sheikh ossuary tomb (Kidron Valley)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = Avigad states it, or it is drawn on his plan. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## The tomb

- **Evidence (p. 1).** Found on 10 November 1941 in the Hebrew University's search for Jewish tombs in the Kidron Valley; excavated by E. L. Sukenik with Avigad. Serial No. XXX. Only a short note appeared before (BASOR 88, 1942, p. 38; *Kedem* 1, 1942, p. 104).
- **Evidence (p. 1).** The tomb is "south of Silwan village at a site named Karm esh-Sheikh on the south-western slope of the Kidron Valley". The entrance, 0.97 × 0.58 m, was closed by a stopper-shaped stone with a marginal draft and a rough boss. The tomb was found intact.
- **Evidence (pp. 1–2, Fig. 1).** One rectangular chamber, 3.1 × 4.65 m and 1.15 m high, with a rectangular standing pit (2.1 × 2.9 m, 1 m deep). The pit leaves three shelves 0.8–1 m wide along the walls, but not along the entrance wall. A rock partition and a row of built stones divide the pit. The pit walls and door jambs have poor mud-and-lime plaster.
- **Evidence (p. 2).** "This kind of single tomb-chamber with shelves and no *kokhim* is rather rare among ossuary-tombs."
- **Evidence (Fig. 1, plan; page image).** The north arrow points left. The entrance (section A) is on the left side of the plan, so the entrance is in the north wall. Ossuaries 1–10 lie along the east side and no. 11 by the west shelf, as the text says.
- **Evidence (p. 2).** Eleven ossuaries; 13 pottery vessels and some sherds; decayed bones on the south and west shelves (primary burial on the shelves, then gathered into ossuaries).

## Date

- **Evidence (p. 3).** The pottery (a cooking pot, piriform unguentaria, globular juglets, a fusiform alabastron and a "Herodian" lamp) is "typical of funerary equipment found in the Jewish ossuary-tombs around Jerusalem". Its "attribution to the first century A.D. (Herodian period) seems to be well established. Some forms go back to the first century B.C."
- **Evidence (p. 12).** Burial took place "during the first century A.D., before the destruction of the Second Temple".

## Inscriptions (pp. 4–11)

- **Evidence (p. 12).** Nine of the eleven ossuaries are inscribed: eight in Greek, one bilingual and one in Hebrew. Several names are common in Cyrenaica; one member came from Ptolemais.
- **Evidence (pp. 10–12).** One ossuary reads "Alexander (son) of Simon", with a Hebrew word read as a nickname, QRNYT (Qornit). Avigad notes the similarity to Simon of Cyrene, father of Alexander and Rufus (Mark 15:21), and calls it possibly "a sheer coincidence". Against Milik, he says the inscriptions show only Alexander was buried in that ossuary.
- **Evidence.** The article does not mention the Copper Scroll.

## What this adds

- **Inference (tomb register).** The register lists two records under this name. ASI 106/38 "Karm esh Sheikh (M)" (`research/regional/tombs/tombs.csv`, T0376; burial cave, ossuaries) is south of Silwan and matches the article's location. Its attributes there are unknown. Avigad gives them first-hand: a single chamber with a standing pit and shelves, no kokhim, entrance in the north wall, intact, and 1st c. CE pottery. The other record, ASI 102/339 Karm esh-Sheikh by the Rockefeller Museum (T0194), is a different place north of the Old City.
- **Inference (entry 56, Kidron candidate).** The tomb is dated in the window, but it fails all three parts of the 56-P triad: it is not west-facing, has only one room, and has no kokhim. It is also on the south-western slope, not in the Silwan necropolis on the east slope. No rating changes.
