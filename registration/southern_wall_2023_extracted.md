## Strata and the c. 40 BCE–70 CE window

### Southeastern corner, Area A

**Evidence.** Area A measures “16 × 11.5 m” and lies “ca. 45 m east of the Triple Gate.” Area B measures “15 × 11 m,” approximately 16 m farther east, against the Ophel Wall. These are gate-relative positions; the text does not supply a surveyed distance from the SE cornerstone to every installation. Ch. 4, “Location,” p. 65.

**Evidence.** Source: Ch. 4, “Area A,” Table 4.1, p. 68; detailed strata pp. 66–79. The table's footnote says post-Byzantine remains, if present, were removed by earlier work. This excavation therefore cannot establish their original absence.

**Inference.** Stratum III is the principal target horizon. Stratum IV can overlap the earlier part of c. 40 BCE–70 CE, but “pre-Herodian” is defined here by its relationship to the expansion works, not by a precisely dated universal boundary. The whole of Stratum III should not be compressed into 40 BCE–70 CE because the table begins it in the first century BCE.

**Evidence.** Stratum II has two phases: IIa, floor L6110 over fill L6111; IIb, floor L6109, fills L6131–6132 and channel L6078. IIa's underlying ceramics extend from the middle first to second century CE; IIb's fills contain second–third-century ceramics. These later contexts can contain earlier objects. Ch. 4, “Stratum II: Late Roman,” pp. 77–78.

## Royal Stoa / Royal Portico and the two ends

**Evidence.** Ch. 38, “The Herodian Period,” p. 603, quotes Josephus, *Antiquities* 15.411, describing the royal cloisters as extending “from the east valley, unto that on the west.” This gives an east–west extent across the southern enclosure in the ancient description. The editors immediately caution that they understand this passage as referring to the southward expansion as a whole, rather than just one structure.

**Evidence.** Fallen decorated architectural fragments near the SE corner are attributed to the Royal Portico, with a possible contribution from the Triple Gate (Ch. 38, “Evidence of Destruction,” p. 604). Ch. 27's narrower pilasters are another proposed Royal Portico attribution (p. 393). Neither statement excavates an eastern or western terminal wall.

**Evidence (negative finding).** The permitted chapters do not locate an eastern terminal exedra at the SE corner, a western terminal exedra at the SW corner, or any named exedra. They do not give a locus or surveyed endpoint for either end of the stoa. Robinson's Arch is described as a western access structure; these chapters do not make its pier the excavated western end of a stoa/exedra.

**Inference.** An eastern-valley to western-valley southern portico is useful broad setting for entry 51. Assigning its “southern corner,” Zadok tomb or exedra pillar to a particular excavated point would require evidence beyond these chapters.

## SW construction chronology: radiocarbon and competing arguments

**Evidence.** The combined twigs below the lowest Western Wall course have a **95.4% calibrated range of 44 BCE–70 CE**. At 68.3%, the range separates into three intervals. The authors conclude that the “precise building date remains open.” Ch. 33, “Western Wall—Under Lowest Stone Course,” p. 515; Table 33.1, pp. 514–515.

**Evidence (authors' conclusions).** The cistern is assigned to the First Temple period; the internal wall's date fits a Herodian or slightly later repair connected with damage during Western Wall construction. The middle channel dates are post-638 CE; the authors prefer a late-seventh-century construction interpretation on historical grounds, while acknowledging the later interval. These channel dates do not date initial construction of the Herodian corner. Ch. 33, pp. 513–516.

**Evidence (disagreement within the volume).** Ch. 27, pp. 421–422, treats coins in the bath fill as a *terminus post quem* for the wall, reporting latest Gratus coins of 17/18 CE and suggesting wall construction in the 20s–30s CE and Robinson's Arch in the second quarter of the century. Ch. 38, pp. 602–603, repeats a post-Gratus wall argument. Ch. 25, pp. 353–355, disputes the sealing relationship of L55: it argues that fill and slabs abut the wall and belong with the street-support works, so they do not securely date the wall itself. Its preferred model links the low beaten-earth surfaces L14003, L14029 and L14042 to building the cells after the wall's first courses.

**Inference.** The book does not deliver a consensus decade for construction of the SW corner. The 44 BCE–70 CE radiocarbon interval is a broad independent constraint, not proof of either a date under Herod or a specific prefect. It overlaps nearly all the project's target window.

## What the book does NOT say in the permitted material

- **Evidence (absence in read scope):** it does not identify a tomb of Zadok at either southern corner or beneath the Southern Wall; it does not connect any excavated locus with Copper Scroll entries 51, 12/12a or 53.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not identify L6124 as a burial cave/tomb, report burials inside the Herodian ramp/support cells, or give the undetermined cavity's full interior dimensions or depth.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not report an exedra at either end of the Royal Stoa, or a pillar/column base above a Zadok tomb. Fallen pilaster blocks and column drums do not supply such an in-situ relationship.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not give excavated terminal coordinates/loci for the Royal Stoa's eastern and western ends. The broad ancient valley-to-valley description is not a surveyed endpoint plan.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not name a rock “knife” or identify a distinctive spur/cliff/scarp with a tomb beneath it.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not provide a secure exterior Herodian street elevation at the exact SE corner, so it does not supply the requested same-point SE street-to-bedrock difference.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not give a corner-relative distance, complete dimensions, depth and date for every installation mentioned. The northern Shukron features and Ch. 33 cistern lack several of those fields within the allowed sections.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not establish a single agreed construction decade for the SW corner. Chapter 25 disputes an inference made in Chapter 27, and the radiocarbon authors explicitly leave the precise date open.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not reconcile the Area B excavation-depth statements, the coin locus contradictions, the phase-caption reversals, or the noted inconsistent numerical/unit statements.
- **Evidence (absence):** it does not show that nine- or sixteen-cubit instructions were measured from any of the excavated street/floor surfaces. It provides no archaeological identification based on those numbers.
- **Evidence (scope limit):** this report cannot exclude evidence in the deliberately skipped chapters, especially the full architectural-fragment study or full cemetery report. Their absence here is a limit of the authorized reading scope, not proof that the volume contains no additional information.
