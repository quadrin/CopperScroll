# Cargill 2009: Qumran water-system phases for entries 20–23 (R01)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = Cargill states it (his views are his interpretation). **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## Dam and channel (§6 intro, §6.1, pp. 125–126; §10.2.1, p. 210)

- **Evidence.** A dam in Wadi Qumran redirected water from Nahal Qumran down a channel cut into the south end of Magen and Peleg's "Flow Basin A" and into the site. This channel was the site's primary water source (p. 125).
- **Evidence.** De Vaux gave no date for the dam and channel, but dated the round cistern L110 to the Iron Age. Cross and Milik reported similar dams and channels at the Iron II forts Kh. el-Maqari and Kh. Abu Tabaq. Cargill concludes that the dam and channel were "initially established during the Iron Age", with L110 as their terminal reservoir (pp. 125–126). His Phase 1 repeats this: the dam and channel "see their origins in the Iron Age"; in the Hasmonean phase L110 was replastered and the channel improved (p. 210).
- **Evidence — citation.** His note 339 (p. 125) cites Magen and Peleg, *Preliminary Report* p. 32, for the view that there was no Iron Age aqueduct, and cites Ilan and Amit 2002, p. 385, "for the Iron Age dating of the aqueduct".
- **Inference.** Stacey cites the same Ilan–Amit page, p. 385, for a date under Herod Archelaus and a low dam ([word-level check](ilan_amit2002_p385_check.md)). Two secondary authors therefore report different dates from one page. The page text is still unread, so neither report can be preferred. Reading p. 385 now settles two citations, not one.

## The L110–L117–L118–L119 junction (§§6.4–6.6, pp. 127–131)

- **Evidence.** L110 is about 6 m deep and 5.3 m across, with no steps; its capacity estimates run from 110 to 138 m³ (p. 127). Its date is disputed: de Vaux and Magness say Iron Age; Hirschfeld and Magen–Peleg say Hasmonean, from its hydraulic plaster; Magness answers that the plaster is a Hasmonean replastering (p. 128).
- **Evidence.** When the Main and Auxiliary Buildings were built, L117 and L118 were dug, and the channel was redirected clockwise around and east of L110 towards L83. Besides an outlet running under the channel towards L118, a "backflow channel" passed water through the small sedimentation basin L119 and south into L117 (p. 128).
- **Evidence.** De Vaux, Magness and Magen–Peleg agree that L117 and L118 belong to the earliest Hasmonean phases (p. 129, citing de Vaux p. 4, Magness p. 49, Magen–Peleg p. 37). L118 is 7.5 × 2.5 m, about 26 m³. A small channel south of L118 runs under the main channel to an opening in L110's north wall and may be an overflow outlet (p. 129, citing Hirschfeld Fig. 37).
- **Evidence.** L117 is nearly the same shape and size as L118. It is fed from L119 and from a channel to the north that starts in L125. Two plaster partitions run down its steps (pp. 130–131).
- **Inference (R01).** Cargill describes how L117, L118 and L119 connect, but gives no section, contact or dated deposit at the L117 outlet, rim or steps. The L117 joint test still lacks its dated contact.

## Sedimentation areas (§6.3, p. 127)

- **Evidence.** L132, L136 and L137 form a flat area that caught flash-flood water entering the sluice gate and let silt settle. Following Magness (p. 61), L132 silted up in Period Ib, and a second channel in L137 then led water south.

## Copper Scroll and Cave 3 (§8.3.3, pp. 162–164)

- **Evidence.** Caves 3 and 11 lie farther from the settlement than the other scroll caves. The Copper Scroll "differs in language, medium, script, and form from all other scrolls." Cargill suggests that the residents of Qumran may not have placed the contents of Caves 3 and 11 (p. 162). Note 478 reports Milik's dating of the scroll to about 100 CE, after Qumran's destruction.
- **Evidence.** The scroll "appears to date to a later period based upon paleographic and linguistic grounds" (p. 163), and its contents appear "to have little, if anything, to do with a desert community" (p. 164).
- **Inference.** This is a secondary judgement that rests on Milik. It adds no new dating evidence for R12.

## Chronology (§10.2, pp. 209–213)

- **Evidence.** Phase 1, Iron II: a small fort and L110. Phase 2: a Hasmonean fortress, 140–130 BCE (de Vaux Ia), whose location was chosen "only because of a preexisting water system". Phase 3: sectarian reoccupation around 110–100 BCE (Ib). Phase 4: 31 BCE to 68 CE (Period II).

## Chapter 6 plates (viewed)

- **Evidence — Pl. 6.1.** Two photographs of the remains of the L138 miqveh show two flights of steps descending into it, beside a reconstruction.
- **Evidence — Pl. 6.2 caption.** L110 "was fed by a backflow channel from the south, which branched west from the main channel to the east. An overflow channel (red arrow) connects Locus 110 to the Locus 118 pool to the north."
- **Evidence — Pl. 6.3 caption.** L118 "was fed by a backflow channel branching from the main channel, as well as from an overflow channel leading from the round Locus 110 cistern."
- **Inference.** The captions make the small channel carry water from L110 to L118. The text (p. 129) only says it "may have served as an overflow outlet". Neither gives a level or a contact, so the direction is Cargill's model, not an observation.
- **Pls. 6.2–6.4, 6.6.** These are renders of Cargill's virtual model (L110 with its channels, L118 and L117 with their steps and partitions, L69). They are reconstructions. They do not record the L117 outlet, rim or step joint as found.

## Chapter 8–9 plates (viewed)

- **Evidence — captions.** Chapter 8 has only three plates: 8.1 the cemetery east of the Main Building (a render), 8.2 a reconstruction of Caves 7–9, and 8.3 a photograph of the opening of Cave 4. Plates 6.15–6.19 are renders of L68, L69–70, L71 and the potter's area; the chapter 7 plates show the Auxiliary Building and potter's workshop.
- **Evidence — Pl. 9.1.** A satellite map of "the string of fortifications the Hasmoneans built along the eastern border of their kingdom": Dok, Kypros, Rujm al-Bahr, Qumran, ʿEin Feshkha, Hyrcania, Kh. Mazin, Machaerus, ʿEin Gedi, Masada and ʿEin Boqeq.
- **Inference.** The book has no plate of the dam, the aqueduct head, the gorge, the tunnel or the natural basin. For those features the Allegro plates (pls. 143–147) remain the only photographs read in this round.
