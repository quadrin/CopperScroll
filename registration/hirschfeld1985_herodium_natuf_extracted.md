# 'Ain en-Natuf in the Map of Herodium survey: extract

**Source.** Y. Hirschfeld, *Archaeological Survey of Israel, Map of Herodium (108/2)* (1985), IAA online edition (2013), exported to PDF.
- English: `herodium_EN.pdf` (218 pp.).
- Hebrew: `herodium_HE.pdf` (226 pp.).

Page numbers below are PDF page numbers. They equal the "Online-source export | N" footer on each page. The export notes that it "preserves the website text and its transcription errors"; typos in quotations (e.g. "leqo'a", "perrenial") are the source's own.

**Labels.**
- **Evidence** means the survey states it. Every item gives the site number, and the edition when it is Hebrew. Hebrew passages are my English translations.
- **Inference** means my own reasoning or measurement.

**Method.**
1. I extracted text with `pdftotext -layout`.
2. I parsed all 123 entries in each language and computed distances from the New Israel Grid values. The grid values are rounded to 100 m.
3. I rendered the illustration pages with `pdftoppm` and inspected them.
4. Search terms included natuf / nattuf / en-natuf / נטוף, dovecote / columbar / pigeon / cote / loculi / שובך / קולומבר / יונים, coin / מטבע, karst / קארסט, Roman / Hellenist / Herod.

## A. Where 'Ain en-Natuf sits in the survey

- **Evidence (site 27, EN p. 79; HE p. 83).** The spring is recorded **only** as feature **No. 12 within site 27**, an unnamed site of Byzantine monks' cells. The English names it "'Ein en-Natuf" and the Hebrew "עין א-נטוף" ('Ein a-Natuf).
- **Evidence.** No other entry, and no part of the introduction in either edition, names the spring.
- **Evidence.** Site 27's reference point is New Israel Grid 616797, 222389 (WGS-84 31.6436426, 35.2354262), field no. 17-11/26/2.
- **Inference.** On Fig. 16 the spring lies within ca. 0.1-0.2 km of that point, about 0.45 km S of the monastery (site 17) and 0.3 km N of the Hanging Cave (site 26). All distances below are measured from the site-27 grid point. Allow roughly ±0.2 km.

### Site 27. Unnamed site

*EN PDF pp. 79-81; HE PDF pp. 82-84. This is the reference site; 'Ein en-Natuf is its feature No. 12 (Inference, computed from the grids below; grid values are rounded to 100 m).*

**Evidence (header fields, verbatim):**

- Field Num: 17-11/26/2
- New Israel Grid: 616797, 222389
- WGS-84: 31.6436425969935, 35.2354262440246
- Period: *(no Period field in this entry)*
- Finds: Hermit's cell,Cupmark,Water cistern,Vault,Spring,Plaster,Hydraulic plaster,Structure,Cement,Terrace,Cell

**Evidence (description, verbatim, English edition):**

> On the slope between the Chariton monastery and the "Hanging Cave", remains of several Byzantine monks' cells were discovered (Fig. 38). Most of them include a cistern covered by a simple structure and a small, adjacent plot. The cells are scattered across the slope, in no particular arrangement, taking advantage of various patches of ground. Of the northernmost cell No. 1, a cistern (4.9x5.9 m.) survives, alongside a terrace wall ca. 8 m. long. Cell No. 2 was built over a rectangular cistern (4.5x6.5 m.), the ceiling of which is preserved to a height of 4.5 m. above the floor. The remains of cell No. 3 are scant; next to it is a round cistern. Cell No. 4 was built over a cistern (5.6x6.6 m.), the arch of which is preserved to a height of 5.2 m. above the floor. South of this cistern, another, smaller one could be discerned, within the foundations of a trapezoidal dwelling unit (cell No. 5). To the west was a broad plot, supported by a terrace wall built on the edge of the scarp. Cell No. 5 leads us to assume that in the Judean Desert monasteries, in most instances, the dwelling units were constructed over cisterns. Another cistern (No. 6; 3.7x6.2 m.), preserved to a height of ca. 3.5 m., apparently had a cell built over it. Cell No. 7 is rectangular (3.6x6.2 m.). No. 8 is a perrenial spring-tunnel. North of it was an elliptical cistern (No. 9; 4.1x5 m. internally). Cell No. 10 is rectangular (3.3x7.8 m.). From this structure northward, a long terrace wall continued along the rim of the escarpment. The cultivated plot here was probably watered directly from the spring above it. Nearby, sparse remains of another cell (No. 12) could be identified. 'Ein en-Natuf (No. 12) flows from within the scarped bank of Nahal leqo'a, ca. 5 m. above the surface of the rock. Its waters are gathered into a small pool (80x90 cm.). This spring, together with the one above it, served Chariton in his seclusion here. Cell No. 13, preserved in its entirety (Fig. 39), is a massive structure built over a large cistern (6.1x10.2 m.). Its walls are preserved to a height of ca. 2.5 m.

**Evidence (EN, site 27):** 'Ein en-Natuf has no site number of its own. It is feature No. 12 inside site 27, the scatter of monks' cells on the slope between the Chariton monastery (site 17) and the "Hanging Cave" (site 26). The EN text also mislabels the cell before it as "No. 12"; the Hebrew gives that cell as No. 11.

**Evidence (EN, site 27):** The spring "flows from within the scarped bank of Nahal leqo'a [sic], ca. 5 m. above the surface of the rock. Its waters are gathered into a small pool (80x90 cm.)". A second spring, No. 8, is a "perrenial spring-tunnel".

**Evidence (EN, site 27):** The entry has no Period field and no "Finding" section, and it names no pottery, coins or other dated finds. The only date given is the description's "Byzantine monks' cells".

**Evidence (EN intro, p. 14):** "In the area between the 'Hanging Cave' and the monastery there were remains of monks' cells, part of the Old Laura (site No. 27)."

**Evidence (Fig. 38, EN p. 80; same plan on HE p. 83):** The plan "Cells south of Chariton monastery" marks "'Ein en-Natuf" / עין א-נטוף (No. 12) as a dot on the cliff line of the main scarp, about halfway between the monastery and the Hanging Cave. It also marks "Spring" / מעיין (No. 8) further west and up-slope, and cells 1-13. Fig. 16 (EN p. 55) marks "'Ein en-Natuf" too.

**Evidence (Hebrew edition, HE pp. 82-84; my translation):**
- The Hebrew counts "remains of about ten Byzantine dwelling cells".
- "There are two springs on the site: a spring-tunnel (No. 8 on the cell plan) and 'Ein [printed עץ, a typo for עין] Natuf (No. 12), which drips in front of the great cliff. The storage installations attached to the springs attest to their use by the monks who settled there."
- "Spring (No. 8): a spring-tunnel active all year round. Together with 'Ein a-Natuf, these were the two active springs within the bounds of the laura of Chariton."
- "Cell (No. 11): sparse remains of another dwelling cell."
- **"'Ein a-Natuf (No. 12): a layer (contact) spring issuing from within the cliff wall of Nahal Teqo'a (Wadi Khureitun). The point of issue is in a natural fissure, ca. 5 m above the rock surface. The spring water collects in a round rock-cut cupmark. From the cupmark a short channel (ca. 2 m) leads to a hewn pool (90x80 cm) in which the water is stored. Its depth reaches 80 cm."**
- The Hebrew does **not** have the English sentence "This spring, together with the one above it, served Chariton in his seclusion here."
- Other details missing from the EN: Cell 1 has walls 1.4 m high, a settling pit (1.5x1 m) and a plot ca. 25 m long. Cell 4's cistern has building blocks up to 1.2 m long and thick hydraulic plaster. Cell 5 has a round cistern 2 m in diameter and 1.7 m deep, and another cistern set in a natural crack. Cell 7 has building stones up to 1.3 m long. Cistern 9 is up to ca. 5.5 m deep. Cell 10 was perhaps divided into two rooms, with a terrace line of 60-70 m along the cliff edge. In cell 13 the present entrance was broken through the east wall (1.5 m thick). Its vault (ca. 4 m high) has two square draw-openings (50x50 cm), and cracks in its plaster were repaired with layers of mortar.
- The Hebrew has no period field, no pottery and no coins either. Unlike most Hebrew entries, it does not say "no sherds were found". It simply gives no find information.

**Inference:** The only cut features recorded at the spring are a round cupmark, a channel of ca. 2 m and a pool of 90x80 cm (80 cm deep). These are water installations. Nothing at the spring is described as a niche, an opening, a cave or a columbarium. The spring sits on the rim of the main escarpment ("rim of the escarpment", site 27), which fits a reading of "edge".

**Inference (location):** Measured on Fig. 16 (1 km scale bar), the spring lies ca. 0.45 km S (slightly W) of the monastery, ca. 0.3 km N of the Hanging Cave and ca. 1.2 km W of 'Ein es-Sakhari. That puts it within ca. 0.1-0.2 km of the site-27 grid point used for all distances here. The scale bar on Fig. 38 is inconsistent with Fig. 16 and with the grids: read literally, it would put the Hanging Cave ca. 1.4 km from the monastery. That matches the intro's "ca. 1500 m" but not the entry's "800 m" or the grids (ca. 0.7 km).

**Inference (1985 map reference):** The online edition gives only New Israel Grid values. Converted to the Old Israel Grid used in the 1985 book (subtracting 50 km E and 500 km N), site 27 is approximately 1723 1167. This matches its field number 17-11/26/2, i.e. km square 172/116.

### Site 28. 'Ein es-Sakhari (M)

*EN PDF pp. 82-84; HE PDF pp. 85-87. Distance from site 27 grid point: ca. 1.26 km ESE (Inference, computed from the grids below; grid values are rounded to 100 m).*

**Evidence (header fields, verbatim):**

- Site Name: 'Ein es-Sakhari (M)
- Additional Names: 'Ain es-Sakhri (S)
- Field Num: 17-11/36/1
- New Israel Grid: 616397, 223589
- WGS-84: 31.6400321719216, 35.2480755013566
- Period: Natufian culture,Byzantine
- Finds: Monastery,Spring,Pestle,Sickle blade,Hydraulic plaster,Bone implement,Cave,Niche,Basin,Basalt vessel,Engraving,Reservoir,Cell

**Evidence (description, verbatim, English edition):**

> Spring surrounded by Byzantine remains, flowing from the northern bank of Nahal Teqo'a. Although low in yield, it is perrennial. An ancient footpath to the site leads from the Chariton monastery. From the east, the path leads from a site which we named the "Rock of The Crosses" (see below). The site lies on a natural rock shelf jutting out ca. 5 m. from the escarpment, and is ca. 70 m. long (Fig. 40). The spring, located in the western part of the site, has a small rock-hewn channel conducting the water to a rectangular pool (5.6x1.4 m.). Overflow was carried by another rock-hewn channel, apparently irrigating a small garden whose remains are visible at the foot of the escarpment. Traces of structures can be discerned on the rock surface. This seems to have been a two-storey building built of wood, the rear side of which abutted the rock escarpment. This assumption is based on two rows of small, rock-hewn sockets for the beams of the structure (Fig. 41). Two Greek inscriptions were discovered in the eastern part of the site, painted in red on the rock-face, 1.4 m. above the natural rock surface. These inscriptions, now illegible, are clear evidence of monks on the site in the Byzantine period. Spacious karst cave east of the Byzantine remains. The cave had been cleared by its Arab owners, and the few finds brought to Rene Neuville. The tools were typical of the Natufian culture — lunates and sickle-blades. The importance of the site lies in several of artifacts, such as a harpoon with a single row of barbs, a carved basalt pestle and a calcite figurine of a couple copulating in an en face sitting position (the carving is schematic and partially unclear).
>
> Finding
>
> bone harpoon, carved basalt pestle, calcite figurine.
>
> Bibliography
>
> Neuville, 1951, pp. 132-134.

- p. 83: 40. Section of the site.

**Evidence (EN intro, p. 16):** The "small monastery built by St. Chariton a mile away from the saint's tomb" (List of the Houses of God, early 9th c.) "fits the remains discovered at 'Ein es- Sakhari (site No. 28), ca. 1.5 km. east of the Chariton monastery".

**Evidence (EN):** "two rows of small, rock-hewn sockets for the beams of the structure (Fig. 41)". The "Spacious karst cave" yielded Natufian finds. No historical-period pottery is listed.

**Evidence (Hebrew edition, HE pp. 85-87; my translation; details missing from the EN):**
- "The point of issue is hidden within a deep fissure beneath the great cliff of the wadi, hence the name 'spring of the rock'." Its yield is "extremely low, but its waters flow regularly".
- The western path branches off the route between the Chariton monastery and Herodium. Byzantine-type retaining walls are visible along it.
- The ledge is ca. 70 m long and 5 m wide on average. It is bounded on the east by "a natural cleft in the rock" (probably the entrance) and on the west by a massive wall (1.2 m thick) built at right angles to the cliff.
- The pool is 5.6x1.4 m and 0.9 m deep. "For purifying the water a small settling pit 50 cm in diameter and 30 cm deep was installed". Both are thickly plastered with hydraulic plaster.
- "Remains of dwelling cells were noted in the eastern part."
- **"In the cliff face two rows of small hewn niches survive. In the first (lower) row 14 rectangular niches (10x12x10 cm) were found, ca. 60 cm apart, 2.8 m above the rock surface. In the second row, ca. 3 m above the first, 7 round niches ca. 5-7 cm in diameter survive. The niches of both rows served to set the wooden beams of the ceiling of the building, which was apparently two-storeyed (Fig. 41). In the lower row the beams were of square section, in the upper of round section."**
- "In the rock floor hewn drainage basins and channels survive."
- The inscriptions were painted on smoothed panels (30x30 cm) ca. 1.5 m up. The left one has 9 lines with letters ca. 2 cm high; the right one is short, with letters ca. 7 cm high.
- **"No sherds were found."** The entry adds "For the identification of the site, see Introduction."

**Illustration note:** Fig. 40 (EN p. 83; section) draws the rows of small rectangular and round sockets on the cliff face above the ledge. Fig. 41 (EN p. 84; restoration by L. Ritmeyer) shows the restored two-storey building against the cliff.

**Inference:** These are the only regular rows of small rock-cut openings recorded within 2 km of 'Ein en-Natuf. Both editions interpret them explicitly as beam sockets (21 holes in two rows), not as a dovecote.

## D. Introduction passages that bear on the questions

- **Evidence (EN foreword, p. 5):** "It is surprising that the survey did not reveal evidence of the Hellenistic, Hasmonean or Herodian periods (apart, of course, from Herodium itself, Herod's outstanding building project in the desert)." The Hebrew (HE p. 6) says the same.
- **Evidence (EN intro, p. 12, "The Second Temple Period (Herodium)"):** "During this period, settlement was apparently concentrated at Teqo'a. In the entire area surveyed, no other site of this period was discovered, besides Herodium." The Hebrew (HE p. 12) says the same.
- **Evidence (EN intro, p. 14):** The Hanging Cave (site 26) is "ca. 1500 m. south of the Chariton monastery"; the monks' cells are "part of the Old Laura (site No. 27)".
- **Evidence (Hebrew geography section, HE p. 20; my translation; the EN geomorphology section on p. 21 lacks it):** "Karst caves also occur here, formed by slow, continuous dissolution of the rock by water... Up Nahal Teqo'a (Wadi Khureitun), in the upper third of the cliff, lies the Chariton monastery, and near it several springs issuing from the hard limestone."
- **Inference:** The foreword's "no Hellenistic" claim sits uneasily with the Hellenistic pottery listed for Teqo'a (site 37). Presumably Teqo'a counts as the expected exception. Either way, neither edition puts any Hellenistic, Hasmonean or Herodian material in Wadi Khureitun.

## Answers

**1. Is a dovecote, columbarium or regular rock-cut opening recorded at the spring itself, or within 2 km?**

- **Dovecote or columbarium: no.** The words dovecote, columbarium, pigeon and cote, and the Hebrew שובך, קולומבריום and יונים, appear nowhere in either edition (**Evidence**, by full-text search).
- **At the spring itself (site 27, No. 12): no opening, niche or cave is recorded.** The EN gives the spring and "a small pool (80x90 cm.)". The HE gives a natural fissure, a round rock-cut cupmark, a ca. 2 m channel, and a hewn pool 90x80 cm and 80 cm deep (**Evidence**, site 27). The only other "openings" in site 27 are two square draw-openings (50x50 cm) in the built vault of cistern-cell No. 13 (**Evidence**, HE site 27). They are not rock-cut.
- **Within 2 km, the nearest regular rock-cut openings are at site 28, 'Ein es-Sakhari, ca. 1.26 km E (Inference from the grids; ca. 1.2 km on Fig. 16).** There are two rows of small rock-hewn sockets in the cliff face (**Evidence**, site 28). The HE gives 14 rectangular holes (10x12x10 cm, 60 cm apart, 2.8 m up) and, ca. 3 m higher, 7 round holes (5-7 cm). Both editions interpret them explicitly as beam sockets for a two-storey wooden building, drawn on Fig. 40, p. 83.
- **Other rock-cut niches within 2 km, none called a dovecote (Evidence):**
  - Site 26, ca. 0.3 km: "several well-formed niches" in the Hanging Cave chapel. The HE gives them as semicircular, ca. 0.5 m wide and 1 m high, with a door rebate and shelf grooves.
  - Site 17, ca. 0.45 km: "many caves and rock niches", walled up in later reuse.
  - Site 45, ca. 1.9 km: a giant hewn niche with crosses, an apse niche, and traces of two more niches.
  - Site 40, ca. 1.9 km: a hewn opening to the crypt.
- **Inference:** Nothing in the survey resembles a columbarium (rows of many small loculi in a chamber). The 'Ein es-Sakhari sockets are the only candidate a reader might confuse with one, and the survey's own reading of them as beam sockets is backed by its section drawing.

**2. Are Roman, Herodian or Hellenistic finds (coins, pottery) recorded at the spring site itself?**

- **No.** Site 27 has no Period field and no pottery, coins or other dated finds in either edition. The only date given is "Byzantine" for the cells (**Evidence**, site 27).
- No coins are recorded anywhere in the survey (**Evidence**, by full-text search, EN and HE).
- Within 2 km, no entry records Hellenistic, Roman or Herodian finds. Several entries explicitly report "no sherds": HE sites 26, 28, 29, 10, 11, 42 and 54. Dated pottery at site 17 is "mostly 5th-7th c. CE" and later (**Evidence**).
- The only Herodian or Roman mention within 2 km is the EN's speculative alternative for the Herodium boundary wall (site 10, 1.36 km): "royal Herodian estate" or "Roman siege complex". That entry records no finds, and the Hebrew version drops the alternatives and says "no sherds were found" (**Evidence**).
- The intro states that apart from Herodium (and Teqo'a), no Second Temple-period site was found in the map area (**Evidence**, EN pp. 5 and 12).
- The nearest Hellenistic or Roman pottery is at Teqo'a (site 37, ca. 2.55 km) (**Evidence**).

**3. Does the survey say the caves at the spring are natural?**

- **English edition: no.** It describes no caves at 'Ein en-Natuf at all, natural or hewn (**Evidence**, site 27). For the monastery (site 17) the EN says caves "were hewn into the rock escarpment".
- **Hebrew edition: partly, for features near the spring rather than at it (Evidence):**
  - The spring issues from "a natural fissure" (site 27).
  - The Hanging Cave's niche and the caves beneath it "form part of the many karst phenomena characterising the cliffs of the wadi" (site 26).
  - A "huge rock shelter", Mughâret Mushmush, lies 400 m NW of the Hanging Cave. "Chariton's Cave", ca. 200 m from it and several hundred metres deep, "is a karst cave" (site 26).
  - The HE geography section notes karst caves and "several springs" near the monastery in the hard limestone (HE p. 20).
  - At site 17 the HE says a cave at the Eastern Tower has a "natural cavity enlarged by hewing".
- **Inference:** Going by the HE distances and Fig. 16, Mughâret Mushmush probably lies in the same stretch of cliff as 'Ein en-Natuf, within a few hundred metres. But the survey never links any cave to the spring or gives a distance between them.

**Hebrew-only details at these entries (summary).** Section B gives each in full; the most important are:
- **Site 27:** the spring is a layer spring from a natural fissure, with cupmark → 2 m channel → pool 90x80x80 cm. The two springs (No. 8 and No. 12) were "the two active springs" of the laura, with storage installations attached. There were ca. ten cells. Cell No. 11 is correctly numbered. The EN sentence linking the spring to Chariton's seclusion is absent.
- **Site 26:** the karst setting, Mughâret Mushmush and "Chariton's Cave"; niche dimensions and shelf grooves; "no sherds".
- **Site 28:** the counts and sizes of the socket niches (14 + 7); "no sherds".
- **Site 10:** no Herodian or Roman alternative; "no sherds".
- **Site 8:** named 'Erq el-Ahmar.
- **Site 17:** pottery 5th-7th c. CE and later.
