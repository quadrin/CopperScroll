# Bethesda: the twin pools and the eastern complex (entries 55 and 56)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = the authors state it. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## Von Wahlde 2009

The article is a critique of the published reports, not new fieldwork: "My account below is dependent upon their reports" (p. 127 n. 47), i.e. A. Duprez, *Jésus et les dieux guérisseurs* (1970), pp. 38–54, and M.-J. Pierre and J.-M. Rousée, "Sainte-Marie de la Probatique", *POC* 31 (1981), pp. 23–42. The Bethesda Project (C. Dauphin, from 1995) is unpublished (p. 112 n. 1).

### The twin pools (entry 55)

- **Evidence (p. 113).** The site has "two larger pools and six smaller pools"; the large pools lie north and south of each other, and "six smaller pools and cisterns are grouped together to the east of these two pools."
- **Evidence (p. 114).** "The dam is constructed of ashlars. At its deepest point there are twenty courses of these ashlars"; about 6–7 m thick and about 13–14 m high (nn. 6, 8). A chimney about 1 m square and 12 m deep has ports into the northern pool; a channel at its foot, about 2 m high, ran through the dam and on toward the Temple Mount (pp. 114–115, 122).
- **Evidence (pp. 115–119).** The southern pool (about 45–65 m a side) has rock-cut steps on the west, in three flights with landings about 2.5 m deep; it "has all the features that would indicate that the basin was intended for this purpose", a miqveh (p. 119), as Gibson and Reich and Shukron had also proposed (n. 24). The northern pool then served as its otzar (p. 120).
- **Evidence (p. 116 n. 18).** "Gibson ('Bethesda' 287) states that the landings were used for the placing of beds."
- **Evidence (pp. 120–123), dates.** Duprez puts both pools under Simon II (219–196 BCE); Pierre and Rousée the northern under Hezekiah and the southern under Simon II; Gibson "both pools were built at the same time and about 25 BCE" (p. 121). Von Wahlde: the northern pool "dates at least from the Persian period" (p. 123); the southern no earlier than the mid-2nd century BCE (miqvaʾot, after Reich); the northern pool's catchment was cut only by Agrippa I's wall, 41–44 CE (p. 120).
- **Evidence (p. 125).** "No columns positively dated to the first century have been found."
- **Evidence (whole article).** The article does not mention the Copper Scroll.

### The eastern complex (entry 56)

- **Evidence (p. 127).** East of the two large pools "there are a number of natural grottoes, some with additional fabricated elements". Gibson holds that they have "nothing to do with the pools to the west other than geographical proximity".
- **Evidence (p. 128).** "The remains from before 70AD are contained in [an area approxi]mately 20m by 20 m, east of the larger 'western' [pools and] are situated around a central cistern. Around this were situated six small grottoes with steps leading down into them. Additional grottoes were found under the Church of St. Anne. These had either been so destroyed or reused that little could be learned from them." (Brackets: OCR loss at the line-starts.)
- **Evidence (p. 128).** These remains are dated together "by the uniform type of plaster used to cover them as well as by the coins", which "range from the time of Alexander Janneus (103-76 BCE) to the time of the procurators. The last coin is dated to 68 AD. Finally, pottery remains at the site come from the first century BC to the first century AD."
- **Evidence (p. 128).** Duprez found "no archaeological evidence that the eastern baths functioned as religiously oriented healing baths in that period"; cult use (Asclepius/Serapis) appears after 135 CE.
- **Evidence.** No threshold, chamber, burial niche, kokh or entrance from the west is described. The only "doorway" in the article is the water outlet in the dam (p. 122).

## Masterman 1905

- **Evidence (p. 96).** The St Anne's pool "is 55 feet long and 12 feet wide, but with its 'twin' pool immediately to the west, from which it is divided by a thick wall of masonry, is over 100 feet long". Both are "filled only by surface rain-water"; "A flight of twenty-four very steep steps leads from the opening at the eastern end to the water level."
- **Evidence (pp. 97–98).** Over the pool are a church "of perhaps the fourth or fifth century" and a Crusader church; a marble votive foot dedicated by Pompeia Lucilia came from the debris (Hadrianic, after Clermont-Ganneau; p. 99 caption).
- **Evidence (pp. 99–102).** Masterman prefers the Virgin's Fountain (Gihon) as Bethesda, for its intermittent flow.
- **Inference.** This is the pre-1960s view of the pools (the small eastern cistern under the churches). It records no cistern or chamber with a threshold. It is superseded for the plan by the later reports.
