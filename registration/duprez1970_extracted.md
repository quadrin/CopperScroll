# Duprez 1970: the St Anne's pools and the eastern baths (entries 55 and 56)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = Duprez states it, or it is drawn on his plan. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

Duprez reports the White Fathers' excavations from 1956 (P. Blondeel, with PP. Pochet and Rousée; p. 33 n. 2), first-hand. Von Wahlde 2009 summarised these pages ([extraction](bethesda_jstor_2026-10-16_extracted.md)).

## The two great pools (entry 55), pp. 33–37

- **Evidence (p. 33).** The central dam is built above and rock below; it is 6.50 m wide at the top, with ashlar faces of courses about 60 cm high and a rubble core.
- **Evidence (pp. 33–34).** A square chimney about 1 m × 1 m and about 15 m deep runs behind the dam's north face. Small openings (20 × 15 cm), about every 2 m, connect it to the northern pool; they were found blocked. At its foot a vaulted channel about 1.10 m high runs south; about 50 m of it was cleared (Pl. VII, 2).
- **Evidence (p. 34).** The southern pool measures about 45 m (N), 50 m (E), 65 m (S) and 47 m (W), and is up to 14 m deep. Its rock steps are "not meant for people to go down", but to break the current of the incoming water (pp. 34–35). At its north-west corner is a settling basin (*bassin de retenue*, Pl. VII, 1), whose east wall is 2.60 m high.
- **Evidence (p. 35).** "Le bassin nord n'a pas fait l'objet de recherches nouvelles." The pools were filled by winter rain, not a spring.
- **Evidence (pp. 35–37; p. 37 on the page image).** Herod's Birket Israʾil makes the two pools useless, so they are older. Perhaps Simon son of Onias (220–195) built them (Sir 50:3). "Ils ont été vraisemblablement aménagés au IIe siècle av. J.-C. et ont été utilisés pour le Temple jusqu'à Hérode Le Grand." (The text layer reads "1°" here; the page has "IIe".) Agrippa I's third wall (he died in 44 CE) "dut rendre inutilisables les deux bassins", because they could no longer fill in winter (p. 36).
- **Evidence (pp. 37–38).** No column base of the five porticoes was found in situ; the four bases with crosses are Byzantine, and the capitals are no earlier than the 2nd century CE. Duprez thinks a sick person could not be dipped in the great pools.
- **Inference for entry 55.** The two basins are first-hand dated: built in the 2nd century BCE (by inference from the Birket Israʾil), and still standing but no longer filling after 41–44 CE. Both stood in 50 BCE–41 CE. Duprez does not support von Wahlde's miqveh reading of the southern steps. Nothing here is a "basin one can enter" at the pools; the settling basin is a small basin of the right size but an inference only. No level changes.

## The first installation, before 70 CE (entry 56), pp. 38–43 and Pl. II

- **Evidence (p. 39).** "Sur une surface fouillée d'environ 20 m sur 20 m on a partout trouvé des traces d'une occupation dont la date terminale est établie avec certitude grâce aux nombreuses monnaies ... dont les dernières sont de 68 apr. J.-C. (pl. II)."
- **Evidence (p. 39), cistern 1.** "Toute l'installation semble s'organiser autour d'une grande citerne centrale 1 (pl. VIII). Cette citerne était alors une grotte naturelle d'une profondeur de 8,50 m environ et de 6 m de diamètre." It was roofed by the rock (part of the vault remains on the south, pierced by a shaft); the vault probably fell at the end of the period. All walls have a grey ash-based plaster, which also dates the neighbouring grottoes. "Primitivement la grotte devait avoir trois ouvertures sur les côtés. Toutes ces ouvertures sont bouchées à notre époque." Pl. VIII, 2: "Un des trous d'accès à la citerne".
- **Evidence (pp. 39–40), grotto 2.** North of cistern 1; it once communicated with it, but the opening was blocked with masonry plastered grey on both sides. Five steps go down from south to north; the entrance is partly built and partly rock-cut "en forme de linteau"; two more steps lead to a small basin under the stair, 2.50 m below the entrance.
- **Evidence (p. 40), grotto 3.** North-east of cistern 1; a stair from the north turns east to a small rectangular basin at the back (Pl. X). The best preserved.
- **Evidence (p. 40), basin 4.** Three metres east of cistern 1: a small basin about 30 cm deep, with three rock-cut steps beside it to the south, all in the grey plaster.
- **Evidence (p. 40), grotto 5.** "Une grotte 5 (1,50 m de large, 2 m de long et 1,50 m de haut) dans laquelle on descend par quatre marches grossièrement taillées dans le roc (pl. IX, 2)." No plaster in situ; it was full of painted plaster of the Aelia phase. It "devait exister lors de la première installation bien qu'on n'en puisse plus connaître l'utilisation précise."
- **Evidence (pp. 40–41), grotto 6.** Further south; a bath at the back (Pl. XI); reached by a stair from the north, then down to the small bath in the north-west corner.
- **Evidence (p. 41, page image), the threshold.** "On a trouvé entre cette grotte 6 et la grande citerne 1 quelques traces de construction notamment un seuil et quelques éléments du pied-droit de la porte (pl. IX, 1). Le tout est d'un bon travail. Ces constructions sont trop détruites pour permettre de reconstituer le plan d'un bâtiment quelconque." The threshold and jamb "sont encore en place" (p. 41).
- **Evidence (p. 41), bath 7.** South of cistern 1; the same plan: a stair down into the grotto, so that the small bath at the back cannot be seen from the entrance (also p. 40 n. 1, for every bath).
- **Evidence (pp. 41–42).** Eleven rough stone basins (about 60 cm cubes, hollowed 15–20 cm), probably before 70. Coins "vont d'Alexandre Jannée (104-78 av. J.-C.) aux procurateurs romains. La dernière est datée de 68 ap. J.-C." The pottery is of the 1st century BCE and the 1st century CE. The installation was abandoned in the war of 70; it began "vers l'époque asmonéenne". Duprez holds it was a cult healing installation (pp. 42–43).
- **Evidence (Pl. II, page image; north is up).** The great pools are at the west edge. Cistern 1 is in the centre, with a small circle (the shaft) in its south-east part, and two dashed lines from its south-west side to a small round feature. Grotto 2 is to the north, entered by steps on its south side. Grotto 3 is to the north-east. Basin 4 is to the east. Grotto 5 is further east; on the plan, its steps are on its west side. Grotto 6 is to the south-east and bath 7 to the south. Two groups of unnumbered blocks lie between cistern 1 and grotto 6; the text puts the threshold there. The plan has no burial niche.
- **Evidence (Pl. IX, 1, page image).** A photograph of the threshold blocks with socket holes, and a jamb. The photograph has no scale or orientation.

## The second installation (2nd–4th century), pp. 43–44

- **Evidence (p. 43).** After 70 the grottoes were filled and the buildings levelled. Cistern 1 alone was kept, with a new roof of stone arches (Pl. XIV, 1).
- **Evidence (p. 44).** A 3rd-century mosaic (8) north of cistern 1; "Un beau seuil a été retrouvé mais non in situ" (Aelia phase, not the window).

## Effect on the ratings (applied 17 October 2026)

- **Entry 56, jer_bethesda: no level change (R 1, P 0, F 1, D 2; label none).** The pre-stated test (`decisive_next`, 16 October) was "a cistern or chamber with a threshold (XII 2–3), entered from the west". Duprez gives two near matches, and neither meets it:
  - Cistern 1 is a specific cistern of the window, and a threshold with its jamb is in place nearby. But the threshold is "between grotto 6 and cistern 1", in a building whose plan cannot be reconstructed. Duprez does not say that it opens into the cistern, and the cistern's three side openings were blocked in this period. The relation "threshold *of* the cistern" is not recorded, so F stays 1 under the rubric.
  - Grotto 5 is a small chamber of the first installation. On Pl. II its steps are on its west side (Inference from the plan; the text gives no direction). But no threshold is recorded for it, and its use is unknown.
- **Inference.** The candidate is now rated on a first-hand report, not on von Wahlde's summary. D stays 2: the complex is dated by coins to 68 CE, but the threshold is dated only by its context.
- **Next record for entry 56.** Pierre and Rousée, *POC* 31 (1981), pp. 23–42, or the White Fathers' field records: the threshold's position, its orientation, and whether its doorway led into cistern 1 or another room.
- **Entry 55, jer_bethesda: no level change.** D gains a first-hand date (2nd century BCE to Agrippa I).

## Pierre & Rousée 1981 read — 22 October 2026

*POC* 31 pp. 23–25, 28–42 (pp. 26–27 not photographed) were read from the owner's NLI photos ([record](../research/sources/nli_photos_2026-10-21/probatique_poc31_1981.md)).

- **Phasing (EVIDENCE, pp. 24–25, 33, 42).** The two basins were not built together: "Nous avons cru longtemps que les deux bassins étaient contemporains. Nous nous trompions." The central dam and the north basin are "à l'*époque royale* de Juda" (Pl. 1a caption: "Digue de l'époque d'Achaz"); the south basin and the vault over the canal are Hellenistic, about Simon son of Onias (220–195). Dated by building technique and texts, no sealed finds. This disagrees with Duprez's single 2nd-c. BCE date for both (above) and with Gibson's Herodian date; all three put both basins standing in the window.
- **Dam (EVIDENCE, p. 24).** About 40 m long, 6 m wide at the top and more than 7 m at the base, at most 13 m high; 20 courses of about 50 cm on cut rock; a 1 × 1 m shaft with 20 × 15 cm openings every 2 m into the north basin; outlet corridor and canal (Fig. 3).
- **Basins (EVIDENCE, p. 25).** North basin extent unknown ("Seul son angle sud-est est assuré"). South basin: NE and SE corners known, plaster ≥10 cm, rock-cut steps on the west side, "irregular and sometimes too high". No dimensions for either basin.
- **Entry 56 (EVIDENCE).** No threshold, burial chamber, kokh or platform in the pages read; natural caves, "la grande citerne", a medieval cistern and the "citerne probatique" are reported. The threshold question goes to pp. 26–27 or the field records.
- **Numbering.** The brief for that reading called the twin-pool entry "56"; in `tables/entry_concordance.csv` and the atlas it is **55** (XI 12–15). Allegro's "Item 56" (1960, 1964) is his own item numbering for the same text.
