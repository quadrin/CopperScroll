## Pixner's model (pp. 325–341, 359–360)

- **Historical deposit, not folklore.** The scroll is an Essene aide-mémoire listing real community wealth. Most of it was common property; some may be deposits from outsiders kept in an Essene "counter-bank" free of Temple impurity (pp. 325–327, 339–340). It was deposited in 3Q in 68 CE together with the leather scrolls. His argument is the archaeology of the cave: the fallen ceiling blocked the copper scroll's niche (pp. 334–335). He visited the cave with É. Puech several times, had it measured by D. Chen (plan I), and fixed the find spot through de Contenson (p. 328 n. 7; p. 334).
- **The "code" is the geographical order.** "the treasure catalogue was composed on a definite geographical plan" (p. 341). He claims this is his main departure from earlier scholars, who place the caches "haphazardly". He divides the 64 items into five regions:
  1. **Px 1–18: the Essene Gate of Jerusalem (south-west hill, Mount Zion).** Px 1–2 are in the valley next to it (upper Hinnom). Px 3–17 are in his "Essene Quarter", next to the Gate of the Essenes (Josephus BJ 5.145), which he excavated in the Protestant Cemetery "some 180 m south of the Cenacle building" (p. 333). Px 18 is doubtful.
  2. **Px 19–34: the Jericho region.** Px 19–25 are around Qumran = Sekakah. Px 26–30 are between Jericho and Sekakah. Px 31–34 are around Jericho (Dok, Kozibah).
  3. **Px 35–47: "the Land of Damascus" = the Yarmuk, on both banks (Batanea).** He matches modern Yarmuk village names from Schumacher's 1896 map and Höhne 1981.
  4. **Px 48–60: around Jerusalem again, outside the quarter.** Px 48–50 are south of the city: Ramat Raḥel, Wadi Yasul, Emeq Rephaim. Px 51–60 run through the Kidron, Gethsemane and Bethesda. In his view this is the community's farmland and burial ground (p. 359).
  5. **Px 61–64: the north.** Gerizim, Beth-Sham, Bazak/Barak, and Sehab = Tell Shihab on the Yarmuk, where he puts the master copy.
- **Kohlit (khlt)** is a technical term for an Essene "monastic centre". There is one in each region: Jerusalem (Px 4, 12), Sekakah/Qumran (Px 21), Sehab (Px 64) (pp. 336–337, 343 n. 6, 358 n. 52).
- **The Greek letters** occur only in the Mount Zion caches (p. 335). He takes them as initials of the trustees and gives Josephus names for them: ΚΕΝ Kenedaios; ΧΑΓ Chageiras (BJ 5.474, after Michel); ΗΝ; ΘΕ Thebuthi (BJ 6.387); ΔΙ Diophantos/Diogenes; ΤΡ Tryphon; ΣΚ Skopas (p. 335 n. 32). He ties ΘΕ to the priest Jesus son of Thebuthi (pp. 344–345).
- **Sekakah = Khirbet Qumran with Wadi Qumran** (after Allegro and de Vaux 1973) (p. 336).
- **Many links to the New Testament and early church**, offered as questions: the Last Supper room (Px 7), Matthias (Px 8), James the Just (Px 53), Mary's tomb (Px 56), the Protoevangelium (Px 58). These are interpretive and do not bear on the readings.
- **Caveat in his own words.** "many of the suggested geographical allocations of the various toponyms must be considered hypothetical" (p. 341).

## New candidate locations and recorded features

These are the places Pixner gives that are not candidates in `ratings.json`, most with a physical feature he describes. Many feature reports are in the present tense ("still visible", "there is"). Pixner lived and taught at the Dormition Abbey and excavated the Essene Gate (pp. 325, 333). Even so, most of these are his reports, not measured surveys. Several rest on his 1976 paper (*Studia Hierosolymitana* I, pp. 245–284), which the repo does not hold.

### A. Mount Zion / Essene Quarter (repo entries 1–15)

**0. Anchor: the Gate of the Essenes.**
- **Evidence (p. 333).** "It can now be said that the Gate of the Essenes mentioned by Josephus in his description of the walls of Jerusalem (Bellum Judaicum V, 4, 2 § 145) has been found and identified. It lies some 180 m south of the Cenacle building."
- **Inference.** This is the anchor of his whole Px 1–17 group. It is an excavated feature (Protestant Cemetery, Mt Zion), but the identification is his. The repo has no Mt Zion candidate for any entry.

**1. Upper Hinnom valley (repo 1–2).**
- **Evidence (p. 342 n. 1).** "preference must be given to the upper wider section of the Gehinnom, which is called 'emeq in Jer. 31, 40 … The large number of recently (1974/75) discovered Iron Age tombs confirm Jeremiah's expression."
- **Inference.** The Iron Age tombs support the epithet "Valley of the Dead"; they are not the entry's "ruin" or "steps". No ruin with eastward steps is named. NEW valley candidate for entry 1, resting on his ʿbwr reading.

**2. Court with a great cistern; Conder's hollowed stone (repo 3).**
- **Evidence (p. 342 n. 5).** "Captain C. R. Conder speaks in his article "The Rock Scarp of Mt Zion" (…), of a hollowed stone (…), which was found during excavations there and which might or might not have been the one mentioned here."
- **Inference.** This is a recorded find (Conder, PEF QS 1875, p. 83), but Pixner himself is non-committal. Worth pulling Conder's QS 1875 p. 83. The repo's `research/history/pef_qs_finds/` might already index it; not checked here.

**3. Tel Kohlit, the "Greek Garden" immersion bath and the Benedictine reservoir (repo 4, 11).**
- **Evidence (p. 343 n. 6).** "If the immersion bath is the one still seen in the "Greek Garden" on Mt Zion (…), the Tel Kohlit must have stood in the area of today's Greek Orthodox cemetery and the adjacent cemetery of the Benedictine monks of the Dormition Abbey."
- **Evidence (p. 343 n. 6).** "To the east of it there [is] in the Benedictine Garden a huge water reservoir, now unused (cf. nr 12)." The OCR prints "18" for "is".
- **Inference.** There are two physical features: an extant rock-cut bath and an unused large reservoir. He makes the reservoir the pool of repo 11 (Px 12). Both are undated in this article.

**4. A stair of 36 rock-cut steps with ritual baths and a postern (repo 5; also 6 "under the steps").**
- **Evidence (p. 344 n. 9).** "Some 40 meters to the north-west of the Gate of the Essenes, in the corner of a tower of the ancient city wall, there is an installation of ritual baths outside the wall"
- **Evidence (p. 344 n. 9).** "The ensemble is on a sort of a rock platform which can be reached over 36 rockhewn steps of which ten are still above the ground. From the platform a still visible small postern (1,20 m wide)"
- **Inference.** This is the most concrete feature in the article: a counted flight of steps, a measured postern, and published references (Pixner 1976 pp. 269–271; R. Reich 1980 pp. 239–241, Hebrew). It is a strong NEW candidate for an entry with steps. Pixner uses it for repo 5, and by implication for repo 6 ("salt pit … under the steps"). The steps' direction and date are not given here.

**5. Pierotti's cave (repo 7).**
- **Evidence (p. 345).** "The cave was very large (ca. 40 m long, 15 m wide, 3 m high), and the diagram drawn by him shows it lying just to the south-east of the "Tomb of David" (Cenacle building)."
- **Evidence (p. 345).** "But this cave has never been found by anybody else, since the entrance to it has apparently been blocked."
- **Inference.** This is a recorded but unverified feature (E. Pierotti, *Jerusalem Explored*, 1864, pp. 214–218, pl. XLVI). Pixner says only that it "might be" the cave of repo 7. Low weight.

**6. Rock-cut cistern under the First Wall (repo 10).**
- **Evidence (p. 346 n. 13).** "At a short distance to the Northwest of the Gate of the Essenes, there is a large ancient cistern hewn from the rock, lying just beneath the First Wall (hwmʾ), fed by channels cut out of the rock"
- **Inference.** This is a physical feature that fits the entry's own words, "cistern under the wall". It is NEW for repo 10, where the repo has only jer_east_gate. Undated here.

**7. The Milḥam rock at the Gobat School (repo 13).**
- **Evidence (p. 347 n. 14).** "As the location for the Milham (= war tower) I suggest the fortress like tower built on top of the still visible rock (circa 10 x 10 m) protruding from the rampart at the entrance of today's Gobat School"
- **Inference.** The rock scarp is real and measured roughly. The tower on it is Pixner's reconstruction. The repo's PEF index mentions "Gobat" (33 files; not checked against this).

**8. An inaccessible tomb NE of the Milḥam (repo 14).**
- **Evidence (p. 347 n. 15).** "Apparently there was one, now inaccessible, outside the northeastern corner of the Milham. It was probably out of use at the time the Essene Quarter existed."
- **Inference.** This is reported and hedged ("apparently"). Low weight.

**9. A cistern with a rock pillar in the Dormition garden (repo 15).**
- **Evidence (p. 347 n. 16).** "There is in the garden of Dormition Abbey, just east of the pool of nr 12 a large cistern (…) with a rock pillar in the middle: indeed an astonishing coincidence!"
- **Inference.** This is the closest feature-to-text match Pixner claims: the entry has "in the pillar on its northern side". It is a physical feature he describes in the present tense. NEW for repo 15, where the repo has only tell_es_sultan. The repo's water-systems register already holds a Mount Zion–Dormition record (HA-ESI 138, 2026: an aqueduct and a cistern dated Byzantine by a coin of 583/4 CE in its fill; `research/regional/water_systems/survey_rows.csv`). Whether that cistern is Pixner's pillared cistern is **unknown**. Do not equate them without a plan.

### B. Qumran (repo 19, 20)

**10. A crevasse north of Kh. Qumran (repo 19).**
- **Evidence (p. 348 n. 20).** "To the north of it there is still a crevasse initiating a wadi taking an easterly course. The hiding place could well have been there."
- **Inference.** This is a topographic feature with no installation. The site is kh_qumran, already in the repo but not a candidate for repo 19.

**11. A stone mount with a cemetery in Wadi Qumran (repo 20).**
- **Evidence (p. 348 n. 21).** "In the Ravine (gy) of Sekakah (Wadi Qumran) there is a stone mount, occupied by an ancient cemetery."
- **Inference.** This refines the existing wadi_qumran candidate: *ygr* = a mound bearing a cemetery. Which cemetery he means is not stated.

### C. Yarmuk / "Land of Damascus" (repo 33–45, 59, 60): an entirely new region for the repo

**12. El-Ekser spring, basalt tower and conduit (repo 33–35).**
- **Evidence (p. 351, quoting Schumacher, ZDPV 37, 1914, p. 266).** "Von der Quelle führt eine Steinleitung in der challat el-'ain 60 m den Abhang hinab und füllt zwei Basaltsarkophage."
- **Evidence (p. 351 n. 29).** "The heap of natural basalt stones, mentioned above (with a photo on Tafel LII A), might be the ygr (stoneheap) of nr 37."
- **Inference.** These are recorded features: a spring in a basalt hole, an "ancient" tower of unhewn basalt blocks, a 60 m stone conduit and a stone heap with a photograph. Pixner fits repo 33 (Beth Akkar / el-Ekser road and conduit), repo 34 (the "Outer Gorge") and repo 35 (the stone heap) to them. All are undated, and the name match Ekser/ʾḥzr carries most of the weight.

**13. Wadi esh-Shaʿeib = Shaveh (repo 36–37).**
- **Evidence (p. 351 n. 31).** "Just north-east of El-Ekser there is a small wadi called Esh-Sha-eib, which offers a better possibility."
- **Inference.** This is a name match only. Note that Pixner himself **abandons** his 1976 Shaveh = Bethso (Mt Zion) equation (after Yadin, 1978). That bears on the repo's jer_baqa / jer_shaveh discussion (Q22).

**14. Kafr Som grave caves (repo 40).**
- **Evidence (p. 352 n. 34, quoting Mittmann 1970 p. 23).** "Die nördliche Steilwand des wâdi birgt eine Anzahl von Grabhöhlen, denen man ihre einstige Bestimmung freilich kaum noch ansieht."
- **Inference.** These are recorded tomb caves at a village whose name matches his *swm* reading. The reading itself is contested: Milik has *ym*.

### D. Jerusalem outside the quarter (repo 47, 48, 50)

- **Inference.** The basin is hypothetical; no feature is recorded. The argument rests on sequence: between Ramat Raḥel and his Yad Absalom.
