# Guil 2017, the Siloam water system: extract for entry 49 (col. X 15–16)

**Citation.** Shlomo Guil, "A New Perspective on the Various Components of the Siloam Water System in Jerusalem", *Zeitschrift des Deutschen Palästina-Vereins* 133.2 (2017), pp. 145–175, Taf. 20–22. JSTOR stable URL https://www.jstor.org/stable/26602274. Read from the owner's Drive OCR text ("Guil 2017 – A New Perspective on the Various Components of the Siloam Water System in Jerusalem (ZDPV 133).pdf", https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JqNCzg26c0gcWd74lVV1rXNW5lbNOMaA/view). Page numbers below are the printed ZDPV pages.

**Scope of reading.** The whole OCR text was read, including the bibliography and the plate captions. The OCR cuts off one column, losing line ends, on parts of pp. 146, 148, 150, 155–156, 160, 166, 168 and 170. Section 12 on p. 170 (the tunnel's purpose) is affected most. The page images were not checked, so wording on those pages may be incomplete. The figures were not viewed as images: their content is known only from the captions and OCR labels.

**What the article is.** It argues for dating the system's tunnels and the inscription: Channel II/Tunnel III in MB II, Tunnel VI with a reservoir in the shaft area under Hezekiah, and Tunnel VIII (the Siloam Tunnel) with its inscription in the Hasmonean period. It is not an excavation report. It publishes no new excavation at the pools and no loci, and does not mention L103 or the small receiving pool.

## Terminology and identifications (pp. 146–147)

- **Evidence (p. 146).** "Siloam (Hebrew: Siloah; Arabic: Silw[ān]): An ancient site located in the City of [David] … east side." The OCR is truncated. He uses *Siloah* only as the site name and does not discuss the name's biblical uses.
- **Evidence (p. 146).** "Siloam Spring (Hebrew: Gïhôn): A siphon-type karst spring fed by groundwater which is located on the lower eastern level of the City of David." In 19th-century usage this was the "Virgin's Fount".
- **Evidence (p. 147).** Siloam Pool (Bərēkat ha-Shiloaḥ): "A pool on the southern slope of the City of David fed directly by the water flowing in the Siloam Tunnel, located at the southern end of the tunnel. The Siloam Pool was constructed in the 5th cent. C.E., during the Byzantine period."
- **Evidence (p. 147).** He adds that the name "is presently often applied to the Second Temple Period Pool which is referred to by that name in the New Testament."
- **Evidence (p. 147), Second Temple Period Pool.** Reich and Shukron "uncovered steps of a large pool located at the Birket el-Hamra site on the southern tip of the City of David, being the lowest point in the area". They also uncovered a paved street leading towards the Temple Mount. Paraphrase: he reports that they assume the pool is late Second Temple and fed by the Siloam Tunnel, that it served Herodian-period purification before entry to the Temple Mount, and that it is the New Testament Siloam Pool. He reports their view without adding pool data of his own.
- **Evidence (pp. 147, 170).** He never uses "Shelah" (Neh 3:15), "King's Pool", "Solomon's Pool", "lower pool" (Isa 22:9) or Josephus's names. He identifies no biblical or Josephan pool beyond the New Testament Siloam noted above.
- **Evidence (p. 147), Rock-Cut Pool.** It is a 15 × 10 m rectangle hewn south of the spring, about 4 m deep, with its floor "approximately 4.5 m above the level of the Siloam Spring". He suggests it was "not a 'pool' functioning as a water reservoir but rather a deep levelled surface serving some other purpose". The Round Chamber in its north-east corner has a floor of about 15 m² and stands about 6 m high (p. 147). This feature is at the spring, not at the Siloam pools.

## Components and phases (pp. 148–151; Figs. 1–4)

- **Evidence (p. 148), Phase I.** "the stone-covered Channel II, built approximately at the level of the Siloam Spring, which leads southward from the spring, probably to an unknown pool, perhaps serving irrigation purposes." Tunnel III descends to the Round Chamber.
- **Evidence (p. 149), feed opening and control point.** The Round Chamber has "a feed opening at ca. 0.5m above its floor", so it served "as a place to draw water from". He writes: "It is assumed that the water flow towards the pool was controlled at the intersection between Channel II and Tunnel III." This is an assumed flow-control junction at the spring end, MB II in his scheme. It is not at the Siloam pools and is not dated to the Second Temple period.
- **Evidence (p. 149), levels.** The excavators give the Round Chamber floor as 635 m a.s.l. (citing Reich and Shukron 2011, 152). Guil estimates that floor at 1.4 m below Channel II, "which implies that the spring level in Phase I was 636.40 m above sea level." This is his estimate and applies at the spring only.
- **Evidence (pp. 149–150), Phase II.** Tunnel VI was cut from the foot of Warren's Shaft towards the spring, about 2.5 m below spring level. Channel I (Masterman and Hornstein) runs nearly parallel to Channel II but lower, "probably to feed an assumed external pool". He describes "excess water" flowing "through Channel I southward to an unknown external reservoir" (p. 150). He gives no terminus or dating evidence for the south end of either channel.
- **Evidence (p. 151), Phase III.** Tunnel VIII is a "90-degree prolongation of Tunnel VI", over 500 m long and cut from both ends. Footnote 25 says the water was diverted south "Probably to an Iron Age II pool where the 'Second Temple Pool' is situat[ed]". Tunnels IV and V belong to this phase.
- **Evidence (p. 162), tunnel data.** The tunnel is 512.50 m long from the VI/VIII intersection "to the southern exit point of Tunnel VIII" (citing Vincent 1911, 20, 44). The figure of 533 m includes Tunnel VI. The average gradient is "0.6m per 1,000m" (citing Shiloh 1984, 23). The meeting point is Conder's point e, Vincent's station XXIX. The southern exit point is mentioned only as the end of the length measurement. He gives no elevation for the outlet and does not describe it.
- **Figures.** Figs. 1–3 (pp. 148, 150, 151) are partial phase plans "based on Vincent (1911)", one each for Phases I–III. Fig. 4 (p. 152) is the "Composite plan of the Siloam Water System, Vincent 1911". Fig. 5 (p. 154) is Warren's 1884 section of Warren's Shaft, showing "Masonry" and an "Iron Ring". Figs. 6–7 (pp. 157) are tracings of the inscription. Taf. 20 shows the shaft enclosure; Taf. 21–22 show letterforms and the empty inscription panel. **None is a plan or section of the Siloam pools, Birket el-Ḥamra or the tunnel outlet.** On the evidence of the OCR labels ("ROCK-HEWN WORKS at VIRGIN FOUNTAIN"), the Vincent plans cover the spring end.

## Reservoir in the shaft area (pp. 153–155; Fig. 5)

- **Evidence (pp. 153, 155).** He proposes "a plastered, masonry reservoir" for Hezekiah in the enclosure at the upper mouth of Warren's Shaft. He measured the enclosure himself (wall 3.20 m; 0.60 m to the shaft mouth; shaft mouth 2.50 m wide; 1.60–2.05 m to the opposite wall). His reconstructed options are 1.20 × 1.90 × 2.5 m (about 6 m³) or 1.80 × 2.10 × 0.7 m (2.65 m³) (p. 155).
- **Evidence (p. 155).** From Warren's "loose stones about a foot cube" and "iron ring", he says "It is therefore evident that Warren had discovered the masonry vestige of Hezekiah's pool". This is his inference from 19th-century notes. "There are presently no signs of such masonry." The structure is up the slope at the spring, in Iron II, and is irrelevant to entry 49's setting at the pools.

## Birket el-Ḥamra, dams and buttress walls (p. 167)

- **Evidence (p. 167).** "There is presently no archaeological evidence that a pool existed in close proximity [to the tunnel's southern opening] during Iron Age II". Birket el-Ḥamra is "the logical candidate for an Iron Age II pool by topographical considerations".
- **Evidence (p. 167).** Bliss and Dickie "discovered two buttress walls east of Birket el-Hamra, which served as protective walls as well as supportive walls for an artificial pool. There are no means to date these walls". The citation is Bliss 1898, 96–103 and General Plan II.
- **Evidence (p. 167).** Paraphrase: Shiloh found nothing earlier than the Hellenistic period in Area A2, north of Birket el-Ḥamra, or in Area A1, east of it. Guil writes: "widespread building activities took place in Area Al during Second Temple times, connected mainly with the dam-walls of the reservoirs at Birket el-Hamra" (citing Shiloh 1984, 5–6, Table II). Mazar (2000, 227) therefore doubts that Bliss's buttress walls predate the Second Temple period.
- **Evidence (p. 167).** In 2012–2013, Weksler-Bdolah, with Lavi and Zanton [Szanton], uncovered Bliss's buttress walls south of Birket el-Ḥamra. "The larger Buttress wall [Bliss, General Plan II, Wall M2–D2] was dated by Weksler-Bdolah to the Hasmonean period of the 1st cent. B.C.E." (citing Weksler-Bdolah 2013, *New Studies in the Archaeology of Jerusalem* 7, 171–194 [Hebr.]). This dates a dam or buttress wall only, through a secondary report. It says nothing of a trough, conduit or junction.
- **Evidence (pp. 167–168).** He argues that a siege reservoir would not sit "at the presumed extreme edge of the city, visible to all". This is an argument and adds no data.
- He gives no sluice, dam channel, branch channel or inlet description for the main pool, and nothing on the Reich–Shukron feeding channel or sluice branch (Galor and Avni 2011, p. 247, already in the repo).

## Purpose, purification and dating of the tunnel (pp. 168–171)

- **Evidence (p. 168).** He lists his conclusions: the tunnel took "three to four years", the technology "was not available during the Iron Age II", and the project "had no economic justification". He therefore concludes it was not Hezekiah's work but "at a considerably later date".
- **Evidence (pp. 168–169).** He criticizes the radiocarbon dates of Frumkin, Shimron and Rosenbaum (2003) from tunnel plaster. The dates are 822–796 BCE (wood, 1σ) and 800–510 BCE (short-lived plant, 2σ). His objections are the old-wood effect, a possible reservoir effect in reeds, and the Hallstatt plateau. The paper gives no new dating sample.
- **Evidence (p. 170, OCR truncated).** He restates that Reich and Shukron uncovered steps and a paved street "from the Herodian Second Temple per[iod]" and "propose that the pool was for purific[ation]". He links the tunnel to "greater religious strict[ness]" from the "middle of the 2nd cent B.C.E." Paraphrase of the truncated text: miqveh water must not be "drawn". Channels I and II along the Kidron were rejected because they ran near First Temple graves and an unclean valley, so a subterranean tunnel was cut "as far away as possible from the graves".
- **Evidence (p. 171).** "Tunnel VIII was hewn by the Hasmonean authorities to transport pure spring water, according to halachic law, to a large pool located in the area known as Birket el-Hamra, which functioned as a large miqwë for the worshippers visiting the Temple Mount." The inscription is "a product of archaism, engraved at the time of the hewing of the Siloam Tunnel in the Hasmonean period" (p. 171). The date rests on palaeography, technology and his argument from purpose. He presents no stratified find from the pool.

## Items searched for and not found

- No trough, basin, hollowed stone, water outlet installation, conduit junction or inlet at the Byzantine pool, the small receiving pool or Birket el-Ḥamra.
- No mention of L103 or any locus number. He does not mention the small receiving pool as distinct from the Byzantine pool.
- No coins, pottery or levels from the Siloam pools. The only levels given are at the spring (635 m and an estimated 636.40 m a.s.l.).
- No bath or bathhouse. The only bathing-type function is ritual immersion (miqveh) in the large pool, which he adopts from Reich and Shukron.

## Inference for entry 49

- **Inference.** Guil does not give a surveyed, phase-dated trough or conduit junction at the Siloam pools, L103 or otherwise. The only junction he discusses is the Channel II/Tunnel III intersection at the spring. There flow control is "assumed" (p. 149), with no survey, and he dates it MB II. That junction does not meet the decisive-next record and does not touch the pools. He prints no plan of the pools (Figs. 1–4 are Vincent's spring-end plans).
- **Inference.** The F basis stands as written: pools and tunnel outlet recorded, no trough established. Guil adds only a tunnel-exit length datum (512.50 m, p. 162) and nothing about the outlet's form or level.
- **Inference, bathing.** Guil's purpose argument is that Tunnel VIII was cut so the Birket el-Ḥamra pool could serve as a large miqveh with undrawn water (pp. 170–171). This is an argued function, not new evidence. It rests on Reich and Shukron's interpretation, already in the repo from Galor and Avni 2011, pp. 248–249, and on his Hasmonean dating of the tunnel. The interpretation is the same one already in the repo, so it adds no independent observation. Ritual immersion is not a bathhouse. Milik's "baths" (בית חמים, which implies heated or bathing facilities) gains nothing specific from it. "Bathing function … unestablished" stays.
- **Inference, dating.** He reports a secondary date for Bliss's larger buttress (dam) wall M2–D2, which Weksler-Bdolah puts in the Hasmonean period, 1st century BCE (p. 167). This dates part of the Birket el-Ḥamra dam system near the start of the 50 BCE–70 CE window. It is a wall, not a trough or conduit. Its primary source, Weksler-Bdolah 2013, *New Studies in the Archaeology of Jerusalem* 7, 171–194 (Hebrew), is worth checking for sections or levels at the dam where an outlet or sluice might show. That is a lead, not a finding.
- **Inference, grooved tank.** Guil does not mention the Bliss-Dickie grooved tank. His statement that the Bliss buttress walls cannot be dated except through the new Weksler-Bdolah excavation does not date the tank.
- **Inference, names.** He identifies the New Testament Siloam with the Second Temple pool at Birket el-Ḥamra (p. 147, following Reich and Shukron). He calls the pool at the tunnel outlet the Siloam Pool but treats it as Byzantine, 5th century CE (p. 147). That conflicts with Szanton's assignment in the repo (Silwan/Siloam pool at the outlet, Solomon's Pool at Birket el-Ḥamra), but Guil gives no evidence for the Byzantine-only date and does not discuss the earlier small receiving pool. He gives nothing on Shelah, the King's Pool, Solomon's Pool or Josephus. His naming should not be used to choose between the two pools.
- **Inference, net change.** Nothing changes F (stays at its current rating). New for the repo, if not already recorded: (1) Weksler-Bdolah 2013 as the primary source for a Hasmonean date of the Birket el-Ḥamra dam or buttress wall; (2) Guil's Hasmonean dating of Tunnel VIII, which is a minority view and an argument, not excavated evidence. If accepted, it would make the whole tunnel-to-pool system no earlier than the 2nd–1st century BCE. That is consistent with the window but does not discriminate any trough.
