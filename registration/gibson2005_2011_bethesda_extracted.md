# Gibson on the Bethesda pools and the eastern complex (entries 55, 56)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = Gibson states it. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## The pools (entry 55)

- **Evidence (A p. 286; B p. 23).** Northern pool 53 × 40 m, the reservoir (*otsar*), about 21,200 m³; southern pool 47 × 52 m, the bathing pool (*mikveh*). Both trapezoidal; two separate basins with a barrier wall between (B pp. 22, 25).
- **Inference.** By these figures the northern pool is the smaller in area (2,120 against 2,444 m²). Gibson does not say so.
- **Evidence (A p. 287, Fig. 1; B p. 25).** The southern pool has hewn and built steps with landings along its whole western side (F306–309). The complex could be reached only from the west (A p. 287 n. 30; B p. 28).
- **Evidence.** No steps or other means of entry into the **northern** pool are recorded in A or B. The north-west corner of the northern pool is still unknown (B n. 15).
- **Evidence (A p. 287, Fig. 2; B pp. 23, 26).** The barrier wall F295 (about 6–6.5 m thick) holds a shaft F296, a tunnel F266 with a sluice, and a trough; surplus water drained south.
- **Evidence (A p. 286; B pp. 24–25, 41, 43).** Both pools were built together under Herod, between 31 and 22 BCE ("perhaps circa 25 BC" in A). The basis is the masonry, the single plan and the order of Herod's works. **No stratified finds, coins or pottery are cited for either pool's construction.** Gibson rejects the Iron Age and Hellenistic dates of Duprez, Pierre and Rousée, and Bahat (A n. 31; B n. 14).
- **Evidence (B pp. 29–42).** Both pools were in use in the first century CE and still held water in 441. The northern pool was largely filled by the Byzantine period (its upper fills 9th–10th c., from Alliata's unpublished sondage); the southern began to fill in the 8th century.

## The eastern complex (entry 56)

- **Evidence (A n. 30; B p. 28).** East of the southern pool is a first-century dwelling of two or more storeys, not a healing sanctuary: rock-cut rooms F160, F163, F167; vaulted basements F5, F11; *miqwaʾot* F181, F213; grey-plastered basins F18, F164, F170; a cistern F190; eleven stone troughs (read as mangers). Painted plaster came from fills "east of the main cistern".
- **Evidence.** **No threshold, sill, jamb, doorway or entrance direction is recorded for any room, basin or cistern of this complex** in A or B. The doorways B describes belong to Byzantine and Crusader buildings (pp. 34–41).
- **Evidence (A n. 30; B pp. 28–29, 42).** Dated by first-century pottery and coins from the original excavations, and by the Bezetha quarter, which recent excavations put in the first century CE "and not before". Gibson calls Duprez's Hasmonean start (Jannaeus coins) "incorrect", without new finds. No feature-level date is given.
- **Evidence (B p. 30).** Cisterns F40 and F190 are also listed among the Late Roman remains east of the pools.
- **Evidence (A p. 285; B p. 19).** The 1862–1967 excavations remain "largely unpublished"; the full results are to appear in a *POC* supplement and a monograph.

## Effect (applied under tests U7 and U8)

- **Entry 56, jer_bethesda: no change (F 1, D 2).** No threshold or doorway of a specific cistern or chamber is recorded (U7, F). The complex is dated in the window, first century CE (U7, D: not lowered). Gibson's "accessible only from the west" refers to the pools, not to a cistern or chamber.
- **Entry 55, jer_bethesda: no change (F 2, D 2).** The northern basin is separate and the smaller, but no entry into it is recorded (U8, F). Gibson's Herodian date (31–22 BCE) rests on masonry and building order, not on stratified finds or a dated phase, and it conflicts with Duprez's 2nd-century-BCE date; under the rubric's "take the lower" rule, D stays 2.
- **Inference (dating conflict).** Duprez (pools 2nd c. BCE, useless after Agrippa I's wall) and Gibson (pools Herodian, in use into the 5th c.) disagree. Both put the pools standing in 31 BCE–41 CE, so the window holds either way.
