# The Mar Saba map: the Kidron, Hyrcania and the Buqeia (entries 1, 16, 17, 24, 29, 35)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = the survey states it, or it is drawn on its figure. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## Coverage

- **Evidence (EN pp. 3–4).** Sheet 18–12 at 1:20,000; field numbers give 1 km squares. **Inference:** the 139 site coordinates span about old-grid E 180–190 / N 120–130.
- **Evidence (EN pp. 4, 17).** The Hyrcania Valley (el-Buqeiʿa) is in the east of the map; the index lists its sites (10, 18–21, 34, 36–41, 50–57, 71–75, 92, 110–114, 120, 121, 130, 131). The Iron II fortresses at Sites 35, 91 and 101 are described as being in the valley (EN pp. 5, 7).
- **Evidence.** Wadi Kuteif is not named in either edition.

## Hyrcania's water system (entries 16, 29, 35)

- **Evidence (EN p. 8).** The northern, shorter aqueduct begins at Naḥal Sekhakha and is Hasmonean; the southern one begins "in Naḥal Qidron" and is Herodian.
- **Evidence (Site 48, EN pp. 65–66).** The northern aqueduct starts at a dam 17.5 × 4–5 m built diagonally across Naḥal Sekhakha, "at the top of the waterfall". Its plaster is described as typical of the Second Temple period. A road from near the dam follows the course of the aqueduct (Site 63, EN p. 79).
- **Evidence (EN p. 8; HE p. 8 figure).** **No intake structure, dam or weir is recorded at the head of the southern aqueduct.** Its first stretch (nos. 1–7) is drawn as a conjectured, dashed line, with no dam symbol.
- **Evidence (Site 83, EN p. 106).** On the east cliffs of the Qidron the southern aqueduct is levelled by walls and cuttings for about 1,250 m from 18142 12448. In the Byzantine period its first segment was blocked and reused as a reservoir, with a sediment pool fed by a small diverting dam.
- **Evidence (Sites 67–69, EN pp. 85–88).** At the saddle bridges, the lower four courses of bridge 69 are Hasmonean and the upper ones Herodian. The modern path to Hyrcania runs along the lower, Byzantine channel at Site 67; a 0.5 m passage runs through bridge 69 (HE p. 88).
- **Evidence (Site 82, EN p. 105).** A footpath climbs from the Qidron bed to the Site 82 complex and crosses the aqueduct at 18142 12448, the first Kidron segment of the **southern** aqueduct.
- **Evidence (Site 69, EN pp. 87–88; HE p. 89).** North of the bridge are two adjoining rock-hewn pools, first quarries, then reservoirs and moats. The north pool is "deep, elaborate", the south one shallower; only the Hebrew gives the north pool's sides (15, 18.2, 16, 19 m). Feldman's probes in the north pool found lime-and-gravel plaster, "Roman hydraulic" (EN) or typical Second Temple plaster (HE). No inlet is described for either pool.
- **Evidence (Site 70, EN pp. 90–91; HE p. 92).** About twenty plastered cisterns and reservoirs (20,000 m³). The aqueduct splits into north and south branches round the summit. Feldman's probe in cistern C found "chiefly Herodian" lamps and jars and a Herodian coin, and no later vessels.

## Cairns along the Kidron (entry 35)

- **Evidence (EN p. 9).** None of the 31 cairn sites lies between the map's western edge and Deir Mar Saba, and no cairn or stone heap is recorded at a gorge mouth anywhere on the map. In that stretch there are only a sheikh's tomb (Site 58) and a Muslim cemetery with stone-pile graves (Site 60).
- **Evidence (EN pp. 185–190).** Downstream of Mar Saba, three cairns stand by the Qidron, all "lacking indicative finds":
  - Site 99 (about 18219 12305): a 3 m cairn on a slope east of the Qidron, about 0.9 km south-east of the monastery (Inference, from the grid).
  - Site 100 (about 18314 12315): a 5.4 m ring cairn on a shoulder north of a Qidron tributary.
  - Site 108 (about 18361 12206): a 3.5 m square cairn on a hilltop south of the Qidron.
- **Evidence (EN p. 195).** Site 117, in the Qidron in the Hyrcania Valley, has nine possible grave mounds (Mamluk pottery).
- **Evidence (EN p. 55).** Site 32, a stone pile on a hilltop near Bir Umm el-Fus ("burial? road marker?"), is not on the Qidron.

## The Buqeia (entries 1, 17)

- **Evidence (Site 35, EN p. 57).** Kh. Abu Ṭabaq is an Iron II fortress. At its reservoir, Magharat Abu Ṭabaq, "steps descend from cave opening … into chamber"; the entrance has ashlar jambs. Pottery: "Iron IIb-c (majority), Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine (scanty finds)".
- **Evidence (EN pp. 117, 186–187, 192).** Hellenistic and Roman sherds are also listed at the fortresses Kurm el-ʿAjaz (Site 91) and Kh. el-Makari (Site 101; its pool was used in Roman times), and Hellenistic sherds at Site 112. Site entries say "Roman" without subdivision.
- **Evidence (EN p. 204).** Site 130 is an ancient road with shallow paved steps (Chalcolithic and Roman pottery). Site 66, just outside the valley in Naḥal Sekhakha, is a stepped tunnel of 50 steps, "apparently" of the Second Temple period (EN pp. 8, 84, 116).
- **Evidence.** No pair of standing ruins is recorded.

## Effect (applied under tests U2–U4)

- **35 mar_saba: F 0→1 (U2).** Three cairns are recorded on the Kidron gorge below Mar Saba (Sites 99, 100, 108), the nearest about 0.9 km downstream. They are generic features of the kind in the district, none at a gorge mouth and none dated. D stays 1: the cairns have no finds, and the Herodian southern aqueduct in the Qidron still gives district use. The weakest link becomes 1, so the label goes from none to **weak**.
- **35 hyrcania: no change (F 0).** No dam on the Kidron feeding Hyrcania; the only dam is on Naḥal Sekhakha.
- **16 hyrcania: no change (F 2).** The recorded path crossing (Site 82) is on the southern aqueduct, not the northern one.
- **29 hyrcania: no change (F 2, D 2).** The north pool is deeper and more elaborate, but which pool is larger is not stated, and no inlet is recorded. Its plaster is dated by type, not by a stratified context or phase; under "take the lower", D stays 2.
- **1 buqeia: D 0→1 (U4).** Kh. Abu Ṭabaq, a ruin in the Buqeia, has steps into its reservoir and Hellenistic and Roman sherds, but the sherds are survey finds and "Roman" is not divided. Under the rubric that is thin evidence for the window (D 1), not occupation (D 2). The frozen test allowed 2 for a ruin with steps, but the rubric sets the ceiling. The label goes from none to **weak**. F was not tested this round; Kh. Abu Ṭabaq is a lead for a future F test (a specific ruin with steps).
- **17 buqeia: D 0→1 (U4).** Hellenistic and Roman sherds are recorded at three ruins in the valley (Sites 35, 91, 101): district evidence of thin weight. No pair of ruins is recorded, so the higher step does not apply. The label goes from none to **weak**.
- **24 kuteif: no change.** Wadi Kuteif is outside the map.
