# Tell el-Qos (Tell el-Qaus), Jericho oasis: site entry

Source: M. D'Andrea and M. Sala, "Catalogue of the sites in the Jericho Oasis and its surroundings", in L. Nigro, M. Sala and H. Taha (eds.), *Archaeological Heritage in the Jericho Oasis* (ROSAPAT 07), 2011. Extract file `qos_padis.pdf`: printed pp. 139, 206, 227 (Pl. III). The text layer and the 200 dpi renders were checked against each other and agree. The plate was also rendered at 400 dpi, rotated upright and zoomed.

## 1. Catalogue entry (verbatim)

**Evidence (p. 139)**, full text of the entry:

> **74) Qos, Tell el-**
> [PADIS code 0021]
> Site location: Lat. +31°51'13.58" N; Long. +35°27'53.28" E
> State of preservation: ruins
> Occupational period: Byzantine
> Site typology: architectural remains
> Public architecture: cisterns, plastered basin
> Material culture: mosaic *tesserae*
> Bibliography: Conder - Kitchener 1883, 222; Augustinović 1950, 266; 1951, 89-91; Cirelli - Zagari 2000, 366; Zagari 2000, 357
> Plates: I, III

- **Evidence (p. 139):** That is the whole entry. It has no prose description and no ancient name. It gives no site extension, no absolute dates and no per-period subsections. Entry 75 (Jebel Quruntul), on the same page, has per-period subsections; entry 74 has none. The line "Plate: I" at the top of p. 139 is the end of the previous entry (73), not part of 74.
- **Evidence (p. 206)**, bibliography for 74, verbatim:
  > AUGUSTINOVIĆ, A. 1950 "Note su Gerico Bizantina", in *La Terra Santa* 25/9 (1950), pp. 264-266 (in particular p. 266).
  > 1951 *Gerico e dintorni*, Jerusalem 1951 (in particular pp. 89-91).
  > CIRELLI, E. - ZAGARI, F. 2000 "Gerico in epoca bizantina e islamica. Problemi e proposte di ricerca", in *Archeologia Medievale* 27 (2000), pp. 365-376 (in particular p. 366).
  > CONDER, C.R. - KITCHENER, H.H. 1883 *The Survey of Western Palestine: Memoirs of the Topography, Orography, Hydrography, and Archaeology. Volume III. Sheets XVII-XXVI. Judaea*, London 1883 (in particular p. 222).
  > ZAGARI, F. 2000 "Appendix H: Remarks on the Byzantine occupation of Tell es-Sultan", in N. MARCHETTI - L. NIGRO (eds.), *Excavations at Jericho, 1998. Preliminary Report on the Second Season of Archaeological Excavations and Surveys at Tell es-Sultan, Palestine* (Quaderni di Gerico 2), Rome 2000, pp. 355-381 (in particular p. 357).
- **Evidence (pp. 139, 206):** Augustinović appears only as a citation: 1950, p. 266 (an article titled "Note su Gerico Bizantina") and 1951, pp. 89-91. The entry does not quote or paraphrase him and does not say which feature comes from which source.
- **Inference:** Every cited work except Conder-Kitchener has "Byzantine" in its title or is about Byzantine/Islamic Jericho. That fits the single "Byzantine" period. The entry's features (cisterns, plastered basin, tesserae) presumably come from these sources, but the extract does not say which one supplies what. Checking needs Augustinović 1951, pp. 89-91, and SWP III, p. 222.

## 2. Pl. III: the Augustinović plan

- **Evidence (p. 227, caption):** "Pl. III - Map of archaeological and historical sites and monuments in the Jericho Oasis registered by A. Augustinović in the 1950ies (after Augustinović 1951, plan)."
- **Evidence (plate):** The plan is printed rotated 90° on the page. Title: "GERICO E DINTORNI". North arrow labelled "NOD" (= Nord). Once the plate is turned upright, north points up. Graphic scale: "0 250 500 1000 m", with "1 km" below. Bottom right: "1950" above the monogram "AA" (A. Augustinović). The plate has **no legend**.
- **Evidence (plate):** At Tell el-Qos, the label "TELL EL-QOS" has an arrow pointing west to a small cluster of about seven solid dots. The cluster sits at the eastern edge of the gardens and buildings of modern Jericho, where the road "PER 'AMMAN" leaves the town to the north-east. It lies just north of the hatched banks of WADI EL-QELT, with double-dashed lines running east-south-east from it along the wadi.
- **Evidence (plate):** No building outline, cave, cistern, basin, bath or channel is drawn or labelled at Tell el-Qos. The site has no date and no attribution next to it.
- **Evidence (plate), surroundings:** About 300 m to the north, TELL HASSAN has a small square or building symbol with an arrow. To the south-west are OSPIZIO RUSSO and CASERMA (a double-rectangle symbol). Farther west are the town gardens, S. ANDREA and the BIRKET MUSA pool, a square symbol. The road to the west is labelled "...ROMANA" with "STRADA" farther west, i.e. the Roman road. To the north-east are TELL MATLAB "(GALGALA BIZANTINA ?)", the EREMITAGGIO NESTORIANO and COL. PROF. To the east are EN-NETELE "(GALGALA BIBLICA ?)" and the sites along the Wadi el-Qelt.
- **Evidence (plate), dating shown anywhere:** The only period words on the plan are "(GALGALA BIZANTINA ?)" at Tell Matlab, "(GALGALA BIBLICA ?)" at En-Netele, "...ROMANA", plus "SINAGOGA", "MOSAICO", "MONASTERO ?" and "(PRODROMOS)" elsewhere. Nothing dated appears at Tell el-Qos.
- **Inference:** The dot cluster is the plan's generic sign for a ruin or tell. Rings or clusters of dots mark Tell Abu Khurs, Tell Matlab, En-Netele, the "Monastero ?" sites and others. It does not depict specific structures. The double-dashed lines are tracks or water channels, but with no legend the plan cannot tell us which.

## Answers

- **Cave, bath-room or building recorded at the site?** No.
  - **Evidence (p. 139):** The catalogue lists only "cisterns, plastered basin" (public architecture) and "mosaic *tesserae*" (material culture). Its typology is "architectural remains" and the site is "ruins". It names no cave, no bath and no specific building.
  - **Evidence (Pl. III):** The plan has only a dot cluster and a name.
  - **Inference:** "Architectural remains" plus tesserae suggests some structure once stood here, perhaps one with a mosaic floor. The entry identifies none.
- **Anything Hellenistic, Roman or earlier than 135 CE recorded there?** No.
  - **Evidence (p. 139):** The only occupational period given is "Byzantine". The entry lists no earlier remains or finds.
  - **Evidence (Pl. III):** Nothing dated appears at the site. "Romana" belongs to the road farther west, not to Tell el-Qos.
  - **Inference:** The extract does not rule out earlier material in the cited sources (e.g. SWP p. 222), but the PADIS entry records none.
- **What exactly is dated Byzantine?**
  - **Evidence (p. 139):** The site's single "Occupational period: Byzantine". The entry has no period subsections, so the Byzantine label covers everything listed: cisterns, a plastered basin and mosaic tesserae, as "architectural remains" in ruins.
  - **Inference:** The entry dates no individual feature separately, and it gives no absolute date or dating method.
