# Shivti'el 2019 and Atkinson 2016: targeted reads for the Hyrcania tunnels (21 Oct 2026)

Read in the owner's signed-in Perlego reader from the cloud session on 21 Oct 2026. Short quotations only. EVIDENCE = the source says it. INFERENCE = this project. BK = background knowledge, not checked here.

## 1. Y. Shivti'el, *Cliff Shelters and Hiding Complexes: The Jewish Defense Methods in Galilee During the Roman Period* (NTOA Series Archaeologica 6; Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht 2019)

Perlego https://www.perlego.com/book/3831099. Read: ch. 4 opening and Table 2 (printed pp. 97–98, reader pp. 98–99), and Summary and Conclusions (printed pp. 211–213).

- EVIDENCE: Table 2 (p. 98) gives the features of the hiding complexes in the Judean foothills, after Kloner & Tepper 1987, pp. 50–71.
  - Entry is by shafts, often a round or square cistern.
  - Passages wind with 90° turns. Each section is about 2 m long, "as wide as a body", and on average 0.6 × 0.7 m in section.
  - Chambers are person-high, and their entrances sometimes have thresholds, jambs and bolt holes.
  - There are locking slabs or roll-stones, lamp hollows or ledges along the passages, and ventilation through existing openings.
  - Storage cavities, plastered cisterns, and converted installations (oil presses, columbaria, cisterns) occur.
- EVIDENCE: hiding complexes are hewn "under or near dwellings in settlements" with a Jewish population (p. 97). Many were first cut for other purposes (storage, ritual baths, cisterns, burial caves) and converted later (pp. 212–213).
- EVIDENCE: the dating rests on finds from excavations in 19 Galilean complexes. Rough complexes gave mostly 1st-century CE finds, and finer ones mostly 2nd-century CE finds (p. 212).
- INFERENCE for the Hyrcania tunnels:
  - The owner's photos (`research/assets/plans/hyrcania-field-2026-10-20/`) show a passage about 0.6–0.8 m wide and above head height, with steps, cut from a cliff-face mouth at wadi level.
  - That does not match the hiding-complex type. A hiding complex has crawl-height passages about 0.7 m high with right-angle turns and shaft entries, and lies under a settlement.
  - Shared features are lamp niches and narrowings. Those are generic to any long rock-cut work done in the dark.
  - So the refuge (hiding-complex) reading of the tunnels is weaker than before. A cliff shelter is also unlikely, because cliff shelters are enlarged natural caves (p. 211).

## 2. K. Atkinson, *A History of the Hasmonean State: Josephus and Beyond* (T&T Clark Jewish and Christian Texts 23, 2016)

Perlego https://www.perlego.com/book/804371. The in-book search for "Hyrcania" gave 10 hits. Read: ch. 8 pp. 165–166 and the matching search snippets.

- EVIDENCE: *War* 1.168 adds that Gabinius destroyed Alexandrium, Hyrcania and Machaerus so that the Jews could not use them (ch. 8, search snippet).
- EVIDENCE (pp. 165–166): Alexandra (III), a sister of Antigonus, captured the fortress of Hyrcania.
  - Atkinson dates this to about 37 BCE. She held it until Herod besieged it in 31 BCE, citing *War* 1.364.
  - n. 35: "An observation tower, camp, and remains of a defensive wall at the site show that he undertook an extensive military operation there", citing H. Eshel 1998.
  - Josephus says nothing about the siege. Atkinson writes that she "presumably died in battle or Herod captured and then executed her", and calls her the last ruler of an independent Hasmonean state.
  - n. 33 distinguishes her from Alexandra (II), the daughter of Hyrcanus II (Kokkinos 1998, pp. 114–115).
- INFERENCE:
  - A royal person, the last Hasmonean ruler, may have died at Hyrcania in 31 BCE. That gives the guide's "tomb for a king" idea a possible historical figure. No source places her burial, or anyone's, in the tunnels.
  - The 31 BCE siege, with a camp and siege works, raises a further reading: tunnels cut during the siege, either by attackers or by defenders. The tunnels lie in the wadi cliffs north of the fortress, so their direction relative to the summit would test this.
  - Antipater's burial at Hyrcania (4 BCE; *Ant.* 17.187) is BK and falls outside Atkinson's period.
