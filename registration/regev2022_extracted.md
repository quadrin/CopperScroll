# Regev 2022: miqvaʾot beside tombs, and Jerusalem burial caves

**Labels:** **Evidence** = Regev states it. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## Ritual baths beside tombs (ch. 2, "Ritual Baths Adjacent to Tombs", p. 36)

- **Evidence.** "Several baths were situated in close proximity to burial caves." Regev names the Tomb of the Kings in Jerusalem; near Jerusalem, Ramat Rachel, Nikephoria and Mount Scopus; and Tell en-Nasbeh, Jericho, Kh. Dar ʿAsi, Wadi Sheiban, Kh. Zikhrin, and three baths at second-century Beth Sheʿarim. His source is Adler 2009: 57–60.
- **Evidence.** He reads them as places to immerse on leaving a tomb, and reports Adler's argument (2009: 66–72) that such baths served second-degree corpse impurity. He cites Tobit 2:9 for washing after burial.
- **Inference (entry 46).** Adler 2009 pp. 57–60 should place a miqveh beside a burial cave at Ramat Raḥel. Entry 46's decisive observation is a window-period pool with a recorded entrance there. This is a lead to a source, not a recorded feature: Regev gives no plan, date or entrance.
- **Inference (entry 27).** The Tomb of the Kings (one candidate for entry 27) has a bath beside it in Adler's list. Entry 27's anchor words do not call for water, so its ratings do not change.

## Size and type of Jerusalem burial caves (ch. 3)

- **Evidence (p. 62).** "Most of the burial caves in Jerusalem and its environs contain between four and eight loculi; very few contain more than 12" (Fig. 3.2). Regev contrasts this with paid public burial (catacombs in Rome, Palmyra, and the later Tombs of the Prophets in Jerusalem).
- **Evidence (p. 69).** Fig. 3.2 covers 306 loculus caves. 213 of them (70%) have no more than six niches; very few have 15 or more. Most date to the Herodian period up to 70 CE; some begin in the Hasmonean period. He estimates about 2,525 deceased.
- **Evidence (p. 66).** "Non-familial burial was not uncommon. It was probably relegated to the poor" (citing Patrich 1994: 191–192). The 1,200 shaft graves at Qumran are treated as a separate case.
- **Inference.** For tomb entries (for example 14, 51–54), a family loculus cave of four to eight niches is the common type in the window. A match to such a tomb on type alone does not discriminate (it bears on U, not on F). Entry 54's "graves of the common people" (בני העם, one reading) fits Regev's note that the poor were buried outside family caves, but he places no such graves.
