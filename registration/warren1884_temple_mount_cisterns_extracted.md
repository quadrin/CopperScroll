# Cisterns and chambers under the Haram al-Sharif: extract from Warren & Conder (1884) and the Gibson & Jacobson lecture

## Sources and method

- **W&C** = C. Warren and C. R. Conder, *Survey of Western Palestine: Jerusalem* (1884), `warren1884.pdf`. It has an OCR text layer. Printed page = PDF page − 16 (e.g., printed 219 = PDF 235). Every citation below gives the printed page. The "Tanks inside the Sanctuary" section (pp. 217–225) is signed C. R. C. (Conder). Its numbering follows the Ordnance Survey (Wilson) numbers. I quote these as **W1–W37** ("Warren's tank numbers").
- **G&J** = S. Gibson and D. M. Jacobson, *Below the Temple Mount in Jerusalem* (BAR 637, 1996), known here only from scans reproduced in `gj_lecture.pdf` (66 slides). G&J number cisterns by **Schick's system (S-numbers)**, with the Wilson/Warren number in brackets (slide 9; concordance table, slide 11). Concordance used here: W5=S28, W6=S1, W7=S4, W8=S3, W9=S8, W10=S6, W11=S5, W12=S26, W13=S25, W14=S24, W15=S23, W22=S15, W28=S22, W37=S27, W36=S2.
- **Lecture labels are not Warren numbers.** The lecturer's "Cistern #N" captions are G&J/Schick S-numbers. For example, the lecture's "#23 near the golden gate" is **W15**, and its "#15 near the fosse" is **W22**. The lecturer's own text is kept apart below, under "Lecturer's commentary".
- Reproduced G&J material identified on these slides: 7 (title page), 9–12 (method, concordance, map), 14 (Fig. 2 caption), 15/56 (Mishnah), 16–18 (historical witnesses), 25/50 (Schick-numbered map), 26–28, 30, 32–39, 41–43, 48–49, 52–54 (catalogue entries and figures). Slides 1–6, 8, 19–24, 29, 31, 40, 44–47, 51, 55, 57–66 are the lecturer's own material (3D models, Bible and Mishnah texts, news items, his own annotations).
- The plates in W&C (Plates II, IV, VI, XI, etc.) were not available to me. All map measurements are taken from G&J's reproduced map (slide 12, 100 m scale bar). That image is low-resolution (428 px), so treat every scaled distance as ±5 m.
- Quotes are at most 25 words. Obvious OCR slips were corrected (e.g., "Gell" → "Cell").

## 1. Golden Gate (east wall)

### Inside the wall

- **Evidence (W&C p. 221):** W15 is "North of the last and west of the Golden Gate. It is about 18 feet in diameter, and nearly circular." Depth 35 ft, "the whole being cut in rock, with a roof also of rock". Rock surface at 2,393 ft, 15 ft below the present surface. No distance from the gate is given.
- **Evidence (G&J via slide 41):** S23 (W15), *Bir (Bab) al-Tawba*, "Well of the Gate of Repentance". It has one chamber, approximately circular, 5.5 m diameter [Wa 1871] or 5.8 m [S 1887], 6 m high, with the floor 10.7–11 m below the Haram. The lower part is rock-cut and the upper part masonry, with masonry vaulting [Schick]. There is "One square shaft entrance". It is "Fed by a conduit from the direction of the Golden Gate to the east". G&J quote Wilson: "near Golden Gateway, not visited; 35 feet … deep, dry."
- **Inference (map-scaled, slide 12):** S23/W15 lies about 28–30 m west of the inner (west) face of the Golden Gate building, measured centre to face, and about 50 m from the outer face of the east wall. The cistern's edge is therefore about 25 m from the gate. No cistern is recorded within about 7–10 m of the Golden Gate, inside or outside, in either source.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 221):** W14 is "A pair of chambers 29 feet deep. They appear to be natural caverns, which have been enlarged", with semicircular vaulting. It lies south of W15. W13 lies "Immediately north of" W12: irregular, 40 ft deep, about 30 ft square, fed by a conduit "from a small cistern 250 feet further north". W12 is "East of the platform", rectangular, 44 ft deep, with a semicircular vault.
- **Inference (map, slide 12):** S24/W14 is about 40 m south-west of the gate. S25/W13 and S26/W12 are further south. S22/W28 is about 65 m west-north-west.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 223):** W28, *Bir el Habash*, "outside the platform on the north-east", about 10 ft diameter and rock-cut.

### Outside the wall

- **Evidence (W&C p. 142):** Warren's shaft was "143 feet from the south end of the gate, and in a line perpendicular to its front". This was the closest point he could reach, because Moslem tombs fill "the whole space in front of the east wall".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 142):** The gallery driven west from that shaft rose with the rock "until at 18 feet 6 inches a tank or rock-cut tomb was crossed. The examination of this was reserved". It is not described again anywhere in the volume.
- **Inference:** The gallery met a massive wall at 97 ft, "46 feet from the Sanctuary wall" (p. 144), so 97 + 46 = 143 ft, which is consistent. The tank or tomb therefore lies about 124 ft (about 38 m) east of the Golden Gate's east face. Its size is not recorded.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 144–145):** A massive wall of rough-faced drafted blocks lies "about 46 feet in front of the Golden Gate". Its cement is like that which the fellahin "stated that such is used at the present day in the formation of cisterns" (p. 145). The wall is not a cistern.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 129):** The only tank recorded against the outer face of the east wall is at the north-east angle (Shaft D): "a small catch-pit or tank, 5 feet 7 inches deep". It measures 2 ft 9 in north to south by 3 ft east to west and is "situated about 1 foot 6 inches from the Sanctuary wall". It had a flagstone cover and plastered sides.
- **Inference (map):** That catch-pit is roughly 150–190 m north of the Golden Gate, so it is not "near" it.

### Cut under the east wall? Sill or threshold?

- **Evidence (W&C p. 225):** W37 was examined by Schick, "a little west of No. 13 … passing beneath the east wall of the platform". This is the east wall of the raised inner platform, **not** the Haram's east wall. It is wholly rock-cut.
- **Evidence (G&J via slide 42):** S27 (W37) is an ovular chamber about 5 m in diameter, extended west by a passage. Overall 22 × 3 m [Schick]. "One square shaft entrance at the east end". Floor 7.5–8.4 m below the Haram. Conder recorded rock "beneath the eastern inner platform wall immediately north of the cistern".
- **Evidence (G&J Fig. 62 caption, slide 43):** Includes "(9) East wall of the inner platform of the Haram" and "(11) Small building in the east wall, with steps" (Schick drawing).
- **Evidence (W&C p. 218):** W5 has "an entrance with a flight of steps at its east end, ascending southwards to the surface outside the platform". At the east entrance "are remains of a door".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 219):** In the platform's east wall, north of W5, are "A door, with a window to the north of it and another to the south". These are closed, and Warren takes them for the Cell of Bostam vaults. Permission to open them was refused in 1881.
- **Evidence (negative):** Neither source records a cistern under the Haram's outer east wall. Neither records a sill, threshold or step above any cistern entrance on the east side. The only sills Warren gives in this area are gate sills: the Golden Gate's sill is at level 2,396 (p. 141).

## 2. Cistern No. 8 ("Great Sea") and the southern cisterns

### W8: the Great Sea

- **Evidence (W&C p. 219–220):** W8 is "Usually known as the 'Great Sea,' is called by the natives Bir el Aswad, or 'The Black Well.'" It is "a fine cavern with rocky piers, reached from the south by a narrow staircase". Depth 43 ft, "partly roofed in rock and partly with flat stones".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 220):** It is "the largest of all the Sanctuary tanks, and has numerous manholes from the surface, three of which are in use". The rock surface is at 2,411, 5 ft below the surface. "A conduit enters this tank from the east. On the north-east there is a small circular chamber." Capacity is "at least two million gallons".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 225):** W32 is "A small tank in the Aksa Mosque, apparently connected with No. 8."
- **Evidence (W&C p. 165):** The passages under the Triple Gate "were found to communicate with Tanks X. and XI., and probably with the Great Sea".
- **Evidence (G&J via slide 32):** S3 (W8). G&J name it *Bir Buhayr* ("The Small Sea"), while slide 34 says it is now commonly called the "Great Sea" (*al-Bahr al-Kabir*).
  - Layout: one principal chamber in three parts, plus a small circular chamber to the north-west. Entirely rock-cut and plastered. Five free-standing piers [Schick].
  - Size: about 40 × 30 m, 10–11.5 m high, capacity 12,000 m³ [Schick] or 9,100 m³ [Barclay]. Floor 13.3–14 m below the Haram.
  - Openings: "Numerous shaft entrances [Pierotti]; 3 in use [Wilson]. Descending staircase from the south-east with 44 steps [Barclay] or 50 steps [Schick]".
- **Evidence (G&J via slides 33–34):** It lies "to the south of the inner platform and is the largest of the underground cavities". Barclay entered by removing "a half-buried marble capital covering the entrance". The steps were "so worn in some places as to have required partial re-cutting, only a few centuries ago". The cistern "had eight shaft entrances but only one was open at the time of Barclay's visit". Whitty (1862) said the opening "is like the mouth of a well, several feet deep, at the bottom of which commences a ruined flight of steps". Pierotti showed passages from its east wall "towards … 'Solomon's Stables'" and "southwards … towards the area of the Triple Gate".
- **Discrepancy (Evidence):** W&C give the name *Bir el Aswad* ("Black Well") to W8 (p. 220). G&J give *al-Bir al-Aswad* to S4 = **W7** (slide 35). W&C instead call W7 "el Bahr, or the 'Sea'" (p. 219).

### Other large southern cisterns

- **W9 (Evidence, W&C p. 220):** *Bir el Warakah*, "Well of the Leaf", under the Aksa. Depth 42 ft, rock roof, central pillar. "An aqueduct leads from the Well of the Leaf through the passage of the Double Gate". Surface drainage ducts enter it.
- **W7 (Evidence, W&C p. 219):** 62 ft deep, a rock-cut chamber, "This cistern has two mouths near each other." Four steps lead up to a flat platform in the south branch.
- **W7 (Evidence, G&J slide 35):** Two square shafts 5.2 m apart. Capacity 8,000 m³, floor 19.5 m below the Haram.
- **W6 (Evidence, W&C p. 219):** T-shaped, 41 ft deep, "shape of a hollow truncated pyramid". The roof is partly rock and partly flat slabs.
- **W6 (Evidence, G&J slides 27, 30):** Cistern S1 is "W36, should be labelled W6". It has a "single round shaft entrance on north side". Schick said "the southern appendage once accommodated steps which led down into the pool".
- **W10 (Evidence, W&C p. 220):** West side of the Triple Gate passage, 30 ft deep. It is "reached through a hole in the wall of the passage", the roof manhole is closed, and it is dry. It "communicates with the rock-cut channels which run under the Sanctuary wall below the Triple Gate".
- **W10 (Evidence, G&J slide 37):** S6, 33 × 5 m, 9.1–12 m high.
- **W11 (Evidence, W&C p. 220):** 61½ ft deep, three tanks each about 26 × 40 ft joined by a 14 ft passage, about 700,000 gallons. "Steps on the west ascend to the mouth of the tank". The Triple Gate passage runs over it.
- **W11 (Evidence, G&J slide 36):** S5. "A long flight of steps descends to the cistern on its west side". It "closely registers with the ramped passage to the Triple Gate".
- **W5 (Evidence, W&C p. 218):** In the south-east corner of the platform. A long passage with a flight of steps at the east end, a manhole at the west end, floor 48 ft below the platform. Modern name *Bir er Rummaneh*.
- **W5 (Evidence, G&J slides 48, 53):** S28. "Four shaft entrances, two circular and two square, and an L-shaped staircase 1.8 m wide with doorways at either end".

### Relation to the platform's courts and colonnades

- **Evidence (W&C p. 219):** "Sir C. Warren places the altar of the Temple over the north-west end of No. 5 tank." On Conder's plan, "the manhole at the north-west extremity would have been just outside the 'Water Gate' of the Priest's Court."
- **Evidence (W&C p. 167):** The Double Gate tunnel "originally opened into the Sanctuary at 190 feet from the south wall". The same was found for the Triple Gate tunnel.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 218):** Warren suggested W3 "is the bath-house of the Temple mentioned in the Talmud".
- **Evidence (G&J via slides 15/56):** "The Golah Cistern was located under one of the six chambers on the south side of the Temple court" (Mishnah, Middot 5:4). In the reproduced pages G&J do not identify it with any surviving cistern.
- **Evidence (G&J via slides 53–54):**
  - Schick associated the two shaft openings of S28 (W5) "with a one-time well wheel, that served the Inner Court of the Jewish Temple".
  - Ritmeyer identifies S28 with the Golah Cistern. Kaufman takes it for the "Great Cistern" of Erubin 10:4.
  - Patrich's identification of S28 with the *Beth Horodath ha-Mayim* is "most improbable" according to G&J.
- **Evidence (negative):** Neither source relates W8 to any court or colonnade. W&C say only that W6 lies "near the Great Sea (No. 8), between the Aksa and the platform" (p. 219).
- **Inference (map-scaled):** W8/S3 lies about 80 m north of the south wall. That puts it north of a south-wall portico strip, in the open area between the Aksa and the inner platform. This is my placement, not a claim by either source.

## 3. Stairs with a shaft, pit or chamber at the head

- **Evidence (W&C p. 125):** At the north-east angle, beside the so-called Tower of Antonia, a light shaft "opens through the roof of the conduit upon the doorway of a staircase leading into the conduit through the solid wall from above".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 125):** The staircase entrance "is in Course N", about 12 ft from the east side of the Birket Israil dam. It is "very steep, at an angle of one in one, and appears to have been cut out of the solid after the wall was built". It was cleared for 28 ft, then abandoned as too dangerous, so its head was never reached.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 130):** The light shaft is "6 feet beyond the north-east angle". The staircase is in the wall or dam just north of the tower, not inside the tower itself.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 222):** W22 is a rock-cut domed reservoir that "has a rock-cut staircase running round the wall. There are two manholes in the roof, both now closed."
- **Evidence (G&J via slide 39):** S15 (W22) has three chambers. "An opening in the wall of the east chamber leads to a staircase along the internal wall."
- **Evidence (G&J via slide 34):** For W8, Whitty described "the mouth of a well, several feet deep, at the bottom of which commences a ruined flight of steps". This is a shaft at the head of a staircase. W&C (p. 220) call it a "narrow staircase" from the south.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 221):** For W16/17, "On the south wall of the tank is a staircase, which led down till recently from the Sanctuary."
- **Evidence (W&C p. 218; G&J slide 53):** W5's stair ascends southwards from its east end to the surface outside the platform. Schick: steps 1.4 m wide run in from the east for 5.25 m to a landing. A further flight then runs north, flanked by a rock-cut banister, for 12 m and a drop of 5.3 m. The "main entrance … lay hidden in fill close to the south-eastern corner of the inner platform". One shaft opening "was situated close to the entrance staircase".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 224–225):** W30 has "A modern flight of steps". W33 is "under the stairs leading to the passage from the Double Gate".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 162):** In the blocked east branch of the Great Passage under the Single Gate, "A shaft leading upwards was here found." There is no stair.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 232):** In the cave south of the Triple Gate, the shaft "is 4½ feet deep, and passes down into the roof of the chamber". There is no stair.
- **Evidence (negative):** No spiral staircase is recorded. The only "spiral" in W&C is in a Josephus quotation about column bases (p. 100). No staircase is recorded inside a tower on the Haram.

## 4. A chamber with a cistern opening in its floor

- **Evidence (negative):** Neither W&C nor the reproduced G&J pages record an underground chamber under the Haram with a cistern mouth in its floor.
- The nearest cases:
  - **Evidence (W&C p. 222–223):** W24 is "not properly speaking a tank at all, but a chamber". Its rock floor sinks below the platform, falls west at about 30°, and the room is used as a store. No opening is mentioned.
  - **Evidence (W&C p. 223–224):** W29 is "not a tank, but a masonry chamber" of vaults against the platform's north retaining wall. No cistern opening is mentioned.
  - **Evidence (W&C p. 219):** In W7, "two entrances on the east, 6 feet above the general level of the floor leading to a side chamber".
  - **Evidence (W&C p. 203):** Outside the Haram, in the causeway vaults west of Wilson's Arch, there is a lower vault (floor at 2,390) with "a shaft to the depth of 14 feet, and from it an aqueduct". The passages above have "draw-well openings in the roof" (p. 202).
  - **Evidence (W&C p. 200):** The "Ancient Hall" near Wilson's Arch "has been used as a cistern". It is outside the Haram.
- **Evidence (G&J via slide 15):** A textual chamber over a cistern: the Golah Cistern under a south-side chamber of the Temple court, with "a wheel (for drawing water)" set over it.

## 5. Dates given by the sources

- **Evidence (W&C p. 222):** W16/17 resembles 13th-century Moslem work. "the reservoir dates about the time when the Moslems repaired the Sanctuary after the expulsion of the Crusading Christians." The basis is ribbed arches with rag-work groins.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 224):** W29's vault "seems clearly to be Arab work not earlier than the thirteenth century, built on to an ancient scarp".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 246):** The vaulting over "cisterns I. and III." shares the Byzantine narrow-keystone peculiarity, so it is assigned to the sixth century. W1's voussoirs are described at p. 217.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 245):** Conder's list assigns "the vaulting … of several of the tanks", together with the Golden Gate, "to the time of Justinian's restorations in 532 A.D."
- **Evidence (W&C p. 141):** The Golden Gate is "possibly a reconstruction of comparatively late date, but it stands on the ancient foundations of a gateway".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 163):** Solomon's Stables are "in part ancient and in part a reconstruction, probably about the time of Justinian".
- **Evidence (W&C p. 193):** W20 "appears to be modern in construction", except for an old arch on its west side.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 231, 233):** South of the Triple Gate, the drain lamps and bottles are "supposed to date about the third century A.D.". The cave yielded a Byzantine candlestick base and early Christian pottery.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 166):** The Great Passage under the Single Gate was "for the exit of some liquid"; what liquid "cannot be determined". No date is given.
- **Evidence (W&C p. 157):** At the north-east angle, discussing dates "would be purely a matter of speculation".
- **Evidence (negative):** Neither source dates W8 (the Great Sea), W15 (Golden Gate) or W37. The only time phrase in the G&J material is Barclay's remark that W8's steps were re-cut "only a few centuries ago" (slide 33).
- **Inference:** Schick's "well wheel" reading of W5/S28 (slide 53) and Warren's altar placement (p. 219) are identifications that imply a Temple-period use. They are not dates.

## Answers

1. **Golden Gate.**
   - Inside the wall the only nearby cistern is **W15** (G&J S23, *Bir Bab al-Tawba*). It is rock-cut, nearly circular, about 18 ft (5.5 m) across and 35 ft deep, with one square shaft, and it is fed by a conduit from the gate's direction (W&C p. 221; slide 41).
   - By the G&J map it is about 25–30 m west of the gate (inference). W14 is about 40 m away and W28 about 65 m.
   - Outside the wall, an unexamined "tank or rock-cut tomb" lies about 124 ft (38 m) east of the gate (pp. 142, 144).
   - **No cistern is recorded within 7–10 m of the gate.**
   - No cistern is recorded under the Haram's east wall. W37 (S27) passes beneath the inner *platform's* east wall (p. 225; slide 42).
   - No sill, threshold or step above an entrance is recorded. W5's east entrance has only "remains of a door" and an ascending flight (p. 218).
2. **W8, the Great Sea.**
   - A rock-cut cavern with rock piers, 43 ft deep, with numerous manholes (three in use), a narrow staircase from the south, a conduit from the east, a small circular chamber to the north-east, and at least 2 million gallons (pp. 219–220).
   - G&J add: about 40 × 30 m, 44–50 rock-cut steps, eight shafts, and passages towards Solomon's Stables and the Triple Gate (slides 32–34).
   - No date is given. Neither source links W8 to a court or colonnade.
   - Court links exist only for W5: Warren's altar placement and Conder's Water Gate (p. 219), and Schick's well wheel for the Inner Court (slide 53).
3. **Stairs.**
   - The NE-angle staircase cut in the wall leads down into the Birket Israil overflow conduit. A light shaft opens at its doorway, and its head was not reached (p. 125).
   - W22 has a stair round the wall and two roof manholes (p. 222).
   - W8's stair starts at the bottom of a well-like shaft (slide 34).
   - W5's L-shaped stair has a nearby shaft (slide 53). W16/17 has a stair on its south wall (p. 221).
   - No spiral stair and no stair in a tower is recorded.
4. **Chambers.** None is recorded with a cistern opening in its floor. The closest are W24, W29, and the causeway vault with a 14 ft shaft outside the Haram (p. 203).
5. **Dates.**
   - W16/17: 13th-century Moslem (p. 222). W29: Arab, not earlier than the 13th century (p. 224). Both are dated by architectural style.
   - The vaulting of W1 and W3, and "several of the tanks": Justinian, 6th century (pp. 245–246). The basis is narrow-keystone voussoirs.
   - W20: modern (p. 193).
   - Neither source dates W8, W15 or W37.

## Gibson & Jacobson 1996 read first-hand — 22 October 2026

The book itself was read from the owner's NLI photos of 21 Oct ([record](../research/sources/nli_photos_2026-10-21/gibson_jacobson1996.md)). It confirms the concordance above from G&J p. 5.

- **EVIDENCE (pp. 5, 33–39, 54–55).** G&J **No. 3 = W8**, Bīr Buhāyr, "commonly" the "Great Sea" (al-Baḥr al-Kabīr). G&J **Cistern 8 = W9**, Bīr al-Waraqa, the Well of the Leaf, under the Aqṣā. These are two cisterns. Their Figs. 25, 87 and 88 show Cistern 8 (W9), not the Great Sea. In this file "Cistern No. 8" in the §2 heading is Wilson's number (W8); read every unprefixed "No. 8" in the repo as Wilson's unless it says G&J.
- **EVIDENCE (table p. 33; pp. 36, 39).** No. 3 is entirely rock-cut and plastered; rock level 734.9 m, 1.5 m below the surface (Warren); stair 44 (Barclay) or 50 (Schick) steps from the S/SE; Pierotti's notches by the small circular NW chamber. The source says north-west for that chamber; W&C p. 220 above says north-east. No date is given for the cutting; the Fig. 18 four-step stair beyond one shaft "may possibly have been a relic of the Herodian Temple complex" (p. 39).
- **EVIDENCE (Fig. 104, p. 230).** The authors' 2nd-c. BCE map marks Cisterns 29 and 15 only, not No. 3.
- **EVIDENCE (Fig. 10, p. 26; Fig. 86, p. 192).** The cistern drawn nearest the Golden Gate is No. 23 (W15); no cistern is drawn in, under or against the gate building. Conduit 23 runs from No. 23's mouth east to the gate outline.
- **INFERENCE (Fig. 10, ±5 m).** No. 23's centre is about 25–27 m west of the gate's west face, consistent with the 25–30 m above and Warren Plate VI's 30.0 m.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 2).** Theoderich (Crusader period) puts "an ancient and ruined cistern" between the Golden Gate and the fifteen steps of the platform; no distance. INFERENCE: this fits No. 23's position; it is a medieval tradition.
