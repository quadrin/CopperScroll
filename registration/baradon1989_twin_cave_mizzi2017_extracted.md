# Twin Cave (entry 25) and Mizzi on *KhQ* IIIA (entries 20–26)

**Labels:** **Evidence** = the source states it, or it is shown on its figure. **Inference** = drawn here. **BK** = none used.

## A. Twin Cave, Bar-Adon 1989

- **Evidence.** It is chapter **2**, not 3; chapter 3 is Qasr el-Yahud. The opening page is unnumbered ([15]); the next two are 16 and 17. The English summary (p. 5*) was not in the capture read.
- **Evidence (p. [15]).** Grid 1937/1293, on the eastern slope of the Qumran cliff, 1 km north of the settlement. A "thick rock pillar" divides the cave into a northern and a southern unit, "and each has an opening"; both openings face east, toward the Dead Sea. Fig. ב1 is a photograph from the south-east.
- **Evidence (p. [15]).** The southern unit has two storeys, its inner part higher and open to the sky, with a narrow, high niche. The northern unit is a closed, single-storey cave, 35 m long and 4 m wide on average.
- **Evidence.** **No threshold, step or sill** is recorded at either entrance. **No plan, section or north arrow** is printed in pp. [15]–17; the figures are the photograph and the pottery plate (ב2).
- **Evidence (p. [15]; editorial n. 1).** A trial dig in the northern cave in 1971; seasons in 1977 and 1982 (the latter without results). The diaries recorded areas and depths but not precise locus boundaries. A thick goat-dung layer (Roman sherds) lay over burnt layers, fallen stones and ash several metres thick, with sherds of Chalcolithic–Early Bronze, Hellenistic, Roman–Byzantine and Early Arab date. Use was "non-continuous, over a long period" (p. 17).
- **Evidence (pp. 16–17, table).** 14 vessels. Window-period parallels: juglet no. 6 (1st c. BCE–1st c. CE), base no. 7 (1st c. CE), jar no. 11 (1st–2nd c. CE), jar no. 12 (1st c. BCE parallels). The glass bowl is tied to the Bar Kokhba war. Find-spots are given only as "Cave A" or "Cave B", with sub-areas ("the pillar", Area A/B, upper niche, side tunnel); the text never says which is north. No coins.

## B. Mizzi 2017

- **Evidence (pp. 193–195).** IIIA covers the stepped pools to the south-east (L68–71, L75), the large cistern and stepped pools to the south-west (L83, L85, L91), and water features to the north-west (L130–138). Humbert reads L34 as a Hellenistic bath; Mizzi calls this debatable. The aqueduct, dam and channels are not discussed, and no water-system phasing is reported.
- **Evidence (pp. 194–196).** IIIA's model: a Hasmonaean villa, remodelled in the late 1st c. BCE as a centre for Passover and first fruits; the 31 BCE earthquake played down (IIIA pp. 25–33); the L89 pottery store's destruction moved to after the mid-1st c. CE (IIIA pp. 327–342). Mizzi rejects the L89 redating: the Agrippa I coin came from L87's upper level (IIIA p. 342), and the pottery points to the last third of the 1st c. BCE. He says dating evidence from foundation trenches and fills remains "highly elusive".
