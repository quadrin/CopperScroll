### Cross-checks and unresolved inconsistencies

**[Evidence]** Entry 3 / Fh 12 is described as plated silver, internally decomposed and illegible, with two silver coins adhering together (p. 217, note 5 / r. 218). The summary does not give an individual mint year. Entry 13 lacks both a locus number and inventory number; its note refers to Spijkerman’s manuscript and Reifenberg (p. 219, note 11 / r. 220).

**[Evidence]** Summary *63 = Fh 105, locus 11, and *64 = Fh 201, locus 19 (p. 222 / r. 223). The extant list reverses these inventories: *63 = Fh 201 and *64 = Fh 105, and attributes them to Coponius or Ambibulus with dates “5–8 or 8–11 CE” (p. 234 / r. 235). The extant list gives no loci with which to resolve that reversal.

**[Evidence]** Summary *66’s card note says “18/19, restruck Δ 17/18” (p. 222, note 24 / r. 223); its extant description gives “17/18 CE” (p. 234 / r. 235). Summary no. 69 / Fh 166 is Valerius Gratus in locus 16, near the large stone vessels, whereas *99 / Fh 166 is an unidentified procurator in locus 1, Trench East (pp. 222, 224 / rr. 223, 225). The inventory duplication is not resolved in these passages.

**[Evidence]** The Pontius Pilate group is dated “29/30 CE” but the printed summary describes its inscription as “year 6”; that wording is preserved as a source discrepancy rather than corrected. The later group is “30/31 CE”, while no. 88’s card says LIH (p. 223, note 34 / r. 224).

**[Evidence]** Summary *100 / Fh 5 is under a heading calling the coins half-shekels and giving “year 2 = 67–68” (p. 225 / r. 226). The extant description calls the same Fh 5 coin a prutah dated “67 CE” (p. 235 / r. 236).

**[Evidence]** Summary *101 = Fh 137, locus 16, “upper level”, dated “132–133 CE”; *102 = Fh 140, locus 16, “West”, also “132–133 CE” (p. 226 / r. 227). The extant list reverses those numbers: *101 = Fh 140, “132–133 CE”, and *102 = Fh 137, “133–134 CE”. Fh 141 / *103 is also “133–134 CE” there, whereas the summary allows “133/134 or 134/135 CE” (p. 235 / r. 236).

**[Evidence]** Fh 67 / *106 is dated broadly to the late Roman period in the summary (p. 228 / r. 229). The extant entry offers two alternatives: Valentinian II, Theodosius, Arcadius, “ca. 378–388 CE”; or Valentinian III, “425–435 CE”, with uncertain mint (p. 236 / r. 237). It is not a uniquely identified coin date.

**[Evidence]** The hoard has its **own catalogue sequence 1–18**, separate from the single finds. Every row has locus **160** and inventories **Fh 143–Fh 160** (pp. 230–231 / rr. 231–232). The scanned register in Appendix 3 also reads “Loc. 160”, and the handwritten introduction says 18 bronze coins were found “13.II.1958” in locus 160, register numbers 143–160 (pp. 265–266 / rr. 266–267). This is not silently changed to locus 16.

**[Evidence]** The note is on card “143 Feshkha 1497”. The original card describes a sack: “enfermées dans un sac dont l’empreinte est visible sur plusieurs monnaies” (p. 230 / r. 231). No floor, fill, depth, sealed layer or precise position within locus 160 is stated in the examined hoard catalogue/register.

## (c) Break around 31 BCE, earthquake, and occupation after 68 CE

**[Evidence]** The conclusions date the beginning of coin arrival/circulation to “ca. 130 BCE”, perhaps earlier, and its end to “around 70–73 CE” (p. 128 / r. 129).

**[Evidence]** Conclusion 4 says long coin use prevents money alone from confirming or refuting de Vaux’s three-period division. Conclusion 5 describes continuous small-coin circulation/supply and attributes fluctuations to changes in minted stock rather than necessarily to interrupted habitation following earthquakes, fires or political events (p. 129 / r. 130).

**[Evidence]** The specific “31 BCE” earthquake appears in Callegher’s account of Mizzi and Magness’s reconsideration: they find continuity from Period Ib to Period II, rather than the proposed abandonment after the earthquake and a later fire (p. 83 / r. 84). This is a report of their argument in Callegher’s discussion, not an independently dated earthquake horizon established by the coins.

**[Inference]** The monetary evidence offers no secure 31-BCE break and cannot alone date an earthquake destruction/rebuilding episode. A 31-BCE earthquake is not disproved merely because coin supply/circulation continued.

**[Evidence]** Coins minted during the revolt could have arrived after 68; Callegher calls continued inhabitants through the interval ending with Masada’s fall in 73 plausible and argues against assuming total abandonment immediately after 68/69. He describes the monetary evidence after 73 as meagre (p. 101 / r. 102).

**[Evidence]** The Bar Kokhba-period concealment in Qumran locus 29 is not treated as evidence of an organized, lasting settlement; it belongs to the wider regional pattern of revolt hoarding (pp. 104–105 / rr. 105–106).

**[Inference]** The book permits continued occupation/use after 68, with circulation extending roughly to 70–73; it does not turn that into proof of uninterrupted, substantial occupation throughout the later Roman period. Feshkha’s later coins and its Flavian hoard are separate contexts and are not automatically dated phases of Qumran.
