# Mount Gerizim Excavations III: The Coins (Magen, Bijovsky and Tzionit 2021): extract for 3Q15 research

Source: Y. Magen, G. Bijovsky and Y. Tzionit, *Mount Gerizim Excavations III: The Coins* (JSP 19, 2021). Local file `gerizim_coins.pdf` (284 PDF pages).

Method: the PDF has a text layer throughout, and all pages were extracted with `pdftotext -layout`. The general site plan (PDF 21) was rendered and viewed. Page offset: **printed page = PDF page − 12**. This holds across the volume, e.g. printed [159] = PDF 171 and printed [59] = PDF 71. Magen's historical chapter, printed pp. 1–78, is PDF 13–90. Bijovsky's numismatic section starts at printed 79 (PDF 91). Her catalogue runs printed 130–204 and the plates from printed 205.

Labels: **Evidence** means the volume states it (printed page / PDF page are given). **Inference** is my own reasoning.

## 0. How the catalogue works (this limits what can be answered)

- **Evidence** (printed 82 / PDF 94): the catalogue is typological. Each entry gives "Contextual data of the coin: area, locus and basket" and then the "Total number of isolated coins of the same type or variant registered in the database."
- **Evidence** (printed 82 / PDF 94): coin-by-coin data (K numbers) "is digitally appended to this book". The digital files hold only identification categories: material, period, ruler, date, mint and denomination. The volume points to the IAA library site for them.
- **Inference**: only the one representative coin of each type has its area, locus and basket printed. Where a type has more than one coin, the volume does not print the find-spots of the others. The online appendix is not part of this PDF, and its fields as described do not include locus.
- **Evidence** (searched across all 284 pages): the volume contains no locus descriptions. No locus register, stratigraphic report or locus-by-locus context appears. The architecture is deferred to later volumes. The Preface (printed IX / PDF 9) names vol. IV (private architecture) and vol. VI (sacred precinct and church).

## 1. Locus P5178 (Area P)

- **Evidence** (printed 159 / PDF 171): Cat. No. 413 is a bronze of Festus under Nero (58–62 CE), minted in Jerusalem and dated 58/59 CE. It measures 13.80 mm, axis 4, 1.66 g. Obverse: within wreath, NEPWNOC. Reverse: LE KAICAPOC, palm branch. Reference CHL: 271, No. 66. **Area P, Locus 5178, Basket 51777, Total by type 3, K. 35264.** The heading reads "Under Nero, Festus (58–62 CE) / 3 coins".
- **Evidence**: no other catalogue entry cites L5178. A full-text search for "5178" finds only PDF 171.
- **Evidence** (printed 146 / PDF 158): the adjacent locus **P5177** appears once. It is Cat. No. 221, a Seleucid bronze of Tyre with [ΒΑΣΙΛΕΩΣ ΑΝΤΙΟΧΟΥ], listed under Antiochus III (223–187 BCE). Basket 51965, total by type 7, K. 36166.
- **Evidence** (printed 106 / PDF 118): the Festus coins are named only as a type: "three of Festus dated to 58/59 CE, during the reign of Nero (54‒68 CE; Cat. No. 413). These coins were minted in Jerusalem."
- **Evidence** (printed 59 / PDF 71, Magen): "Three coins were found, dated to 58/59 CE, of Festus, Procurator of Judea in 58–62 CE". No locus is given.
- **Context of L5178. Evidence (negative)**: the volume never describes L5178. It does not say whether the locus is a building, room, installation, fill, surface, topsoil or dump, and it gives no phase or date for it. The catalogue does use the explicit labels "Surface" and "Dump" in its locus column for some coins (e.g. Cat. 412 "Surface"; Cat. 976 "K, Dump"). Cat. 413 instead has a numbered locus.
- **Evidence** (plan, printed [6] / PDF 21): Area P has two named structures just south of the sacred precinct. No. 18 is the "Public building (Building P-II)" and No. 19 is the "Mansion (Complex P-I)". The plan carries no locus numbers.
- **Evidence** (printed 30 / PDF 42): "Building P-I was built at the end of the second century BCE". It yielded 75 Ptolemaic coins.
- **Evidence** (printed 114 / PDF 126): the Seleucid silver hoard was "apparently hidden in the northwestern wall of structure P-II, which collapsed (Area P, L4749, B47518". This is the only Area P locus in the volume that is tied to a building.
- **Inference**: L5178 cannot be assigned to P-I or P-II from this volume. That L5177 (a Seleucid coin) sits next to it suggests, but does not establish, that the excavators were working in Hellenistic contexts there. The volume does not say whether the Festus coin lay in a destruction-period or a later context. Answering that needs the Area P locus register or field records (vol. IV or VI, or the IAA archive).
- **Inference**: we cannot tell whether the other two Festus coins also came from L5178. "Total by type 3" counts the coins of the type across the whole site, not within the locus.

## 2. Coins of c. 50 BCE–70 CE as a group

### 2a. What the catalogue contains

- **Evidence** (printed 157–158 / PDF 169–170): the Hasmonean section lists John Hyrcanus I (68), Aristobulus I (3), Alexander Jannaeus (528) and "Uncertain Hasmonean" (77, 129–76 BCE). **No coins of Hyrcanus II or Mattathias Antigonus are listed.** Hasmonean total: 682 coins, of which 6 are unidentifiable (printed 103 / PDF 115).
- **Evidence**: **No Herodian coins and no First Revolt coins are catalogued.** A full-text search for Herod, Archelaus, Agrippa, Antipas and Revolt finds only historical and bibliographic mentions. No Pilate, Coponius or Gratus coins are listed either.
- **Evidence** (printed 159 / PDF 171), Roman coins of 7 BCE–68 CE with their representative find-spots:

| Cat. | Issuer | Mint, date | Area | Locus | Basket | Total by type | K. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 411 | Augustus, as | Rome, 7 BCE | S | 7027 | 10182 | 1 | 28309 |
| 412 | Ambibulus under Augustus | Jerusalem, 9/10 CE | S | Surface | – | 1 | 33434 |
| 413 | Festus under Nero | Jerusalem, 58/59 CE | P | 5178 | 51777 | 3 | 35264 |
| 414 | Ascalon, autonomous | "Late 1st c." | S | 443 | 4886 | 1 | 18975 |
| 415 | Nero | Caesarea, 68 CE | S | 461 | 6026 | 1 | 22280 |

- **Evidence** (printed 59 / PDF 71, Magen): the post-destruction group is counted as Augustus, Ambibulus, 3 Festus, Nero and the Ascalon coin ("dated to the end of the first century CE"): "The seven coins mentioned above".
- **Inference**: the 50 BCE–70 CE count is **6 coins**: Augustus 1, Ambibulus 1, Festus 3, Nero 1. The count is **7** if the Ascalon coin (late 1st c., possibly after 70 CE) is included. The categories break down as follows:
  - late Hasmonean: 0;
  - Herodian: 0;
  - prefects and procurators: 4;
  - First Revolt: 0;
  - plus 1 Roman imperial and 1 Roman provincial.
- **Inference** (possible complication): Cat. 409, the 490 crude Jannaeus "anchor/star" imitations (80/79–76 BCE "onwards", representative coin at S835, B8222), may have been struck or used after 76 BCE. Bijovsky (below) says many may postdate Jannaeus. The volume, however, does not date any of them into the post-50 BCE window. They are not counted here.
- **Evidence and Inference on distribution**: the five printed find-spots are Area S, the sacred precinct (4 coins: L7027, Surface, L443, L461), and Area P (1 coin: L5178). The other two Festus coins have no printed find-spot.

### 2b. Interpretation in the volume

- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 59 / PDF 71): "The seven coins mentioned above are not related to historic events occurring at the site, but came there by chance."
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 59 / PDF 71): "No coins have been found from the two Samaritan attempts to ascend and take control of Mt. Gerizim, in the days of Pontius Pilate". The sentence continues "…and Vespasian, in 67 CE."
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 59 / PDF 71): "After being destroyed, the site was abandoned for around 400 years".
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 59 / PDF 71): "Two post-destruction coins were found." (Augustus and Ambibulus. He then adds Festus, Nero and Ascalon, which makes seven.)
- **Evidence** (Bijovsky, printed 106 / PDF 118): "after the destruction of Mt. Gerizim by John Hyrcanus I it seems that the site was not resettled until the end of the third century CE."
- **Evidence** (Bijovsky, printed 129 / PDF 141): "A very small number of Roman provincial coins, however, were discovered during the excavations and are most likely random finds."
- **Evidence** (Bijovsky, printed 129 / PDF 141): "the site was destroyed and abandoned not before 110/109 BCE."
- **Evidence** (Bijovsky, printed 129 / PDF 141): "The numismatic evidence indicates that the site was only resettled toward the last quarter of the third century CE".
- **Evidence on the Hasmonean garrison, which applies only to the Jannaeus-era coins** (Bijovsky, printed 105 / PDF 117): "There is no doubt that most coins reached the site after it was destroyed by fire by John Hyrcanus I."
- **Evidence** (printed 105 / PDF 117): "the presence of the garrison explains the large quantities of Jannaeus coins at the site". This reports Magen's view.
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 39 / PDF 51): "Until the time of Alexander Jannaeus, and possibly even later, a Hasmonean garrison remained in the city to prevent the Samaritans from returning".
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 45 / PDF 57): "They also built a fortress in the northern part of the mountain, close to the main ascent from Shechem". This was where a Roman temple was later built.
- **Evidence** (printed 45 / PDF 57): "this fortress was built during the Hasmonean period and continued in use in the Roman period."
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 46 / PDF 58): "The excavations at Mt. Gerizim reveal that the Hellenistic city was not restored or rebuilt after its destruction."
- **Evidence** (Magen, printed 47–48 / PDF 59–60): Mt. Gerizim "was deserted and in ruin during the entire period", i.e. up to Herod's death. Pilate "preemptively seized the ascent" (printed 48). In 67 CE "This was no innocent mass pilgrimage to Mt. Gerizim, but rather a revolt" (printed 48).
- **Evidence** (Bijovsky, printed 106 / PDF 118): Augustus coins struck in Rome "are very rarely found in our region".
- **Evidence (internal inconsistency)**: printed 106 counts 13 Roman provincial coins, but printed 129 says "three of eleven Roman provincial coins were minted at Neapolis".
- **Inference**: the volume's interpretation is **stray or chance losses on an abandoned site**. It is explicitly *not* a garrison, renewed settlement or event horizon. Neither author mentions pilgrims for this group. Magen's pilgrim explanation applies to the Seleucid-period coins (printed 39 / PDF 51). The garrison explanation is confined to the Jannaeus coins. The one Roman-period military presence the volume proposes is the Hasmonean fortress on the *northern* slope, "continued in use in the Roman period". The volume does not tie any 1st-c. CE coin to it, and the printed find-spots are on the main summit (Areas S and P).

## 3. Stairs, cisterns, pits

- **Evidence (negative)**: no catalogued coin of 50 BCE–70 CE is reported from a staircase, cistern or pit. The printed contexts are L7027, Surface, L5178, L443 and L461, and none is described. Search terms: stair, cistern, pit, fill, topsoil, dump, surface.
- **Evidence**: the only cistern and fill references in the volume concern other periods or sites. Persian-period finds at Samaria and other sites "came from fill strata and cisterns" (printed 23 / PDF 35). L4201 is "a fill west of the church" with Byzantine coins (printed 110 / PDF 122). The monumental staircase is described architecturally (plans, printed 6–14) without coins of this period.
- **Inference**: the volume cannot answer whether L5178, L7027, L443 or L461 are stairs, cisterns or pits. This needs the field locus register.

## Answers

1. **L5178 (Area P).** The catalogue prints only one coin from this locus: **Cat. 413**, Festus under Nero, Jerusalem, 58/59 CE, basket 51777, K. 35264 (printed 159 / PDF 171). The type totals 3 coins site-wide, and the other two have no printed find-spot. The volume says **nothing** about the context of L5178: no building, room, fill, surface, phase or date. Area P contains Complex P-I (mansion, built at the end of the 2nd c. BCE) and Building P-II (public building, with the silver hoard in L4749). Neither is linked to L5178. The neighbouring L5177 produced a Seleucid coin of Antiochus III (Cat. 221).
2. **The 50 BCE–70 CE group.** It has 6 coins (7 with the late-1st-c. Ascalon coin):
   - Augustus, 7 BCE (S7027);
   - Ambibulus, 9/10 CE (S, Surface);
   - Festus ×3, 58/59 CE (one at P5178);
   - Nero, Caesarea, 68 CE (S461);
   - Ascalon (S443).

   There are **no** late Hasmonean (Hyrcanus II or Antigonus), Herodian or First Revolt coins. Both authors read these coins as chance losses on an abandoned site. Magen: they "came there by chance" and "are not related to historic events" (printed 59). Bijovsky: they are "most likely random finds", and the site was "not resettled until the end of the third century CE" (printed 106, 129). The garrison explanation is applied only to the Jannaeus coins. Magen's one Roman-period presence is a Hasmonean fortress on the northern slope "continued in use in the Roman period" (printed 45). No coins mark the Samaritan attempts under Pilate (dated "26–32 CE" on printed 59) or in 67 CE.
3. **Stairs, cisterns, pits.** None is reported. No coin of 50 BCE–70 CE is assigned to a staircase, cistern or pit, and none of the five relevant loci is described.
