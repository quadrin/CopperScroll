# Entry 25: Twin Cave and Cave IV/11

Research check: 1 October 2026. This follows the [entrance review](entry25_cave_access_review_2026-10-01.md). Entry numbering follows the project's Puech concordance.

## Result

Twin Cave near Qumran supplies a published, feature-specific comparison for entry 25. Keep it separate from Cave IV/11 on the Abu Saraj cliff, and from manuscript cave 3Q. Its historical identification with the scroll still requires a measured entrance plan, excavation contexts and the period surface at the northern opening.

The ancient description remains the project's edition-based reading: a pillar cave, two openings, an eastward aspect, and digging three cubits at the northern opening. Puech and Lefkovits entry pages were not newly inspected. Milik's 1960 printed p. 140 was visually rechecked from the existing local ADAJ scan: his item 27 is project entry 25. His translation attaches the eastward aspect to the cave and gives three cubits at the northern entrance, a jar containing a book, and 42 talents beneath it. It supplies no claim that this book is a duplicate Copper Scroll. This is a recheck of an already accessed publication, not a new manuscript reading. The Hebrew qualifier's attachment remains an interpretive question.

Taylor describes two adjacent caves near 11Q, with intervening rock resembling a pillar. She associates Bar-Adon's name Twin Cave with the old grid string `19371293` and cites *ʿAtiqot* Hebrew Series 9 (1989), pp. 15–17. Her account includes several occupation periods and excavations in 1971, 1977 and 1982. Fig. 1.4 places 11Q to the north. Retain the grid as printed; its CRS, precision and relationship to either opening have not been verified.

Eshel p. 22 locates Bar-Adon's proposed pillar cave south of 11Q, opposite Kalia, and supplies the same pp. 15–17 reference, dated תש״ן. JSTOR's volume contents display תש״ן / 1989 together; use 1989 while retaining the Hebrew year. Eshel discusses the oil juglet north of 3Q and the arrows south of Qumran as separate findspots. These finds cannot establish a deposit inside Twin Cave. His quantity and duplicate-scroll interpretation differ from the project's entry record and require edition review.

The 2022 parks text labels the conspicuous pair as cave 3, then calls it Twin Cave; it later again equates 3Q and Twin Cave. Taylor and Eshel place the historical proposal south of 11Q. Treat the guide as a source warning. Its repeated association with the Copper Scroll adds no independent archaeological confirmation.

PADIS identifies IV/11 with the modern pillar-cave name and Khirbet Bureikut, citing Sion 2002 p. 52 and Greenberg–Keinan 2009 site 292, p. 65/map 2. This provides a retrieval cross-reference for the Abu Saraj candidate. It supplies no inspected orientation plan.

## Spatial test and next source

Maintain a separate Twin Cave lead in the research record. A rock partition between adjacent caves may explain the pillar wording, but the interior relationship and ancient entrances remain untested. Obtain Bar-Adon pp. 15–17, including all drawings and captions. Determine whether both openings belong to one connected cavity, their bearings, and whether the northern threshold has a documented early surface.

For IV/11 obtain Feig 2002 Hebrew pp. 85–90 and Sion p. 52. The publisher PDFs previously returned 403. Neither access history nor the modern cave name resolves the entrance test.

No map point or confidence score is changed by this check.
