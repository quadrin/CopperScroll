# Qumran waterworks: supplied original drawings and phase evidence

1 October 2026, R01 follow-up. Original pages inspected directly from three user-supplied PDFs. This review updates source access, feature descriptions and conditional geometric comparisons; it adds no field measurement or geographic registration.

## Result

Schulz 1960 Fig. 2, printed p. 53, is a compass-oriented route sketch with approximate segment lengths. His pp. 54–57 supply a first-hand account of the northern side branch, a long tunnel and two different openings. Opening B intersects a natural fissure crossed by built channel flooring; opening A is interpreted as artificial. These distinctions sharpen the entry 22 comparison, while its named reservoir and architectural date remain unresolved.

Strobel 1972 Fig. 1, p. 56, is the settlement's distribution plan, derived from de Vaux 1956. It cannot supply a measured upper-gorge frame. His separate gorge discussion, pp. 65–68, provides observations and a hydraulic alternative: a channel about 5–7 m above the contemporary natural basin floor led him to question impoundment to channel height and propose water lifting. That is his interpretation, without an excavated lifting installation.

Taylor–Gibson 2020 pp. 215–216, note 58, gives a detailed participant account of the November 2009 visit. It records conflicting dam, bypass and spillway interpretations and Peleg's statement that he had prepared a new aqueduct map. This establishes a specific field-record lead; no map, licence or archive identifier was recovered here.

The three supplied sources close the stated reading-access gaps. R01 remains open: none establishes the complete reading, named-feature relationships and period construction sequence required for entries 20–23.

## Schulz: route, openings and physical limits

The caption on p. 53 confirms article-level **Fig. 2**. Its north/east/south/west cross and approximate 100 m / 100 m / 500 m segments give route orientation and a reported length framework. The sketch has no metric scale bar, geographic grid, survey stations, contours, feature elevations or explicit observed/reconstructed-line legend. It depicts the route into a northern tributary and toward the settlement; it does not label Reeder's boulder, pothole and wall. No uniform pixel-to-metre transformation is justified.

Page 54 reports about 700 m overall and explicitly locates the channel's beginning in a smaller northern side branch of the wadi. The discussion traces upstream from the settlement, rather than numbering modern Ilan–Amit points. Page 53 distinguishes an initial excursion on 9 September 1959 from his more detailed aqueduct investigation on 16 September. Page 57 says he encountered Masterman's report after his investigation. The earlier report contributes its own field account, while repeated details in later publications require dependence checks.

## Strobel: figure dependence and hydraulic alternative

Fig. 1 on p. 56 shows settlement loci, channels and basins, with a north arrow and 0–20 m scale. Its caption explicitly derives the plan from **de Vaux, Revue Biblique 1956**. It excludes the upper gorge from its frame. This closes the narrow coverage test: Fig. 1 cannot provide the proposed common measured frame for intake, wall/boulder and eastern fissure. Its scale supplies settlement-relative geometry, without an upstream registration control or feature chronology.

Pages 65–66 explicitly use Schulz for route particulars and add observations. They cannot count as an independent repeat measurement of every listed Schulz dimension. Page 65 prints about **70 m** as Schulz's total, while Schulz p. 54 prints about **700 m**; Strobel then discusses a roughly 400 m settlement-to-cliff stretch in note 15. Preserve the printed inconsistency; a dropped zero is a plausible editorial-error inference.

The separate upper-gorge text and plates matter:

- p. 66 describes the channel about 5–7 m above the contemporary floor of a large natural basin, an approximately 3 m short tunnel on its southern side and a northeastern collecting channel.
- pp. 66–67 describe a natural triangular basin with approximate sides 45 / 38 / 32 m and an outlet roughly 2.5–3 m wide / 2 m deep. These are the author's reported contemporary features, not restored ancient floor levels.
- p. 67 treats raising floodwater to channel height by artificial construction as technically untenable in his model. An unobserved lower channel also remained archaeologically unsupported. He proposes a lifting mechanism; p. 68 develops its seasonal-supply interpretation. No excavated lifting apparatus is documented in this coverage.
- Plates 7–10 give route, channel, tunnel, fissure and basin views, without survey control. Plate 7B's dotted aqueduct line is an annotation on a photograph, not a measured plan.
- The short tunnel/fissure labels in the inspected Plate 9 captions and the p. 66 A/B references require care: caption A identifies the fissure/basin view and B the tunnel above it. Retain the actual captions instead of silently assigning the prose's references to the opposite image.

**Project inference:** the apparent lower-basin/upper-channel head difference is a discriminating hydraulic requirement. Survey ancient channel-bed levels, restored basin floor and any dam/spillway crest together before selecting gravity diversion, local-gully collection or lifting. Present-day floor heights and speculative ancient ponding are different inputs.

## Taylor–Gibson: the 2009 disagreement and map lead

Note 58 spans printed pp. 215–216. Gibson's account names the November 2009 participants and lists the inspected waterfall base, proposed dam axis/ponding area, rock-cut channel, tunnel/bypass and southern spillways.

| Reported position | Evidence and interpretation must stay separate |
| --- | --- |
| Peleg | Considered the dam absent from the original system, supply drawn from gullies on the southern side of the wadi, the loop an unsuccessful precursor superseded by the tunnel, and the spillways natural. These are reported opinions. He said he had prepared a new map and planned to update it after the visit. |
| Amit | Disagreed with the failed-loop interpretation, pointing to adhering plaster near the tunnel junction as evidence of function. Identified at least one worked southern spillway, linked to no. 5 on his published map, and recalled earlier tooling/tunnel preservation. |
| Authors' dam model | Proposes ponding of at least 2 m and flood destruction of a wall; reports no visible construction/wall in that context. This supplies no excavated dam or demonstrated identity with Reeder's surviving upstream wall. |
| Chronology | Original aqueduct date remains uncertain. Hasmonean Period Ib and post-31 BCE proposals are cited. The authors report Period II use; that statement does not date every construction/repair segment. |

The 2011 manuscript note 85 and 2020 note 58 concern the same 2009 visit, not two independent field campaigns. The later note adds participant disagreement and the prepared-map statement. The map's surviving location, version, datum, coverage and existence today remain unverified. Peleg's reported southern-side supply also needs to be checked against the northern-branch descriptions; do not harmonize the directional wording by assumption.

Printed pp. 196–201 separately argue for earlier cistern-house use and later rebuilding. The roughly 1 m raising of L.110 is attributed to Magen–Peleg, while Iron Age attribution/reuse remains the authors' interpretation against competing readings. Figs. 15–16 are photographs, not stratigraphic sections. Replastering and modern restoration complicate phase inference. This settlement discussion cannot date the upstream tunnel or wall by transfer.
