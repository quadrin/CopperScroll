## Confirmed description and figure identity

P. [15] prints the cave locator `1937/1293` and places Twin Cave on the eastern Qumran cliff slope about 1 km north of the ancient settlement. A thick rock pillar divides northern and southern units, each with an opening. Both openings face east toward the Dead Sea. The northern cavity is enclosed, single-level, 35 m long and 4 m in average width. The southern cavity has two levels, an interior open to the sky and a narrow high recess.

These are cavity descriptions, not measured entrance widths, heights, spacing or azimuths. Internal communication is not explicitly described. Pillar dimensions and a worked/unworked assessment are not provided. Natural-rock character remains an interpretation, not an explicit observation about tooling.

Fig. B1 is an exterior photograph; its caption places the cave at the right and gives a view from the southeast. It has no scale, north arrow or labelled mouths. Fig. B2 consists of ceramic/glass drawings, not a cave plan. Neither figure supplies measured mouth geometry or a northern-threshold section.

The p. [15] locator agrees in nominal form with the independently inspected excavation catalogue's Twin Cave site-centre reference `193700/129300`. Retain the original grid string and the catalogue's Old Israel Grid convention as separate provenance. No individual entrance coordinates, registered footprint or WGS84 point follow from this agreement.

## Direct checks of deposits, dates and recording limits

P. [15] reports work in 1971, 1977 and 1982; the last season yielded no results. Sheep/goat dung and the trial-cut Roman sherds are described, followed by front/interior/rear excavation areas, burning deposits, fallen stones, ash and a several-metre occupation deposit. Several ceramic periods are represented. Prolonged pen use and discontinuous long-term use are the excavator's interpretations. A specific disturbance mechanism or a fully mixed deposit is not demonstrated in these pages.

Editorial note 1 explicitly preserves the distinction between recorded excavation areas/depths and unrecorded precise locus boundaries. The source does not say that all depth records or diaries were missing.

Pp. 16–17 confirm the previously extracted typological comparisons: B2:6 spans first-century BCE/CE parallels, B2:7 has a first-century CE Jericho parallel and B2:12 first-century BCE parallels. B2:9 has a Qumran parallel. Later material includes B2:4, B2:5 and B2:11; B2:14's Bar Kokhba-era comparison remains a vessel-type comparison, not a sealed threshold date. These references support period-compatible use, without dating an entrance, pillar or floor phase.

The catalogue assigns B2:4 and B2:8 to Cave A's “pillar” locus, and B2:14 to an eastern trench. It does not establish Cave A/B as northern/southern units. The named pillar locus is not a construction date. No northern-threshold elevation, dated threshold surface, section or depth below that surface is supplied.
