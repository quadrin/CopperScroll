# ESI 6 cave survey: Choziba and Twin Cave checks

1 October 2026. R03 / Q39 and R02. Original project analysis of a user-supplied primary report.

## Result

The original survey locates the reported late first-/early second-century CE pottery in a natural cave east of St. George's monastery. It supplies no stratigraphic connection to the opposite-monastery wall or its lower culvert. The Roman pottery therefore cannot establish the target wall's construction date.

The report treats 3Q and Twin Cave separately. Fig. 29 is explicitly a plan and sections of 3Q. The Twin Cave excavation paragraph reports no dating finds from that season and supplies no measured entrance plan. Entry 25's geometry and period surface remain untested.

## R03: the Roman context and wall date

**Published observation, p. 66:** natural caves east of the monastery show disturbance. A storage-jar rim from one cave is assigned to the late first or early second century CE. This report gives no numbered findspot, sealed deposit or target-wall association for that rim. The introductory reference to earlier Roman finds in the area also supplies no wall-specific context here.

The same page describes another group of monastic cells, mainly on the north bank, and a path crossing the wadi on surviving bridge piers. Fig. 26, p. 67, maps cells and associated caves, burials, paths, water cisterns and other remains with a 0–500 m scale bar. It provides regional context without identifying the SWP opposite-monastery wall, its lower outlet or a measured masonry section. Correlating the reported bridge with Jisr ed-Deir requires an explicit surveyed correspondence.

**Bounded check 1:** does the cited Roman pottery context establish the proposed wall's period? **Result: insufficient evidence.** The find dates pottery from a cave; the required connection to the wall is absent. This report cannot supply that phase constraint. The Wadi Qelt candidate remains open under the project's alternative outlet/head and built-wall/rock readings. No depth model can be placed on an ancient surface from this evidence.

**Next discriminating evidence:** Patrich 1990 p. 208 n. 24; an identifiable PEF aqueduct plan/tracing; a wall-specific section linking the lower mouth, surface and construction joints.

## R02: 3Q plan identity and Twin Cave coverage

**Published observations, pp. 68–70:** the April 1986 work at Qumran includes 3Q, the Copper Scroll find cave. The report identifies it with cave 8 of the 1952 survey. Fig. 29's internal titles name 3Q; its plan and sections include a 0–5 m scale and marked scroll find position. These illustrations cannot supply Twin Cave's openings or bearing.

The 3Q excavation recovered ash over windblown sand and a few Chalcolithic sherds below fallen roof material. The report concludes that the collapse preceded Second Temple-period placement of documents in shallow recesses. Its further geniza interpretation is the excavator's explanation of deposition. It does not establish the Copper Scroll's composition date or a location described in its inventory. The stated 0.30–0.40 m find depth belongs to 3Q and cannot replace entry 25's three-cubit instruction or Twin Cave's period surface.

The separate Twin Cave paragraph describes that expedition's fourth season, earlier Bar-Adon supervision and the identification theory motivating the excavation. It reports no sherds or other indicative material in this season's work. This is a limited negative result for the 1986 excavation; it cannot erase finds from other seasons or exclude ancient use of the cave.

**Bounded check 2:** does this report provide a measured, dated Twin Cave entrance arrangement for entry 25? **Result: insufficient coverage.** The measured figure belongs to another cave; the Twin Cave account supplies neither opening bearings nor an early threshold surface. The identification theory is explicitly a motivation for excavation, rather than a demonstrated result.

**Next discriminating evidence:** Bar-Adon, *ʿAtiqot* Hebrew Series 9 (1989), pp. 15–17, including drawings and find contexts. Test connected versus adjacent cavities, the pillar relationship, the cave-level eastward aspect and the northern opening independently.
