## Published description, as recorded in the extraction

### Two openings, units and pillar — p. [15]

The cave lies on the eastern slope of the Qumran cliff, about 1 km north of the ancient settlement. A thick rock pillar divides northern and southern units, each with an opening. The text describes both openings as facing east toward the Dead Sea.

The northern unit is enclosed and single-level, 35 m long and 4 m in average width. The southern unit has two levels; its higher interior opens to the sky and includes a narrow, high recess. These measurements describe the northern cavity. They are not entrance dimensions. Neither entrance width/height, mouth spacing, surveyed bearing nor southern-unit dimensions are reported in the extraction's page coverage.

The text does not explicitly establish internal communication between the units. Pillar dimensions and evidence of working, tooling or construction are unreported. Interpreting the pillar as natural rock is the source reader's inference from wording and photograph; “unworked” is not a documented excavation observation.

The p. 17 catalogue assigns B2:4 and B2:8 to a “pillar” locus in Cave A. The extraction does not establish Cave A/B = northern/southern unit. Retain those labels separately. Their poorly specified spatial and vertical relationships cannot date pillar construction.

## Deposits and chronology

### Reported observations — pp. [15]–17

A trial excavation in the northern cave took place in 1971, with subsequent work in 1977 and 1982. The final season is described as producing no results. First-season work encountered thick sheep/goat dung; a test through it yielded several Roman sherds. In the second season, front, interior and rear-recess areas were distinguished. Beneath dung were burning layers, fallen stones and ash. The occupation deposit was several metres thick and contained pottery of widely separated periods.

Prolonged animal-pen use and long, discontinuous occupation are the excavator's interpretations. Dung, burning, rubble and ash do not establish a particular disturbance mechanism, modern intrusion or fully mixed deposit. The extraction expressly limits those claims.
