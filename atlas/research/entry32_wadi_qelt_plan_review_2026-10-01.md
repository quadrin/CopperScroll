# Entry 32: Wadi Qelt plan and phase review

1 October 2026. Targeted review of Ze'ev Meshel and David Amit, “Water supply to Cypros fortress” (אספקת המים למבצר קיפרוס), in David Amit, Yizhar Hirschfeld and Joseph Patrich (eds.), *Ancient Aqueducts of Israel* (1989), pp. 229–242. Original Hebrew page images inspected in the [Kotar viewer](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Viewer.aspx?nBookID=6765980). This records selected route, feature and phase passages; it is not a complete book scan.

## Textual test

Repository translation VII 14–16 gives a water-outlet mouth at Koziba and digging three cubits to a row of stones. Three cubits is a digging depth, not a measured outlet-to-wall separation. This entry supplies no compass bearing. The previous edition review retains Koziba/Buz and row/wall/parapet/mountain/rock alternatives. Archaeology must not choose among those readings.

## Original plan observations

- **Route, Fig. 1, p. 230; numbered descriptions, pp. 230–231:** the drawing distinguishes open channels, bridges, tunnels, reservoirs and runoff channels. A regional route plan cannot resolve a three-cubit deposit.
- **Point 25, p. 231; Fig. 24 and discussion, p. 241:** excavation at the eastern tunnel outlet exposed two phases. The later channel was cut into the earlier tunnel floor at a lower level. Inside it remained an open channel; paired ceramic pipes occurred after its exit. This is a specific outlet comparison, but its relationship to the scroll's second landmark is unproved.
- **Point 27, pp. 231 and 242:** the later pipe route separates from the earlier aqueduct near the southern road. This is a phase-specific branch, not an automatic identification with SWP's Jisr ed-Deir division.
- **Point 29, pp. 231, 233 and 242; Figs. 5–6:** near Beit Jabr el-Fauqani the later pipe route approaches a reused reservoir. The authors identify its surviving structure as early Arab and interpret it as a destination of the pipe system. Reused pipes and an earlier channel passing above it do not date the reservoir to the Second Temple period.

## Dating and identification limits

The authors associate the earlier supply system with Herod and distinguish Hasmonean runoff collection near Cypros (p. 233). They assign the pipe aqueduct to the seventh–eighth centuries CE on architectural and historical grounds (p. 242). Their observed superposition establishes relative sequence; the calendar attribution is their interpretation, not a sealed dating assemblage for each outlet or wall.

Point 25 now supplies an original section for testing an outlet interpretation. Its later modifications must be separated from the earlier bed. The chapter passages checked do not establish that this outlet is the SWP cliffside-wall feature, nor a contemporaneous row of stones reached at three cubits. Preserve the spring head, Jisr ed-Deir and monastery-opposite wall as distinct comparisons.

Reading remains disputed; Wadi Qelt remains a medium, conditional site proposal; exact feature and deposit position remain unresolved. No atlas coordinates or confidence ratings changed.
