# Historical geography, Roman/Byzantine books and Siloam plans
Research date: 30 September 2026 (America/Los_Angeles).

## Result and coverage

This intake records the targeted reading completed in this conversation. It supplements the earlier three-book Kotar intake; it does not replace it. No site confidence, exact-feature identification, atlas coordinate or deposit geometry changes.

The Kotar volumes were read from rendered Hebrew pages. Coverage was selective, not a complete scan of the books or their maps. Some pages failed to render. The observations below are source summaries; their implications for Copper Scroll candidates are separate inferences. Descriptive volume labels and exact Kotar book IDs identify the sources where full edition metadata has not been checked.

## Avi-Yonah historical-geography volume
[Kotar book 95253029](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Viewer.aspx?nBookID=95253029).
Targeted pages inspected included 81–82, 97–104, 119, 179 and appendix 184, with partial-page coverage at some positions.

- **p. 119, Jericho:** distinguishes the Roman-period settlement at Tulul Abu el-Alayiq from Tell es-Sultan and modern Jericho. Describes two aqueduct sources: Wadi Qelt/Ein Fara and Achor, identified in this account with Wadi en-Nu'eimeh. This is a witness to the northern Achor geographical tradition, not a decision between that tradition and Buqeia.
- **p. 119, forts:** distinguishes Jannaeus's Threx and Herod's Cypros in the Jerusalem–Jericho route setting. Neither name establishes the Scroll's individual landmark.
- **p. 104, Hyrcania:** associates Hyrcania with Khirbet Mird and mentions its aqueduct supply. The desert-region discussion uses Kidron as a boundary. No measured entry-29 pool or entry-35 gorge/cairn match was obtained.
- **p. 102, Beth Horon:** identifies upper/lower Beth Horon with Beit Ur and its ascent. This supplies regional context for entry 40; it does not identify a burial feature.
- **pp. 81–82, roads:** distinguishes earlier public routes from later Roman paving and development associated with Trajan, Hadrian and the Bar Kokhba context; the Jerusalem–Jericho road receives a probable Hadrianic attribution.
- **p. 179:** the broad inventory of roads/stations, bridges, forts and water installations supplies search classes rather than an entry-level match.

**Use:** keep the Jericho tell and palace/settlement systems distinct; retain competing Achor traditions; test the construction phase of any road used in a spatial model.

## Political, social and cultural volume I
[Kotar book 7693028](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Viewer.aspx?nBookID=7693028).
Only the opening chapter, especially printed pp. 11–14, was checked.

- **p. 14:** the Jericho balsam estates are discussed in relation to the Roman treasury, with a citation to Pliny, Natural History XII.113. Pliny was not independently read in this intake.
- **p. 14:** discusses confiscations after CE 70 and continued cultivation under tenancy. Ownership changes do not by themselves establish abandonment or cessation of water use.
- **pp. 11–12:** administrative lists and continuing geographical units require separation of place-name continuity from jurisdiction and boundary continuity.

**Use:** contextual evidence for land use and regional names; no new physical feature or deposit identified.

## Archaeological and art volume II
[Kotar book 7904705](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Viewer.aspx?nBookID=7904705).
Checked aqueduct passages on printed pp. 54–55 and 57, burial introduction/illustrations on pp. 143–144 and 146–147, and later burial illustrations on pp. 384–385. Aqueduct p. 56 and burial p. 145 did not render successfully; this is not complete chapter coverage.

### Waterworks

- **p. 54:** traces Hasmonean/Herodian beginnings and major second–third-century development. Distinguish initial construction from Roman enlargement and repair.
- **p. 55:** describes contour-following routes and valley detours. Gives Jerusalem's lower aqueduct as approximately 61 km against about 20 km straight-line distance. A route length cannot substitute for a horizontal distance in a Scroll model.
- **p. 57:** replacement plaster and terracotta pipes may be difficult to date securely within Roman versus later maintenance. Surviving fabric does not establish the system's original arrangement.
- **p. 54 n. 31:** points to David Amit's edited collection on ancient aqueducts of Israel, especially its bibliography pp. 28–29. This is a bibliographic lead; it does not supersede the repository's already accessed Ilan–Amit and Patrich chapters.

### Burials

- **p. 144:** Samaria tomb E22 has a courtyard with a cistern collection system; the caption dates the tomb to the third century CE. A tomb–courtyard–cistern combination requires independent dating before comparison with a Scroll entry.
- **p. 147:** Beth She'arim cave 14 extends approximately 44 m; cave 20 has a courtyard 11.70 × 16.20 m and an entrance in the courtyard's northern wall. The chapter places the cemetery's main use between the second and mid-fourth centuries. These are architectural comparisons, not Beth Horon or Kidron identifications.
- **p. 384:** the fourth–fifth-century Ein Yabrud tomb has three burial benches and accumulated material from repeated use. Date the structure and individual deposits separately.

## Jerusalem: Roman and Byzantine periods
[Book of Jerusalem, Kotar book 4620704](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Viewer.aspx?nBookID=4620704).
Directly checked selected passages on pp. 160–163 and 321–322. The Kidron section listed at p. 331 failed to render and remains unchecked.

- **pp. 161, 163:** discusses continued use of cisterns, the Bordeaux pilgrim's Siloam description and an adjacent pool possibly collecting surplus water. The pilgrim calls Siloam a spring without describing the supplying tunnel. Keep the source, outlet pool and possible overflow pool distinct. This intake did not independently read the pilgrim's original text.
- **p. 163:** upper-aqueduct pipe inscriptions attest Roman military maintenance/reconstruction. Repair evidence alone does not date all of the earlier aqueduct.
- **p. 321:** reports a roughly 16 × 15 m basin, porticoes and western steps from the earlier excavations; the author allows substantial Roman rebuilding of a Second Temple installation. These dimensions refer to a reconstructed historical pool arrangement and must not be applied to every installation called Siloam.
- **pp. 321–322:** subsequent church building, streets, drainage, cisterns and multiple Byzantine phases complicate feature matching.

**Entry 49:** the repository already distinguishes the small Silwan tunnel-mouth pool and Birkat el-Hamra and records Szanton 2023's competing identifications. These book passages add phase cautions and original-excavation locators, not independent proof of the name reading or trough.

## Siloam primary-source follow-up

**Bliss and Dickie:** Frederick Jones Bliss, Excavations at Jerusalem, 1894–1897, plans/illustrations by Archibald Campbell Dickie (London: Palestine Exploration Fund, 1898).
[Scan and foldouts](https://archive.org/details/excavationsatjer00blis); [searchable transcription](https://archive.org/stream/excavationsatjer00blis/excavationsatjer00blis_djvu.txt).

The PDF was obtained and the following plans visually inspected: Plate XI (drain section), Plate XVIII (church/pool plan and sections), General Plans I–II, and enlarged details of General Plan II and Plate XI. Direct text checks included printed pp. 133–136 and 154–158. In this particular scan, Plate XI is PDF page 135, Plate XVIII page 235, General Plan II page 464 and General Plan I page 465 (one-based PDF numbering).

### Small tank with grooved outlet channel

- **pp. 134–135:** at section point b, describes a cement tank 3 ft 4 in square and 25 in high, with its floor 5 ft above the main drain bottom. A small masonry-sided channel leaves its top; the channel has a groove in its rock floor, which is the top of the main drain's southern wall.
- **General Plan II / Plate XI:** locates the drain-exit sector at the southern gate d2, southwest of the area labeled Old Pool. This is spatially distinct from the smaller tunnel-mouth pool farther north. The plan supports a sector-level locator; no modern coordinates or exact tank footprint have been registered.
- **1895 versus 1898:** Bliss's Sixth Report (PEF Quarterly Statement, October 1895, p. 305) initially interpreted it as a catch-pit. The 1898 book p. 135 explicitly withdraws that interpretation and places construction after the drains were disused. This is relative sequencing, without a secure calendar date.
- **Assessment:** retain an undated comparison feature. A groove in a channel does not establish that it is the installation meant by entry 49. Location and unresolved chronology provide no added support for the existing tunnel-outlet candidate.

The 1895 report was located in searchable text at [PEFQS 1895, pp. 305 onward](https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/pefqs/1895_04_305.pdf); retrieval of that PDF failed. The revision is directly readable in the obtained 1898 scan.

### Pool enclosure and stairs

**pp. 154–158 / Plate XVIII:** distinguish the pool visible in the excavators' day from the larger reconstructed enclosure, stairs, paved court and covered arcade. These observations identify access and enclosure features, without an entry-49 trough identification in the passages checked. Some corners derive from Guthe's earlier work rather than fresh independent observation by Bliss and Dickie. Guthe's original plate remains unread in this intake.

### Later excavation and dating controls

Shlomit Weksler-Bdolah and Nahshon Szanton, “Jerusalem, Silwan,” Hadashot Arkheologiyot 126 (22 September 2014), preliminary report of permit A-6709:
[report](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/Report_Detail_Eng.aspx?id=10572&mag_id=121&print=nopic), especially phases 4a, 3e and 3d–3c and Figs. 2–4.

- **Channel L133:** tentatively belongs to an earlier phase and was blocked when the Second Temple gateway was installed. Its presence cannot establish an operating channel in the Scroll's period.
- **Tower L119:** contains a separate reservoir, approximately 4.20 × 6.53 m internally in Bliss–Dickie's account. The later excavators place its insertion tentatively in phase 3d of the Second Temple sequence.
- **Pool L124:** a separate plastered stepped installation, partly exposed at 3 × 2.5 m and about 2.5 m deep, with repairs and six steps.
- **Limit:** the report does not identify or date the small grooved tank of Bliss–Dickie pp. 134–135. The tower reservoir's date must not transfer to that tank. A modern-locus correspondence for the small tank remains unestablished.

## Remaining tests

1. Obtain Guthe's original pool plate and distinguish his surveyed corners from later reconstructed enclosure lines.
2. Check outlet-specific stratigraphy and published installation descriptions against entry 49's independently selected trough/gutter reading. Existing Szanton plan checks are prior work, not a new confirmation.
3. If pursuing the small southern tank, first establish a modern excavation-locus correspondence and a dated construction context. Its sector position is insufficient for a deposit coordinate.
4. Revisit failed Kotar pages and obtain complete chapter/edition metadata before claiming comprehensive book coverage.
5. Use the Achor, road-phase, Beth Horon and burial observations as controlled comparisons without changing existing candidate rankings.
