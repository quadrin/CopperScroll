# Entry 32: SWP wall and outlet correlation

30 September 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). Follow-up to the [original aqueduct phase review](entry32_wadi_qelt_plan_review_2026-10-01.md).

## Sources and observations

Conder–Kitchener, *Survey of Western Palestine*, III (1883), original page images 205–206 and 227–228 inspected in the [public scan](https://archive.org/download/surveyofwesternp03conduoft/surveyofwesternp03conduoft.pdf).

SWP places Jisr ed-Deir west of the monastery. Ein Qelt water divides there into northern and southern routes. Opposite the monastery, a cliffside wall about 30 feet high carries an upper channel and a lower culvert near its base (pp. 227–228). The drawing on p. 228 depicts the bridge described on p. 205; it supplies no surveyed footprint for the separate wall. SWP offers provisional Roman/Byzantine dating.

Milik, [ADAJ 4–5 (1960), p. 149](https://publication.doa.gov.jo/uploads/publications/38/ADAJ_1960_4_5-137-155.pdf), directly inspected. He identifies the Ein Qelt distribution system and retaining walls through SWP, while proposing preservation of a Herodian layout under later rebuilding. This establishes the origin of the identification argument, not another observation dating the wall.

Meshel–Amit 1989 p. 229 starts the Cypros supply at Ein Fawwar and discusses extension toward Ein Fara. Point 25's two-phase outlet section (p. 241, Fig. 24) belongs to that surveyed route. No checked source identifies this point with SWP's monastery-opposite wall. Source, route, outlet and construction phase require separate correlation.

## Provisional feature model

The lower culvert's emergence at the base of the south-bank wall opposite Deir el-Kelt is the most specific wall-associated outlet comparison justified by SWP. The emergence itself is inferred from its through-culvert description; SWP does not plot or excavate that mouth. Treat this as a relative source-description anchor, with unknown footprint and modern coordinates.

Under the project's outlet-plus-stone-row reading, test a three-cubit downward excavation from ground at the proposed outlet to a buried stone course. An illustrative cubit range of 0.45–0.55 m gives 1.35–1.65 m; these are modeling assumptions, not measured archaeology. The entry supplies no bearing. Visible wall masonry does not establish the buried endpoint.

A spring-head interpretation weakens this downstream candidate. Mountain/rock alternatives require a different second-landmark test. Later rebuilding, bedrock geometry or incompatible outlet/course phases could reject this particular pairing while leaving the regional proposal open.

Reading remains disputed; site remains medium and conditional; individual feature is provisional; geographic position remains unresolved. Atlas geometry and confidence ratings remain unchanged.

## Next discriminating evidence

Obtain a wall-specific plan or historical photograph showing its culvert exit and tie it to the original survey route. Establish ground level, bedrock and construction sequence at that outlet. The bridge elevation and point 25's tunnel section cannot supply those missing wall measurements.
