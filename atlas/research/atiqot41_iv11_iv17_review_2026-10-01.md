## IV/11: published observations and limits

Feig p. 85 describes a natural chamber, about 5.5 × 6.5 m and 3.0–3.5 m high. The 2.5 m pillar stands at the southwest end of the hall; its lower portion is natural and its upper portion rock-cut. The chamber was enlarged principally northward toward the opening and slightly eastward toward the cliff. An entrance forecourt, L14, measures about 3 × 5 m. Plan 1 has a north arrow and a 0–4 m scale; Figs. 1–2 show the excavation and W1 approaching the pillar.

The report describes an opening in the singular and does not label a pair of exterior mouths. Plan 1 supplies chamber and forecourt geometry, but does not establish the required two-opening arrangement. This is a coverage conflict for the proposed match, not proof that a second ancient opening never existed. W1 and W2 partition the interior into rooms; interior room boundaries cannot supply two exterior entrances without additional evidence.

Pp. 85–86 distinguish earlier and raised living levels, separated by about 20–25 cm. The later deposit is disturbed, with Iron Age and Hellenistic/Roman material; Feig leaves its construction attribution uncertain between those periods. Pp. 88–89 place the reused jar and flask in an Iron Age discussion. The jar cuts into earlier deposits, so its discovery at the old floor level does not date it to that floor.

P. 89 places five coins in the upper fill, about 60–80 cm above the Iron Age living surface in L10/L11. Two are Hasmonean, two are Elagabalus coins dated 218–222 CE, and one was unidentified. Feig explicitly says the coins alone do not establish settlement; passing visitors or later pastoral activity are possible explanations. These finds do not date the pillar cutting, entrance enlargement or northern threshold. The 60–80 cm find height and 20–25 cm floor difference cannot become the scroll's three-cubit depth.

**Bounded check 1 — pillar name and opening geometry:** IV/11 supplies an internal pillar, but the inspected plan/text do not document the two required exterior openings. The modern descriptive name supplies no identifying weight. Retain IV/11 as a limited comparison, with weak support for the full entry-25 arrangement.

## IV/17: a separate two-opening comparison

Sion p. 63 calls IV/17 the Cave of the Built Wall. It has two openings in the eastern cliff of Abu Saraj and two spaces, northern and southern, separated by a rock-cut pillar. The northern opening is 0.8 m wide and leads to a natural space, 2.2 × 4.5 m, containing a hewn compartment. The southern opening is partly blocked by a built wall, 2.4 m long and about 0.4 m thick. The southern space measures 2.15 × 4.20 m. The cave has a reported area of 33.5 m² and height of 1.4–1.7 m.

Plan 5 on p. 63 includes a north arrow, 0–3 m scale, two marked mouths, the intervening rock and wall stones at one mouth. The text distinguishes the northern and southern openings; the drawing establishes their current surveyed arrangement. The statement that they lie in the eastern cliff is a published observation. It does not by itself establish two due-east mouth bearings. No entrance azimuth was computed in this pass.

Excavation concentrated in the centres of the two spaces beneath animal-activity remains. The reported finds include Hellenistic sherds, coins, arrowheads and organic material. Sion infers use from the first half of the second century to the first half of the first century BCE, and interprets the southern blocking as concealment in conflict involving the Hasmoneans. This is the excavator's historical reconstruction, not an independently dated wall-building event. P. 64 illustrates the arrowheads. No first-century CE northern threshold, pillar-cutting date or measured digging datum is established in the inspected passage.

**Bounded check 2 — period control:** IV/11's upper-fill coins cannot date its entrance arrangement. IV/17 has a surveyed two-opening arrangement and a published Hellenistic-use interpretation, but the inspected context does not date the blocking or the northern threshold to the proposed scroll period. Both period tests remain inconclusive. Sion and Feig describe related work within the same survey programme; their repeated IV/11 finds are not independent corroboration.
