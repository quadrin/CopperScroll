## Beth Guvrin: earlier caves within the later cemetery

Source: Gideon Avni, Uzi Dahari and Amos Kloner, *The Necropolis of Bet Guvrin–Eleutheropolis*, IAA Reports 36 (2008). [IAA record and chapter downloads](https://publications.iaa.org.il/iaareports/41/); [JSTOR book overview with chapter openings](https://www.jstor.org/content/oa_book_monograph/j.ctt1fzhdgj).

Chapter 4, pp. 119–120, opening excerpt: Hellenistic loculus caves in Maresha's northern cemetery, adjoining Bet Guvrin's southern cemetery, were reused during the first–fourth centuries CE. The report identifies caves I.89–I.92 and describes added bone repositories in chamber walls and floors, with occasional ossuaries. It cites Oren and Rappaport (1984).

Chapter 5 opening, p. 121: the South Cemetery lamp assemblage indicates use from the late second or third century through the first half of the eighth century CE. These are assemblage/use dates, not construction dates for every wall or cave. The earlier reused group must retain its separate chronology.

Project inference: screen I.89–I.92 separately if a Scroll reading supplies an independently justified regional association. Obtain their plans and contexts before assigning an entrance, repository or floor to a scroll-period phase. Broad first–fourth-century reuse alone supplies no precise contemporary feature.

Next original report: E. D. Oren and U. Rappaport, “The Necropolis of Maresha–Beth Govrin,” *IEJ* 34 (1984), pp. 114–153, [JSTOR identifier 27925934](https://www.jstor.org/stable/27925934). Bibliographically verified; full article unread here.

## Ashqelon: directly accessible comparison, separate from the 1999 tomb

Source: Shira Bloch-Lifshitz, “[Ashqelon, Ben ‘Amar Street](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/Report_Detail_Eng.aspx?id=26284),” *Hadashot Arkheologiyot* 135 (16 March 2023), permit A-8789. Report text and [Fig. 2 plan and sections](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/Images/8789-2.jpg) inspected. This is a different excavation from the Migdal painted tomb.

Observed: vaulted chamber L114, approximately 2 × 3 m; arched entrance in southeastern W4 near its southern corner; northeastern opening interpreted as a link to L120. Vaults L118–L119, approximately 1 × 2.5 m, lie below the chamber-floor level and abut W6 of L120. The plan marks reconstructed outlines in red dashed lines. The full structure remains incompletely exposed.

Dating: limited excavation inside the vaults yielded sixth–seventh-century ceramics, including a slipper lamp. Surrounding accumulation contains fourth–eighth-century material. The report provides a broad Byzantine–Early Islamic attribution; these finds do not separately date each wall's initial construction.

Project inference: this is a control for reading access and vertical relationships in a burial plan. Its published evidence does not establish a first-century landmark. Retain observed masonry, reconstructed extent and dated deposits as separate records.

## Maresha northern necropolis: firsthand supplementary observations

Follow-up, 1 October 2026 (UTC). Boaz Zissu and Amos Kloner, “The Necropolis of Hellenistic Maresha, Judean Foothills, Israel,” *Hypogea2015*, pp. 100–114. [Publisher PDF](https://www.operaipogea.it/wp-content/uploads/1995/03/15-Zissu-Kloner-hellenistic-Maresha_compressed.pdf). Northern-necropolis discussion pp. 111–113 read; Fig. 15a–b, p. 114, visually inspected. This is a supplementary firsthand report; the original Oren–Rappaport 1984 article remains unread.

Tomb 515: the authors identify two levels of burial niches omitted from the earlier plan. During Roman reuse, limestone slabs narrowed the original entrance to a square frame holding a blocking stone found in place. Wall and floor niches accommodated collected bones. Entry through a connected burial niche from Tomb 514 explains the surviving closure. Fig. 15 shows the two levels and blocked entrance; it is a photograph, not a measured phase plan.

Dating limit: the authors attribute these modifications to Roman reuse. Second–third-century lamps came from looter debris, not a sealed construction context. Their wider first–fourth-century cemetery chronology partly repeats Oren–Rappaport; it is not independent dating of each alteration.

Project inference: documented relative sequence (original tomb → entrance reduction and repositories), but no securely dated first-century installation. The mapping between tomb numbers 514/515 and the 2008 catalogue's I.89–I.92 is unverified. Do not transfer numbering, geometry or phase dates between catalogues. No Scroll entry assignment or confidence change. Next discriminating evidence: the 2008 catalogue cross-reference and original 1984 plans/contexts.
