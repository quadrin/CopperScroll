### Page-specific findings

- **Viewer 1:** an undated Manchester College of Science and Technology statement referring to early 1956. It expresses the institutional belief that the opening had not made any character unrecognisable. This general contemporary claim gives no character-by-character inspection or measured cut-edge loss.
- **Viewer 2:** Wright Baker's Registrar minute dated **6 September 1955**, with a later **10 October 1955** filing stamp. It records the preliminary meeting with Bowden, Baker, Harding, Fish and Allegro; a demonstration on an actual piece of scroll; authority to proceed; and arrangements for a temporary assistant. The handwritten marginal name remains untranscribed. The typed date and filing stamp are separate events.
- **Viewer 3:** Harding to Bowden, **12 October 1955**, confirming administrative arrangements, precautions, publicity and access. It refers to an Allegro progress letter. These arrangements are not observations of damage or a cut-position record.
- **Viewers 4–5:** Wright Baker to Harding, **19 October 1955**, reference **15879/55**, with the continuation's HWB/MES initials. He says opening and rough cleaning of the first scroll took **4½ days** after equipment assembly; more detailed cleaning had taken about as long again and continued under an assistant. He expected to finish a **compound photograph of the interior and all lettering within two days**, then a transcription. That photograph was prospective on the letter date. He also reports **many photographs taken during the opening stages**, and discusses assembling pictures and mounting the sections for an exhibit. His requested second scroll had not yet arrived in this account; the ten-day estimate for opening it is a forecast. The letter supplies no frame numbers or assignment to cut 21/22, and establishes no pre-cut image of the target.
- **Viewer 6:** Wright Baker to **J. Waldron, Rathbone Books**, **6 March 1956**, reference **3709/56**, copied to the Registrar. He describes spindle support, adhesive coating and strips generally extending about half the circumference. The nominal saw dimensions are **1¼ inch diameter and 0.006 inch width**, equivalent to **31.75 mm and 0.1524 mm**. He reports generally well-preserved lettering with some corrosion damage. The leaf explicitly lists **“Enclosure – Photograph of cutting.”** The supplied PDF contains no such photograph.
