## Workbook observations

The XLSX contains `LF`, `LF_Transposed_Counts`, `LF_Transposed_Mass` and `HF`. All are visible. There are no hidden rows or columns, comments, defined names, charts or embedded images. Exact populated-cell coordinates and formula text are retained in the private reading receipt. Numeric zero ranges were read explicitly; blanks, `?`, `NW`, `NDA`, `N/A` and approximate counts remain distinct. Formula cached values were read without editing or recalculating the original workbook.

The recovery exposes alignment and unit problems that must be resolved before reusing spatial patterns or densities. In `LF`, column B identifies sample 26a, basket 10847, and has a wood count of 330 at B16. In `LF_Transposed_Counts`, row 2 identifies sample 3, basket 10370, but also assigns a wood count of 330 at H2. The corresponding mass sheet row 2 similarly assigns the LF B17 weight to that different context. This is direct disagreement between source tabs. Sorting or transposition may explain it, but the original field records are needed to establish the correct association. The tabs must not be treated as independent agreeing observations or joined blindly by their sample labels.

The count and mass categories also contain conflicting values. `LF!AM80` is 0.0121 under the whole-grape count label and `AM81` is 1 under its weight label. Rows 82–83 mix integer and decimal values under fragment-weight and fragment-count labels, and their total formulas combine those values as supplied. `HF` has similar count/weight tensions in its grape and date rows, including `AM106:AM107`, `AU106:AU107` and `AX106:AX107`. The transpose retains these conflicts rather than resolving them. These are source-cell observations, not corrected botanical totals.

The notes add limitations omitted by a simple numeric extraction: a wet vial ruined one sample before weighing, dung may be modern, the flax identification needs checking, one unidentified sample may be missing, and some fruit or barley weights combine whole and fragment material. Lower-bound strings such as `712+` and `500+` are not exact counts. An unknown soil volume remains `?`; a recorded volume of 650 also needs source verification before assuming a decimal correction or using it as a denominator. The supplement expands the audit of the published assemblage while requiring reconciliation before quantitative reuse.

## Video and missing figure

The publisher labels Video 1 as an AVI of 161602 kB, but its linked player serves an MP4 transcode named for the same chapter's MOESM1. The recovered video is 1280 × 720, with a 30.04-second visual stream and 751 frames. Sixty decoded views at two frames per second were inspected across the full orbit, rather than claiming that every frame was inspected. The audio track was decoded in full and every sample was zero. The sequence shows a textured terrain model changing viewpoint, with incomplete model edges and visible vegetation. It provides no displayed scale, north arrow, phase annotation or sediment-age section. It is the model presentation described in the chapter, not an additional erosion measurement or archaeological construction date. Its original AVI bytes remain unverified.

For Gadot's map, the publisher PDF endpoint returned subscription-preview HTML rather than a PDF. The web reader also reported that the PDF URL was inaccessible. The TAU record points back to the DOI and to Scopus, and supplies no separate manuscript or map. The bounded search did not locate a relevant publisher correction or author-hosted replacement. This preserves the supplied copy's missing Figure 1 as an unresolved gap; it does not establish that no original map exists. No substitute map was drawn.
