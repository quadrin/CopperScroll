### New observations, corrections and dependencies

- **New remeasurement, pp. 858 and 880–882/Fig. 23:** Gibson, Lewis and Taylor report that the inner end of Mugharet Abu-Tabaq stops short of the compound's north wall, correcting Cross and Milik's stated underground extent. Their chamber length is 41.50 m, versus 34.7 m reported by Cross and Milik and 35.3 m by Masterman. These extents have different observers/conditions and should not be averaged or silently substituted. Their plan and section show the entrance wall, ceiling shaft and cobbled floor; the reported shaft diameter is 0.90 m and mouth-wall thickness 0.90 m. Patrich's earlier ashlar jambs, metal hinge and entrance steps were no longer visible. The chapter labels the water installation Iron Age, but supplies no sealed construction context for each wall, floor and shaft; retain observed geometry separately from that attribution.
- **New archival correction, pp. 864–865 and 883/Fig. 12:** Ory's 1928 photograph and description show the es-Samrah courtyard depression before the later military damage invoked by Stager. The authors observed no reservoir undercutting of the adjoining rooms. This contradicts a wholly modern military origin for that depression. It establishes pre-1928 existence, rather than a secure Iron Age construction date or a same-phase wall–pool contact. Ory's report is S.317, 18 January 1928.
- **Observed fittings and uncertain date, pp. 884–888/Figs. 28–30:** el-Maqari's adjoining cisterns have separate mouths, a common intervening rock wall, a small exterior catchment basin, a rock-cut channel into one chamber and surviving plaster. Their entrance masonry is explicitly later; the mouths are partly blocked by soil. Proximity to the Iron Age enclosure and a water-use interpretation cannot date these individual alterations. The plan supplies an exploratory two-chamber hydraulic control, not a Copper Scroll cave identification.
- **Published quantity discrepancy, p. 885:** native text prints **26,495 litres** for both twin cisterns, alongside dimensions 13.90×4.80×2.05 m and 10.90×5.60×2.10 m. A project calculation treating the chambers as rectangular boxes gives **264,960 litres**, approximately ten times the printed quantity. This exposes an arithmetic/unit/transcription issue for review. The chambers are irregular and partly silted, so the box calculation is not a corrected measured usable capacity; preserve both the printed value and the stated assumptions.
- **Newly documented pool/channel relationship, pp. 886 and 890/Figs. 31–32:** the pool northeast of el-Maqari is reported 73 m from the enclosure and measures 27.50×23.80 m, with a reported 2.60 m excavation depth, earth banks and a stone dam. A 0.18 m plastered wall segment may be a diversion channel toward a plastered tank corner exposed by later disturbance. This adds an observed hydraulic candidate/control feature and its published plan, but the function and construction phases remain unresolved. Surface pottery spanning periods cannot establish the date of the tank–channel–pool junction.
- **Phase separation, Table 1 and pp. 858, 868–869:** Abu-Tabaq and Maqari South have reported Bedouin trench graves; Maqari South's exposed surface also had late Ottoman-type Gaza ware and bracelet glass. Graves beside a cave or pool therefore cannot inherit the Iron Age date of a nearby enclosure or the Roman date of mixed surface pottery. The graves, reservoir, cave openings and settlement require separately dated relationships.
- **Author's alternative model, pp. 866–879 and 887–894:** the authors reinterpret the enclosed alluvial plots as improved pasture and the compounds as specialist pastoral settlements. Their critique notes the small seed sample, absence of securely identified granary contents and the distinction between consumed plants and locally grown crops. Herd capacities, household sizes, seasonal movements and military patronage are model-dependent reconstructions. Their alternative remains a question to assess against the original Stager data, not a new observation proving a Scroll-period land use or owner.
