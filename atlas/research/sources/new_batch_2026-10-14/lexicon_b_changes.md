## Additions and qualifications

- Printed436–437/viewer 222 gives mouth-related examples in several contexts, including a well-mouth; printed 472–473/viewer 240 distinguishes grave/burial and receiving or inserting senses. These are contextual lexical attestations. No physical opening or ancient burial relationship was independently observed.
- Printed492–493/viewer 250 distinguishes a Latin-derived column term from sound/news forms; printed 498–499/viewer 253 distinguishes fortress, screen and water-tower reservoir terms. Graphic similarity alone cannot select an installation.
- Printed506–507/viewer 257 says a city/village term ranges over settlements of different sizes and context can leave the sense undecided. Printed542–543/viewer 275 similarly distinguishes street, district, outside, square and marketplace uses. These are warnings against importing a fixed site scale or architectural form into a Copper Scroll reading.
- Printed546–547/viewer 277 gives a pit/cavity/ditch entry with a Jaffa literary citation. Printed564–565/viewer 286 distinguishes watering-trough/pool/canal and lower-mountain/vessel contexts. These entries supply source leads and semantic alternatives, with uncertainty retained; they do not date or identify a candidate water system.
- Printed592–593/viewer 300 distinguishes entrance, gateway, gate and door senses and cites a cave entrance in Hagiga 78a(1). The index route is now read directly, but the cited manuscript and edition have not been independently collated in this pass.
- The indexes separate editions, manuscripts, glosses and variant references. Several entries can point to one underlying passage; their repetition supplies no additional independent attestation. The dictionary ends at printed 594, indexes continue through the assigned scope, and Tiberian Masora references continue in the root reader's following spreads.
