## New qualifications and source checks

- **Pool capacities need source-specific values.** Galor pp.292–293 says2,000–204,000litres, but Table1p.293 and Fig.25p.313 give locus71 as300m³; Table1 gives locus69 as7m³ while Fig.25 gives10m³. Native pages verify these are printed discrepancies. Do not average them or convert the general prose into an exact capacity constraint. Her p.318n.6 traces many earlier estimates to Laperrousaz's calculations without sections. These different estimates are partly inherited calculations, rather than independent measurements.
- **Inlet/outlet identity is disputed.** Galor p.302 reports that the supposed overflows in loci71/91 lie above their supply inlets. Her p.318n.12 identifies the southwest opening in locus138 as deVaux's overflow and Strobel's supply. Figure arrows express the published hydraulic interpretation; a plan alone supplies no independently verified flow direction. For locus117, p.295 expressly leaves the supplying sequence difficult because four channels survive; one western channel predates the aqueduct. These qualifications reinforce the existing construction-phase/contact gap.
- **Observed joins support relative relationships.** Galor pp.301/310 describes the locus34 overflow crossing the later48/49 footprint and continuous western plaster across85/91. Her reconstruction of original steps beneath the fill in91 remains unexcavated. The proposed common date of the56/58 and85/91 divisions is an analogy, not an observed joining deposit. Native p.301 gives the fragmentary western tub approximately1.3×1.2m; the extraction's4.2m width is OCR error.
- **Masonry and rarity require separate tests.** Galor p.306 leaves masonry-style differences open to substrate variation rather than chronological sequence. Her p.318n.34 excludes comparison sites with fewer than ten stepped pools; n.33 and p.319n.46 acknowledge large isolated pools elsewhere. Her selected concentration comparison cannot establish regional exclusivity of an individual pool or channel. Her dual-use interpretation in pp.316–317 remains interpretation, rather than an observed ritual function.
- **Plaster chemistry does not fix a construction date.** Shimron pp.331/335–336 divides cadmium concentrations using explicitly chosen boundaries and tentatively associates locus71 construction, locus110 repairs and the upper aqueduct plaster. Figure10b displays that grouping. This is a proposed material/chronological correlation without an absolute date or a measured wall–floor–opening section. Galor's caution about masonry and Shimron's chronological hypothesis must both remain visible.
- **Contamination has multiple histories.** Shimron p.331 allows grinding-vessel contamination for Ni/Cr. His pp.333–334 leave metallic objects near the pools and industrial use as alternatives; the suggested connection to the L120 coins or L52 tools is speculative. His p.334 expressly lacks the concentration of contaminants in the water above the plaster, so the plaster values do not establish ancient exposure or poisoning. His p.335 allows salt formation after installations ceased functioning, qualifying the use-history inference from evaporites. His p.336 concludes that the available inorganic chemistry does not unambiguously distinguish cisterns, baths and industrial installations.
- **One sample's label is not another observation.** Shimron's Table1p.324 labels the upper-aqueduct sample Q10a, whereas Fig.2p.323 labels the photographed section Q10b. Preserve suffixes and the difference between chemical samples and illustrated plaster; no unrecorded equivalence is imposed here. His pp.331/334 also stress overlapping ore fields and mixed plaster/metal lead signatures; ore attribution cannot locate a deposit.

## Architecture and reassessment, pp.419–482

All chapter text, catalogues, notes and captions at viewers449–512 are now read, with all30 numbered native figures (Humbert/Chambon's numbering continues across the final part). Publisher back matter at viewers513–516 is read. Image-only backs/cover pages outside the chapters do not receive reading credit from their OCR noise.

**Repeated arguments have identifiable publication histories.** The first essay, pp.419–425, is explicitly an updated translation of Humbert's1999 essay; pp.425–438 update his2001 Modena lecture. Repeated square-core, residence and late-earthquake proposals within these chapters, and pp.467–482, are one author's developing model. They cannot become three independent confirmations.

**A useful dated field-note lead concerns the broken channel.** Humbert p.437 quotes deVaux's1April1954 diary, locus72, about an unrepaired break of the main supply channel. He argues that this is incompatible with assigning the same break to31BCE while keeping downstream pools in use afterwards. Figure10p.443 overlays proposed cracks; it is an interpretation, without a dated fracture section. This directs attention to QumranI p.315 and the actual channel contacts, rather than establishing Humbert's late-earthquake chronology. His alternative30/40BCE–68CE continuous occupation remains a proposal. Adjacent p.435 explicitly warns that dating surrounding ceramics by Qumran and returning those dates to Qumran would be circular, and that Jannaeus coins continued circulating after the ruler's death.

**Door morphology does not establish synchronous thresholds.** Humbert p.477 says the twin west doors380/381 have different masonry and thresholds20cm apart; their belonging to one construction episode is unknown. His p.478 says the relocated threshold KhQ2972 cannot be restored precisely from the surviving drawings; its excavated position need not be original. The catalogue p.447 calls the passage door282, while the Figure13p.450 excavation photograph labels482. Preserve the documentary discrepancy before joining threshold records. These are Qumran building doors, not the cave thresholds required for entry25.

**The architectural collection is selective.** Chambon pp.445–446 states that this catalogue selected pieces considered favorable to a residential building; some worked blocks were omitted. Figure28p.464 plots localized examples, not a complete unbiased inventory. Several pieces' excavated positions are unknown, and later storage/display locations must remain distinct from findspots. Figure27p.463 expressly depicts proposed/artistic arrangements, not observed flooring or an in-situ triclinium. Humbert p.480 likewise makes the south-wing reconstruction depend on assuming little displacement during reuse. Native diagrams verify that restored profiles and observed fragments are presented separately.

**Fill under a channel has a limited observation.** KhQ2967 is a column drum in locus91 beneath the retained fill carrying channel655 (catalogue p.447; Fig.29a p.465). Humbert p.477 explains that the fill was left to protect the channel and rejects treating the drum as a surviving stair because the reconstructed descent would be unusually steep. Galor p.310 had left possible underlying steps open to excavation. Keep both assessments; the unexcavated fill cannot establish the original stair or absence of one.

## Textile analysis and its annex, pp.277–288

Müller et al. pp.277–286/viewers307–316 and Penelope Walton Rogers’ annex pp.287–288/viewers317–318 are read in full, including bibliography, Table1 and native Figures1–10. Figure1’s two landscape pages are also inspected upright. Viewer319’s ritual-baths divider title is read from extraction; its photograph and viewer320 remain unclassified and unviewed.

**Technical identification does not establish depositional age.** Müller p.277 says eighteen specimens had been submitted for radiocarbon dating but results were unavailable to that chapter. The observed bast fibre, cotton and wool classifications therefore cannot date a cave occupation or establish contemporaneity with the settlement. Rogers p.287 received numbered specimens without contextual information; her identifications are an independent laboratory examination of those same specimens, not independent excavation provenance. Her p.288 contextual discussion remains a secondary historical comparison.

**QUM512 remains a disagreement within the volume.** Müller p.284 reports that re-examining the same textile excluded accidental sample mixing, while Rogers p.288, after repeated microscopy, drying-twist and ash tests identifying flax, suggests confusion in the numbering of the samples or SEM images. Figures1g and7 document the laboratory representations being compared. Neither explanation is silently accepted; the two analyses do not become independent corroborations of an unusual imported fibre.

**Units and figure labels require reconciliation before numerical reuse.** Native Table1 p.281 labels crystal-size columns in ångströms and prints57 for QUM525’s200 reflection. Native p.283 instead prints a cross-section size of about60nm for the ancient and modern cotton. Keep the unit inconsistency rather than silently converting the prose into a corrected laboratory result. Figure1j p.279 labels QUM518 as celluloseIV, whereas Figure6p.282 and the following discussion attach that comparison to QUM502. Preserve the labelled panel as printed; do not transfer identification across sample IDs.

**Soil and corrosion alternatives limit colour interpretation.** Müller pp.285–286 distinguishes diffraction from attached quartz/calcite and DeadSea-mud phases from dyes. The green particles on QUM524 from Tomb1 may be metal-corrosion or burial alteration products; their crystalline phase is not securely identified. This is no independently observed associated metal object or deposit. QUM502 is an unidentified plant-stem/leaf fibre rather than verified processed flax; Rogers p.287 describes QUM517 as fur with skin and bone/cartilage, plausibly small-mammal remains, with species unresolved. Neither is automatically a textile concealment wrapper.
