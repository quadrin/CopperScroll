The Hebrew p. 75, slice 6, already states that the 15 cm channel beside the metre-wide threshold could not be shown to carry water inward or outward, and its function could not be established. This is the same uncertainty recorded in English p. 4, rather than an omitted hydraulic result recovered from the original. Hebrew p. 76, slices 6–7, also retains the uncertain roof proposal and the limited excavation of the two eastern rooms. They remain author reconstruction and excavation limits.

Hebrew pp. 84–87, slices 17–23, record disturbed graves, predominantly north–south orientations, the exceptions, four cemetery pottery vessels, and the distinction between physical finds and comparison with Qumran. Assigning the cemetery to the adjacent community and identifying a sect are Bar-Adon’s arguments. Pottery typology and grave resemblance are not sealed construction contacts. The negative investigation of apparent marks on grave stones appears already at p. 87 n. 14 and continues below; English p. 24 n. 1 republishes the same UV, botanical and geological checks. It is a shared original negative observation.

One bounded update in wording is visible: Hebrew p. 87 n. 14, slices 22–23, cites the investigation of red-stained bones as **in press** and already describes ingestion of a plant extract used to obtain dyes; English p.24 n.2 gives the1971 publication and names alizarin, madder and *Rubia pinctorum*. That is a publication-stage change in the reported interpretation of staining, not another excavation or a fresh chemical test in this sweep. No broader claim of complete equivalence or exhaustive variant recovery is made.

The full read also makes the status of the closing geographical proposals explicit: Hebrew p.89, slices24–25, favours equating Qumran with Sekhakhah, withdraws Bar-Adon’s earlier equation with the City of Salt, and leaves the other coastal names unresolved pending fuller regional excavation. Note21 cites Allegro, Cross and Mazar’s reported opinion. These are source-attributed geographical interpretations; they provide no independently surveyed scroll-landmark identification.
