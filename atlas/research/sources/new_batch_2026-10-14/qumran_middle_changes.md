## Sheridan, Ullinger and Ramp, printed129–169/viewer159–199

Complete text,148notes and bibliography read; all16figures and18tables inspected natively, including the six rotated pages of Table7. Viewers158 and200 are blank versos verified natively. This is not a full native audit of every prose leaf.

**Provenance correction, p.165 n.33:** the authors explain that TombsA/B were provisionally called T9(A)/T10(B) after a misreading of de Vaux’s notebooks. Finding Tomb10 in Paris and rereading the notebooks corrected that identification after Zias’s2000 paper was already published. Preserve the alternative labels with their dates; do not collapse A/B,9/10 and the different northern-cemetery designations into one record. Figures5–8 combine the earlier de Vaux/Humbert–Chambon map/photo lineages with a genuinely later April2001 resurvey by Rosenberg and Meyers. Their discrepancies and missing surface tombs remain explicit; the resurvey does not date a grave.

**Sampling and dating limits, pp.132–137,163–164:** old excavation selected crania and pelves, so the present absence of other bones cannot establish originally poor preservation or a later burial. Figure9 directly compares original photographs with the curated subset. The authors report failed direct bone radiocarbon work due to inadequate endogenous protein and contaminated coffin wood. They retain the question mark when revising Tomb7 from female? to male?, and explicitly disallow extrapolating this small selected sample to the whole community. Their three independent modern measurements are repeat measurements of the same bones, not independent site samples. The proposed future analyses are not completed results.

## Jewellery and trace elements: completed chapters, distinct claims

Clamer, printed171–183/viewer201–213: complete text, notes, bibliography and bead catalogue read; all six figures and the material-summary tables inspected natively. Figure6 explicitly shows the beads strung **after excavation**, so it is not an in-situ arrangement. Her proposed Late Roman/Byzantine or later dates for the marginal burials rely on typological comparanda with explicitly long use/reuse histories; cultural, religious and social assignments remain the author's arguments. The pierced Feshkha coin and metal ornaments were unavailable for her renewed inspection. A native-verified internal label conflict remains: the ring is KhQ3651 in Figure2 and the initial discussion, but the ring paragraph on p.173 calls it KhQ2671, also the earring's label. Do not normalize those objects silently. The comparative catalogue repeats some dimensions differently from the primary descriptions, so any quantitative reuse needs an object-specific reconciliation.

Rasmussen et al., printed185–189/viewer215–219: complete text/bibliography, all six native figures and Table1 inspected. The eight bone aliquots were not cleaned or pretreated; the authors observe soil-associated element correlations and make their dietary/social ranking conditional on excluding diagenesis. The preceding anthropology chapter (p.164) says diagenesis precludes dietary interpretation from these bones. Preserve this disagreement within the same volume; neither paper's hypothesis is an independently established community diet or social hierarchy.

## Radiocarbon chapters: completed available content

Van der Plicht et al.193–196, Higham/Taylor/Green197–200 (including full calibrated-age annex) and Taylor/Doudna201–204 are complete, including bibliographies and native tables/charts. The charcoal-like “bitumen” sample KLR2612 proved to be a charred date with an intact stone and insect traces (p.194). The T18 coffin-wood pretreatment removed paraffin to the stated detection limit after49hours; the result is a wood-age bound, not a precise or latest burial date, because timber reuse or reburial remains possible (pp.203–204). KhQ519's provenance is explicitly only probably Cave9 owing to storage/inventory mismatches (p.201). The Groningen/Waikato results are duplicate aliquots or related material combined under a contemporaneity assumption, not independent field contexts. The synthesis repeats slightly different calibrated limits from the laboratory chapter; retain edition/page and calibration/version provenance rather than merging the numbers without notice.

Native Table1 on p.198 also prints99.5/99.3 “%modern” beside1927/2037BP results. This is an internal quantitative inconsistency in the printed source, not an OCR repair; original laboratory records are needed before reusing that column. The laboratory-age results and the author's historical interpretations remain distinct.

An unidentified archival sheet at viewer220 has not been opened; the adjacent viewer221 credit says only “Album photo. Qumran, École biblique, p.529.” Its subject could not be independently established, and garbled extraction is not visual coverage. This is an explicit remaining image/content gap, separate from the completed chapters.

## Bélis textile catalogue: available text completed, visual exceptions preserved

All available body text, catalogues, captions, bibliography and225notes on printed207–276/viewers237–306 are now read. All nine numbered figures and all five numbered tables were inspected natively, including the complete rotated Table1 (viewers242–248) and its30notes, the two-page Christmas Cave catalogue (250–251), and the Crowfoot working list (282). PlateIII (286) was inspected with all twelve textile photographs and their scales. PlatesI and II (284,285) retain caption-only coverage. PlateIV/viewer287 received a later panel-by-panel caption preflight and native inspection, recorded in continuation_visual_coverage.json; its six subjects are archaeological photographs. The title/adjacent leaves235–236 remain uninspected. This completes available text, not all publication content or every native page. The author explicitly presents a provisional, incomplete corpus. Lots were commonly pooled during excavation without level records; a date on a box can be a later Friday sorting date and need not uniquely identify a site (pp.207–208). “SPI” remains an uncertain label with competing origin hypotheses, not securely KhirbetQumran. Early “AF” on1Q photographs means the cave near Feshkha and must not be normalized to the Feshkha settlement (p.223). The date-bearing1952 “G.8” linen lot is3Q, distinct from1955 “Gr.8Q” (Table1 n.5 and dated rows); keep those provenance systems separate.

The apparent rolled object in linen (pp.224–225) is a difficult, incomplete observation followed by a hypothesis, with missing original provenance and subsequent unavailable material. Its suggested manuscript/content must not be promoted to an identified new scroll. The proposed identification of Libby's sacrificed linen as an Isaiah wrapper (pp.226–227) reconstructs a chain from indirect reports and a popular-book caption; it is not an inspected attached manuscript or surviving sample. ChristmasCave's preliminary textile inventory depends onAllegro's1960–1961 expedition material and1994 archival recataloguing; the actual cave position and salt-deposit/submergence explanation remain uncertain (pp.211,219). No independent new field observation follows from its republication.


**Packaging, placement and provenance, pp.228–229,241–247:** an Amman jar/linen photograph published in1954 was later reused to illustrate1Q discovery, although Bélis had not examined the object and could not establish its original provenance. Repeated captions are not independent in-situ observations. The Feshkha bronze stack AF143–160 retained textile corrosion traces photographed before cleaning destroyed them; the textile type cannot be determined from those traces. The original notes place it on an abraded wall crest on13February1958; descriptions vary between the locus15/16 boundary and the east wall of21. Bélis explicitly questions whether the stack was concealed at all (p.244). That context must remain distinct from the Qumran silver pots under the upper floor in locus120, where the original21–22March1955 notes record three containers. Post135/Antonine deposition and subsequent sediment concealment are her reconstruction, not a sealed archaeological date. The published report, unpublished draft, field notes and11May1958 letter are versions of the same campaign.

**Hydraulic source trace, pp.245–246,274 n.85–96:** Bélis reproduces de Vaux's unpublished Feshkha draft and administrative report. The observed distribution tank, two feeder channels, pool-floor outlet, external plastered pits and paved stepped work area are useful hydraulic comparisons. A tannery function, and later a linen-retting interpretation (p.249), are competing functional hypotheses; tannin had not been detected. The north-court inlet above the modern springs supports a question about an earlier source, but the proposed higher source/aqueduct had not been located. These are Feshkha records from the existing excavation lineage, not observations at Atara.

**Wrapper and retrieval hypotheses, pp.231–240,261–269:** Figures1–5 distinguish measured textile patterns and photographs from schematic folding reconstructions. The proposed TQ1–Hodayot correspondence does not produce complete coincident cut lines, and the author admits unsatisfactory folding results (pp.236–237). Her identification of manuscript retrieval from folded/triangular-cut cloth, possible visitors, and a coordinated textile workshop is interpretation. The Cave29 entrance passage is described as40cm high, with2m passage and3m chamber from the earlier de Vaux account; jar counts vary within the discussion (seven intact plus twelve fragmentary jars versus the quoted twelve jars). Stacked lids, a lamp and textile fibres do not independently date a retrieval event or establish a manuscript once present there. Five wooden poles from crevasse17 are observed; a tent or vertical loom is a functional proposal. Figure6b is a Gallo-Roman loom reconstruction, not an excavated Qumran loom. Figure8 is a regional historical operations map, and Figure9 a distribution overlay on the Qumran plan; neither supplies a new phase-controlled deposit section.

**Results and unresolved identifiers, pp.253,258–266,275–276 notes:** the study states that its new textile radiocarbon programme produced no satisfactory dates (p.259); fibre identification cannot be converted into a deposition date. SPI provenance remains expressly unestablished. D058 from the1952 Grotte8/3Q lot was present in1997 but missing after the2000 transfer. The p.264 description identifies a date as QUM509/D009, whereas native Table5 (p.260) assigns D009 textile toQUM506, its associated date toQUM507, and QUM509 to the4Q linen/leather attachment D052. The identifiers must remain unresolved with exact page provenance. Note158 assigns the southernTomb1 ornaments toKhQ2671; this does not silently repair Clamer's alternativeKhQ3651 figure label. The repeated suggestion of a post68/Bar Kokhba garrison protecting remembered caches is a historical reconstruction from coins and textiles, not a newly excavated observation. Printed p.276 note219 (viewer306) calls the youngest coin a terminus ante quem while correctly saying that burial cannot precede it; that described relation is a terminus post quem, so preserve the source's terminology error rather than adopting it.

The shelf photographs at220 and textile inventory plates284/285 remain unviewed; captions alone do not establish each photographed item's identity. No protected Copper Scroll target was opened. The exact middle receipt ends at306; Müller/Rogers307–318 and separators319–320 belong to the separate continuation reader.

**Additional source locator, p.276 n.199:** Bélis reports a larger calcined fragment found at the bottom of cistern71 in July2001, citing Y. Peleg’s oral communication of November2002. This supplies a field-record retrieval lead. This chapter does not provide its object number, detailed stratigraphic relation or an independently checked date. It cannot date the cistern or establish a concealed textile deposit.
