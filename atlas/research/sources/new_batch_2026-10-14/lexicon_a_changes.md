These are 178 double-page spreads, read visually in full, including the visible citations, examples and lexical notes. The [coverage record](lexicon_a_coverage.json) gives the exact scope. This completes this assigned portion; it does not certify the other reader assignments or independently collate the dictionary's cited manuscripts. ## Changes to how the existing comparisons should be used

- **Deduplicate a literary witness across headwords.** Printed192 (*hurvah*), 212 (*hafar*) and375 (*simah*) cite the same Vayiqra Rabbah 621:2 passage in connection with a ruined building, digging and treasure. These entries furnish several lexical access points to one text. They supply no independent corroboration of three concealment observations. Other treasure examples on375 have their own citations and require separate contextual reading.
- **Keep water and burial senses contextual.** Printed101 differentiates well/pit uses;122 includes pit, cistern and winepress-cavity senses;203 distinguishes a cave/cavity entry with uncertainty in one subdivision;323 includes burial use of a cave and a cave-mouth expression. Printed356 includes a tomb/monument sense under *nefesh*, alongside person/soul senses. These make possible senses available for comparison. They do not select a Copper Scroll reading or identify an archaeological feature.
- **An opening need not be an entrance.** Printed215 supplies a window/opening entry;360 a hole entry;361 a rock-cleft entry;403 distinguishes eye and an oven's draft hole, while404 gives the separate spring headword. Printed431's Greek-derived city-gate/entrance entry includes a cave entrance example (Kohelet Rabbah 26d35). A future argument must establish the relevant lemma and contextual sense before applying any of these to a site plan.
- **A direction or spatial relation needs its own argument.** Printed155 distinguishes south from the southern region around Lydda;274–289 includes outward, outside, inward, staircase and alley/courtyard-entrance expressions;393–394 includes farther-shore and Transjordan uses. Printed406–407 differentiates several prepositional constructions: on/upon, against, concerning, measurement expressions, near/next to and on a bank/shore. The bare gloss cannot determine a measuring origin, wall face or straight route. Printed416's circuit/detour example similarly needs its own context.
- **Keep units and homographs separate.** Printed94's land-area examples cannot be treated as linear measures;254 distinguishes a capacity term from a furnace homograph;257 distinguishes talent and loaf senses;338 gives a general weight term without fixing its mass. Printed364's *seah* references and379's coin *sela* example provide source-specific relations, not a metric conversion for the Copper Scroll. Printed422 gives a tenth-of-ephah term. None establishes which ancient unit system an entry uses.
- **Do not turn a label into a field observation.** Printed289's tower/chest distinction,326's cistern/aqueduct usages,370's ladder/promontory senses,377's threshold entry,385's row-of-stones example,395's basin example and411's literary cistern-depth example are lexical evidence. They do not supply a surveyed threshold, dated conduit, measured basin or excavated junction. Printed375's identifying-mark and mnemonic senses,430's memorandum/slip,431's writing tablets and432's legal deposit term likewise do not establish a second key document or an actual treasure deposit.

These are direct-source controls on vocabulary, provenance and double counting. They add no independently observed installation or deposit location. The Greek-derived terms and later literary witnesses remain dated to their own contexts. Dense unvocalized examples and fine diacritics may need magnified reinspection for a specific proposed reading; no uncertain detail was promoted into an entry identification.
