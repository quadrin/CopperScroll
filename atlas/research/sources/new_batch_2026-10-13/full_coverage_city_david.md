## Observations and limits that affect use of the report

- **Locus identity is area-specific.** Chapter 3, p. 69, says independent area registration reused numbers. A matching locus number does not establish matching archaeological context. Floor and overlying accumulation can share a locus number; channels and their sediments can have different registrations.
- **Excavation coverage and post-excavation records are incomplete.** Chapter 3, pp. 66, 70 n. 1 and 72: some features were not excavated to bedrock or full depth; Area J's field records were lost in a fire; some pottery could not be located for preparation. These are limits on stratigraphic reconstruction and negative evidence.
- **The report's area crosswalk differs from a site-wide label.** Table 3.1, pp. 66–67: Area N is the stepped-pool campaign; Area L concerns refuse and wet sifting; F concerns Channel II and the spring; K 2 concerns street and drainage. These assignments do not supply the requested main-basin L 103 junction section.
- **Channel II's excavation and proposed access are distinct.** Chapter 4, pp. 106–113: the excavated stretch includes natural cavities, a shaft interpreted as possible rock-cutters' access, modern concrete intrusion and a southern obstruction preventing further excavation. Historical water-outlet interpretations are discussed rather than directly established by every opening.
- **Channel II's fill chronology is internally qualified.** Chapter 4 p. 106 uses a first-century BCE date, Chapter 5 p. 127 gives first-century CE, Chapter 6 pp. 155, 159 argues disuse before Herod, while the Area A index pp. 166–167 assigns CE or BCE–CE pottery to several tunnel fills. Preserve these accounts separately; fill dates alone do not date construction.
- **Cooking-pot contexts are deposition observations.** Chapter 6, Table 6.1 p. 136 and pp. 140–142: 28 intact pots occur in three recorded levels; a small lamp was found within one pot (L 9, basket 188). Intentional placement, abandonment and the reason for deposition are separate claims. Chapter 7 pp. 161–164 places its later Augustus and Nero coins in wall-top cleaning and surface contexts respectively, rather than treating them as sealed construction deposits.
- **Area J does not establish one shared wall phase.** Chapter 8 pp. 171–213 describes restricted bedrock exposures, multiple components of W 501/W 502 and unexcavated spaces. It expressly leaves the relative chronology of parallel walls and Channel I unresolved. Reich's discussion and Shukron's fortification interpretation (p. 212 n. 3) remain separate author accounts.
- **Absence of water outlets has a bounded extent.** Chapter 8 p. 181 reports none in the 13 m exposed in Area J and 9 m exposed immediately south. The authors use this in their wider interpretation; it is not a complete inspection of every channel segment.
- **Channel I's construction and fill have different date controls.** Chapter 8 p. 201 and Chapter 10 pp. 224–226 identify the wall-adjacent L 86 fill as the construction-related context. Chapter 11 p. 240 discusses first-century CE pottery within Channel I, which concerns its later fill/use history. The source's use of a terminus ante quem for channel use is retained as its interpretation, not silently converted into a construction date.
- **The readable extraction cannot certify complete tables.** Chapter 9's extracted Fig. 9.1 catalogue ends at row 9 although p. 222 discusses rows 10–11; Fig. 9.2's catalogue jumps from row 10 to row 16. Native table pages are needed to determine whether extraction omitted rows. Chapter 10 pp. 237–238 and Chapter 11 pp. 247–248 also contain figure-number discrepancies requiring native verification.
- **The coin catalogue corrects an earlier absence claim.** Chapter 12 pp. 252–255 records a First Revolt year-2 coin in L 167 (catalogue 32, IAA 121652), despite the 2003 report's assertion that none came from Area J. The author retains a pre-revolt interpretation of the main dump while treating this coin as exceptional. That interpretation and the actual find remain separate. The chapter expressly says its coins do not come from well-defined cooking-pot contexts and cannot date those depositions.
- **A cleared feature does not retain its original deposits.** Chapter 13 p. 273 says Parker–Vincent completely cleared Channel I and Tunnels III–VII; the current campaign found no original deposits there. Under the staircase, modern rebuilding and backfill coexist with small surviving original deposits (pp. 290–291); L 1504 and L 1506 are adjacent, not superposed.
- **An observed threshold does not establish its entire access phase.** Chapter 13 pp. 292–296 describes a threshold at 638.92 masl, a 0.80 m opening, a door-pivot socket and in-situ iron shoe, plus surviving edges of a west-descending staircase. Their contemporaneity is inferred because the original stratified junction deposits did not survive. The current staircase and later vault extensions are distinct alterations.
- **Channel II has explicit unexcavated intervals.** Chapter 13 pp. 300–304 and Fig. 13.58 divide the conduit by excavation campaign. Three unexcavated intervals total an estimated 70–80 m of its estimated 400–420 m length. Surface excavation at the channel/tunnel transition had not reached channel depth when the authors wrote in March 2018 (p. 303). The diagram is a retrieval and exposure map, not proof that every segment was investigated.
- **Repeated names are not geometric descriptions.** Chapter 13 p. 304 n. 3 explains that the conventional “Round Chamber” is neither round nor roofed by rock. A model must use its observed form and access, rather than infer those properties from the retained name.

- **A wall's date can depend on unretained pottery.** Chapter 13 p. 313 n. 5 says the pottery used to discuss W 150's late-eighth-century BCE association was not kept; the reported dating derives from Shukron's on-site reading. His alternative Middle Bronze interpretation and the source's phase discussion must retain that evidential limit.
- **The rising branch at the bifurcation was not fully excavated.** Chapter 13 pp. 325–328 distinguishes the eastern continuation of Channel II from a westward rising approach interpreted as serving a later columbarium. The latter excavation stopped because of collapse danger. Function and chronology are author interpretations based on surviving niches and walls.

- **Channel I retains an unexcavated basal layer.** Chapter 13 pp. 338–341 describes roughly 80 m cleared but only 61 m surveyed, a collapse that halted the Area J approach, and a basal 20–30 cm debris layer left unexcavated. Exterior dating contacts were not reached in this part. Reused inscribed capstones and modern replastering concern later alterations.
- **Tunnel geometry in the older plans is partly stylized.** Chapter 13 pp. 341–347 corrects Tunnel V's course, opening alignment and measured rise; Tunnel VII was only partly cleared and not properly surveyed. An incised line in Tunnel IV is measured at 636.98 masl, but its function as a working datum remains the authors' interpretation.
- **The windows critique retains local alternatives.** Chapter 13 pp. 358–360 argues against a general irrigation-outlet system, while allowing that L 106-2/L 106-3 might be windows leading to a local pool. The proposed original southern destination pool remains undiscovered. Keep the specific opening observations, author alternatives and unexcavated destination separate.
- **Authors differ within the same chapter.** Chapter 13 p. 356 distinguishes Reich's revised two-period interpretation of the pool/chamber from the former single-project account. Shukron's p. 363 addendum separately proposes a raised water level that concealed access through Tunnel III. These are published reconstructions, not independently observed ancient concealment arrangements.

- **Pottery chapters qualify simplified architecture dates.** Chapter 16 pp. 375–379 dates L 1504's threshold-sealing pottery to the late third/early fourth centuries CE and distinguishes the Early Roman L 1501–1502 fill. Chapter 13's table and description differ on some of those period labels. The latest pottery and secondary deposition qualify both construction and blockage inferences; they do not establish a single contemporaneous architectural phase. Chapter 15's extracted catalogue also omits row 10, and its opening/table commentary differs on the number of the possible Persian bowl; native verification remains necessary.

- **Area H supplies use/collapse limits, not its own construction date for the tower.** Chapter 17 pp. 385–400 notes that Square 2 did not reach bedrock and supports obstructed upper sections. The authors explicitly say H did not date tower construction: their Middle Bronze attribution relies on Area C and differs from Regev et al. 2017's ninth-century radiocarbon interpretation. Iron Age deposits between fallen boulders concern later events. Chapter 20 p. 410 also states that Early and Late Roman plates represent pottery from the same mixed loci, rather than separate stratified phases.
- **Area F's index retains internal label conflicts.** Printed pp. 380–382 give L 1506 an Early Roman label despite the Late Roman description on p. 291; the W 104 index reverses eastern/western vault forms relative to Chapter 13 pp. 284–287. These conflicts remain extraction-level observations pending native verification.

- **A proposed cache has an observed fill context but no storage mechanism.** Chapter 23 p. 445 and Chapter 25 pp. 457–459 place 199 coins together in quarry-sealing L 463, just above bedrock (index p. 460). Only 46 were cleaned and 11 identified. The authors propose a hoard/cache label despite the absence of evidence for its storage method. Some catalogue entries swap locus 463 and registration 5075; native verification is required. This new observation does not itself establish intentional concealment or enter the frozen hoard calculations.
- **The quarry's use is bounded more weakly than its sealing.** Chapters 23–25 pp. 445–459 distinguish a quarry cutting an earlier ritual bath from its later filling. Chapter 24 explicitly says the pottery cannot show how long the quarry had operated before sealing. The proposed association with Eudocia's building projects is historical reconstruction, not a sealed construction context. The sixth/seventh-century wording varies between chapters.
- **Area L's sampling is not a locus-by-locus excavation.** Chapter 26 pp. 463–473 describes wet sifting en bloc without issuing locus numbers, a ravine later filled in 2010, and several publications of the same project. The authors interpret differences between L and southern dump coins geographically; Chapter 12's First Revolt coin from J remains a qualification to any general absence claim for the southern dump.

- **Dump fauna is a bounded sample.** Chapter 28 pp.478–489 describes sandbag sampling and redeposition/preservation biases. Its pig absence concerns that sample; interpretation as a representative city-wide diet remains the author's inference. Its chronology ending at 66CE also differs from Chapter 26's coin horizon 67/68CE.
- **Decorated and marked fragments do not establish original storage contexts.** Chapter 29 pp.496,498 places both Middle Bronze scarab impressions in secondary deposits. Chapter 30 p.509 places all figurines except one in mixed debris and rejects deliberate breakage as established; several discussion figure references differ from catalogue captions. Chapter 31 p.516 places its early stamped handle in first-centuryCE dump L 30 and notes two missing locus attributions. Chapter 33's introductory item/locus references differ from the numbered catalogue(pp.523–525); these require native checking rather than silent correction.
- **ChannelII's latest marked handle is Islamic.** Chapter 33 p.523 retracts a formerly Yehud identification; Chapter 37 pp.574–575 places the Arabic-stamped handle in Area A,L 52 ChannelII fill and cites script comparanda dating to the late eighth/ninth centuriesCE. This concerns a later find in fill, not ancient channel construction.
- **Object illustrations and counts need exact provenance.** Chapter 35 p.560 says some amphora handles were misplaced before photography and illustrates those by rubbings. Its introduction's Knidian/Koan/Latin item ranges differ by one from catalogue(pp.553–558). Chapter 40 begins with 19 EarlyRoman weights but its body says 20(pp.580–581); measured and amended masses are separate columns, so reconstructed masses cannot become observed values.
- **The stone-vessel argument has internal qualifications.** Chapter 41 p.589 infers pre-siege dumping from absence of charring, but its catalogues explicitly list soot/charred pieces(pp.591,597,599). Its discussion distinguishes production waste from inferred local workshops and says stone mugs are not calibrated measuring cups(p.586). The extracted AreaH catalogue skips rows 21–22(p.594); native figures/tables remain unavailable. No city-wide dumping date or workshop location follows directly from these fragments.

- **The synthesis retains explicit reconstruction limits.** Chapter 48 p.664 acknowledges that Warren's Shaft System has no surviving archaeological dating evidence; the Middle Bronze attribution follows its proposed relation to the spring. P.673 says the late Iron II blocking action was not discovered. Pp.674–677 retain the southern destination pool as undiscovered and describe the southern conduit date from resemblance to the Siloam Tunnel rather than a new sealed deposit.
- **ChannelI's measured gradient and proposed purpose are distinct.** Chapter 48 pp.677–678 lists south-to-north decreasing basal elevations (Area A 633.65–633.41; Area J 633.17–633.15masl) and says Area F's base was not reached. Its use as an external spirit-level for tunnel cutting is explicitly a hypothesis.
- **The synthesis does not resolve contradictory deposit summaries.** Chapter 48 p.679 describes two stacked cooking-pot groups, while Chapter 6 Table 6.1 has three recorded levels. Its pottery-only absence argument(p.672) is explicitly estimated rather than counted and cannot prove no human activity. Its complete cessation of Greek amphora imports(p.686) is stronger than Chapter 35's qualified later imports. Preserve those chapter-specific accounts.
- **Late blocking has level evidence but uncertain rediscovery.** Chapter 48 p.689 compares the upper mud 643.45masl with vault intrados 643.50masl, combines ceramic absence and historical accounts, and states that the means and reason of reopening are unknown. The Cairo Genizah text is a documentary inference of renewed access, not an excavated reopening context. The medieval route through Warren's Shaft System is the authors' preferred conjecture.
