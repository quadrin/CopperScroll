# Synagogue lane: what the new sources change

This lane covers 19 explicitly assigned sources. `changes.json` records per-source inspection scope. Complete extracted text is available for most items, but the large volumes and image-only browser captures have only partial substantive coverage. Full-volume reading is not claimed.

## Substantive changes

**Ethri now has primary archaeological concealment contexts.** Zissu and Ganor 2009 pp. 96, 99 n. 39 and 102 n. 46 identify separate village-corner denarii groups, a half-shekel in system XIV and a worn Hellenistic coin in a chamber-floor niche. The existing `n_ethri` row derives an unread 2012 publication title; its identity with any 2009 group remains unresolved. Preserve the groups separately and exclude single coins from the frozen hoard comparison. `ethri_evidence.md` gives exact original pages, verbatim extraction, visible captions and a proposed existing-record paragraph. `ethri_proposed_observations.csv` is a proposal, not an applied catalogue amendment.

**Ethri also supplies phase-controlled reuse examples.** P. 95 ties small western underground systems to first-century house construction and First-Revolt abandonment. Pp. 101–103 describe older baths/shafts/reservoirs joined beneath the later public building; p. 104 describes an early hiding/storage chamber reused for burial. The cave's present shape and the coin's mint date require separate phase evidence. These observations improve comparanda and the interpretation of survey features; they do not reopen entry 25's registered site test.

**A negative excavation report needs its stopping level.** Kobrin 2019, Rimmon report text pp. 437–439 of the captured report, says conservation excavation cleared later fills/collapse and generally did not reach original floors. Its adjacent cistern and passageway supply structural observations with uncertain function. An absence of a deposit at this excavation cannot establish absence at an unexcavated ancient target level. Figure 2 is the plan/section, Figs. 3/5/6 the room/passage photographs, and Figs. 7/8 the earlier/later wall relationship.

**Synagogue function remains an interpretation in several reports.** Rapuano 2001 pp. 51–55 explicitly derives its dimensions from published schematic plans, compares different construction stages and notes different entrance arrangements at Qumran and Jericho. Its dimensional similarity supplies exploratory analogy; it does not supply new independently surveyed measurements, a channel datum, or an observed scroll-storage installation. Tel Rekhesh 2019 pp. 134–139 has an observed benched room and northern entrance, while the link to northern farmstead rooms remains unexcavated and the construction date relies partly on lamp production and missing earlier coins. Har-Even 2013 pp. 29–32 provides a preserved threshold, hall benches, nearby bath and site activity ending with First-Revolt coins; the coins in sealing collapse do not independently date every structural element.

**Late building deposits widen context types without dating the Copper Scroll.** Baram, Aviam pp. 159–163 and 169: floor-covered coin groups and a rolled bronze amulet beneath a stone in a later small-synagogue phase; a partially exposed tunnel is proposed as a secret system, with its floor unexcavated. Sumaqa, Dar/Mintzker p. 163: Byzantine coins under L286 slabs and unresolved Phase II function. Current Issues 2021, Stern pp. 225–229: metal-sheet deposits include Maon/Nirim apse-floor finds with competing interpretations and Meroth threshold concealment. This is Maon near Nirim, distinct from the separate Maon-in-Judea article. Stern's Baram paragraph depends on Aviam's excavation, so it must not become independent corroboration.

**A channel's morphology alone does not fix function or date.** Onn 1994 pp. 117–120 describes the north-draining Kafr Misr hall channel under benches/portico/presumed doorway; pp. 126–133 separates early ceramics lacking associated architecture from the later dated synagogue phases. Kohn-Tavor/Arubas 2023 pp. 35–39 likewise distinguish mixed early finds/reused early-looking elements from the extant later building. The proposed early synagogue and the small chamber's treasury function remain interpretations.

Qiryat Sefer, Umdan and Maon-in-Judea are image-only browser captures whose available native screenshots have now all received substantive visual review. Every original-line join has not been certified; `changes.json` gives exact URLs and scopes. Qiryat Sefer supplies primary accounts of existing CHRE8172/8173, not new hoards. Umdan supplies distinct hall/bath phases and partial underground-room reach. Maon supplies an earlier bath and adjacent tunnel, with its Second Temple bath date remaining conjectural. Detailed original findings are in `capture_evidence.md` and `maon_evidence.md`. The Diab capture was visually read across all eight PDF pages: it represents Qadmoniot 151 (2016), pp. 49–53. Its 2023 filename mislabels the publication. It repeats the 2013 Diab report lineage and must not count as independent excavation evidence. An image-only file is not an access denial. The titles/catalogue entries are not treated as inspected article content.

Ancient Synagogues Revealed 1981, Current Issues 2021, Promise and Redemption 1996 and the Bet Shearim 2014 UNESCO nomination have targeted reading only. The Bet Shearim cave inventory/descriptions pp. 25–38 and 58–63 offer courtyard/door/level comparanda principally from later periods, with reuse/damage. They do not supply the missing IV/17 ancient entrance/wall observations. The Wadi Hamam introduction overlaps the regional lane's full volume and is deferred for deduplication.
