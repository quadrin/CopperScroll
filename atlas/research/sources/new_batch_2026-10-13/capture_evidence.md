Date not independently resolved here; later-source bibliographies use1998/1999, so retain the title/page identity rather than silently choosing a year. The Umdan article bibliography links this precise title to Qadmoniot117 pp25–32. The native8displayedpage article is preserved in12PDFscreenshots.

What changes: the two hoards are already catalogue rows chre8172 and chre8173. This article supplies a direct earlier find account and building identities. Add source provenance, not duplicate hoards. P27 reports BuildingIII's central-room bedrock crevice and BuildingV's eastern room; p31 discusses chronology. ## Umm el-Umdan

Actual article: Alexander Onn and Shlomit Weksler-Bdolah, Hebrew Qadmoniot130, pp107–116, cited as2005 by Har-Even2013 bibliography. Every available screenshot visually reviewed. Catalogue year/dating detail can be grounded with the original journal metadata rather than relying on filename.

What changes: explicit phases replace an undifferentiated early hall/bath association. Pp108–110 describe a Seleucid public hall, a later Hasmonean benched hall with modified entrance and possible central installations, and still-later Herodian alterations (pp111–113). P112 places a two-chamber rock-cut immersion installation west of the hall, built during an early stage. The doorway linking its rock-cut chambers is photographed onp114. The hall's early entrance was west, moved east in the Hasmonean arrangement; the Herodian stage added further access and columns. A measurement or orientation taken from one drawing cannot be applied to all stages.

Native passage review:

P110/PDF5, early hall internal installations:

Meaning: two protruding installations were found in the hall centre. The authors discuss a square platform and a circular base; identification of a reading platform and the object once placed on the circular base remain interpretations.

P111/PDF6–7:

Meaning: a developed complex of rock-cut subterranean rooms was found beneath and south of the hall and explored only partly. The reported pottery derives from the earlier Hellenistic phase; the article does not make this a dated treasure hideout. Preserve the partial reach and phase rather than code all voids as concealment chambers.

P115/PDF12–13, destruction/rebuilding: burnt ash and fallen buildingstones are reported in the hall and adjoining buildings; the text cites coins fromyears2and3of the GreatRevolt in destruction debris and a Nero68CE coin in the foundation of the subsequent floor. The authors propose an association with CestiusGallus's66CE campaign. These dates create a chronology tension if all the contextual attributions are accepted literally: year2/3Revolt coins postdate66. Record the proposal as an author event-association requiring the coin/locus details, not a securely fixed66CE destruction date. The successive rebuilt floor and sealed-context attribution need independent report-level verification before chronological scoring.

The source reports later village activity, continued bath use and a BarKokhba year2coin near the bath (p115/PDF13). The authors suggest the hall had already ceased by the time of final village abandonment; exact final event remained uncertain. No new coin cache/hoard is described in this inspected article.
