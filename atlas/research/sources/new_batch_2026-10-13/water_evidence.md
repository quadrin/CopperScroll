## What changes first

Arumah gains individually measured cisterns, entrance spacing, and an observed feed-conduit segment. ## Elmakayes, Ben-Shlomo and Frumkin 2023, Khirbet el-Hamam

Source: [Drive original](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12GFfpipvq1u8Ey1G6XzjcYbjoP8WK0Ka/view). IEJ 73, pp. 56–72. Local hamam.pdf: 18 PDF pages; PDF1 journal cover, printed page = PDF page +54 for PDF2–18. All available text read. All figures screened; Fig11 checked at full page scale. This is a complete article, not the full journal issue.

Recorded observations and published measurements:

- Printed56/PDF2: northern hill A elevation216m, southern hill B246m; site approximately10ha. Printed60/PDF6: inter-hill ramp about40m wide,130m long, approximately2m above natural saddle. The Iron Age date and later Roman widening of the ramp are author hypotheses.
- Printed61–62/PDF7–8, Figs4–6: three northern-slope reservoirs. R1 length12m, width6m, estimated depth6m, shaft4m, floor-to-shaft-top about10m; capacity approximately450m³. R2 length12m, width5.5m, depth6.5m, south opening-to-bottom9.5m; floor unexposed, could lie2m below fill; estimated500m³ including shaft. R3 length14.5m, width6.5m, exposed average depth5.5m; floor fully covered, assumed2m below fill; estimated650m³. Capacities involve fill-depth assumptions.
- Printed63/PDF9: remains of connecting drainage channel visible between R2 and R3. South openings207–208m asl; ramp highest point199m; southern peak quoted248m. RTK GPS/GIS measurements reported in note5. R1 slightly higher than R2/R3. The three reservoirs lie below/outside the summit fortification wall.
- Printed64–66/PDF10–12, Figs7–10: summit depression about10m diameter,4m deep; enclosed plastered access channel0.7–1m wide, described as7m long on p64; main crescent chamber17m long,2.5–3.5m wide, almost4m high in centre and2.5m at filled ends. P65 describes4m of north-descending channel,0.7m triangular opening, built stones approximately0.1×0.2m, deliberately plastered brick blocking wall about1m high, breached now; drop to chamber floor1.5m beyond blockage. The4m and7m descriptions are retained separately; no assumption that they measure the same segment.
- Printed66/PDF12: eastern chamber end has plastered closing wall; western end obscured by soil/rubble. Moist earth observed there on six visits. Capacity340m³ depends on assumed original depth7–8m (note7); access channel contributes approximately7m³. Do not treat340m³ as a fully excavated measured volume.
- Printed67/PDF13 Fig11: one successful radiocarbon determination on innermost earlier plaster in R3; authors summarize roughly200–50BCE. Visual figure reads OxA-41307 R_Date(2151,19), calibrated95.4% interval2145–2001calBP. It directly dates sampled plaster material; extension to initial excavation, all reservoirs and continuous use to the Great Revolt is the authors' contextual inference.
- Printed68/PDF14: summit system has at least three plaster layers, earliest white3–5cm, middle grey up to5cm, outer brown-red with stone inclusions. No absolute construction date for that system is provided.

Author interpretations kept separate:

- Printed63/PDF9: one common fill system inferred from similar south-opening elevations; inverted siphon from southern hill proposed. Footnote6 explicitly says the siphon has not been discovered. This remains hypothetical plumbing.
- Printed64–66/PDF10–12: tower function above summit system and collapsed western original entrance are interpretations of masonry/rubble/moisture; no excavated entrance or tower-to-reservoir construction contact is given.
- Printed67–69/PDF13–15: slope reservoirs assigned Hasmonean origin and Early Roman use by typology, plaster analogy and R3 determination; summit system suggested Iron Age II by form, plaster and fortification position. The summit date is not independently fixed by the R3 sample.

Internal issues requiring preservation:

- Southern hill elevation is246m on p56 versus248m on p63.
- Slope-reservoir earliest white plaster is3–5cm on p62,10–15cm on p67; do not harmonize without determining sample/section identity.
- P61 gives capacity range450–600m³, while detailed R3 estimate on p62 is650m³. Detailed total450+500+650=1600m³.
- Table1 p68 uses nine Arumah reservoirs from2019; the2011 article tabulates ten including a reused winery system. Counts require object-definition and source-lineage reconciliation, not averaging.

Project inference: this source supplies a phase-qualified comparative system and a dated layer; it does not yet enable a Copper Scroll identification. Any future test must freeze the text/phase/axes before using an unused observation. These plans and descriptions are now exposed.

## Zissu and Raviv 2011, Arumah / Kh. el-Urmeh

Source: [Drive original](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1c4OP5OMisHv82SsQBxWrejWMEb8h_53p/view). Hebrew article printed143–158, cover plus16 article pages; printed page = PDF page +141 for PDF2–17. All Hebrew text, notes, appendices and captions read; all imagery screened; AppendixA table checked against rendered page. Translations below are project paraphrases, not published English translations.

Recorded observations:

- Printed143/PDF2: summit843m asl, roughly15dunams; southern saddle connects it with ridge. Printed146/PDF5:2009 partial/hurried survey after mechanical development and looting exposed four further cistern openings beyond earlier survey. This is a damaged-site recording; distinguish surviving and reconstructed boundaries.
- Printed148/PDF7: reservoirs cut in Moza marl below Aminadav dolomite; marl layer10–15m thick. Rectangular/trapezoidal chambers with narrow-side hillside entrance; walls/floors plastered but ceilings not. Horizontal water lines observed. Dating to Hasmonean period relies on plaster/typological analogies.
- Printed153/PDF12 AppendixA: ten individually numbered records with dimensions, heights, capacity estimates and printed grid coordinates. Preserve exact multi-values rather than reducing irregular forms to one rectangle. Cistern3 estimate3500m³, dimensions23/21.5m long,13.5/7m wide, estimated12m high. The ten listed capacities sum14950m³ (project arithmetic), consistent with rounded15000m³ text. The printed mean capacity1650m³ does not equal the mean of all ten; it approximates the mean when150m³ reused-winery record9 is omitted. Definition not supplied, so retain unresolved.
- Printed158/PDF17 AppendixB1–2: western seven entrance approaches1.50–1.60m wide. Adjacent entrance spacing1–2:17.5m;2–3:17.5;3–4:15.5;4–5:17.5;5–6:15.5;6–7:15.5;7–8:32.5;8–9:15.5;9–10:34. The8–9 distance explicitly uses a blocked original8 opening and a presumed9 opening; it is not an observed open-to-open distance.
- AppendixB5: cistern8 original entrance deliberately blocked by about8 courses of fieldstones; present entrance apparently later crudely breached. Two fieldstone terraces divide chamber into three levels. The blocking date is absent.
- AppendixB7/13: record9 connects four Iron Age Gibeon-type wine-storage pits; three circular openings open, fourth western opening blocked. It received water via a winery opening crossed by aqueduct; route through one opening is inferred.
- AppendixB9–12: white hydraulic plaster with small gravel across reservoirs; earlier survey reported two layers. Exposed plastered floors in3/5/6/10. Built/plastered aqueduct sections above3/4/9 and around10. Approximately9m plastered conduit near10 leads water to that reservoir. Figs21–22 printed157/PDF16 show exposed cistern10 opening and feed-channel masonry.
- AppendixB14: reservoir entrances around824m asl, described as approximately15m below summit. P143 summit843m makes a19m arithmetic difference; retain authors' approximate statement rather than silently correcting it.
- AppendixB16 and printed148/PDF7: no main aqueduct remains found on southern saddle/hill; rock-cut channel fragments seen, interpreted as possible feeders. Height deficit at saddle3–4m; embankment, arched aqueduct bridge or inverted siphon suggested, none survives.

Author hypotheses: two further cisterns suggested from wide32.5m/34m entrance gaps (AppendixB3–4); neither observed. Southern-hill runoff collection and missing bridge/siphon inferred from total storage and topography, not established by in situ conduit.

Source issues: AppendixA reverses coordinate-axis order for rows8–10 relative to1–7. Preserve printed x/y verbatim and flag for site-plan normalization; do not map them directly as homogeneous OIG pairs. Exact surveyed entrance orientation, phase-specific thresholds, datum uncertainty and construction contacts remain unestablished.

Project inference: Arumah becomes a usable exploratory control for a cistern-plus-conduit motif and deliberate blocked openings. It supplies no basis for concluding that either motif is rare. The measured gap-based hidden-cistern suggestions illustrate predictions that would require later independent observation; the suggestions are not found structures.

## Frumkin 2014, bedrock water tunnels

Source: [Drive original](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1L4hxL3Mngn6SvP7zTatsgHJ2_CHeWFXN/view). Cura Aquarum in Israel II, article printed43–52. Supplied14-page PDF includes4 preliminary leaves; printed page = PDF page +38 for PDF5–14. All available text/references read, all imagery screened, Table1 native rendered page checked. This file is the complete article plus preliminaries, not the entire proceedings.

Observed/surveyed geometry reported:

- Printed45/PDF7: ICRC surveys used tape, compass and inclinometer;5 systems combine new and published surveys. Horizontal deviation D near excavating-team junction measured against between-shaft surface separation S; angular deviation arctan(D/S). This is a study of construction meeting error, not uncertainty of modern plan registration or Copper Scroll distance instructions.
- Printed46/PDF8 Fig3: Armon Hanatziv tunnel402m,6 vertical shafts,24–135m apart, up to40m deep. Approximate1st-centuryBCE date cited from earlier studies. Friable chalk/later modification obscure original toolmarks/junctions; deviations of a few tens of centimetres may have disappeared.
- Printed46–48/PDF8–10 Figs4–5: Dahr Baku approximately500m;460m surveyed because central section mud/water-blocked.11 shafts, vertical height1.9–33m; inclined shaft lengths up to48m. Text says33–51m spacing, Table1 lists29.8–55.6m; definitions/coverage need reconciliation. Meeting deviations up to10.4m. Excavation directions inferred from toolmarks.
- Printed49/PDF11 Fig6: Jisr ez-Zarqa442m,15 shafts, vertical6.6–13.4m deep. Text interval11.5–37.4m and maximum horizontal deviation0.9m. Table1 printed48/PDF10 includes40.1m interval17–18 and deviation0.6m, angular error0.9degrees. Do not copy prose0.9m as a verified table value.
- Printed49–51/PDF11–13 Figs7–8: upper Taninim systems mostly inaccessible through mud/water; shafted spring/aquifer collectors. Roman date probable, not securely fixed. Text describes few-metre-deep inclined stepped shafts30–50m apart and deviations commonly up to8m. Table1 contains En Ami4–5 distance45.3m, horizontal deviation20.9m and angle24.8degrees; retain the exception.
- Printed50/PDF12/Table1 printed48/PDF10:21 vertical-shaft segments mean0.04±0.19degrees;15 inclined segments mean7.7±5.9degrees. Values belong to this selected construction sample; they cannot be adopted as search tolerances around a preferred candidate.

Author interpretations: vertical versus inclined shaft geometry attributed as major source of direction-transfer error; two plumb-lines suggested for vertical shafts; stepped inclined shafts possibly preferred for maintenance. Specific ancient instruments remain inferred. Toolmark interpretation gives excavation direction, which must remain separate from hydraulic flow direction.

Project inference: freeze geometry conventions separately for walking distance, conduit path length, horizontal straight distance, wall-face offset and shaft projection. A generic straight-line fit can misdescribe a system with team-junction detours. This study does not license widening a test tolerance after seeing a miss, and does not supply Siloam's main-pool junction or Atara's hydraulic gradient.

Author interpretations / disputes:

- Draft1/25–29: Hasmonean founding of Lower Aqueduct supported historically while absolute archaeological proof acknowledged absent. Herodian founding of Upper Aqueduct around25BCE proposed from palace need, early segments, and possibility that AgrippaI section is a rebuilding. Date the respective observed segments first; neither argument automatically dates all channels or missing joins.
- Draft20 n89: Amit proposes original Upper Aqueduct on bridge near775m; Gibson proposes pressure-siphon connection across station depression because bridge remains are absent. Neither proposed infrastructure is observed there. Elevation transfer needs a hydraulic profile and phase-coexistence check before either reconstructed connection can be treated as functioning.
- Draft25 quotes23cubits as approximately30m. This conversion is internally incompatible with ordinary cubit scales and must not enter project metrology. Preserve the textual statement and separately sourced elevations; verify original cited rabbinic passage before quantitative use.
- Secondary-use legion inscriptions/tiles date their respective construction phases; recycled material, displaced channel lines, replastering and later floor deepening complicate the original-system reconstruction.

Project inference: the new archival illustrations are useful source observations and exact-file routes; their published appearance is now exposure. They improve phase and relation controls, but provide no Atara gradient or Siloam main-pool junction. A future candidate comparison must use a common contemporaneous floor/channel datum and verify connectedness rather than connecting mapped fragments by visual proximity.
