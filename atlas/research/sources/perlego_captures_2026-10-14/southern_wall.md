## What changes against the baseline

Those are source-association proxies, not coordinates of an excavated tomb, digging surface or terminal portico feature. The new report supplies excavation-scale observations that must remain separate from those proxies. The phase 1 summary already distinguishes the Zadok sequence from a tentative Temple-precinct cluster. Its sequence cannot date a chamber or identify a southern corner.

The reviewed feature-workbench assessment JSON has no individual 12/12a/51/53 assessments. This report therefore supplies exploratory evidence for those questions, rather than reopening a registered terminal result. No grade, marker or outcome change follows from its numerical comparisons.

Existing regional unit `ATQ85-097-coins-excavations-near-triple-gate-temple` records Ariel 2016, pp. 97–100: 41 coins, 22 identified, mostly mixed contexts, no eligible feature or hoard. Different totals require source/campaign/catalogue reconciliation; author and general area alone do not make them independent corroboration or prove catalogue identity.

## Datum and excavation reach

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 16 pp. 222–224:** western foundation bedrock is 710.06 m asl at C1/C2 and 709.18 m asl at C3; the first-century street is 715.35 m asl. **Direct capture, Fig. 16.4, p. 221:** L14049/C1 and L14050/C2 carry 710.06; L14051/C3 carries 709.18. The full primary capture retains the north arrow and 0–5 m bar; the enlarged version clips some peripheral drawing. Fig. 16.6, p. 222, shows the stepped wall footing and labels the drawing's viewing direction west, although the supplied caption calls it east.

**Our arithmetic:** street minus these local bedrock levels gives 5.29 m and 6.17 m respectively. The extraction's cubit interval 0.45–0.52 m gives nine cubits  4.05–4.68 m and sixteen cubits  7.20–8.32 m. Neither local subtraction matches those intervals. These comparisons cannot exclude a Scroll entry without an independently established ancient digging surface, endpoint, phase and reading. Do not convert them into a new fitted origin or enlarge frozen tolerances.

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 16 p. 224:** work stopped beneath a roof at 712.81 m asl and left approximately 2.5 m above it unexcavated. **Our arithmetic:** street-to-roof is 2.54 m, with 2.75 m and 3.63 m respectively between roof and local bedrock. A photograph or section of the low foundation cells therefore cannot establish a negative search through the entire street-to-bedrock volume.

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 3 p. 48; Ch. 27 p. 386:** the southeastern bedrock is 695.85 m asl; the Grand Course base/internal vault-floor datum is 725.11 m asl at the corner and 725.42 near the Triple Gate. The report supplies no secure exterior Herodian street elevation at the exact southeastern corner. Subtracting 725.11 − 695.85 would join an interior/architectural datum to a foundation point and does not measure the requested exterior digging depth. Area A floors 714.76 m and proposed lower route 714.49 m are other positions; later 715.30/715.90 m floors are other phases. The report's Area B 705.05 m excavation-limit statement conflicts with its later reached-bedrock wording. A surveyed level register and sections must reconcile these before a complete excavation-reach claim.

## Chronology and source disagreement

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 25 pp. 353–357 versus Ch. 27 pp. 421–422 and Ch. 38 pp. 602–603:** Baruch/Reich use coins in L55's filled bath as a lower date boundary for the wall, favouring construction after Gratus issues 17/18 CE. Hagbi/Uziel argue that the fill and paving abut an already standing wall and date the later street/support construction instead. Their 2×10 m foundation excavation distinguishes quarrying, wall construction and the filled-cell system; it does not directly date the wall. Preserve these as competing contact interpretations. Resolve the physical sealing relationship before transferring a fill's latest coin to the wall.

**Direct capture, Table 33.1, pp. 514–515:** combined RTD-11170.1/.2 beneath the lowest wall course gives a 95.4% calibrated interval 44 BCE–70 CE; its 68.3% alternatives are 36–14 BCE,4–28 CE and 45–58 CE. That range retains several first-century construction possibilities. It does not choose a prefect or a specific decade. The extraction reports the authors' conclusion that the precise construction date remains unresolved. Ch. 33's methods were outside the supplied reading scope, so sample selection, treatment and combination assumptions remain unreviewed here.

**Direct capture, Table 33.1:** cistern RTD-10670 and its internal wall RTD-10673 have different ages and contexts. The wall's 68.3% interval 49 BCE–8 CE dates its sampled construction/repair material, not the initial cistern cut. The combined middle-channel dates 665–710 CE and 720–774 CE at 95.4% concern a later wall groove. Do not transfer them to the initial enclosure corner or to all channels along its wall.

## Structures, function and identifiers

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 4 pp. 72–76, Figs. 4.5–6/11–13:** L6124 is an incompletely characterized rock-cut space below C10, next to the possible bath L6125. Its entrance is partly blocked. It has no reported full interior dimensions, burial assemblage or demonstrated funerary use. **Direct capture, Fig. 4.11 p. 75:** labels L6124/L6125 and W673/W663 locate the exposed space beside the stepped installation; the photograph leaves the cavity's interior outside the view. The image cannot turn that space into Zadok's tomb.

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 4 pp. 79–87:** C17/C18 are ramp-support compartments with differing rock levels, approximately 3.1×3.1 m interiors and incomplete closure/excavation relationships. Opposing 0.1 m cup marks atop W662/W668 have no demonstrated column-base function. L6127 is a narrow wall-foundation cutting. **Direct captures, Figs. 4.18–19 pp. 82–83:** the plan and broken section locate B1/C1 and distinguish wall L656/W661 and successive deposits; they do not supply a full bedrock-to-street section at the exact corner.

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 16 pp. 219–235:** C1–C3 at the southwest are intentionally filled support cells, with earlier quarrying/depressions beneath. The 2×10 m excavation did not find baths or basements like those farther north. Northern L55/L71/L91 and the later cemetery beneath Robinson's Arch cannot be imported into these southern cells. **Direct capture, Fig. 27.36 p. 416:** street paving, wall contacts and L19 fill belong to the illustrated northern section, not automatically the exact corner excavation. A visual contact alone does not decide when every deposit accumulated.

**Extraction-reported, Ch. 38 pp. 603–604 and Ch. 27 p. 393:** the southern portico setting derives partly from Josephus and fallen architectural fragments. These passages give no surveyed terminal exedra, in-situ pillar over a tomb or eastern/western endpoint locus. Ch. 27 pp. 408–409 and Ch. 38 p. 606 discuss a much later cemetery/building reconstruction; it supplies no first-century Zadok identification. The supplied chapters identify no burial under an entry 53 rock feature.

**Source conflicts to preserve:** Fig. 16.3 calls cells Phase 3 and Fig. 16.4 calls bedrock Phase 1, reversing the narrative sequence (Ch. 16 pp. 219–220); Ch. 25 p. 354 also uses the reversed upper-fill label. These require source verification, not silent normalization.

**Direct catalogue captures, Ch. 11 pp. 177–178:** coin 4 is A/6112; coin 11 is A/6112; coin 12 is A/6115.
