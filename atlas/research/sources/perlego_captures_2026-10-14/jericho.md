## Archaeological deltas and limits

Butler pp.58–62/r.72–76 reportedly describes tombs revealed by latrine digging among camp dwellings and one shaft filled when excavation threatened a house. This adds a process/coverage qualification to the broad description that the camp covers the strip. The exact episodes lack tomb identifiers and mapped positions. They cannot establish that the skipped strip was systematically searched, identify an ancient pit mouth there or show contemporaneous access to pit and graves.

Taha p.277/r.291 supplies follow-up leads outside a demonstrated SULTAN-1 footprint: Jisr Abu Ghabush's 1994 Early Roman stepped cistern (2.88 × 2.70m; steps descend 1.42m); Suwwanet eth-Thiniya's Early Roman domestic/bath context, stated 1.5km north of Tell es-Sultan; a 2002 late-Hellenistic tomb on Nueima's north bank west of Tell el-Mafjar; and the 2010 eth-Thiniya tomb north of Tawaheen es-Sukkar. The text does not map these to the immediate north-foot–250m strip. The Jisr distance is from Jericho generally, not explicitly the tell. The eth-Thiniya comparison date, mid-first-century BCE–first-century CE, belongs to Tulul Abu el-Alayiq comparanda and must not become this tomb's direct date. Taha's Suwwanet overview dates (1998,2010 at p.270) differ from the detailed 1997 account (p.277); retain the discrepancy.

Nigro p.183/r.197 describes north-west limestone caves converted into Early Bronze tombs. It supplies neither a survey of undated cavities in the target strip nor a first-century accessible-use date. AreaF is on the northern plateau; AreaC lies north of TrenchI, on the western tell slope; AreaL is the north-west fortification corner. Those relative descriptions must not relocate their Bronze Age features beyond the tell's northern foot. The report preserves an EIII–IV/HIII–IV prose/figure-label discrepancy (Nigro pp.183,186), and a TombD.641 prose/caption MB dating discrepancy (pp.199–200). None supplies a Roman pit–grave phase tie.

Jennings pp.260–263/r.274–277 groups the 1999 walking-survey ceramics into broad Hellenistic/Roman versus Byzantine/early Islamic bins after finding some original counts doubtful. Zone8 material therefore supplies no narrow c.200 BCE–70 CE dating of a specific strip feature. His historical-image description of Zone8's sparse vegetation is already exposed as derivative text; it cannot be reused as an unseen prediction about Dalman fig.71.

Arce's proposed Mafjar fort and streams (pp.168–170; fig.18.1 at168) are interpretations of 2014 geophysics, with alternative functions still open. They supply neither an excavated Early Roman fort in the target strip nor a location for the literary 68 CE camp. Mafjar's subterranean hall (Whitcomb p.250) belongs to an Umayyad interpretation. Its presence must not become a natural-cavity observation at Tell es-Sultan.
