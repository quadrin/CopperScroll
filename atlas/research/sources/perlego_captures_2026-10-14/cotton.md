## Concrete additions

**Numerical function requires a relation.** The extraction reports P.Se'elim 4's age column beside names, including κε = 25 and ξζ = 67, with the age word preserved or restored (pp. 181–182; repeated pp. 375–377). Its layout pairs name and age columns and indents subordinate names by slightly over 1 cm. Cotton interprets households and male service obligations; these interpretations remain distinct from the reported layout. This supplies another function control for the existing inventory-role comparison: an explicit age heading and row alignment support a number role. It supplies no equivalence to a Copper Scroll letter group. The same papyrus in two essays supplies one documentary witness, with no additional independence from the 2022 reprint.

**An appended abbreviation has its own formula and uncertainty.** At p. 125 n. 48 the extraction reports Cotton's tentative expansion of the ending of P.Yadin 18 line 76 as a subscriber's title, with a raised abbreviation stroke. Cotton reportedly used a photograph because the feature does not appear in the cited edition's Plate 19. The supplied folder has no photograph. Preserve the proposed expansion, disagreement over the witness reading and missing image together. This is a retrieval lead for a specific subscription, without an established inventory-label function or a Scroll-group match.

**Authentication has several physical forms.** The extraction describes tied double deeds, outer text, signatures on the verso beside stitching and orientation dependent on the languages of deed and signature (pp. 150–153). The reported examples P.Yadin 11, 12 and 20 have different Greek/Semitic witness combinations. P.Hever 60, a receipt dated 125 CE, reportedly ends with an issuer's Aramaic authentication; Cotton distinguishes the person commissioning the writing from the scribe (pp. 155–156). The purported seal formula in P.Yadin 25 also survives without an attached seal (pp. 157–158). These controls separate carrier, placement, writer, authorizer and function; a short addition alone cannot assign any of them to the Scroll.

The cross-signature examples are temporally remote: P.Mich. XI 607 is Egyptian, 569 CE; the Ravenna examples belong to the sixth or early seventh century; Irene's novella belongs to 797–802 CE (pp. 120–122). Keep them outside any first-/second-century Judaean comparison population unless a new exploratory scope explicitly includes them.

**The census comparison table is modern.** The reported p. 370 table compares P.Yadin 16 and P.Hever 62 with a Roman census form. It cannot establish the ancient papyri's table layout. The documents reportedly enumerate plots with names, area, tax and abutters, in 127 CE (pp. 369–374). Amounts written in words, local units and mixed cash/in-kind liabilities establish accounting components. They provide no Scroll unit calibration or mass total. P.Mur 89–107 are called probable tax-receipt lists at p. 375; the extraction supplies no separator, subtotal or grand-total observations for them.

## Conflicts and dates to preserve

The extraction reports a minimum of 49 persons in P.Se'elim 4 while its cited component minima, 14 + 13 + 21, sum to 48 (p. 376). Preserve the discrepancy as a **report-level verification target**. The supplied extraction cannot establish whether Cotton's page, its transcription or the accounting scope explains it. Neither number is an ancient total line, a complete register denominator or a regional population count.

P.Se'elim 4 has no preserved exact date or place. The first-half-second-century attribution and compilation shortly before the second revolt depend on Cotton's reconstruction and a conditional refugee-deposition argument (pp. 181–182, 375–377). It cannot become a securely dated pre-70 comparator or date the Scroll.

For 4Q348, the extraction reports a first-century BCE palaeographic attribution in the 1999 paper (p. 136 nn. 38–39) and Eshel's 46/47 CE attribution in the 2003 paper (p. 176 n. 15). Keep both attributed datings. They concern one documentary witness; editorial/reprint dates create no independent dating observation.

For P.Mur 29 and 30, the reported reassignment to the First Revolt combines Jerusalem as contracting place, formulae, palaeography, coin parallels and qualified radiocarbon evidence (pp. 130–134; reiterated p. 176). Absence of the leader's name is expressly insufficient because a securely Second Revolt document also omits it. The extraction's 67/69 CE conversions are its inference from revolt-year numbering; it reports no exact Julian equivalents printed by Cotton. Hebrew documentary usage during both revolts, coexisting languages and the 4Q348 exception preclude an exclusive language-based Scroll date. The selected passages provide no direct Scroll/Mishnaic-Hebrew comparison.
