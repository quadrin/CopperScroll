## Inspected source observations

Adam Zertal and Shay Bar, *The Manasseh Hill Country Survey, Volume IV: From Nahal Bezeq to the Sartaba*, copyright 2017: printed pp.454–456 / source PDF465–467, Figs.325–328; p.470 / PDF481, Figs.335–336; p.471 / PDF482. Direct page-image inspection.

- Figure325 presents the lower aqueduct and siphon, with contours, a north arrow and 50 m scale. It is a 2001 survey plan; its caption labels the siphon Hellenistic. The caption supplies no independent dated construction contact.
- Page455 reports an approximately 250 m siphon, covered 60 × 60 cm channel, approximately 2 m base and several construction phases interpreted possibly as repairs. The authors explicitly leave multiple channels unproven pending excavation.
- Page455 reports a 20 × 7 m expansion beside the western part of the siphon. An internal wall divides it into unequal parts; its function remains pool **or structure**. Thus it cannot be treated as an identified reservoir with a dated rim or floor.
- Page456 states the eastern connection was cut by a modern unpaved road; survey-area sherds are 70% Hellenistic and 30% Early Roman. Those proportions date an assemblage, not the siphon fabric, expansion or reference surface.
- Figure327 is an aerial photograph looking east (2013); Figure328 shows a cistern (1997). Neither identifies the reservoir or deposit point required by a Copper Scroll entry.
- Figure335 reproduces Abel's 1910 measured plan; Figure336 reproduces his summit plan. These are historical observations reproduced in a later survey, not two new survey campaigns by the present authors.
- Page471 summarizes excavations on the fortress's eastern side: Hasmonean, Herodian and first-century-CE occupation, including a cistern and small pool. It says the excavations have not been published in full. The page does not connect those phases stratigraphically to the saddle siphon or the divided expansion.

Günter Garbrecht and Yehuda Peleg, “The Water Supply of the Desert Fortresses in the Jordan Valley,” *The Biblical Archaeologist*57(3)(1994), printed pp.168–169 / supplied PDF9–10, reinspected as images. This source was already inspected previously and adds no unique source intake.

- Alexandrion/Sartaba collects runoff around Ras Kuneitra and carries it across a saddle to cisterns. The 1994 account reports a 190 m siphon with 0.60 × 0.80 m interior channel. Manasseh IV reports approximately 250 m and 0.60 × 0.60 m. Different extents or measurement definitions could explain these differences; no common endpoint/section specification is supplied here. Do not average the values or infer architectural alteration from the disagreement.
- The page169 composite section distinguishes existing remains and a reconstructed upper channel, attributed to Amit1989. The proposed construction stages are explicitly probable.
- The article's table gives cistern storage at Sartaba, Doq, Cypros, Hyrcania, Herodion, Masada and Machaerus. This defeats an inference that fort-associated aqueduct/cistern types alone uniquely select Hyrcania. It does not test the full entry-specific geometry.

## Bounded check

Hypothesis: possession of fort-associated aqueducts and reservoirs/cisterns uniquely supports Hyrcania's entry16/29/35 association.

Result: rejected to the generic-feature scope. Sartaba independently supplies such features. Distinctive entry wording, northern-reservoir identity, distances from a specified inlet and a phase-specific reference surface remain necessary. No four-side offset or depth calculation is justified for the 20 × 7 m expansion: its function, usable ancient perimeter, hydraulic connection and phase remain unresolved.

The inference concerns diagnostic value of shared feature types; it does not reject Hyrcania as a site candidate and assigns Sartaba no probability or rank.
