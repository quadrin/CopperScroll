## Observations and author interpretations

**Complete plan, p. 100:** The unnumbered plan of the Hasmonean palace shows both large rectangular pools and their full boundaries. Its caption assigns the pair to label 4 in the Pool Complex, with surrounding paved areas label 7 and pavilion label 6. Label 3 designates a different pair west of the central palace building. A north arrow and scale are present. This is a general complex plan; it supplies no target-specific preserved inlet, vertical section, inlet elevation or dated hydraulic junction. Complete pool outlines are now available in an original publication by the excavation director, while the later Trümper Figs. 20–22 remain cropped as previously recorded.

**Dimensions and arrangement, p. 101:** Netzer describes the pair northwest of the central palace as approximately **18 × 13 m** each, separated by an approximately **4 m strip**. A paved area and columns surrounded the new complex. The northern candidate is one of this pair. These dimensions identify genuinely unequal sides and support using an 18 m side as the longer-side branch of our model. They do not make the scroll’s damaged large-side expression wholly preserved or select which longer side/face starts the measurement.

**Supply context, pp. 98 and 101:** The text describes two regional aqueduct supplies and a later aqueduct carrying Na’aran spring water. Near the palace, the latter divided into two estate routes; the southern route crossed the palace area. This broad supply account locates the pair in a hydraulic landscape. It does not independently identify the central inter-pool line sampled in the 2018 figures as a surviving inlet of the northern pool. Other pipes and small pools described around pp. 99–101 belong to other installations and are excluded from the target’s inlet model.

**Rebuilding context, p. 104:** Netzer associates Herod’s second palace with rebuilding of the Hasmonean complex after earthquake damage, attributed in this account to 31 BCE. This is the historical/construction narrative surrounding the changes, rather than an independently dated contact for the joining operation.

**Joined-pool plan, p. 105:** The upper plan is explicitly the complex of Herod’s second palace. Its caption label 2 identifies the two Hasmonean swimming pools as joined by Herod into one pool. A lower, distinct plan concerns Herod’s third palace; it is excluded from this pool comparison.

**Joining and revised interpretation, p. 106:** Netzer describes removal of a strip between the two pools, approximately 2 m wide in this account, to create one approximately **32 × 18 m** basin surrounded by a garden. The footnote states that an earlier interpretation assigned the already joined pool to the Hasmonean period; further research instead established two pools first and their subsequent joining. Keep the p. 101 approximately 4 m separation and p. 106 approximately 2 m removed strip as separately reported measurements. Their wall/surface datums are unspecified here, so they cannot be reconciled by silently changing either number.

## Effect on entry 29

The general plan and reported 18 × 13 m dimensions reduce uncertainty about the early pool outline and the longer-side orientation. They add no new measured inlet offset. Our frozen phase-3/5 source-plan calculations remain unchanged: inner-face width in those picks is about 13.6 m, which is broadly compatible with the rounded published 13 m width but does not independently validate their channel identification or accuracy.

The joining creates a specific phase problem for interpreting the scroll as a **separate northern member of this pair**. The published later arrangement contains one basin across both earlier footprints. For a proposed first-century CE concealment/use model, earlier Hasmonean construction alone no longer suffices as evidence that the separate northern reservoir and original side datum remained available. An earlier instruction date, use of a surviving northern portion, or historical persistence of a name would each require its own evidence; none is established here.

This conflict applies to unqualified survival of the separate-pool arrangement. Jericho and the northern footprint remain possible comparisons. The source does not date the scroll instruction or all later pool alterations. The candidate remains low-confidence and inconclusive, with a strengthened, explicit phase requirement. The 24/27-cubit uncertainty and control results are unchanged. The merged basin remains a separate potential northern-reservoir hypothesis: a reservoir may be northern relative to the complex without being one of a preserved pair. Its reported 32 × 18 m dimensions also change which sides are longer, rotating that reference-axis branch relative to the earlier 18 × 13 m pools. The frozen earlier-pool offset results cannot be transferred to that merged-basin model.
