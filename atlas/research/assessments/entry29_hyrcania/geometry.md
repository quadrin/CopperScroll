# Hyrcania: northern-pool offset and phase review

Reviewed 2 October 2026. This review reuses Patrich 1989 and Garbrecht/Yehuda Peleg 1994, previously inspected sources. It visually rechecks Patrich pp. 255–256 and 258; it adds a conditional source-image offset model, not a new excavation observation or geographic registration.

## Result

Under Eshel’s proposed entry-29 model, measure 24 cubits north of the northern member of the double pool. At 0.4–0.6 m per cubit the locus lies 9.6–14.4 m from the selected datum. The source does not establish whether that datum is the rim, centre, or another named point. Figure 22 supplies a 50-m bar but no visible north arrow. Text identifies the double pool north of bridge 48 and separate filled pool south of it; this supports a northern sector, not an independently validated azimuth.

The JSON gives two explicitly provisional page-up loci: one from the pool’s approximate centre and one from its northernmost rim. The source-image datum separation is about 11 m in that direction, comparable to the whole proposed offset. A centre model approaches the northern rim; a rim model falls beyond it on the adjoining ridge/terrace. They cannot be merged into one digging point. No depicted installation uniquely selects either locus. The circular cistern east/northeast of the pool is a competing nearby feature, but the model does not establish that it lies on a north bearing or at the required distance.

Pixel picks are illustrative, with approximately 15-pixel picking uncertainty and additional unquantified orientation/scan error. They use the full archived 1488×2033 image, top-left origin, x right/y down. They establish reproducible hypotheses for revision once direction and datum are settled; they do not warrant WGS84 coordinates.

## Dimensions and archaeological phases

Patrich p. 255 gives the northern pool’s four sides: south 19 m, west 15 m, north 18.2 m, east 16 m, depth reaching about 5 m; the southern member reaches about 2.6 m. Quarried rock 4.8–5.4 m separates them. Text calls the general form quadrangular and notes unequal sides and uneven bottom/wall surfaces. Hydraulic plaster containing lime and crushed sherds is described as characteristic of the Second Temple period. This is a material/typological assignment, not a sealed threshold or construction date. Benches above the northern basin support Patrich’s swimming interpretation; they do not identify a scroll datum.

These basin side lengths address an alternative side-length interpretation. They do not test a 24-cubit distance north of the pool. Earlier perimeter/side failures must remain conditional on those different readings.

The distinct southern pool is filled, lacks exposed lining at the published depth, and may have served a moat; retain it as a separate, poorly dated candidate. Figures 26–27 p. 258 show cisterns S and F with steps and 2-m scales. They are other installations, not detail plans of the double pool.

Garbrecht and Yehuda Peleg, pp. 167–168, describe bridge superstructure 112 m long and 16.80 m high, two masonry phases possibly Hasmonean and Herodian, four lower southern cisterns followed by twelve higher ones, and three unroofed pools approximately 18×15×5 m. This generalized treatment differs from Patrich’s individually unequal pool dimensions and depths. It cannot independently assign 5 m to the shallow southern basin. The discussion supplies no dated pool-edge datum. The authors’ middle-column photograph of an inverted siphon concerns Alexandrion, not Hyrcania; do not import that engineering feature into this candidate.
