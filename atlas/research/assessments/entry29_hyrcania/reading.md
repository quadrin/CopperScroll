# Hyrcania: reading and feature-identity audit

2 October 2026. Contribution to a conditional candidate assessment. Sources reused; no additional inspection KPI proposed. Current GitHub AGENTS.md and OPEN_QUESTIONS.md were read before this pass.

## Result

Hyrcania supplies three distinct proposed models for entries 16, 29 and 35. Their required installations and starting datums differ. The most immediate geometric test is Eshel's entry-29 hypothesis: a position 24 cubits north of the northern member of the double pool west of the fortress. This remains edition-dependent and does not establish an identifiable deposit point.

Two earlier numerical checks have narrower scope than a test of Eshel's models. Comparing 24 cubits with pool side lengths/perimeter does not test his northward offset. Comparing entry 16 with the surviving 11.5 m of covered passage 49 does not test his proposed trail–northern-aqueduct intersection.

## Entry 16, IV 3–5

Eshel explicitly states that the destination is lost and that restoring Hyrcania assumes geographical order in the list. His original wording identifies the northern aqueduct and places the proposed measuring origin at the point where the trail meets that aqueduct west of Hyrcania. It does not identify the covered passage at station 49. Source: Hanan Eshel, “Aqueducts in the Copper Scroll,” in George J. Brooke and Philip R. Davies (eds.), *Copper Scroll Studies*, supplied 2004 paperback of the 2002 publication, printed pp. 102–103, PDF pp. 119–120. These actual page images were checked in this pass.

Required feature: a demonstrated trail/channel intersection on the northern aqueduct, with ancient channel direction, trail phase and measuring axis. The surviving aqueduct is archaeological evidence independent of the scroll. The assignment of this entry to it depends on the restored destination and sequence argument. No trail-intersection control or ancient surface has been established here.

Retain 14/40/41-cubit and horizontal-distance/depth branches from the existing edition record. At the project's sensitivity range 0.445–0.525 m/cubit these give 6.23–7.35 m, 17.80–21.00 m, and 18.245–21.525 m. These ranges do not identify a channel point by themselves. Passage 49 remains a separate project comparison, not Eshel's published datum.

## Entry 29, VII 3–7

Eshel proposes Kidron, hence the southern aqueduct of Hyrcania, and identifies the reservoir as the larger northern pool north of the bridge west of the summit. He explicitly concludes with a 24-cubit northward offset. His preceding discussion retains a left/north ambiguity. Source: same chapter, printed pp. 103–105, PDF pp. 120–122; actual images of pp. 103 and 105 checked; p. 104 text extracted.

Required feature: the northern basin in the double-pool group; the necessary supplying channel connection and reference edge remain to be identified. Do not substitute the smaller southern basin, the filled pool south of the bridge, northern intake dam 44 or a generic summit cistern. Patrich's independent surveyed plan and feature descriptions establish that these installations are distinct: “אמות המים להורקניה,” *Ancient Aqueducts in the Land of Israel* (1989), pp. 255–256, Fig. 22 p. 256, previously read and archived.

Geometry branches should remain explicit: cardinal-north offset, leftward offset defined by a specified travel direction, and a Puech long-side model. A cardinal-north offset requires a reference point or boundary along the pool. A 24-cubit locus north of a whole basin produces an area/line family, not a unique point. No inlet or wall corner can become the starting origin simply because a numerical fit improves.

Puech 2015 printed p. 64 notes 252–258 moves the large-word to line 5, disputes the left/north restoration, retains a restored measuring origin, and supplies Jericho as a restoration. His apparatus says Eshel's reconstructed reservoir line is too short and suggests that accepting the Kidron proposal would require a longer Hyrcania-restored qualification. This is a competing editorial spatial model. The existing image-checked Puech pp. 62–65 assessment and extracted p. 64 were reread here; no new manuscript inspection is claimed. See the [existing reading note](https://github.com/quadrin/CopperScroll/blob/main/research/assessments/entry29_jericho_pools/reading-note.md).

The independent archaeological existence of a northern pool supports feature availability. Eshel's proposal associates that feature with the scroll; it is not a second archaeological observation. Eshel's pool illustration reproduces Patrich, so those two figures cannot supply independent survey confirmation. Chronological compatibility and a phase-specific surface remain separate requirements.

## Entry 35, VIII 8–9

Eshel's dam interpretation locates this entry at the start of Hyrcania's southern Kidron aqueduct (printed p. 102/PDF 119, image checked). Under the existing Patrich inventory that is intake station 1, distinct from station 42 and northern-system station 44. Cairn versus dam, gorge versus peak, and Kidron versus pottery readings change the landmark class and site association. Retain these from the existing reading record; This worker did not freshly image-check Puech 2015 pp. 72–73 or Lefkovits p. 262; the integrator separately inspected Puech pp. 69 and 73. See [entry 35 edition supplement](puech-entry35.md).

Required feature under Eshel: southern intake dam plus a defined digging datum and ancient construction/use phase. Under cairn: a documented mound near the specified Kidron landmark; neither the dam nor a modern rubble heap supplies that observation. No exact digging point is defensible in this pass.

## Confidence and next discriminators

Reading: conditional for all three associations. Site: archaeological aqueduct/pool existence is established, scroll association remains low/provisional. Feature: northern basin and distinct intake/passage classes can be separated on the source plan; the entry-16 trail intersection is unestablished. Phase: unresolved for the required datum. Position: source-plan-relative only; geographic pool registration remains unaccepted.

Next: test northward entry-29 envelopes from both northern-basin corners/edge and both boundary faces, retaining the reading assumptions. Locate the named trail intersection before running entry-16 distances. Treat entry 35 as its own dam/cairn test. No supported model can yet yield a unique ancient reference surface or deposit location.
