# L103: bounded test against entry 49

Inspected 2 October 2026, UTC. This pass reinspects sources already counted in cycles 8–10. It adds one bounded feature/material/relative-phase assessment, with no new source target, excavation campaign, geographic coordinate or confirmed deposit.

## Observed feature

L103 traverses dam W1 from the main Siloam Pool and enters the attached plastered pool L102/L104. Its preserved length is 2.2 m, width 0.65 m and depth 2.3 m. These describe the exposed conduit, rather than a deposit volume or a full route.

The channel is built of stones. The authors describe small unsmoothed stones in the upper wall construction and medium stones in the lower portion. Fig. 5 shows the built sides. Most surfaces lack plaster; one interior stone about 0.8 m above the bottom retains thick hydraulic plaster. The authors infer a formerly plastered conduit. They suggest repairs and re-use of the pre-existing dam masonry during channel insertion. Neither the photograph nor the description identifies a single stone hollowed out into a trough. (p. 76*; Fig. 5; Plan 1.)

The channel bottom is reported 0.75 m above the attached pool floor and above the lowest excavated main-pool level. The authors propose overflow transfer from the main pool to the attached pool. The hydraulic function is an interpretation supported by placement and relative heights; it identifies no Copper Scroll deposit. (p. 76*.)

## Branch test

**Strict Puech material model:** Puech p. 93 defines the terminal feature as a stone hollowed into a trough or gutter. L103’s documented masonry construction fails that specific material/form requirement. Exclude the exposed L103 installation under a model requiring that definition. A hypothetical missing carved stone, an earlier stone insert or a different adjacent trough would constitute a separate undocumented feature and cannot rescue this candidate without evidence.

**Broader outlet plus gutter/overflow model:** L103 provides a water exit from a pool and a conduit capable of overflow transfer. It survives a broad functional gutter comparison if the semantic model permits a built, formerly plastered channel. This broadening requires an explicit lexical decision; the hydraulic resemblance alone cannot establish it. The Siloam association still depends on the disputed name division and supplied genitive particle. A model requiring a natural spring directly at the terminal feature needs an additional connection; the report places L103 at a pool outlet, not at the Gihon spring.

**Bathing-pool/Siloam branch:** The report connects L103 to a plastered basin. It supplies no independently established bathing function for this attached pool and no carved terminal trough. This branch remains untested at the required feature/function level. The alternative comes through Puech’s discussion and the inherited apparatus; this pass does not independently reread Milik’s original commentary.

**Sanitary plus personal-name branch:** L103’s published function concerns overflow. No latrine, bathing-house or Rachel/Jehu association appears in the inspected excavation pages. This branch receives no candidate support. Original competing-edition arguments remain outside this reinspection.

**Roads-family alternative:** A built conduit cannot decide whether the manuscript names a trough or roads. No roadway identification or independent character-level control arises from L103. Preserve this as an unresolved textual alternative.

## Phase gate

The original Hebrew account reports an irregular removal of pool plaster at the channel opening and infers installation after the pool. The English summary compresses this relationship; it does not supply an independently earlier conduit phase. (Greenhut–Mazor p. 76* and English p. 137.)

Coin 20 from foundation L105, basket 1092, dates to 69/70 CE. Accepting the foundation assignment and the pool’s initial construction interpretation, the excavated floor was installed after the coin’s minting. If the observed channel cut records initial L103 insertion, the exposed conduit also postdates that floor and minting. This is a chained conditional lower bound, with no exact completion date or assumed date for the Copper Scroll. (Ariel pp. 90*–91*.)

Under those assumptions, a deposit model requiring this installation before the relevant 69/70 issue fails the phase test. A model allowing construction and deposition afterwards remains chronologically possible. A model proposing an earlier pool or outlet must demonstrate a distinct earlier fabric/phase at L103; dating an older dam plaster or nearby installation cannot supply that identity.

Coin 17 from L103, basket 1072, dates to 68/69 CE. Its channel-find context does not establish a sealed construction foundation. It dates a coin deposited in the conduit; it provides no independent installation date and cannot override the observed cut or pool-foundation terminus.

## Under-feature datum and measurement limits

Plan 1 gives a 0–2 m scale, orientation arrow and two sections with a numbered elevation axis. Section 2–2 depicts the conduit relative to the attached pool floor; the text supplies the 0.75 m relative difference. These controls justify the published feature-scale relation. They do not establish a geographical deposit point, modern benchmark, projection or uncertainty budget.

Puech’s entry gives an under-feature relation with no offset, compass bearing, cubit unit or depth. Seventeen counts the treasure. No plan measurement can derive a search radius from that number.

The channel’s exposed lumen bottom, the underside of its structural masonry, and an earlier ground surface represent different possible datums. The report illustrates the conduit but supplies no measured, phase-controlled deposit location beneath the entire structural base, or evidence identifying which underside the entry intended. It reports no sealed cache context beneath L103. Keep the hypothetical locus beneath a verified feature as a qualitative relation. Do not convert the 0.75 m floor difference or 2.3 m conduit depth into an instructed digging depth.

## Result and next discrimination

The strict hollowed-stone version conditionally excludes exposed L103. The broad gutter/overflow version survives as a functional comparison, subject to lexical, phase and under-feature controls. Physical construction is now documented as built masonry; feature identity remains unresolved. Site-confidence grade and candidate coordinates stay unchanged.

The next bounded discriminator is a lexical/material comparison across the original competing editions, coupled with evidence for any earlier L103 fabric or a separately documented carved trough. A phase-controlled section through the structural base would address the under-feature datum. These tasks require records or interpretations beyond the published resemblance.
