## Surviving wording, restoration and grammar

Puech defends **bʾmʾ šl q[** from surviving traces (p. 63). This edition-based core consists of a locative expression concerning a conduit, followed by **šl**, an associative/possessive relationship, and the beginning of a damaged word. It supplies no preserved motion verb or construction equivalent to water flowing **into** the main pool. The initial **b-** concerns the location in the conduit; it does not encode the water's direction. This is grammatical analysis of the edition's defended reading, rather than a fresh manuscript transcription.

Puech notes an inadequately documented letter after yod in **qy°[** and explicitly treats the continuation as conjectural. His proposed **qyb[wṣ hmym š]l** supplies the collection/water/tank wording and a restored relational connector. His p. 62 printed line displays part of that proposal before the bracket; p. 63 explains its evidential limitation. Accordingly, the full word must not be promoted to an entirely preserved hydraulic label. Neither the proposed noun for collection nor the associative **šl** specifies which side of the principal reservoir receives the flow.

The expression can associate a conduit with a collecting installation attached to a reservoir without specifying the installation's topology. An inlet could convey collected water into the basin. An outlet could carry reservoir water into an associated collecting installation. The second possibility is an analytical construction, not an interpretation explicitly endorsed by Puech. It requires an actual collection arrangement if the full proposed restoration is accepted. Treating the discovered A(B)171 drain alone as an automatic equivalent of the whole restored phrase would drop that requirement.

Milik's p. 140 translation retains the damaged **Qe[...]** as a possible name rather than supplying Puech's collection expression. The English noun “aqueduct” and the incomplete relationship to the northern reservoir supply no explicit hydraulic direction. Translation vocabulary alone cannot establish an engineering supply function. Milik's article supplies no target Hebrew transcription/apparatus from which to resolve the Qe continuation independently. Puech's later reports of Milik's 1962 edition and other scholars' readings are secondary testimony here; their original apparatus remains outside this pass.
