# Small Siloam receiving pool: bathing-function check

Inspected 2 October 2026, America/Los_Angeles. Milik 1960's original p. 142 locates the under-pipe relation in the pool of Siloah's baths. It supplies no passage-to-baths wording. This pass tests the particular hypothesis that the small L102/L104 receiving basin supplies that bathing pool, using its existing published correspondence with L124/W50 and Guthe plate III. It adds one bounded function check and zero source targets, assessments, decisive tests, closures or confirmed deposits.

## Feature observations and measurements

The 2009 report exposes the northwestern portion of the small pool: 8.4 m length, 3 m width and 3.1 m height, with a plastered floor and rounded floor/wall junction. These describe excavated remains rather than a full footprint or operating water depth. The floor crack supports the authors' settlement/short-use inference (p. 75*; Plan 1).

The adjoining 2013 exposure, published in 2014, records the partially exposed L124 pool as 3 m long, 2.5 m wide and approximately 2.5 m deep. It records **six steps**, tread and rise approximately 0.4 m, and repairs/changes. Its southern wall overlies L115 pavement; it postdates that pavement. The report joins its northern part to the 2009 excavation. Original Fig. 3 plots L124 north of gateway L127. These partial measurements cannot be added into a complete footprint.

The steps establish access into the installation. They could serve maintenance, collection or human entry; their presence alone does not establish immersion or bathing. The published target observations do not specify an operating water level or show a separate controlled bath circulation route. Seven steps beside W12 descend into **tower reservoir L119**, a separate feature south of the gateway; those are not extra L124 bathing steps. L133 is a separately blocked channel, not a newly verified pool outlet.

L103 traverses W1 into the small pool. Its preserved dimensions are 2.2 × 0.65 m, with 2.3 m reported depth. Its bottom is 0.75 m above the small pool floor. Most surfaces lack preserved plaster; one stone retains thick hydraulic plaster. The authors infer formerly continuous lining and transfer of surplus main-pool water. These observations establish a water supply comparison; they do not identify a bathing regime (p. 76*; Fig. 5; Plan 1).

## Published function and human-use evidence

Greenhut–Mazor's original Hebrew conclusion **assigns the small pool a storage function** and calls L103 an overflow channel (p. 83* / PDF 14). Its Fig. 3 Hebrew caption also labels the small basin an overflow pool (p. 73* / PDF 4; earlier archive). These are author interpretations grounded in the hydraulic setting, not evidence of a Copper Scroll identification.

The 2024 discussion separately identifies two small pools as excess-water collectors, including this southeastern pool. It distinguishes them from the Silwan pool and larger Birket al-Hamra complex (p. 34*). Its stair/seating interpretation on pp. 35*–36* concerns the monumental northern/eastern structure at the larger pool. It cannot assign the small L124 steps either seating or bathing function.

The 2020 pottery and stone finds have recorded foundation or sealing-fill contexts. The stone-vessel fragments are assigned to the upper fill L104 in the accompanying discussion; Fig. 10 items 1–4 list L104, while weight item 5 lists L101. They do not form a documented bathing assemblage on an occupied basin floor (pp. 82*–83*). The targeted report pages supply no bath-specific human-use residue, diagnostic wear argument, bath inscription, heated-room connection or demonstrated bath-use deposit. This is a bounded publication finding, not a claim that those features never existed on site.

Waterproof plaster, steps and available water make human entry physically plausible. Storage/overflow can coexist with human use. The authors' storage interpretation therefore supplies an alternative documented purpose rather than a categorical exclusion of bathing.

## Phase gate remains

The original Hebrew p. 76* observes an irregular cut through the small pool plaster and infers later insertion of L103. The English summary's loose period linkage cannot reverse that specific relationship. The already audited L105 foundation coin 20, dated 69/70 CE, supplies a conditional floor-construction lower bound; accepting initial construction and the cut interpretation chains that bound to L103. The 2014 broad Hasmonean/Herodian assignment and 2024 proposed earlier phase do not supply a newly demonstrated earlier bathing installation.

The 2020 authors record approximately 2.5 m of sealing fill and an overlying stone layer of unclear function, with defense-related sealing suggested; two post-Revolt rims retain alternative late-activity/sealing explanations (p. 77*). This evidence concerns abandonment and fill, not bathing operation.

The 2025 main study and appendix sample W001/W114 dam mortar. The appendix documents W114 attachment to W001 and sampling controls, without a small-pool-floor or L103 functional context. Dam dates and sampling contacts cannot establish human bathing in the receiving pool.

## Result and next discriminator

**Bathing function remains inconclusive.** The published evidence supports a stepped, plastered, accessible basin, while the inspected excavation interpretations support storage and overflow collection. It supplies no positive bath-specific identification of the small receiving pool. The hypothesis equating this small basin with Milik's bathing pool leaves its function requirement unfulfilled; neither the material comparison nor this function audit upgrades L103. A separate hypothesis could assign the bathing pool to the larger main basin, but must establish that the relevant under-pipe location lies in that basin. This small-pool function check neither tests the larger basin's bathing function nor establishes that spatial relationship. The receiving basin's storage role alone cannot exclude every version of L103.

Next seek phase-controlled evidence specifically identifying small-pool use: an original occupation-floor assemblage, diagnostic access/immersion arrangement, residue/use-wear study or published bath interpretation whose argument names L102/L104 or L124/W50. General labels applied to the larger Siloam pool cannot fill this gap. Any earlier-use proposal also needs a distinct fabric and floor relationship at the small basin/conduit.
