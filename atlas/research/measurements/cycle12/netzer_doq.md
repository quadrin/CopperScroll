## Published observation and author interpretation

Netzer describes Doq west of Jericho and roughly 300 m above the town. He identifies its conspicuous summit enclosure as construction on the eve of World War I. That enclosure therefore supplies no late Second Temple wall or corner merely through its visible shape. (p. 206)

The author infers continued Herodian use and probable enlargement from the strategic location, surveys and his own small excavation in 1982. He describes runoff channels feeding rock-cut cisterns below the fortress and a western moat which he regards as probably Hasmonaean. These are site-level descriptions and interpretations. They neither date a particular northeastern-spur room nor identify the surface from which a digging instruction would begin. (pp. 206–207)

**Original p. 207, note 25:** “The finds from the one-week dig have not yet been published.” The note attaches to the Doq 1982 excavation. This is an explicit publication-status statement as of the 2006 book, not a claim about all subsequent scholarship.

## The Netzer–Birger citation belongs to a separate fieldwork context

On p. 207, Netzer places Nuseib Uweishira north of the Wadi Qelt mouth, approximately 3 km south of Doq. He reports his **1976–1977** soundings there, below a Byzantine monastery. Note 27 cites Ehud Netzer and Rivka Birger, “A Byzantine Monastery at Nuseib ‘Uweishira, West of Jericho,” in Giovanni Claudio Bottini, Leah Di Segni and Eugenio Alliata, eds., *Christian Archaeology in the Holy Land: New Discoveries* (1990), pp. 191–200. Note 25 and note 27 concern different sites and campaigns.

Keep the earlier verified 2001 cross-reference: Netzer's *The Palaces of the Hasmoneans and Herod the Great*, p. 140, chapter 9 note 9, explicitly follows its Qarantal 1982 excavation statement with a reference to that 1990 monastery article. Cycle 11 read that cross-citation correctly. The present 2006 account narrows its usefulness: the monastery article remains a possible comparative or incidental-comment source, while the Doq finds are explicitly described as unpublished. The article itself remains unread, so its complete coverage cannot be inferred from either book's note.
