## Urn, book and silver

Puech p. 60 interprets the vessel as a wide-mouth jar capable of holding a manuscript. He recognizes two possible antecedents for the following positional expression: the urn, or the book inside it. He prefers the treasure beneath the urn, relying on comparison with other deposits and a concern that coins under a manuscript inside the jar could distort it. This preference is an editorial interpretation; neither an excavated vessel nor the deposit’s material arrangement was observed here.

The project should preserve both deposit models:

- A jar contains a book/scroll, with the monetary deposit below the jar.
- A jar contains a book/scroll, with the monetary deposit below the book inside the jar.

Puech gives 42 and interprets the abbreviated monetary unit as silver karsh on pp. 59 and 131. Keep that attribution beside the project’s edition-unit alternatives; the number does not establish a particular coin assemblage. The spatial clause gives a northern opening and a three-cubit indication. It supplies no ancient floor elevation, vertical benchmark or field section. Converting the project’s exploratory 0.40–0.60 m cubit to 1.20–1.80 m remains conditional on both unit and depth/distance interpretation.
