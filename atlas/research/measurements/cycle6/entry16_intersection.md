# Entry 16: trail–aqueduct intersection audit

2 October 2026. Reused source target; no new source inspection claimed. Read current AGENTS.md, OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, Hyrcania reading audit and historical primary follow-up before this pass.

## Result

A published trail/channel crossing exists at the western end of the eastern bridge, station 48. Patrich places it 112 m west of the bridge's eastern end (1989 p. 252). This is a source-relative alternative control for investigation. It does not establish Eshel's proposed entry-16 origin on the northern aqueduct: station 48 is downstream of the northern/southern confluence, on their shared approach to the fortress. No unique crossing on the northern-only course has been identified in this pass.

Eshel's original p. 103 states his restored Hyrcania destination and 41-cubit position from where the trail met the northern aqueduct west of Hyrcania. His page supplies no survey-station number or point annotation. Hyrcania is an editorial/site-association assumption, not a surviving place name. Passage 49 remains an unrelated project comparison.

## Independent archaeological controls

Patrich, “אמות המים להורקניה”, in *אמות המים הקדומות בארץ־ישראל* (1989), pp. 243–260:

- Fig. 1, p. 243, distinguishes the northern intake 44, northern channel 45, junction 43 and bridges 46–48. The map has a north arrow and 2-km scale. At this map's scale the 6–22 m offsets cannot be confidently digitized as deposit points.
- p. 251: station 43 is the meeting of the northern and southern aqueducts near the western end of bridge 46. The precise junction is not recognizable on the ground. This prevents promotion of the drawn symbol to an observed surviving intersection datum.
- p. 251: northern station 44 dam supplied the channel southward; about 300 m from the dam exposed channel plaster is attributed to the Second Temple period. No Byzantine repairs were recognized along this northern stretch. Patrich infers either survival/reuse in that period or no renewed use. These alternatives leave phase of any trail crossing unresolved.
- p. 252/Fig. 16: Byzantine aqueduct attaches to the northern side of the older western bridge 46. Downstream two slightly different alignments continue: the earlier channel higher, while the trail follows the lower Byzantine channel. A present trail/aqueduct alignment therefore carries a construction-phase ambiguity.
- p. 252: bridge 48 carries the approach trail. Its western end, at the point where the trail coming from its right crosses the aqueduct, is 112 m west of the eastern end. This explicitly documented crossing is the most specific current control, labelled **station-48 western crossing, conditional alternative**. The source does not date the trail/channel crossing surface.
- p. 253/Fig. 17 combines a general view with a reconstruction. The approach depiction cannot establish the ancient trail's surveyed crossing phase.
- p. 254/Fig. 21 labels a trail beside the western bridge-48 section. It supports recognition of the published approach feature but supplies no excavated dated contact.

That numerical accommodation does not select this crossing or validate any restoration. Directions still require decisions between fortress-bound travel, reverse travel, along the channel and movement from the crossing into an installation. Channel direction and ancient trail direction cannot be replaced with page-up orientation. A depth branch lacks an identified ancient starting surface.
