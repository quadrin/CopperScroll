## Citation chain and independent evidence

1. **Milik 1960 p. 148 / PDF 12, reinspection of an existing source.** His commentary proposes a residence of the priestly Bene ha-Qos. He reasons from the adjacency of rebuilding groups in Nehemiah 3 and their association with people of Jericho/the Jordan district. He then identifies the modern north-bank hill near Wadi Qelt's passage out of Jericho, acknowledging phonetic uncertainty. He cites the Survey and Augustinović, *Gerico e dintorni*, pp. 89–92. The family-to-estate and estate-to-hill links are Milik's inferences. Jerusalem wall-repair team order does not locate an estate or cave in Jericho.
2. **SWP III (1883) p. 222 / local PDF 248, actual page image rechecked.** Tell el Kôs, grid Ps, resembles the preceding Tell el Jurn: stone heaps, outside the strict class of settlement mounds. The entry supplies no dated phase, cave, cool room or family inscription. Phase 5 already inspected this page; this pass adds no source-inspection count. The old phrase “no ancient remains reported” exceeded what this short entry alone could establish.
3. **Palmer, *Arabic and English Name Lists* (1881), printed p. 351 / PDF 361, original page newly recovered and image-checked.** The Sheet XVIII entry prints Tell el Kôs, grid Ps, Arabic `تل الكوس`, and explains it as a mound of accumulated sand hills. This independently predates Milik's identification and establishes a recorded modern place name. Its descriptive Arabic explanation provides an alternative to survival of a priestly family name. It neither proves that explanation historically nor rules out older name survival. The Arabic spelling and the recorded Hebrew household reading require a demonstrated phonetic history; visual resemblance alone does not supply it. Palmer and SWP derive from the same survey campaign, so they constitute one observation family, not two independent corroborations.
4. **PADIS official catalog record, Tell el-Qos, site 7.4 / code 0021, newly inspected synthesis.** It reports ruins, Byzantine architectural remains, cisterns, a plastered basin and mosaic tesserae. It cites SWP 1883 p. 222, Augustinović 1950 p. 266 and 1951 pp. 89–91, Cirelli–Zagari 2000 p. 366, and Zagari 2000 p. 357. These reports qualify the earlier blanket absence claim. The catalog supplies no first-century estate ownership, frigidarium, cave, room-to-cave relationship or six-cubit reference level. Its Byzantine classification does not establish the absence of earlier occupation. Treat it as a bibliography-based catalog synthesis, not a newly inspected excavation context.

## Position and uncertainty

The project preserves a georeferenced Sheet XVIII anchor at 31.8551, 35.4656 with approximately 200 m positional uncertainty. PADIS reports 31°51′13.58″ N, 35°27′53.28″ E: decimal 31.85377222, 35.4648. A local equirectangular comparison, provisionally treating both as the same latitude/longitude datum, gives approximately 166 m separation (148 m south, 76 m west). That is compatible with the existing approximate 200 m map-label uncertainty. PADIS does not state a datum or feature-position accuracy on the inspected page. Its decimal precision cannot justify a precise cave point. Retain both as source-specific site/area anchors; do not silently replace the existing coordinate or assign the catalog point to a cave.

The earliest map-label evidence already recorded in the project is Sheet XVIII, dated May 1878, with Tell el Kôs. This pass did not recover that original map image; its label and date remain inherited observations. The earliest original name-list page directly image-checked here is Palmer 1881 p. 351. Neither statement asserts the earliest attestation in all surviving sources.

## Explicit test outcome

Support: a pre-Copper-Scroll-discovery modern name, a plausible Jericho district setting under the family-name reading, and later reported water installations.

Limits/conflict: descriptive Arabic name interpretation; unresolved phonetic continuity; no ancient family inscription or estate record at this hill; reported Byzantine remains do not date a first-century cool room or cave; the summer-house variant removes the name-based anchor. SWP's short stone-heap entry cannot establish either occupation chronology or total absence of ancient remains.

Outcome: **inconclusive for ancient identity and individual feature**. The audit corrects the evidence description; it does not raise candidate confidence or confirm the sequence group's geography. Common baths/caves in the district and nearby sequence assignments must not count as independent corroboration.

Next discriminating record: Augustinović pp. 89–92 and a phase-specific plan/report for PADIS 0021 identifying any cave, cool room or estate inscription. A building-type match alone will still leave the household-name reading unresolved.
