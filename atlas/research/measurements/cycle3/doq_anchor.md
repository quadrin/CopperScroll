# Doq: regional anchor and feature-phase audit

Reviewed 2 October 2026 UTC. R10 / M05, entry 31. This is a reinspection and assertion audit of an existing candidate, independent of its position in the entry sequence. No new excavation evidence or original manuscript reading was acquired.

## Readings and required feature

The existing apparatus and [feature-test note](https://github.com/quadrin/CopperScroll/blob/main/research/sites/dok_achor_feature_tests.md) retain three hypotheses: the eastern corner of a spreading/drying place; the eastern corner of a guard post; or a corner of the eastern guard post. Recorded references are Lefkovits 2000 pp. 234–235 and Puech 2006 p. 192 / 2015 p. 67. This pass read those project records, not the original apparatus pages or metal. Seven cubits is a depth instruction under these readings; the current summit marker supplies no ancient ground datum.

## What the ancient references establish

[1 Maccabees 16:14–15, NRSVUE electronic text](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Maccabees+16&version=NRSVUE) places Simon's visit at Jericho and names the stronghold Dok in the ensuing episode. [Josephus, Antiquities 13.230, book XIII chapter 8.1](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-13.html) describes Dagon as a fortress above Jericho. Both passages were read as electronic translations; Greek manuscripts and textual apparatus were not checked. These references support a named fortress in the Jericho setting. They provide neither modern coordinates nor a dated first-century CE guard post or drying installation. They concern the same historical episode; this audit has not established their literary independence.

The existing project associates the fortress with Ras Qarantal and retains Ain Duk as a different possible anchor. That association still needs its own topographic and archaeological chain. The ancient text does not name the modern summit or spring. A related surviving name and regional waterworks cannot choose the Scroll's building reading.

## Phase and plan limits

Earlier source records cite Garbrecht–Peleg 1994 pp. 164–165 for the fortress/water system and a non-rebuilding argument. An absence of rebuilding is not evidence that every structure remained unused. It also cannot establish the date of the required installation. The atlas previously presented the fortress reading as in use through 70 CE; the inspected evidence does not support that specific feature-phase claim.

David Amit, “מפעל המים של מבצר דוק (דגון),” in *אמות המים הקדומות בארץ־ישראל* (1989), pp. 223–228, remains the concrete plan target. The earlier [Kotar opening excerpt](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Index/Chapter.aspx?nBookID=6765980&nTocEntryID=7085524) names Ras Qarantal and the 1972 northern-spur survey but does not expose Fig. 1. The full chapter and figure remain unread in this pass. Obtain the labelled plan and phase discussion, then test the required building and its corner under each viable reading. The summit's eastern edge is not itself a documented building corner.

## Result and product correction

Retain the broad Doq association, alternative Ain Duk anchor, grades and coordinates. The exact feature, ancient surface, construction/use phase and digging point remain unresolved. Correct the atlas period field to distinguish the Hasmonean fortress context from the undated required installation. Entry 32 likewise requires its particular outlet/wall relation and date; a regional aqueduct comparison does not establish either. Entry 30's site-specific estate/cave chronology is also unestablished.

Accounting: zero new counted primary targets, bounded checks, decisive tests or closures. Reinspection of these literary references adds no independent archaeological observation. This note audits existing assertions; the separate Tell el-Qos association test has its own result and accounting.
