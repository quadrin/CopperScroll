## What the originals establish

**Pool identity:** Greenhut and Mazor, printed p. 75* / PDF 6, explicitly identify the southeastern corner exposed by Weksler-Bdolah in 2013 as part of their pool, and cite the 2014 report. That publication describes L124; the 2022 report explicitly associates L124 with W50 and Guthe plate III. The chain is therefore **2009 L102/L104 → 2013 stepped corner → L124 → W50/Guthe III**. This is a published identification chain, without a newly registered footprint. It resolves the prior unmatched-pool limitation. Guthe plate II's tunnel-mouth GHJK and Bliss's grooved tank remain separate.

**Pool construction:** the 2009 report identifies foundation loci L105/L105A beneath the plaster floor, with first-century CE ceramics and seven catalogue coins, and attributes installation to the Great Revolt, possibly its last year (pp. 75*, 83*; English summary p. 137). This refines or conflicts with the 2014 broad Hasmonean/Herodian designation. Keep the authors' construction interpretation separate from independently audited coin termini. L105A was assigned during material processing and represents upper L107; it yielded the three earliest coins. That recording detail requires a context audit and does not itself prove disturbance.

The pool fill L102/L104 contains two post-Revolt jar rims, about 2% of the reported 93-item assemblage. The authors retain later activity and a post-destruction sealing-layer terminus as alternatives (p. 77*). Their proposed filling for defence before the destruction remains interpretive. The companion coin catalogue has not been inspected.

**Earlier dam plaster:** yellowish plaster sample L108/B1028 belongs to the lower face of W1 beneath the pool floor, beside Iron Age fills L107/L109 (Plan 1 sections; p. 75*). It is separate from the upper Second Temple coatings and the later pool foundation. Boaretto reports one dated charcoal fragment, RTT 6089, not a suite of independently dated coatings.

Its calibrated 95.4% set is **540–530 BCE (0.3%) plus 520–190 BCE (95.1%)**, with a 540–190 BCE envelope (Boaretto pp. 79–80/Fig. 1). Greenhut–Mazor's 520–200 BCE summary differs from the final laboratory calibration. Boaretto leaves disagreement with the abutting Iron Age archaeological context unresolved. The old-wood qualification and poor preservation do not establish a cause. This is an earlier dam-context discrepancy, not a direct radiocarbon-versus-coin disagreement about pool construction. The 2024 tentative earlier-pool-phase proposal still requires an independently documented pool-phase contact; this sample alone cannot establish it.

## Entry 49 feature comparison

**L103** cuts through W1 into the same receiving pool. Published dimensions are 2.2 m exposed length, 0.65 m width and 2.3 m depth; its floor is 0.75 m above the receiving pool floor. The irregular cut through pool plaster leads the excavators to place it after pool construction and interpret it as an overflow outlet (p. 76*/Fig. 5/Plan 1). A retained hydraulic-plaster patch supports their original lining reconstruction; that reconstruction is not a complete surviving lining.

For Puech's outlet/Siloam branch, L103 is a **provisional source-plan feature comparison**. If the terminal trough/gutter term can denote this masonry conduit, the under-feature relation would refer beneath its floor. That conditional area follows the exposed conduit in Plan 1; it supplies no surveyed deposit point, metric offset or digging depth. Neither a hollowed stone trough nor an engraved-name identification is established here. The name/word division and disputed strokes remain separate textual tests. The exposed segment is incomplete, and no absolute ancient underside datum is accepted in this pass.

No feature-grade upgrade, new geographic coordinate or confirmed deposit follows. Published pool correspondence improves the archaeological frame; the disputed trough identity remains unresolved.
