## Constraints selected from the edition

1. Puech's opening term describes a water exit/source. On p. 91 he discusses both a metathesized exit term and a possible small added mem that would suggest a plural outlets term. On p. 92 he retains the singular. A candidate can start at a water source or exit, but this edition alone cannot choose the Gihon spring, a tunnel exit, an overflow exit, or a particular named pool. An enclosed pool is not a prerequisite in this reading.
2. The Siloam interpretation requires a supplied genitive particle, marked as an insertion in the edition, and a particular word division. Puech chooses cursive waw over resh or zayin in the name sequence. The earlier published-image audit also leaned toward waw, while retaining the hand's waw-like resh. The site association remains conditional; finding a suitable hydraulic installation cannot choose the engraved reading.
3. The final instruction establishes an **under-feature relation**. Puech retains the short trough term and interprets a hollowed stone trough/gutter (p. 93). His argument for the terminal taw uses the pre-opening verso photograph; he attributes apparent extra strokes to folds, rather than accepting the expanded spelling/roads alternative. This pass could inspect the cited original plate, but cannot independently settle the terminal letter or the folds. A channel-shaped structure meets only the broad functional comparison until its material, date and under-feature relation are established.
4. Entry 49 gives **no compass bearing, offset distance, cubit instruction, excavation verb or explicit digging depth** in the inspected edition. Its numerical notation records treasure quantity; Puech renders it as 17 monetary-weight units on pp. 86 and 139. The entry cannot support a 17-cubit radius or depth. Nor can a direction or depth from entry 48, 50 or another entry be imported into this one.
5. “Under” does not specify the ancient ground surface or an amount below it. If a feature is established, the defensible modeled locus is the area beneath that feature, with uncertainty in its original underside and construction phase. No metric buffer or modern coordinate follows from the words alone.

## Alternatives that change the archaeological test

- **Puech outlet/Siloam model:** identify a relevant water source/exit; test a separate trough or gutter and the under-feature relation. The place association depends on the name reading and inserted particle. A complex-level hydraulic resemblance establishes neither the individual trough nor the deposit.
- **Milik bathing-pool/Siloam model:** Puech pp. 91–92 and the earlier project record report a bathing-pool phrase and an adjectival Siloam reading. This model requires a basin associated with baths, rather than any hydraulic exit. The original Milik discussion pages were not read afresh here. Bathing function and Siloam association each require evidence.
- **Water-closet plus Rachel/Jehu model:** Puech p. 92 notes 407 and 409 report the competing personal-name divisions, and the inherited apparatus records Lefkovits's Jehu reading. This model requires the proposed functional installation and named association; it supplies no independent Jerusalem or Siloam locator. Original Lefkovits pp. 352–357 remain necessary for a fresh cross-edition comparison.
- **Expanded trough/roads-family alternative:** Puech pp. 92–93 discusses the uncertain added vertical traces and rejects the expanded reading in favor of the short trough term. Preserve this as an apparatus issue awaiting independent image control. Do not silently count every mapped road or every drainage channel as evidence for it.

No alternative licenses a compass-bearing or cubit-distance search grid. The parallel archaeology worker received these constraints before site comparison.
