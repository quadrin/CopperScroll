## What the archaeological source supplies

Patrich, “אמות המים להורקניה”, in David Amit, Yizhar Hirschfeld and Joseph Patrich (eds.), *אמות המים הקדומות בארץ־ישראל* (1989), pp. 243–260:

- **Observed double-pool geometry, p. 255:** two rock-cut basins north of the bridge, divided by an approximately 4.80–5.40 m rock partition. Their unequal sides and floors are described; the northern member reaches about 5 m below the surrounding rock, the southern about 2.6 m. Remnant hydraulic plaster is attributed typologically to the Second Temple period. Rock quarrying and use as a swimming installation are discussed, including rock-cut benches west of the pool. These observations identify the installation, without documenting its inlet.
- **Water handling interpretation, p. 255:** Patrich treats the pools as large reservoirs for the slope and summit cisterns, with water potentially carried uphill by pack animals when necessary. This is a handling proposal; it does not locate a basin feed or overflow.
- **Actual destination of the northern branch, p. 255:** after crossing the bridge, the aqueduct divides into north and south branches around the summit. The northern branch supplies the **eastern cisterns**. That branch name does not mean it supplies the northern double-pool member.
- **Southern branches and phases, pp. 255–257:** surviving southern channels occur at two levels. Patrich associates the lower with the lower Hasmonean bridge, the upper with the elevated Herodian bridge and higher cisterns. A subsidiary channel descending from upper to lower is proposed to retain lower-cistern supply; he explicitly says this connecting channel has not yet been located. The lower southern system's cistern destinations are discussed on p. 257. These phase-dependent reconstructions concern hillside cisterns, without establishing a northern-pool inlet.
- **Fig. 22, p. 256:** the plan labels the double pool, southern pool, upper/lower channels, bridge and cisterns. The regional approach passes beside the double pool and continues towards the hill. The drawing supplies no independently labelled northern-basin inlet, valve, overflow, feeder-junction section or pool-specific surveyed elevation. Its aqueduct symbols do not encode which segments survive and which are reconstructed. A nearby drawn course cannot be promoted to an observed junction.
- **Byzantine distinction, p. 259:** the later aqueduct probably supplied only E and J; the settling basin at E has characteristic reddish plaster. This documented later supply destination cannot establish a late Second Temple feed to the double pool.

The regional northern and southern aqueducts join near bridge 46, upstream of bridge 48; the precise junction is no longer recognizable (p. 251, previously inspected). Downstream naming must distinguish (1) the northern-only regional aqueduct, (2) their common course, and (3) the north branch around the summit. None selects a feed to the northern basin.

## Later claims and their limits

Eshel, “Aqueducts in the Copper Scroll”, *Copper Scroll Studies*, printed p. 105, conditionally restores the Kidron aqueduct entering a northern large reservoir and proposes the large pool north of the bridge. He associates this reading with the southern regional aqueduct. His conditional text-to-site proposal contains no measured inlet, station number or inlet-specific archaeological contact. The reservoir association cannot authenticate its own restored entering conduit. Left/north and 24-cubit origin alternatives remain as in the existing reading audit.

Garbrecht and **Yehuda** Peleg, “The Water Supply of the Desert Fortresses in the Jordan Valley”, *The Biblical Archaeologist* 57(3) (1994), p. 168, say the three open pools filled after the slope cisterns were full. They infer that pool water was consumed first, retaining cistern contents for later use or siege. The page provides no pool-specific overflow route, invert elevation, gate, inlet or flow-control observation establishing that sequence. It also summarizes all three pools as approximately 18 × 15 × 5 m, whereas Patrich's paired basins have unequal outlines and depths. Treat this as a hydraulic synthesis, not independent evidence of equal basin geometry or northern-basin inlet fabric. The authors also give phase-qualified bridge/cistern assignments; those do not date a double-pool contact.

## Outcome and next discriminating evidence

A shared upstream supply and eventual filling are hydraulically possible; published proximity alone does not select the channel-to-basin connection. We cannot fix the entry-29 origin at an inlet, outlet, canal/pool junction, centre or rim through this evidence. No ancient inlet reference surface or surveyed invert level is supplied in the inspected coverage.
