# Qumran water system: visual and plan crosswalk for entries 20–23

Reviewed 30 September 2026. This is a source audit under the **provisional** Sekakah = Qumran hypothesis. It identifies physical correspondences among published surveys and photographs. It does **not** identify a Copper Scroll hiding place. “Direct image match” below means that distinctive shapes agree in two views; “plan match” means that route order and relative position agree; “caption inference” means that an author's label supplies the identity or orientation. None is a geographic registration.

## Result

The **western mouth of the long eastern tunnel** remains the one secure visual bridge: Reeder–Jol 2006 Fig. 2, Magen–Peleg 2018 Fig. 87, and Christopher Davey's 1974 photograph CJD979 show the same aperture. That establishes a reusable photographic landmark. Its *western/upstream* designation follows Magen–Peleg's text and caption, while Reeder's Fig. 2 calls the photographed mouth an “exit.” The images alone do not settle hydraulic direction.

A new comparison of the **two internal breaks** gives a stronger topological test. Masterman 1903 places them 11 and 23 feet from the entrance, or approximately **3.35 and 7.01 m**. Magen–Peleg 2018 places the first 3.10 m from the western entrance and the second another 4 m along, or **7.10 m** from that entrance. The respective differences are about **0.25 and 0.09 m**. This is close positional agreement for the *long-tunnel* identification, conditional on both writers using the same entrance and measuring along a comparable path. The published tunnel lengths differ by about 0.71 m (13.1 versus 12.4 m), and neither work supplies a survey uncertainty for the offsets. Masterman predates the Copper Scroll's discovery; his physical description is historical evidence, although later authors may have consulted it. This agreement does **not** establish that either break is the fissure in entry 22.

The upstream intake–boulder–pothole–wall connection remains **unverified**. The two printed plans are consistent with a shared collection sector, but neither the retrieved photographs nor the exploratory plan fit identifies Ilan–Amit point 3 with Reeder's boulder or surviving wall. Ilan–Amit point 4 is an inferred main-wadi dam with no remains reported; it cannot be substituted for Reeder's observed wall.

## Long-tunnel geometry and what it can test

| Position along tunnel from western entrance | Masterman p. 267, derived from feet | Magen–Peleg p. 79, printed metres | Absolute difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First break | 11 ft = 3.3528 m | 3.10 m | 0.2528 m |
| Second break | 23 ft = 7.0104 m | 3.10 + 4.00 = 7.10 m | 0.0896 m |
| Eastern end / reported length | 43 ft = 13.1064 m | 12.40 m | 0.7064 m |

The feet conversion uses 0.3048 m/ft. Four-decimal converted values in the table are arithmetic, **not** claims of millimetre survey precision. The first break is approximately one quarter of either reported tunnel length from the west; the second is around the middle. This constrains a future photo sequence: starting at the matched CJD979/Fig. 87 mouth, the first candidate break should be approximately 3 m in and the second around 7 m in. A photograph showing one south-facing gap without an entrance-to-gap traverse is insufficient to assign its ordinal position. Conversely, finding the reported pair with opposite order or on different walls would weaken the Masterman–Magen correspondence.

The surviving 2018 text prints one intervening repair-wall height as “0.16 cm”; the [source extraction](../../registration/magen2018_extracted.md) flags the likely unit error. No repair-wall height is used here to identify or scale a break.

## Upstream plan and photograph test

The [two-anchor fit and inputs](../../registration/upstream_similarity.json) align approximate centres of the two tunnels in Ilan–Amit Fig. 1 and Reeder–Jol Fig. 4. It puts Ilan point 3 about 8 m from a manually sampled Reeder rock position on the **printed drawings**. The fitted scale is approximately 13% below the scale-bar ratio; its two anchor residuals are zero by construction. A ±4-pixel analyst perturbation over 20,000 trials yielded roughly 1–17 m for the drawing-implied intake-to-rock separation. That exercise excludes survey distortion, rock-pick uncertainty and the possible nonidentity of depicted branches. It supplies **no surveyed ground distance, unique intake position or probability interval**.

I rechecked the public 800-pixel [Davey CJD980](https://dqcaas.com/photographic-collections/photographic-collections/) and [CJD981](https://dqcaas.com/photographic-collections/photographic-collections/) views. CJD980 has a large, close foreground block, but the photograph lacks a scale, a recognizable channel cut adjoining that block, and the full pothole–wall relationship. CJD981 connects the gorge view to the probable long-tunnel mouth but does not look back through the upstream collection works. Neither permits a photographic match to Reeder's boulder or Ilan point 3. This negative result is **lack of diagnostic coverage**, not evidence that Reeder's features were absent in 1974. The [archival/drone audit](qumran_archival_photo_video_review.md) likewise found no secure wall or intake trace in its sampled aerial frames.

## Next discriminating acquisition

1. **One continuous, close view from the small north-bank ravine to the main channel:** include a measured scale, the Reeder boulder, the pothole, wall and the first unmistakable channel cut. This is the missing observation for the entry 21 intake–rock relation. The original 2002 Reeder survey stations, benchmark coordinates, datum and plan notes would allow a stronger test without field photography.
2. **One west-to-east tunnel traverse from the already matched western mouth:** record the first and second south-facing breaks with distance readings, wall/floor views and the eastern exit. This would assign Ilan's interior light patch and test whether Masterman and Magen describe the same two breaks.
3. **The complete Ilan–Amit 2002 English Fig. 1 and original image plates:** compare its feature numbers and captions with the 1989 Hebrew plan. At present the later chapter has only a [word-level p. 385 check](../../registration/ilan_amit2002_p385_check.md); identical numbering and linework should not be assumed.

None of the checked material justifies changing site confidence, drawing precise map geometry, or claiming discovery of a deposit. The productive near-term task is to tie the photographs to **named survey stations and construction phases**, then test the scroll readings against those separately identified features.


## Schulz A/B and 2009 phase models: original-source follow-up (1 October 2026)

[Direct inspection of the supplied originals](https://github.com/quadrin/CopperScroll/blob/main/research/sources/qumran_supplied_originals_review_2026-10-01.md) adds Schulz p. 55's explicitly labelled eastern/western mouths and internal offsets. Reversing his upstream traverse gives a conditional western sequence B then A. B is about 4.05 m from the west; A is about 4.05 + 3.00 = 7.05 m. The derived total is about 11.55 m, compared with Magen–Peleg's 12.40 m. This is approximate arithmetic, without survey-error estimates or independently verified common endpoints.

Schulz p. 56 attributes B to a natural fissure crossed by dressed stones/mortar, while A appears artificial; Magen–Peleg describes both western-order breaks as natural cracks. The second offset's agreement does not settle the first break, the description conflict or individual image identities. Existing Masterman/Magen agreement remains the previously inspected baseline. Preserve separate letter labels, ordinal labels and mouth references.

Strobel's Fig. 1 covers the settlement, with a de Vaux 1956 dependency; his separate gorge text and plates document a higher channel and lower natural basin without registering Reeder's wall. Taylor–Gibson 2020 note 58 is the detailed account of the same 2009 visit referenced in the earlier manuscript. It supplies a prepared-map lead and competing dam/loop/spillway models, without a dated upper construction sequence. These source additions retain low exact-feature confidence and conditional entry 22 reservoir/fissure comparison. No geographic transformation or new control point is generated.
