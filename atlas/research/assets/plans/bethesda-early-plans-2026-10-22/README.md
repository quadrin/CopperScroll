## Findings

- EVIDENCE (Schick 1888 pp. 118–120): the pool then shown at St Anne's was reached by a flight of steps 19 ft down to a tank; "now 55 feet long, but this was most probably the breadth, and 12 feet 6 inches of an average in width". Its bottom is at 2,359 ft at the foot of the steps, 2,376 ft on the south and up to 2,390 ft on the east (feet above the Mediterranean, the survey's datum). A small church with an apse stood over the eastern part; Schick puts the font "above the centre of the pool below".
- EVIDENCE (Masterman 1905 p. 93, citing the *Quarterly Statement* of January 1897): a model maker held that "the pool now shown as the Pool of Bethesda at St. Anne's is only a very small portion of the original pool".
- INFERENCE (registration): Plate No. 1 is fitted to the mapped St Anne's church, city wall and Lions' Gate (turn −4.8°, scale 2 % above the printed bar). The church, wall and gate fall on their outlines, and Birket Israel on today's car park over the filled pool. Schick's pool then runs west–east about 40 m, from (−31, −21) to (9, −24) m from the `jer_bethesda` anchor, with the apse at (11, −23); about ±2 m.
- INFERENCE (against the atlas model): the model places its dam at (11.7, −11.6) on the centroid of the mapped excavation area. Schick's pool lies about 10 m north of that line, inside the model's north pool. The model's eastern complex is drawn about 75 m east of the dam, east of the church, outside the mapped excavation area; its own note calls that distance schematic.

## What this does not settle

No source read here says which part of the two large pools Schick's pool is, or where the dam lies relative to St Anne's. The model is not moved. Gibson 2005 (*POC* 55, Fig. 1; in the owner's Drive) or Pierre & Rousée 1981 Figs. 2–3 would give that tie. The 1888 levels are of the pool's floor and the 1888 surface, not of an ancient ground; they are not used as ancient ground.
