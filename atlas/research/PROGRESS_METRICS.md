## Definitions

- **Identification**: a source-supported reading, observed feature relations, the relevant phase and a justified datum, which together distinguish the candidate from plausible alternatives or controls. Generic resemblance, a restored name, period-compatible pottery, a convenient numerical fit or an excavator's function label alone does not count. A landmark can count without a deposit.
- **Whole-candidate exclusion**: a stated entry/feature hypothesis contradicted across all its retained branches.
- **Conditional model exclusion**: one material, date, origin or unit model rejected, with its conditions kept. It does not reject the site or region; state any assumed date for the scroll.
- **Nonunique comparison**: another documented feature meets the tested subset of constraints. Record which it meets and which not. A selected control set gives no random-match probability.
- **Excavation-coverage record**: the ancient target, phase/datum, excavation footprint and depth, disturbance and detection limits. A published plan or an excavated site is not coverage. A **negative excavation** counts only if the excavation examined the target's relevant volume and could detect the feature or deposit; earlier removal is not assumed to rescue a failed model.
- **Unexcavated-alternative inventory**: built from entry constraints and a declared regional boundary, with surveyed, excavated, unexcavated, destroyed and status-unknown features under the same criteria and their detection coverage recorded. Atlas markers are inputs, not a denominator.
- **Rarity** needs a defined comparable sample, a fixed region, and constraints and tolerances frozen before fits are seen.
- Several scopes in one publication, or several publications from one excavation, are one observation. Later citations, reprints and inherited plans add no corroboration.
