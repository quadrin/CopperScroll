## What each counter means

An **independently discriminated identification** joins a source-supported reading, observed feature relations, relevant phase and justified datum/uncertainty, then distinguishes the candidate from plausible alternatives or controls. Generic resemblance, restored place names, period-compatible pottery, a convenient numerical fit or an excavator's functional label alone does not qualify. An identified landmark can count without a recovered deposit; these remain separate outcomes.

A **whole-candidate exclusion** contradicts a stated entry/feature hypothesis across its retained defensible branches. Rejecting one material, date, origin or unit model counts as a **conditional model exclusion** with the conditions preserved. It does not reject the region or site. If a candidate is excluded only under a particular date for the scroll, state that date assumption.

A **nonunique comparison** shows that another documented feature satisfies the tested subset of constraints. Record which constraints the control does and does not satisfy. A small selected control set cannot establish a random-match probability. Estimating rarity requires a defined comparable sample, fixed search region and constraints/tolerances frozen before viewing fits.

An **excavation-coverage record** identifies the proposed ancient target, archaeological phase/datum, excavation footprint and depth, intervening disturbance and detection limits. A published plan or an excavated site does not establish target coverage. Count a **negative excavation at the predicted target** only when evidence shows that the excavation examined the target's relevant volume and could detect the proposed feature/deposit. Record uncertainty and alternative explanations; do not assume earlier removal to preserve a failed model.

An **unexcavated-alternative inventory** starts from entry constraints and a declared regional boundary. Include surveyed, excavated, unexcavated, destroyed and status-unknown features under the same selection criteria. Record how maps/surveys detect them and where coverage is incomplete. Existing atlas markers are inputs to this audit, not a complete regional denominator. Distinguish modern observations and archaeological function interpretations from claims about the ancient wording.
