# Site exploration and photo integration

The atlas now has two primary views: **Explore** and **Read the scroll**. Find opens searchable entry browsing. The selected map card's **Explore candidate** opens an interactive 3D site explorer for Qumran, Jericho royal palaces, Siloam, Gerizim, Doq, Hyrcania and the provisional Abu Saraj IV/17 comparison. Candidates without a specific source plan open a catalogue of documented regional excavations and the entry’s unresolved feature requirements. Regional plans are context, not substitute identifications. Saved older city-map views migrate to this archaeological starting point.

## Current interaction

- The default local site view is a 3D schematic. Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom; switch Pan/Rotate or right-drag to orbit. Top gives a plan view, and Show whole site restores the overview. Keyboard arrows pan, Shift+left/right rotates, and plus/minus zooms. Select a numbered structure or its listed feature to focus the camera and open the inspector.
- Source plan retains the original image or project tracing in its independent pan/zoom frame. Historical Sheet XI stays a two-dimensional survey map.
- The cave roof/cutaway toggle exposes the two openings and pillar. Pool interiors and cistern mouths are open volumes rather than filled blocks. Model assumptions lists every display height and simplification.
- The inspector connects the selected feature to its description, source references, interpretation limits and relevant scroll passage.
- Qumran uses the existing independent Ilan–Amit and Reeder–Jol source-plan frames. The intake, basin, proposed dam, branch/short tunnel, boulder and long tunnel remain separate proposals.
- Jericho offers the licensed Area AC figure and early/later reconstructed Pools Complex figures. A(C)90, A(C)94, AC44, the distribution basin and aqueduct approaches have approximate image selection regions. The northern complex remains separate from Area AC.
- Siloam uses a labelled project tracing of selected W1, L103, L102/L104 and W3 relations from Greenhut & Mazor 2020 Plan 1, p. 74*. It is a simplified source-relative diagram.
- Photos opens the candidate's existing annotated photograph in the same inspector. Select a photographic detail, pan/zoom, or enlarge the image. Photograph highlights retain their image coordinates. A source-plan feature is not automatically equated with a photographed feature.
- Regional terrain is a secondary physical-geography locator. Modern buildings, street geometry, land-use/POIs and modern-raster fallback have been removed. Historical-map records remain in the optional archive tools.
- Back and browser Back/Forward restore entry, candidate, source plan, feature, photo/detail selection and recorded map/model/plan/photo cameras and model navigation mode. Reload restores the saved history entry. Scroll column changes also enter browser history.
- Historical maps are under Map layers. Dossiers and research tools remain available on demand. The persistent three-panel register and primary Landscapes/Scene navigation have been removed.

Source-plan geometry is not geographic geometry. These figures have no established registration to the terrain; registration requires control points and a recorded residual error before any traced feature can become a terrain overlay. Source and transformation details are in [the manifest](../research/assets/plans/atlas-explorer/manifest.json), mirrored under `atlas/research/`.

## 3D geometry and source limits

`app/atlas-model.tsx` renders the models using Three.js 0.186.1. `app/atlas-model-geometry.ts` builds source-related volumes, open basins, cistern shafts and selected steps. The renderer uses orthographic projection, selectable feature markers, warm stone materials and shadows. It renders on camera changes rather than running a permanent animation loop.

IV/17 uses a sampled Plan 5 outline and its 3 m scale. Its uniform 1.55 m height is a display midpoint within Sion’s published 1.4–1.7 m range; rock thickness, roof cap and blocking-wall height are illustrative. Hyrcania retains the northern approximately 5 m maximum depth and southern 2.6 m depth separately, and uses the midpoint 5.1 m of the reported 4.8–5.4 m rock separation. Other pool, bridge and summit dimensions are qualified. Gerizim uses the reported enclosure, mansion, building IV, citadel and eastern stair dimensions. Its platforms and most heights simplify the source; the western landing is not equated with a southern flight. Doq and the existing Qumran/Siloam/Area AC frames use relative geometry with illustrative depths and heights. Early and joined Jericho pool states remain separate.

The original source view, attribution and feature evidence are available from each model. Architectural assumptions appear in the model itself, with detailed source and transformation metadata in the existing mirrored manifest.

The model’s surrounding ground now comes from Mapterhorn’s modern elevation tiles around the existing mapped site anchor. A feathered local bench exposes the architectural volumes; room divisions, model position and compass bearing remain schematic. The flat display plinths have been removed. Gerizim’s illustrative platforms blend into sloping ground. IV/17 has no geographic anchor, so its natural rock setting is clearly labelled illustrative. Elevation tiles, decoded samples, source licences, hashes and sampling limits are archived in the [terrain manifest](../research/assets/plans/atlas-explorer/terrain/manifest.json) and its atlas mirror.
