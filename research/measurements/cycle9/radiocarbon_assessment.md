## Sample and analytical context

Boaretto reports one dated specimen, wood charcoal **RTT 6089**, extracted from plaster collected in **L108, B1028**, near the outer face of the wall damming the Siloam Pool. Although the opening sentence refers to plaster samples in the plural, the article supplies one radiocarbon determination. It gives no second dated sample, wall number, numbered coat, wall elevation or sample coordinate. (p. 79 / PDF 2)

Associated fill abutting this plaster, **L107 and L109**, contained solely Iron Age IIB pottery according to the cited Greenhut–Mazor report. Its archaeological expectation spans the seventh century to 586 BCE. That expectation concerns the sample's associated dam context. (p. 79)

The plaster contains a friable binder and aggregates of varying size. Microscope inspection found charred fragments several millimetres across embedded in the plaster. RTT 6089 underwent Acid–Alkali–Acid pretreatment. It lost **79.9%** of its mass, which Boaretto interprets as poor preservation. Carbon comprised **52%** of the cleaned material, consistent with charred remains. The article reports no initial mass, cleaned mass, wood species, ring position, replicate determination or measured δ13C value. (p. 79)

D-REAMS Radiocarbon Laboratory at the Weizmann Institute analyzed the specimen. Its conventional radiocarbon age is **2300 ± 55 BP**, with BP defined against 1950. The authors correct ages for fractionation to the standard **δ13C = −25‰** wood value. Calibration uses **IntCal20** (Reimer et al. 2020) and **OxCal v4.4.2**. (p. 79; references pp. 80–81)

## Actual calibrated ranges

At 1σ, body text gives total probability **68.2%**, divided into **410–350 BCE, 37.5%**, and **290–210 BCE, 30.7%**. Fig. 1 prints the heading **68.3%**, although its two rounded components add to 68.2%. Preserve that small published discrepancy. (p. 79 text; p. 80 Fig. 1)

At 2σ, body text gives a **540–190 BCE** envelope with **95.4%** probability. Image-checked Fig. 1 separates that probability into **540–530 BCE, 0.3%**, and **520–190 BCE, 95.1%**. The gap between these intervals is outside the plotted 95.4% set. (pp. 79–80)

Consequently, **520–200 BCE at 95.4%** does not reproduce this article's final calibration exactly. It omits the low-probability early interval, rounds or changes the younger endpoint, and attaches the combined probability to a shortened range. Cite the complete final result as the two 95.4% components, or use the **540–190 BCE envelope** while identifying it as an envelope. The quoted 520–200 BCE summary can remain attributed to its own publication.

Boaretto attributes the widened older boundary at 2σ to the Hallstatt Plateau; calendar calibration has multiple intervals because of irregularities in the calibration curve. (pp. 79–80)

## Interpretation and unresolved discrepancy

Boaretto assigns the calibrated result to the Persian/Hellenistic periods and offers two interpretations: the age may approximate plaster production, or it may provide a **terminus post quem** because a small piece of wood charcoal can retain an old-wood age. Neither interpretation identifies an exact construction year. Sample association with a plaster coat remains an archaeological constraint, separate from its measured radiocarbon age. (p. 80)

The article explicitly leaves unexplained the disagreement between this result and the **seventh-century–586 BCE** expectation from archaeological material and stratigraphy. It calls for future excavation and additional samples. It supplies no tested mechanism of contamination, repair, intrusive charcoal or residual pottery. Poor preservation and the old-wood qualification are stated limitations; the article does not establish either as the cause of the conflict. (p. 80)

The radiocarbon article supplies no coin dates and makes no comparison with late-first-century CE pool foundation coins. Applying those coin dates to RTT 6089 would require the separate excavation report's context correspondence. Parent project's parallel Greenhut–Mazor audit reports L108 as yellowish plaster on lower W1 below the later pool floor, with foundation coins assigned to different loci. That is cross-source information awaiting integration into the joint note, rather than a finding from this three-page article.
