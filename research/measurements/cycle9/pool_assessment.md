## Identity

The authors explicitly connect their plastered pool L102/L104 to the southeastern corner exposed by Weksler-Bdolah in 2013, with six narrow steps, identifying Area B in Fig. 2 and citing Weksler-Bdolah and Szanton 2014. This appears on p. 75* / PDF 6. Fig. 2, p. 72* / PDF 3, places the 2009 excavation immediately beside the dam and the 2014 Area B installation. Thus the 2009 pool is connected by the authors to the southern stepped pool. Earlier project inspection established L124 → W50 → Guthe plate III through the 2014/2022 reports; those locus names and Guthe plate reference do not appear in this 2020 text. Record this as a chained published correspondence, not a fresh direct equation of every identifier in this article.

The report does not identify its pool with the tunnel-mouth basin GHJK on Guthe plate II, or with Bliss and Dickie’s small grooved tank. Its introductory Bliss reference concerns the old pool/dam excavation and tunnelling over the southern wall (pp. 71*–72*, PDF 2–3), rather than the particular small tank. These identities remain separate.

## Observations and relative sequence

The 2009 rescue excavation covered about 50 m² outside the southeastern face of the main dam W1. The exposed dam segment was about 12.5 m long; the larger visible wall is described as about 50 m long and 3.5 m wide. The attached pool was only partially exposed: its excavated northwestern part measured 8.4 m long, 3 m wide and 3.1 m high. Those values describe the excavated portion, not a complete pool footprint. (pp. 72*–75*, PDF 3–6; Figs. 2–3; Plan 1.)

The authors distinguish upper dam-face plaster phases a, b and c, assigned by their character to the Second Temple period. Pool north wall W3 carries phase-b plaster; this observation supports a later pool installation against an already plastered dam. A crack follows much of the junction between the floor and western wall. The authors infer settlement and short use; a crack supplies no calendar date independently. (p. 75*, PDF 6.)

The floor test exposed foundation L105/L105A, then clean gravel, soil/stone fill L107, and brown soil L109 containing Iron Age II pottery. Excavation stopped for safety before reaching the dam foundation or bedrock. All lower exposed deposits abutted W1; no foundation trench cutting them was recognized. The authors infer a dam date no later than the late First Temple period. (pp. 75*–76*, PDF 6–7; Fig. 4; Plan 1.)

**Recording nuance:** p. 75* note 2 says L105A was assigned during material processing. It represented the upper part of L107, resembled L105, and was classified with the pool foundation; it yielded the three earliest excavation coins. Preserve this context qualification. It does not establish a specific disturbance mechanism.

Below the pool floor, W1 bore a yellowish plaster recorded as L108. This lower wall lacked the Second Temple plaster coats visible above the floor. The authors compare its character/texture to tunnel and inner dam plaster, but do not demonstrate identical material or application date. Their reported radiocarbon range is 520–200 BCE at 95.4%; Boaretto’s original calibration text/curve must control any refined endpoints. This dates material in the lower dam-face plaster context and cannot automatically date the later pool floor or upper coats. (p. 75*, PDF 6; Plan 1 section 2–2.)

Channel L103 crosses W1 and enters the pool. Its exposed length is 2.2 m, width 0.65 m and depth 2.3 m. Most of it was unplastered, although one thickly plastered stone was observed about 0.8 m above its bottom. The authors infer a formerly plastered conduit. An irregular cut through the pool plaster supports their interpretation that the channel postdates the pool. Its bottom stands 0.75 m above the pool bottom and above the lowest excavated main-pool level; overflow transfer is the proposed function. This is a measured conduit, with no published identification as the Copper Scroll’s trough. (p. 76*, PDF 7; Fig. 5; Plan 1.)

## Calendar dating and limitations

The authors report seven coins from pool foundation L105/L105A: Ariel catalogue nos. 1–3, 8, 15, 18 and 20. They place the foundation assemblage in the later first century CE. Pottery catalogue Fig. 9 assigns items 1–4, 6–9, 12–13, 15–16, 18 and 20 to L105; the catalogue itself gives no independent construction year. Their conclusion assigns pool construction to the Great Revolt, possibly its final year, explicitly treating the assemblage as evidence for installation rather than later plaster/foundation repairs. (p. 75*, PDF 6; pp. 80*–83*, PDF 11–14; English p. 137, PDF 18.)

The article contains no individual coin descriptions, images or mint-date catalogue. Therefore this pass verifies the authors’ assertion and context references, but cannot independently establish the youngest foundation coin or an exact final-Revolt-year terminus post quem. The next source is Donald T. Ariel’s companion *The Coins from the City of David (‘Ir David), Siloam Road*, in the same volume, particularly the seven numbered specimens and L105A context.

Pool fill L102/L104 and channel fill accumulated before a laid stone layer, up to 1 m thick, whose function remains unclear. The authors suggest a defense-related sealing in the Revolt’s last stages. They also report two post-Revolt jar rims, Fig. 9:21–22, from L102, about 2 of 93 assemblage items. They retain two explanations: later local activity contributing those items, or a post-destruction terminus post quem for the stone covering. Therefore a strictly pre-70 CE sealing remains an interpretation needing context audit. (p. 77*, PDF 8; p. 82*, PDF 13; Figs. 6–7.)

The authors explicitly acknowledge that the lower plaster radiocarbon result is later than their archaeological late First Temple dam date and cannot explain the mismatch. This disagreement concerns W1 chronology. The earlier project’s 2014 Hasmonean/Herodian assignment for the southern stepped pool and the 2020 Revolt-construction assertion now belong to the same published pool correspondence; preserve both dated interpretations for comparison. (pp. 83*–84*, PDF 14–15.)

## Result

The pool-identity access question is resolved at the published-correspondence level. Pool chronology and the separate trough/tank identity remain unresolved. L103 provides a bounded future conduit assessment with observed dimensions and relative cutting sequence. None of these results confirms a Copper Scroll deposit location, changes the ancient reading, or supplies a northern/compass/depth instruction to entry 49.
