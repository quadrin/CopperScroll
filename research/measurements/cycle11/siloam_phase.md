# Siloam: earlier dam phases and the year-four pool foundation

Inspected 2 October 2026, UTC. This thread compares the proposed earlier pool phase against the original-source results from cycles 9–10. It adds a new scoped reading of the 2025 dam study. Candidate coordinates and grades remain unchanged.

## 2024 phase proposal

Nahshon Szanton, Filip Vukosavović and Itamar Berko, *New archaeological discoveries in the excavations of Birket al-Hamra in Jerusalem*, in Eyal Meiron (ed.), *City of David: Studies of Ancient Jerusalem*, 25th Annual Conference (2024), printed pp. 23*–42*. [English PDF](https://cityofdavid.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/New-archaeological-discoveries-in-the-excavations-of-Birket-al-Hamra-in-Jerusalem.pdf).

Fresh text scope: pp. 31*–36*, especially 33*–35*. The authors distinguish older W114/W185A from later staircase construction. W114 has successive plaster coatings. Their p. 33*, note 3, explicitly leaves the W114/dam contact unresolved because excavation stopped at depth and incoming water. Page 34* tentatively attaches Greenhut–Mazor’s dam-top pool to the proposed early phase using Boaretto’s 520–200 BCE summary. Pages 34*–35* discuss that small southeastern receiving pool separately from Birket al-Hamra and the tunnel-mouth Silwan pool. No L105/L105A foundation contact or Ariel coin-20 assessment accompanies the proposal.

The original PDF binary still returned HTTP 403. Web text supports this assessment; screenshot calls supplied text placeholders rather than usable image payloads. No figure was independently image-checked from that report.

## Original 2020 contexts retained

Independent image review of Greenhut–Mazor p. 75* / supplied PDF 6 confirms the yellowish L108 plaster sample below the pool floor and the foundation loci beneath that floor. The report separates upper coatings and lower plaster. Cycle 9 supplies the final laboratory calibration; cycle 10 supplies the independently checked catalogue association **coin 20 / L105 / basket 1092 / 69–70 CE**. Those existing inspections add no source increment here.

A floor constructed after that coin’s minting can coexist with an older dam. Assigning that same floor to Persian/Hellenistic construction would require an independently established older pool floor, a revised foundation association or another demonstrated sequence. The 2024 passage supplies none of those links. Its cited radiocarbon material dates the earlier dam context. The late foundation terminus therefore remains the eligible constraint for the excavated small-pool floor and the proposed later L103 installation.

## New 2025 dam study

Johanna Regev, Nahshon Szanton, Filip Vukosavović, Itamar Berko, Yiftah Shalev, Joe Uziel, Eugenia Mintz, Lior Regev and Elisabetta Boaretto, *Radiocarbon dating of Jerusalem’s Siloam Dam links climate data and major waterworks*, PNAS 122(35), e2510396122 (2025). [Publisher](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2510396122) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12415248/).

Full main-article HTML, methods and both figures inspected. Three mortar samples came from vertical W001: RTD 12353 and 12351 uncharred straw, 12352 charred twigs. RTD 12948 was straw in W114’s sloping-core mortar. The authors combine them to **805–795 BCE (68.3%) / 809–792 BCE (95.4%)**. They attribute fresh-straw age to construction. Figure 1 separates the sampled wall parts; its map has a 50 m scale and north arrow, without a coordinate grid or vertical datum. Figure 2 labels those samples separately from tunnel, tower and pit comparisons.

These measurements give evidence about dam construction, with a newly sampled material/context basis. They establish no identity between the sampled mortar and small-pool foundation L105, sample L108/RTT 6089 or conduit L103. The article cites the older date without explaining the discrepancy. Its supporting appendix remains unread.
