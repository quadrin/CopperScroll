## Separate premises

1. Puech restores a pool destination in IV 3 from fragment traces, available space and sense (p. 50). His claim concerns an edition restoration; the destination is not fully surviving manuscript wording.
2. His Koḥlit association comes from the preceding cistern context. Entry 16 itself supplies no independently preserved Koḥlit name. Entry 15's Koḥlit and northern direction also depend on restoration (pp. 49–50).
3. Entry 11 independently mentions a pool east of Koḥlit in the edition tradition. Identifying entry 16's destination with that same pool would add another assumption. Shared installation vocabulary does not establish identity.
4. Puech reads fourteen cubits and interprets distance rather than depth (p. 51). This branch requires a canal entrance, inward axis and period-specific surface. A pool corner, cistern pillar or tell-centre marker does not automatically provide that datum.
5. An exploratory fourteen-cubit interval is 5.6–8.4 m for 0.40–0.60 m units; the narrower 0.445–0.525 m scenario yields 6.23–7.35 m. Without a phase-specific entrance and measuring line, these are lengths only. No coordinate, ring or corridor is generated.

## Ancient name witnesses

The earlier statement that no ancient outside text *places* Koḥlit means no geographically decisive witness was recovered; it should not be read as absence of outside name attestations. Phase 2 already records b. Qiddushin 66a and m. Parah 11:7. Puech p. 14 explicitly discusses the former's desert setting and warns that the name need not be unique. These are rabbinic textual traditions with their own composition/transmission history, not contemporary geographically located inscriptions at Tell es-Sultan. This worker inspected Puech's discussion, not original rabbinic page images or manuscript witnesses. The hyssop name alone locates no pool. The broad desert description cannot select Jericho, Samaria, Carmel or Transjordan by itself.
