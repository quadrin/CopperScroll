# Entry 29: Herodian joined basin A(B)101

## Result

The joined A(B)101 pool supports a distinct, phase-compatible reservoir comparison: Netzer associates it with Herod's Second Palace and reports a surviving Herodian drainage channel east across the ruined storerooms. Its approximately 32 × 18 m outline rotates the longer-side reference from the earlier pair's north/south sides to the merged basin's west/east sides. The earlier separate-pool offsets cannot be reused. The joined basin remains low-confidence and inconclusive: its supply inlet is unknown, the pool-wall outlet junction is not located in the inspected record, and the restored measurement instruction supplies neither displacement direction nor a unique wall face or measurement axis.

## Observations, reconstructions and phases

**Original report p. 74:** the approximately 32 × 18 m pool was visible before excavation. All sides except the eastern side survived almost to original height. The western walls of the earlier two pools align; their eastern walls have a slight angular difference. The report recognizes a central podium created in the joining. These facts support a broadly four-sided external basin, while qualifying an exact rectangular footprint. Original walls taper: approximately 1.5 m at the base versus 1.2 m at the top, except the southern pool's southern wall. The published 32 × 18 m dimensions do not specify the measurement face. Neither the top, inner waterline, outer wall nor structural underside should be silently equated.

**Hydraulic controls, pp. 78–84:** the original Na'aran route between the earlier pools was removed when they joined. Its course was reconstructed from surviving sections. The northern-side supply branch and A(B)99 outlets belong to successive Hasmonean arrangements. The original report explicitly leaves the joined basin's Herodian supply unknown (p. 84). A surviving old channel is therefore insufficient to establish a contemporaneous Herodian inlet or a start for route-distance measurement.

**Herodian surface, pp. 85 and 100:** the Plaza became a formal garden, with about 0.30 m soil above its earlier plaster floor. The author associates joining with the Second Palace, while allowing later flowerpot installation. The garden-to-rim level relationship remains unresolved: no raised-rim additions or proposed perimeter retaining walls survived. W57/W43 are observed walls; their function as a channel base and connection with another channel are interpretations. The inferred southern route cannot be promoted to a surveyed inlet. The Second Palace phase gives chronological compatibility with subsequent first-century CE use, without demonstrating a concealment date or uninterrupted availability of every datum.

**Eastern outlet, pp. 87 and 131–133:** the report attributes a channel from the merged A(B)101 basin across the filled eastern storerooms toward fields to the Herodian period. A segment was found in Storeroom A(B)171; the author also links destruction of an earlier W331/W338 corner to this channel. This is a phase-specific archaeological conduit comparison. The inspected text gives no measured pool-wall aperture, centerline tie, profile or surviving connection at the east side of A(B)101. Do not equate it with the separate Grand Bypass/Na'aran junction at A(B)239: that junction is documented, but no inspected observation identifies it as the merged pool's outlet connection. A later blocked southern offshoot at A(B)239 also cannot supply an inlet anchor.
