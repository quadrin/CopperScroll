## What the epochs show

1. **The SWP wadi line is generalised.** On the georeferenced sheet, the wadi line runs about 150–250 m south of the real gorge, which the 1940s map, the 1940s photo and the 2023 image agree on. The label is printed north of the line, so "the north side of the valley" is the north side of the real gorge, not of the SWP line.
2. **The lower Wadi Ekteif gorge makes a loop here.** On the 1940s map and photo, the gorge loops north and back. The modern Jerusalem–Dead Sea road (Route 1) follows the loop's western limb and cuts across the top of the loop.
3. **The road already existed by 25 June 1974** as a single carriageway (HEXAGON F014). It is not on the 1940s map, whose main road runs farther north-west by the "Old Police Post". So it was built between the 1940s and 1974.
4. **In 2023 the road is a dual carriageway.** Light fill and spoil cover the inside of the loop and the north wall of the gorge's eastern reach. There are culvert outfalls and a straight channel that runs down from the road into a deep head-cut hollow. In 1974 this ground was still natural.
5. **Parts of the north side that survive.** (Z1) the north bank of the western limb, about 400 m long, between the road and the higher ground. The vegetated channel and its north slope are intact in 2023. (Z2) the deep head-cut hollow inside the loop, visible on both 1974 and 2023. (Z3) the parts of the eastern reach's north wall that are not under fill.
6. **No cut of the SWP size is visible** in Z1–Z3 at 0.5 m (2023), about 1 m (1974) or about 2 m (1940s). The SWP describes a passage about 37 m long that slopes about 1 in 2 with cut steps and ends at a choked chamber. Such a passage would be 1–3 m wide and largely roofed or in shadow. At these resolutions, absence in the images says little.

## Conclusions

- **Position.** If the SWP placed the label correctly, the excavation is on the north side of the lower Wadi Ekteif gorge, within the loop reach. Most of that north side is now under Route 1 and its fill. So destruction or burial during road building between the 1940s and 2023 is the most likely single explanation for why no survey since 1873 records it. INFERENCE: this has not been checked on the ground.
- **What survives to check.** Z1 (the north bank of the western limb) and Z2 (the head-cut hollow) are the only intact parts of the north side near the label. They are the field targets, in that order. The positions are in `private/E24b/`.
- **What it was.** A passage 37 m long that slopes 1 in 2 (a drop of about 16 m) and has cut steps fits a stepped access to a cistern or water chamber better than a tomb entrance. Tomb dromoi in this region are short. ARGUMENT: Milik's "probablement un tombeau" is the weaker reading of the SWP description. A vaulted or roofed underground chamber would suit the word Kippa ("vault") at least as well as "tomb" does.
- **Entry 24 (ARGUMENT).**
  - The lower Wadi Ekteif lies where the Jericho–Nebi Musa tracks cross the wadi. The SWP's road from Jericho up to the Buqeiʿa and Hyrcania also starts here (Memoirs III p. 188; card C2-3).
  - If Secacah is in the Buqeiʿa (Cross & Milik's Kh. es-Samra, or Kh. Abu Ṭabaq), this wadi is on the natural way from Jericho to Secacah. If Secacah is Kh. Qumran, the wadi is near the route along the foot of the escarpment.
  - So Wadi Kuteif / Ekteif stays a reasonable reading of "the ravine of the Kippa on the way from Jericho to Secacah". The Mughâret Ekteif excavation is the only recorded artificial chamber in it, but its kind and date are unknown.
  - The identification rests on the route and on one undated, unvisited feature that has probably been damaged. It is plausible, not shown.
- **Next useful observation.**
  - A field visit to Z1 and Z2.
  - Road-construction photographs or reports for Route 1 near Nebi Musa (1950s–70s), which may record a rock-cut passage found during the works.
  - Higher-resolution imagery than 0.5 m (e.g. a drone survey) of Z1.
