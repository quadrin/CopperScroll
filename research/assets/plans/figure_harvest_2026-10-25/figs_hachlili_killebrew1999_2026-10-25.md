## Most useful for which entry, and what could be measured

1. **Entry 60 ("a pit … graves at its mouth"), form analogue. INFERENCE from EVIDENCE.** The typical Jericho tomb (p. 50) is a square chamber with a rock-cut **standing pit** in the floor; benches are left on three sides and the **kokhim open off the pit at bench level**. The best plan-and-photo pairs: **Tomb A2 (II.5 + II.6, pit with kokhim 3–6)**, **Tomb F4 (II.62, deep central pit with steps)**, **Tomb D12 (II.39–41, coffins on benches and in pit)**, **Tomb H (II.68–77)**. Measurable: pit L × W × depth against kokh openings (Table II.3 gives chamber and pit dimensions; e.g. A1 pit 150 × 170 × 33 cm per OCR, to be checked on the image). This is a "pit with graves at its mouth" in miniature *inside* a tomb, not an open pit beside a settlement. Whether entry 60's pit could be such a chamber pit is a reading question, not settled here.
2. **Entrance facing.** EVIDENCE: Hill A entrances all face north (p. 5); Hills B and D mostly east (pp. 9, 12); Hill F south examples (p. 51); the authors state entrances "face all directions" (p. 51). Tomb H is entered from the courtyard on its north side (II.67). Measurable: facing of every tomb from the north arrows on II.1, II.2 and the individual plans. For entry 60, this argues **against** reading a compass rule into tomb entrances at Jericho.
3. **Tomb and entrance dimensions.** Tables II.3–II.5 (pp. 53–57) give chambers, entrances, sealing stones and kokhim in cm. Every plan has a metre bar and a north arrow, so the tables can be checked. The kokh standard cited from rabbinic sources (4 cubits long, 7 spans high, 6 wide; p. 53) can be tested against Table II.5.
4. **Dated contexts.** EVIDENCE: Tomb D18 (II.52) coins of Yehohanan Hyrcanus I near the entrance and of Herod Archelaus (p. 28); Tomb D9 (II.30, II.35) two coins of Agrippa I in a skull in Kokh 1 (p. 16); Tomb H and its mourning enclosure Herodian (Netzer, pp. 45–49; II.68–83). Chapter II's summary (p. 57) dates the coffin tombs to the 1st c. BCE, with bone collection in ossuaries in the 1st c. CE. Useful as a dated Jericho baseline for any "graves" reading of entries 59/60.
5. **Aqueduct and water features near tombs.** EVIDENCE: aqueduct from ʿEin ed-Duyuk along Hill B's crest (p. 9; drawn on II.1); plastered steps to a miqveh on Hill C (II.20); Tomb H's courtyard miqveh. The mourning enclosure was first noticed "during a survey of ancient water channels" (p. 45). Measurable: tomb-to-aqueduct offsets on II.1 (0–20 m bar).
6. **Blocking stones.** II.84 typology, II.10 (reused capitals), II.32–33, II.56; three sealing methods described on p. 53. Comparanda for any entry that speaks of a stone at an opening.
