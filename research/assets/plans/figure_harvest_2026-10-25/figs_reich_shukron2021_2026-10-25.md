## Concealment-relevant observations in the text (EVIDENCE, exploratory; quotes ≤ 12 words)
- **Shaft above the spring, blocked:** found "blocked with a large boulder stuck firmly in it", covered by debris with 1st-century BCE pottery (Locus 806, Area C); the shaft is 3.25 m deep (p. 267; Figs 13.10–13.13).
- **Channel II covered by boulders:** the authors say Channel II was covered by large shapeless boulders "pushed down the rocky, sloping surface into the channel", not by cover slabs (p. 362; Figs 13.62–13.72, 13.76–13.79).
- **Openings blocked with masonry:** a Channel II opening blocked with masonry (Fig. 13.91, p. 321); an opening into a karstic cavity blocked by Wall 151 (Fig. 13.102, p. 325); Shiloh's plastered cavity closed by Wall 61 (p. 358).
- **Early Roman ashlar doorway beside the Spring House:** threshold with door socket and a low door stop; the door opened inwards (northwards); an iron "shoe" on the door pivot was found in the socket (pp. 293–296; Figs 13.42–13.50).
- **Area L** is a refuse ravine about 150 m north of the Gihon spring, not the L103 conduit of the Siloam receiving pool (p. 464; already recorded in `research/sources/new_batch_2026-10-13/root_water_scope.md`).

These are comparanda for how openings and channels were closed in the 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE. They change no rating, test or outcome.
