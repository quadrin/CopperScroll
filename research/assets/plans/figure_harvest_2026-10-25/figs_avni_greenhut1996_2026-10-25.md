## Site and dates (EVIDENCE, text)
- Three rock-cut burial caves at the confluence of the Kidron and Hinnom valleys, lower Hinnom, south of the Old City (p. 1; Figs 1.1–1.2). Caves 1 and 2 share one courtyard; Cave 3 lies beside Cave 1 behind a thin rock wall.
- Use: Jewish burial from the mid-1st c. BCE (Cave 1, Chamber A: a coin of 40 BCE in the lowest layer) to 70 CE; a coin of 67 CE in Ossuary 9 (Cave 2, Chamber B) is the latest Jewish use; Late Roman cremations (2nd–5th c. CE lamps); Byzantine mass burials (pp. 32–34).
- Families named by ossuary inscriptions: the "Eros" family (Cave 2) and the "Ariston" family of Apamea (Cave 3) (pp. 15, 23; ch. 3). The OCR of ch. 3 finds no Zadok, Obed or Jericho.

## What this volume can test (INFERENCE)
- **Entry 56 (western resting chamber):** the plans give chamber and loculus orientations with north arrows, so the facing of each chamber and of each kokh can be measured; a dated (1st c. BCE–70 CE) comparison set of Jerusalem kokhim tombs, not a candidate.
- **Entries 53 (gallery tomb) and 54 (tomb of the common people):** dated multi-chamber loculus tombs with repositories; dimensions of chambers, loculi, steps and shafts are given in text and measurable on the plans. Comparanda only; the volume names no "Jericho" tomb.
- **Closures ("stones at the mouth"):** an in-situ square sealing stone with its recess (Cave 1), square blocking stones (Caves 2, 3) and two pivoting stone doors (Cave 3), all dated to the Second Temple phase by the authors.
- **Entry 51 (portico, exedra pillar, tomb of Zadok):** Cave 3, Chamber C has low-relief pilasters and pseudo-Doric capitals (p. 32), a decorative parallel only; no portico or exedra; no Zadok inscription.
- **Entries 2 and 48 (Kidron monuments):** nothing; Fig. 1.2 shows the lower Hinnom necropolis, not the Kidron monuments.
