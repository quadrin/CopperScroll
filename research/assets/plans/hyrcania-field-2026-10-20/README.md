## Group D, the tunnel interior

These are observations from the reduced copies.

- **Section:** 08921–08923 show a narrow, tall passage. With a person in the frame, it is about 0.6–0.8 m wide and above head height (INFERENCE from the person). The walls carry pick marks.
  - Steps are cut in the floor.
  - The passage narrows in a series of door-like frames, one behind the other.
  - 08931 shows a wider section with a rounded top.
- **Junction:** in 08934 a side passage opens to the right of the main one.
  - An electric cable lies on the floor here and again in 08937 (INFERENCE: lighting left from the excavation).
  - A dark painted mark on the ceiling can't be read at this resolution. The originals may show whether it is a survey mark or graffiti.
- **Fill:** 08937 shows a stepped passage going down, with loose rubble in it.
- **Wall marks:** 08936 shows small rectangular cuts and a metal peg in the wall. The photo is blurred.
- **Other finds:**
  - Modern litter and ash lie on the floor in 08931.
  - Two scorpions sit on rubble in 08924 and 08926.

**Limit:** photos record shape. They don't date the cutting, so they don't change ledger row S31 (Gutfeld's clearance, result unknown), and they don't decide what the tunnels were for.

## The guide's opinion (oral, 20 Oct 2026)

The owner relayed this by message at 22:33 local time. Achia Kohn-Tavor's view, as passed on:

> "the diggers were not stupid. 2 km to creek, and it regularly fills with silt/flood water. They must have wanted it to be sealed forever. It might have been a tomb for a king"

- This is an opinion, not a published finding. It is recorded as an unregistered observation, not as a test.
- "2 km to creek" is unclear as relayed. It could mean the tunnel's length, the distance to the stream, or the length of the catchment. Ask before anyone uses it.
- **Related ideas in hand:**
  - Haber & Gutfeld 2024 n. 4 raises a tomb idea for the SW monument (Antipater), per the field list.
  - BK, not checked here: Josephus says Herod's son Antipater was buried at Hyrcania.
  - Neither source puts a royal tomb in the tunnels.
- **Bearing:** the tunnel purpose remains unknown. The self-sealing idea (flood silt fills the tunnels) agrees with the rubble fill in 08937. It agrees equally with ordinary post-use silting, so it can't tell the readings apart. No reading, identification or confidence level changed.

## Owner's follow-up (20 Oct 2026, 22:57 local)

- **Lamp niches with soot:** present, per the owner. They are not in the photos above. Soot can come from any later visitor (Bedouin before 1960, the 1960 expedition, 2000–04), so niches alone don't date the cutting.
- **End walls:** the owner photographed a face that he says is the end. There are two phone selfies and one low-resolution frame (chat attachments, not in Drive).
  - The face behind him has the same coarse diagonal pick scars as the side walls. There is no dressing, no plaster and no blocking masonry.
  - INFERENCE: this is a working face where the cutting stopped, which fits an unfinished or abandoned project.
  - Caveat: from these frames it isn't certain that the face is the tunnel's end.
- **Pottery:** the owner says pottery was seen at the site. He photographed one sherd that he has with him (IMG_0415–0421, iPhone, photographed after the visit).
  - It is a small wheel-made body sherd in a red-orange fabric, with grey edges and a white crust. One surface has a faint horizontal ridge.
  - It has no rim, handle, base or decoration, so it is **not datable** from photos. A plain body sherd in this fabric can belong anywhere from Hellenistic to Early Islamic times.
  - Its findspot (tunnel fill, spoil heap or wadi bed) isn't recorded.
  - The HEIC files carry the phone's location at the time of the photo, which was not the site. They are not kept in the repo.
