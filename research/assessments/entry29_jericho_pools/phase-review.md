## Exact target and orientation

The target is the northern of the two large phase-3 pools in the Pools Complex, distinct from A(C)94 and A(C)90 in area AC. Fig. 18 (p. 281/PDF27) provides a full overview: north points up, and the target is the upper of the paired pools marked 3. It gives phase 3 as 103–76 BC, phase 5 as 76–67 BC, and phase 6 as 67–63 BC. These are palace phases; they are not independently precise dates for every drawn channel segment.

Figs. 20–22 (pp. 284–286/PDF30–32) rotate north to the right. Therefore the right-hand of the two partially shown pools is the northern pool. Each includes a 0–10 m scale. These are cropped plans of the western supply area: only western ends of the pools appear along the bottom edge. Full pool boundaries and eastern ends cannot be measured from these figures alone. Do not call the visible west edge a long side. Fig. 18 is useful to recover overall geometry, but registering it to a detailed plan adds uncertainty.

## Hydraulic chronology

The Pools Complex was built during Alexander Jannaeus’s enlargement of the estate (p. 283). The Na’aran aqueduct originally passed between its two large pools. The text reports an apparent slope error that prevented direct supply of both pools from that main branch. A separate higher channel and distribution installation further west presumably supplied the southern pool; a connecting pipe is reconstructed as supplying the northern pool from the southern one. Subsequently the northern pool received its own aqueduct branch. Later, after the main aqueduct was redirected around the complex, a western settling basin supplied both pools through two channels (pp. 284–285).

The prose describes three hydraulic arrangements but does not explicitly assign every sentence to the numbered palace phases. The figure captions explicitly identify palace phases 3, 5, and 6. Avoid equating the prose’s “third” hydraulic stage with palace phase 3. A geometry model can keep three separate figure-specific arrangements; precise feature dating remains conditional on the original report.

The decisive preservation qualification is on p. 285: Herodian remodeling left neither original inlet nor the inter-pool connecting pipe preserved. Thus a drawn connection is not a directly inspected preserved inlet. The plans contain solid and broken lines, but these pages supply no legend establishing that every solid line is excavated and every broken line restored. Use explicit text and captions to classify evidence; classify ambiguous segments as drawn in a reconstructed plan.

The paragraph continuing at the top of p. 284, including a preserved open-channel segment north of a pool’s center and an absent terminal connection, concerns A(C)94. Its elevation data and footnote 58 must not be transferred to the northern Pools Complex pool. Likewise the 12 cm pipe and 40 cm burial depth on p. 283 concern the estate/earliest palace supply, not this target’s northern inlet.

## Source lineage

The illustration credits (p. 296) identify:

- Fig. 18: Trümper after Netzer 2001a, p. 6, Fig. 8.
- Fig. 19: Trümper after Netzer 2001a, plan 13.
- Fig. 20: Netzer 2001b, p. 93, plan 17.
- Fig. 21: Netzer 2001b, p. 96, plan 19.
- Fig. 22: Netzer 2001b, p. 7, plan 20. This is the printed reference; its surprising page number has not been checked or corrected.

Netzer 2001a is *Hasmonean and Herodian Palaces at Jericho: Final Reports of the 1973–1987 Excavations*, Vol. I, *Stratigraphy and Architecture* (Jerusalem: Israel Exploration Society, 2001). Trümper lists Netzer 2001b as *The Palaces of the Hasmoneans and Herod the Great* (Mainz: Philipp von Zabern, 2001). The IES publisher record for the English 2001 edition instead names Jerusalem, Yad Ben-Zvi Press/Israel Exploration Society; the exact edition behind the reproduced pagination remains to be checked. They are separate books. The accessible reproduced phase plans are credited to the illustrated palace book, whereas Trümper’s hydraulic discussion cites the final report: pp. 74–84/plans 14, 17–21 (p. 285 n. 60); the aqueduct’s 0.60 m deficiency to p. 79 (p. 284 n. 59); rerouting to pp. 92–100/plans 17–22 (p. 283 n. 54).

Trümper’s discussion and reproduced plans belong to the same Netzer excavation evidence lineage. They provide an accessible scholarly assessment, not an additional independent excavation attestation.

## Limits and next discrimination

The phase plans are available and adequate for reproducible conditional channel-distance tests. They do not show a surviving pool inlet to attach the Copper Scroll distance to, and all three crop out the complete pool sides. Distances from a reconstructed channel to a selected side can support or conflict with that narrowly specified geometry model. They cannot establish an ancient inlet coordinate, date a concealed deposit, or identify the northern pool from a geometric coincidence alone.

A complete source-linked phase plan or excavation elevation record could test whether the relevant drawn channel and reference edge coexisted. That missing information is a remaining discriminator, not a reason to abandon measurements of the accessible figures. No original Netzer book pages were accessed in this review; their citation targets are recorded above. The publisher PDF itself was accessible.
