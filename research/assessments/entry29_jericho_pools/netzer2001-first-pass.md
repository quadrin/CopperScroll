## Observations and qualifications

- Printed p. 74 describes the approximately 32 × 18 m combined pool and the surviving central podium where the two pools were merged. The original walls taper: most are 1.5 m thick at the base and 1.2 m at the top, including a 0.4 m bench. The southern wall of the southern pool differs (2.0 m / 1.7 m). These are published measurements; an inner-face distance cannot be converted to an outer-face distance by one universal correction.
- Printed p. 82 treats the initial north-pool supply from the southern pool as an assumption. A subsequent small channel followed the northern sidewall of the Na’aran Conduit, diverging over its last 4.5 m toward the northern pool. Its addition is tentatively assigned to Stage 4, potentially Stage 3. Later construction obscured parts of this earlier channel.
- Printed p. 84 identifies two channels issuing from Settling Tank A(B)99. The northern one replaced the earlier northern-side channel and supplied the northern pool; the southern one supplied the southern pool. The excavator reports both preserved for almost their entire lengths. This system is assigned to the final Hasmonean supply arrangement.
- The same p. 84 explicitly leaves the supply of the combined pool in Herodian times unknown. Preserved Hasmonean channels therefore cannot simply be relabeled as demonstrated operating inlets for the merged basin.
- Printed p. 85 associates the merger and conversion of the plaza into a garden with Herod’s Second Palace. The garden attribution includes qualifications: flowerpots may be later, and the relationship of garden level to renovated pool wall tops remains unresolved. Low walls W57 and W43 are tentatively interpreted as a channel base potentially connected to the Herodian channel in the Western Garden. W43 continues along the southern side of the combined pool. These are a new plan-tracing target, not a verified inlet or measured Copper Scroll match.
