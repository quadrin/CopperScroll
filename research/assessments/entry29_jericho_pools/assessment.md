# Entry29: independent source-plan geometry audit

Source: Monika Trümper, “Swimming Pools and Water Management in the Eastern Mediterranean World of the 4th to 1st Century BC” (2018), printed pp.281,284–286, Figs18,20–22. Publisher PDF: https://edition-topoi.org/download_pdf/bsa_053_10.pdf . Images were inspected through local fitz rendering. The PDF was available. This reanalysis reproduces Netzer-derived plans; it is not an independent excavation record.

The north arrow points right in Figs20–22. The right-hand large pool is therefore the northern pool. All three detailed plans crop the pools at their lower edge: only the western short sides, stairs and short adjoining segments of the two long sides are visible. Fig18 shows the complete pools but its small overview scale endpoint could not be read confidently; no full-pool metric dimensions are claimed from it. Fig18 supplies orientation/aspect context. Numerical results below use the unambiguous 10m bars of Figs20–22.

The tested hypothesis is deliberately narrow: a perpendicular offset from either inner long-wall edge to the visible central route between the two pools, restricted to points whose perpendicular projections land on the visible finite wall segments. It does not test all channels, channel chainage, a vertical offset, or extended wall lines. Preserved wording does not uniquely select this measurement model. At the visible ends, the long-wall edges are parallel to the central route. Source plans are explicitly reconstructed; geometric agreement cannot establish an ancient inlet or construction phase.

Manual picks are recorded in measurements.json and recalculated by measure.py. Units are an exploratory0.40–0.60m per cubit:24cubits9.6–14.4m; published alternative27cubits10.8–16.2m. Each endpoint pick receives±3pixels; combined scale-length uncertainty±4pixels. A wider stress envelope uses±5pixels per pick and±6pixels on the scale length. Both are assumed envelopes, not confidence intervals. Propagated intervals quantify digitization only and exclude archaeological plan accuracy and unknown distortion.

For phases3 and5, the northern pool is about2.1–2.3m from the central route on its nearer long side, or15.7m from its opposite long side. The nearer manual intervals remain below2.9m; the far intervals are14.6–16.8m. Neither overlaps the24cubit exploratory band under the tighter manual envelope. The wider stress envelope gives14.05–17.43m for the northern far-wall offset and does overlap the24cubit band; the nominal miss is therefore not a robust exclusion. The27cubit band overlaps the far interval. Using the outer edge of the far wall would increase the distance, so cannot improve the24cubit fit; reference to the inner edge is explicitly an assumption.

The southern pool gives nearer offsets3.3–3.5m and an opposite-side offset16.8m. Its far uncertainty interval15.7–17.9m overlaps the27cubit band too. Thus a27cubit far-wall fit under these uncertainty assumptions does not distinguish the northern from the southern pool.

Phase6 has a visible central line whose identity as a water channel is unresolved. Its northern far-side offset14.2m, manual interval13.3–15.3m, intersects the24cubit band. This is an ambiguous-line geometric coincidence and is excluded from qualifying channel hits. It cannot rescue the candidate without identifying and phasing the line. The southern far offset17.4m lies outside both bands under the tighter envelope; the wider envelope intersects the27cubit band. This remains a geometric sensitivity result for an unclassified line.

The numerical scope supplies a nominal mismatch for the24cubit perpendicular central-route model in phases3/5 that is sensitive to digitization assumptions, and a nonunique27cubit overlap. It does not reject the pool identification globally. Original inlet positions and the connecting pipe are reported unpreserved (printedp.285). Chainage requires a justified junction/datum and remains untestable here. The complete eastern ends and alternative routes require a complete detailed plan or original report.
