# Nuseib ʿUweishira stepped pool: check for entry 57 (Jericho "Gerizim") — 15 November 2026

## Result first

1. **The full Hadashot notice adds no size.** I read the whole Nuseib ʿUweishira notice in HA 59–60 (1976) p. 38, all of it, from the Drive copy of the volume. It says: two weeks of test excavation in January 1976, as part of the Tulul Abu el-ʿAlayiq work; the site is on the north side of the Wadi Qelt outlet, about 1 km west of the Hasmonean palace and 800 m north of Cypros; a small Byzantine monastery with a chapel and a mosaic with a dedication; **below the Byzantine building, building remains and a stepped water pool** of a type common in late Second Temple remains; the earlier layer is not yet dated; **the moat at the west edge of the site** (like the moat west of Qarantal) may belong to that layer. No plan, no locus number, no size, no stair count.
2. **The project already had more, and the entry 57 row did not use it.** Three assessments of 5 November (Drive) read Netzer 1978, Netzer and Birger 1990, Netzer 2001 and Meshel 1978. With these, the pool is a **specific recorded feature**: locus 16, a stepped pool below room 14, in fabric that Fig. 7 of 1990 marks as Herodian; coated with grey lime plaster (1978). The row `jericho_gerizim` (PR 145) cited only HA p. 38 and Netzer 2006, and said the pool has "no recorded form". That statement was wrong. Correction row D27 is in the edit log.
3. **Against the rubric, F 2 is possible but not certain.** F 2 needs "a specific recorded feature with the landmark's kind plus at least one described relation or attribute; the others unknown and none contradicted". Locus 16 is specific and has steps (the entry's "step"). It is the installation on the summit, above the two cisterns that are north of the summit (the entry's "upper" — my inference, not a published relation). The weak point is the kind: the entry's word is a pit or shaft (שיח), and locus 16 is a stepped pool. The precedent in entry 1 (Jisr Abu Ghabush: a measured cistern with three steps, held at 1 because it is not known that its steps are the entry's steps) points to 1.
4. **No level was changed.** F stays 1 until the owner decides. If F goes to 2, D stays 2 (the pool's own date is not fixed; the Herodian label is the excavators' attribution, and HA says the earlier layer is not dated). The row would then be weak on P only.
5. **A separate data error was found.** In `water.csv`, row `WBADB-S2514-reservoir_pool` (Nusib ʿUweishira; survey reference "Hirschfeld 1983, Site 17") has the cross-link "same site as ASI 108/2/17". ## Fig. 7 of 1990, seen again today

- Locus 16 is drawn with about five treads, between rooms 14 and 4, in dotted (Herodian) fabric. Its foot has a spot level; I do not read the figures with confidence on the photograph.
- The drawn stair block is about 3 m by 1.6–1.9 m against the 0–10 m scale bar. This is an **order of size only**. The photograph is taken at an angle, and the plan may not draw the basin below the steps. It is not a measurement and I do not use it for a level. The 5 November assessment gave the same caution.

## The rubric, part by part (entry 57: "under the step of the upper pit")

| Element of the entry | Locus 16 | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Kind: pit or shaft (שיח / שית / שוחה) | a stepped plastered pool, small | **Arguable.** A stepped pool is a water installation cut or built down from a floor; the editions read a pit or shaft, not a pool |
| "step" (המעלא) | the pool has steps | **Present** (observed, on a plan) |
| "upper" (העליונא) | on the summit, higher than the north-shoulder cistern and the valley cistern | **Inference**, not a published relation; not contradicted |
| "under" — the deposit place | not known | unknown |

Two readings:

- **F 2.** Locus 16 is a specific, observed feature with one of the entry's attributes (a step). "Upper" is not contradicted. The rubric does not need the kind word to be the same word, only the kind of landmark.
- **F 1.** The kind is not shown: a stepped pool is not a pit. This is the same judgement as entry 1 (Jisr Abu Ghabush, "whether its steps are the entry's steps … is unknown. Between 1 and 2, so 1"). The steps of a stepped pool are part of the pool. That is all that the entry's step relation can show here.

My view: the case for F 2 is stronger here than at Jisr Abu Ghabush, because the entry itself joins a step to a pit ("the step of the pit"). But the kind question is real. This is the owner's decision.
