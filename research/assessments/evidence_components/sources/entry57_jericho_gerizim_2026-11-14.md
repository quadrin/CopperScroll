# Entry 57: the Jericho "Gerizim" — sources for the candidate row `jericho_gerizim` (14 November 2026)

Added at the owner's request (14 November 2026): one candidate place for the two mounds at the outlet of Wadi Qelt, because the sources do not fix one mound. The Jebel et-Tur candidate (`gerizim`) stays preferred.

## The proposal

| Source | What it says | How held |
|---|---|---|
| Allegro 1960, *The Treasure of the Copper Scroll*, pp. 75–76, n. 298 | The scroll's Gerizim is not the Samaritan mountain but the Jericho "Gerizim" of Jewish and early Christian tradition: Cypros (Tuweil / Tell el-ʿAqaba), with Taurus as Ebal. He saw a tower, a well blocked with rubble and rock-cut cisterns lower down (p. 76). The Zealot hold until 68 CE is his terminus (n. 298) | `research/history/allegro1960_treasure_extracted.md`, item 58 |
| H. Eshel, in E. Stern and H. Eshel (eds), *Sefer HaShomronim* (Jerusalem 2002), p. 201 (Hebrew) | The scroll's Gerizim probably rests on an interpretation that put Gerizim and Ebal near Jericho, at the outlet of Wadi Qelt onto the plain: Tell el-ʿAqaba and Nuseib ʿUweishira. He takes the interpretation to be of the Second Temple period. Note 45 cites his thesis, pp. 193–195 | page image read 12 Nov 2026 (owner's copy; not in the repository) |
| Eshel and Safrai 2002 (*Cathedra* 103); Eshel 2015 (English), ch. 7 p. 127 | The same two mounds ("Nuseb el-Auyašira and Tell el-ʿAqaba"). N. 59 gives the ancient sources (below); n. 60 cites Allegro 1960 pp. 75–76 and Luria pp. 123–124 | Drive (Eshel 2015) |
| F. Dexinger, in *Sefer HaShomronim* p. 498 | Jewish midrash put the Gerizim and Ebal of Deut 11:30 "near the Jordan", not among the Samaritans (Sifre, ed. Horovitz p. 123); Jerome, *Onomastikon*, "Gebal" (PL 34 col. 946). The Samaritan Pentateuch adds "opposite Shechem" at Deut 11:30 | page image read 12 Nov 2026 |

## The ancient sources (as cited by Eshel 2015 n. 59)

y. Sotah 7:3 (21c); b. Sotah 33b; Eusebius, *Onomasticon* §307 (Melamed 1978 p. 13; Notley and Safrai 2005 pp. 63–64); the Madaba map (Avi-Yonah, *Eretz-Israel* 2, 1953, pp. 143–144). None of these was read for this row; they are taken through Eshel.

## The two mounds

| Mound | Position used | Water installations reported | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cypros (Tell el-ʿAqaba), south of the Wadi Qelt outlet | summit PG 190.44/139.165 (hexagon notes 31 and 43) | cisterns and pools fed by the Wadi Qelt aqueducts (Meshel and Amit); the preserved cistern is about 17 × 7 m and 8 m deep; Allegro's blocked well and rock-cut cisterns | Hasmonean–Herodian fortress; destroyed by the rebels in 66 CE (Josephus, *War* 2.484) |
| Nuseib ʿUweishira, north of the outlet, about 840 m from Cypros | pool point 31.85266 N, 35.42679 E (WBADB E360; HA 59–60 p. 38) | a stepped pool (HA 59–60 p. 38); a ritual bath among meagre Herodian-period finds below a Byzantine monastery (Netzer 2006 p. 207). **15 Nov:** the pool is locus 16, plastered, on the summit building; three cisterns also (see `entry57_nuseib_stepped_pool_2026-11-15.md`) | Herodian-period finds; Hasmonean construction is an inference (regional inventory of 6 Oct) |

The atlas point (31.8489 N, 35.42647 E) is between the two mounds.

## Correction, 15 November 2026

The F line above says that the Nuseib stepped pool has "no recorded form". That is wrong: Netzer and Birger 1990 (Fig. 7, locus 16) and Netzer 1978 (grey lime plaster) record its form and place; the size is still not published. The F and D basis texts in `ratings.json` were rewritten on 15 November; the levels are not changed (F 1, D 2). Detail: `entry57_nuseib_stepped_pool_2026-11-15.md`; edit log D27.
