# Original-edition audit: E47 and E50

These observations check the editions, not the metal. Sources are exact held Drive books downloaded by the integrating worker. Private page images under inputs are for visual verification and should not be published with this assessment. Summaries below are original analytical paraphrases.

## E47 (X 8–11; Lefkovits47, Milik49, Allegro48)

Lefkovits2000 printed pp335–345, viewer356–366: the published translation at p335 places the entrance two cubits from a black stone on the western side. The explanatory discussion at p345 explicitly retains two attachments: (1) stone→two cubits→entrance, and (2) two cubits→stone, with stone coinciding with the entrance. The second does not identify the measuring origin. These are distances, not a stone's length. The project schematic's drawn two-cubit stone length is an unverified geometric invention. A excavation depth is also unsupported by these pages.

Puech2015 printed pp85,88–90, viewer96,99–101: printed text X8 has בצריח מעינו, X9 has black-stone/two-cubits wording after emendations. His commentary prefers a spring-associated underground feature over the west phrase. p90 attaches the feminine pronoun to the stone, which marks access. The translation locates the stone at two cubits; no measuring axis or datum is specified. Puech's spring and Lefkovits's west cannot be merged into one required conjunction.

Milik1960 printed p141, viewer5, item49, visually read: he instead describes a stone held with two clamps on the west side, marking the entrance. He does not require a black stone or a two-cubit dimension. Lefkovits p344 and Puech pp89–90 separately discuss this interpretation. Therefore a black-stone screen cannot exclude the Milik branch.

Valley names remain edition-dependent: Puech Job-like rendering, Lefkovits Pure Valley with several alternatives, Milik's main name unresolved. Puech p89 n392 explicitly says the Job-name antiquity requires verification; modern Bir Ayyub does not independently verify the ancient name. Lefkovits pp335–339 reports the Zered/Wolters interpretation and his maʿah-coin clause p339/p344; Zered and coins form a separate reported branch without a two-cubit constraint. Puech disputes Zered's letter reading (p89); no manuscript adjudication performed.

No author on the inspected pages makes black imply basalt, imported stone, or a bedrock source. Colour must be recorded as appearance, with material independently observed if available.

## E50 (X 17–XI 1; Lefkovits50, Milik52, Allegro51)

Puech2015 printed pp85–86,93–95, viewer96–97/104–106: the text prints בגנת צדוק בארבעת, followed by corners and gold/offering vessels. p94 explicitly disputes the pool restoration and identifies a courtyard associated with Zadok's tomb. This is a tomb-court reading; its historical name attachment requires independent evidence.

Lefkovits2000 printed pp358–362, viewer379–383: his principal reconstruction is an inner court; most of the opening phrase is supplied. p359 attributes Upper Pool to Wise, and p360 lists it as one of many suggested restorations. p359 states those restorations are conjectural. The project translation's displayed Upper Pool therefore does not supply a secure elevation requirement or a stable pool type. p362 counts a deposit at each corner but does not give their metric positions or depth. Four corners is generic geometry until the opening noun/name is fixed.

Lefkovits pp358/360 report Milik's Siloam-pool restorations and joined Beyer division. Puech p94 n415 independently reports the Addenda pool restoration with printed pp301–302 (not the derivative reading-record's pp299–301). The exact DJD held file is an 88-page plate volume; it contains no text Addenda. Consequently Milik1962 Addenda is SECONDARY-REPORTED in this audit, not directly inspected. No holdout plate was opened and no E49 closed test was reopened.

Milik1960 printed p142, viewer6, item52, visually verified: the opening noun is left unfilled and only the four-corner description follows. It supplies neither an Upper Pool nor an excavated court. A blank restoration in his 1960 translation must not be presented as his 1962 Addenda reading.

Gold is excluded from the landmark screen. Different deposit contents cannot identify a surviving courtyard or pool without a specified deposit-context observation.
