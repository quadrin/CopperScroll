This supplies native building/phase evidence for the earlier atlas comparison only. No political account or custodial claim is evaluated here.

pp34*–36*, Plans1–2: this describes a large subterranean vaulted hall in the southeastern platform and its gate passages. Its measured footprint is irregular in extent; a rectangle drawn in the E50 text schematic cannot be matched directly to this plan. The four-sided blocks in its reused piers do not represent four corners of a tomb court.

pp37*–39*: the report distinguishes outer enclosure masonry, reused blocks, earlier arch springings, later gates and reconstructed vaults. The dating of visible vaults remains architecturally debated; some earlier features may be Herodian, but their date cannot transfer to the entire surviving hall. The plan's generic southeastern position is not a Zadok-tomb association. The report gives no dated four-corner tomb-court relation for E50 in the inspected text.

p51* note1: Plans3–4 are adapted from photographs; their measurements are approximate. They cannot support a sub-metre cubic-distance test without independent controls. That matters for source use generally, though E50 has no cubit clause.

Branch result for this comparison: E50-P court of Zadok and E50-L inner-court remain **not identifiable from available evidence**. The publication's subject and plan coverage do not establish the absence of an ancient courtyard beneath/near the platform. E50-U upper pool has no same-feature pool observation here. No archaeological identification or candidate polygon is added.

Prior/new distinction: earlier schematic named the southeastern enclosure/Stables or slope as a possible low-confidence setting. This read obtains native documentation of the building's form and phase ambiguity; it does not add an independent match to that prior name.
