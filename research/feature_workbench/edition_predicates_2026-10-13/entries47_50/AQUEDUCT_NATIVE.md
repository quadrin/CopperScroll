# Native check of water-table leads for E47/E50

Held original: Amit, Hirschfeld and Patrich (eds)1989, The Aqueducts of Ancient Palestine (Hebrew), exact Drive ID1-6s-nqSD-PP_FpZ788Tc0dvH_z7eziZg, sources/aqueducts1989.pdf. A. Mazar pp183–185 (viewer196–198) and E. Damati pp299–300 (viewer312–313) visually inspected from the original scans. No source passage below is a fresh field campaign; all are rechecks of existing water inventory's citations.

Mazar1989 p184 Fig15 distinguishes Solomon's upper, middle and lower pools. Upper pool is depicted as rectangular, 118×71m, with substantial depth; these are basin dimensions and must not be misused as E47 stone or entrance measurements. Mazar describes repeated changes and repairs, precluding a confident original-form reconstruction from the current basins. He supplies no construction date for this upper pool on pp183–185. E50-U, as a restored Upper Pool reading, receives a limited current-form/upper relation fit here, but ancient phase and deposit corners remain unresolved. This is an undated comparator, not a geographical identification or a rarity finding.

Mazar1989 p185 Fig16 describes Ein Saleh's spring chamber, partly cut and partly built, 14.3×4.3m, approximately200m northwest of the upper pool. Its current access steps date to the nineteenth century; the original access is unresolved. For E47-P this establishes only a spring-associated underground form. The inspected source does not locate a black stone at a dated entrance, does not establish a two-cubit origin, and does not provide an ancient Job/Zered name. Missing colour and entrance data remain unknown. The springroom is not itself coded as the required basin; connection to a separate basin is another untested relation.

Damati1989 p299 dates the Martyrius monastery to the early470sCE and describes three irrigated gardens. In the upper garden it gives a small pool1.2×1.5m below a collecting cistern; Fig2 p300 shows its remains. The water table's name Upper Garden Pool is accurate, but the adjective modifies the garden. This source does not independently establish a paired Upper Pool to satisfy E50-U. The reported monastic garden-use configuration postdates the retained ancient window, so that configuration cannot supply the contemporary E50 landmark. The passage does not independently date the pool's construction fabric or exclude reuse of an earlier basin. Physical basin identity and earlier phase therefore remain **not identifiable from available evidence**. This is a conditional exclusion of the named monastic configuration, not of the whole pool, site or reading.
