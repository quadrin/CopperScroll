# Column V image access audit: original and later photographs

30 September 2026. This report traces independent image witnesses for 3Q15 column V, entries 21–23 (V 1–11). It supplements the [blind-reading audit](qumran_20_23_blind_reading_2026-09-30.md). I did **not** make a new transcription: the publicly downloadable photographs checked here are too small for reliable letter-level decisions at the damaged loci. No copyrighted image is included in this repository.

## The primary catalog trail

[Stephen A. Reed's *Dead Sea Scroll Inventory Project: Qumran Minor Caves*, fascicle 2](https://lyingpen.uia.no/dssinventoryproject/fascicle2/) identifies DJD III **plate LVII** as the column V photograph, covering cuts **10*–11**, and plate **LVI** as its hand copy. Its photographic-archive concordance identifies **PAM 42.981** with DJD III LVII and **PAM 42.993** with the LVI hand copy. Reed cautions that the PAM set largely contains photographs *of the published plates*: it does **not** contain the original Ashton or Starcky negatives used for DJD, whose locations were unknown to that inventory. PAM 42.981 therefore is not automatically a second image witness merely because it has a separate catalog number. The inventory has an apparent typo in one abbreviated list for PAM 42.994 (LVII); its expanded plate concordance calls 42.994 the **LVIII** hand copy. The column V assignment of 42.981/LVII and 42.993/LVI is consistent across the relevant listings.

The same [inventory](https://lyingpen.uia.no/dssinventoryproject/fascicle2/) describes Bruce and Kenneth Zuckerman's **1988 Amman** photography, made on both faces in color and black and white with right, center, and left views under top and bottom lighting. Its identifiers are **AWS 21 = cut 10**, **AWS 22 = seven loose fragments from cut 10's lower right edge**, and **AWS 23 = cut 11**. These are genuinely later photographs of the metal and thus an independent photographic witness to the published DJD plate and Puech's copy photographs.

## Image-first observation and limit

I looked at the two downloaded USC images **without applying a proposed location**. The cut-10 photograph shows a narrow, corroded strip with severe discontinuities; much of the inscribed surface has weak contrast. Cut 11's incised letterforms are clearer, but the full strip is still only 593 pixels wide at the most generous downloadable size. Neither full-strip preview lets me isolate V 1's broken linkage, the missing V 2–3 noun, the V 5 east ending, the V 7 word boundary, or the opening of V 9 at a resolution sufficient to separate intentional incisions from corrosion or from adjacent strokes. Without a defensible registration of each queried line onto the two cuts, describing a particular preview mark as one of those letters would be circular. **No previous reading in the blind-reading audit is upgraded or overturned by these previews.**

The strongest next text experiment is a controlled re-read of **the full-resolution AWS 21/22/23 masters** (all lighting directions), with DJD III LVI/LVII and Puech's radiographs kept as comparison layers. A reader should first register lines to physical cuts and record stroke observations under each lighting direction before seeing editorial transcriptions or geography. In particular, compare V 1's preserved edge, V 2–3 loss, V 5 and V 7 groups, and V 8–9 spacing. The archive's public “Request this asset” link is an access route, not evidence that a master has been obtained; no request was sent.
