## Facts extracted (EVIDENCE unless marked)

Location and work
- Map reference 1760/1369 (Old Israel Grid; converted with EPSG:28191: 31.82488 N, 35.27357 E; phase-2 zone Z3_JUDEAN_DESERT, INFERENCE from the box rule). Precision of a 4-digit reference: 100 m.
- Salvage excavation, May 1999, "following the breaching of an illegal road"; on behalf of the Archaeological Staff Officer of Judea and Samaria; directed by B. Har-Even.
- The site lies "on a hilltop that has two domes"; most central remains are on the southwestern dome. The excavated features lie near the top of the hill's northern slope (paraphrase).
- Earlier work cited (not coded here): burial caves and dwellings of the Persian period excavated on the hill's western slope in 1973 and 1981 (reference printed as "ESI 5:1", OCR "£575:1").

Cave A (Figs. 94–96)
- Rock-cut in soft chalk; partly collapsed, filled with alluvium; two chambers, one behind the other; probably looted, perhaps in antiquity; "the entrance was not preserved".
- Outer chamber 3 × 4 m, "carefully hewn"; low benches (width 0.4 m, height 0.2 m) along the west wall and in the northwest and southeast corners; two loculi in the west wall (L26: 0.6 × 1.7 m, height 0.8 m; L28: 0.75 × 2.0 m, height 0.7 m); L26 damaged the bench, so the loculi postdate the cave. The outer chamber "may have served at first as an anteroom".
- A square dressed stone (0.40 × 0.60 × 0.86 m) lay in the centre of the outer chamber, "suitable for closing the doorway" (0.45 × 0.80 m) between the chambers; "This stone could have also sealed the cave’s entrance" (excavator's inference). Fig. 95 caption: outer chamber and the closing stone, "looking south".
- Inner chamber 3.25 × 3.50 m, max. height 3 m, arched ceiling, walls carelessly hewn. A breach in the upper part of its south wall was caused by a cist grave dug above the cave.
- Standing pit L45 (1 × 2 m, depth 0.85 m) in the centre; three shelves, each a shallow rock-cut burial trough (1.0 × 2.1 m, depth 1–2 cm) with a rectangular headrest; square bone repository L44 (0.65 × 0.82 m, depth 0.85 m) in the southwest corner.
- Bones of at least eight individuals (L26 two; L28 one; trough L13 at least two; repository at least two; standing pit one).
- Dating as given: plan of the inner chamber suggests first use "possibly during the First Temple period"; pottery "from the Hellenistic period to the Early Roman period": a bowl, cooking pot, jar and lamp of the 2nd c. BCE; a jar, fusiform juglet, cooking pot and pared lamp of the mid-1st c. BCE to mid-1st c. CE.

Cave B (Figs. 97–99)
- "c. 150 m west of Cave A"; looted, almost devoid of finds.
- Courtyard L22 (1.7 × 1.8 m) and square burial chamber L33 (3.2 × 3.3 m, height 1.7 m). In the southeast corner of the courtyard, a hewn square entrance (0.50 × 0.55 m) "flanked by a drafted frame"; a step descends to a dressed standing pit L42, with three shelves; square rock-cut repository L43 (0.70 × 0.85 m) in the southeast corner of the chamber, with the bones of a male over 20. Chisel marks of a straight cutting edge; ceiling straight, curved at the wall junction.
- Dating as given: by plan "to the time of the First Temple"; sherds near the entrance of the Iron Age and Persian period (mortarium, krater) and a cooking pot of the end of the 1st c. BCE–beginning of the 1st c. CE; "may have been in use also during the Second Temple period".
- The direction the entrance faces is not stated; Fig. 98 caption: burial chamber "looking northwest".

Cist graves (Figs. 94, 100)
- Three graves excavated (loci printed as 45, 47, 48; "45" is also the number printed for the standing pit of Cave A) "out of some 50 graves" discerned in the vicinity; cut in hard nari directly above Cave A; looted, filled with alluvium; rectangular, 0.40 × 1.75 m, depth 1.3 m, with notches 0.1 m deep c. 0.85 m below the surface for covering stones, one in situ. No datable finds; they postdate Cave A; "probably" Byzantine by shape.
