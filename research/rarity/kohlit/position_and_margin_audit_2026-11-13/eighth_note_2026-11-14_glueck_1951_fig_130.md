# Glueck 1951 read deeper: fig. 130 fixes Tell Abu Sidreh; the 1943 road passes the west end of the Kh. es-Saleh spur; S845 now holds as Probable (14 November 2026)

This note is beside the registered Koḥlit count. It does not change the count, a match, a rating or a candidate.

**Addition, 14 November, later (blind second reading).** The tell is confirmed (15 m). The road is confirmed north of N 179.39 and the spur track is confirmed as not the 1943 road; but the road place at the wadi is now "between E 201.89 and 201.97, Probable", and the Roman camp of Kh. el-Gharur is lowered to Probable. **S845 goes back to "not decided; it leans to hold"** (edit log B91 to B93, D16; note `blind-second-reading-of-14-november-…`). Results 4 and 6 below are to be read with this.

Source: N. Glueck, *Explorations in Eastern Palestine, IV* (AASOR 25–28, 1951), Part I. Bucket: `pdf/Explorations in eastern Palestine_ IV.pdf` (452 PDF pages; printed page = PDF page − 24). Read: the section "West side of the Jordan", printed pp. 404–420 (sites 249 b to 256), and fig. 130 (p. 421).

## Result first

1. **Fig. 130 is on the map grid.** It is a vertical Royal Air Force photograph. I fitted it to the 1 December 1974 image (F014 b) automatically:
   - 70 blocks, residual 22 m (root mean square); 5 to 10 m near the arrow.
   - Scale 3.47 m for one pixel of my copy; the frame covers about 5.4 × 5.2 km (PG 199.1–204.6 / 175.8–181.3).
2. **Tell Abu Sidreh is Checked at PG 203.12/177.93** (32.1944 N, 35.5614 E by the transform; error about 40 m).
   - The arrow of fig. 130 points to a small oval mound, about 90 × 60 m, on the 1974 image. It is in the Zor, directly north of the place where a dry wadi comes out of the badlands. This is Glueck's description.
   - The SWP sheet has the tell 240 m to the north-north-west (203.01/178.14).
   - **The database point of unit S857 (202.6/178.4, the grid 2026/1784 of Zertal and Bar) is about 700 m north-west of the mound, in the badlands.** S857 has no match (all conditions UNKNOWN), so the count does not change.
3. **Glueck's distance does not decide Kh. es-Saleh.** He writes "about a km. to the n. w.–w. n. w." of the tell. From the mound the spur is 1.40 to 1.55 km away at 319° to 325°. The west end and the east end differ by 6° only.
4. **The road decides more.** Glueck puts the ruin "on the e. side of the modern n.–s. Ghor road", with sherds "also on the w. side of the modern road", and the Roman road about 70 m west of the modern road.
   - The road of fig. 130 is a clear light line. I traced it from N 180.4 to N 179.2. At N 179.5 it is at E 202.00; at N 179.2 it is at E 201.99.
   - On the 1974 image this line is the east, unpaved band of the road corridor. It is **not** the paved road (E 201.89 at the wadi) and **not** the track on the spur (E 202.09). The question of the seventh note ("which of the two is Glueck's road") is answered: neither; the 1943 road is between them, at E 201.97 where it crosses Wadi es-Salih.
   - So the ruin is directly east of E 201.97 to 201.99, on the south side of the wadi. A ruin with sherds on the two sides of the road is not 200 to 350 m from the road. **The east two-thirds of the spur (E 202.19 to 202.33) are now improbable.**
5. **A group of three small rises is at this place**: PG 201.98/179.13, 202.01/179.12 and 202.00/179.10, between the old road and the track, south of the wadi head, with a gully to the south. Gophna and Porat write "a ruin on three hilltops". Status: **Possible** as the ruin (the rises can be natural; no wall is read).

## Limits

- Fig. 130 is a printed halftone of a scan. A road and a mound are readable; a ruin is not.
- The fit of fig. 130 is a six-parameter fit on badland texture. It is good in the badlands and the Zor; on the flat Ghor west of the road there are few blocks.
- The road trace is clear north of N 179.35. Between N 179.35 and the wadi it is faint; the place at the wadi is from the 1974 band that continues the line.
- The three rises are not shown to be a ruin. The ground was a minefield in 1974 and Zertal and Bar did not go there.
- One reader. No blind second reading.
