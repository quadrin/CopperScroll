## Kypros: which pool, and where

- The count's feature is the sentence of the SWP memoir (vol. III, p. 24, at Beit Jubr et Tahtani): "Immediately south of the fort is a small ruined birkeh". The coder put it at the point of the Nigro catalogue, no. 36 (191.20 / 139.60, 890 m, 51.8°).
- **SWP sheet XVIII (registered, step 28) puts that birkeh elsewhere.** The ruin of Beit Jabr et-Tahtani is at 190.75 / 139.65; a round "Bkt" sign is directly south of it at 190.83 / 139.47. That is 370 m from the Nigro point. A second "Bkt" sign is 350 m further east, beside the road (191.18 / 139.75); the Nigro point is 150 m from that one. (Check of the sheet here: its square "Bkt" sign of Birket Musa is 95 m from the measured pool.)
- **On HEXAGON 1978** a basin of about 50 × 40 m with a dark rim and a channel running into it from the west is at 190.875 / 139.542, 82 m from the round SWP sign. Status: Probable the place of the SWP birkeh. Whether this basin is the ruined birkeh of 1873 or a later basin at the same place is not decided (it has an inflow channel and looks in use).

- East sector: 45° to 135°. **From the database point, the birkeh "immediately south of the fort" is 7–8° outside.** The second SWP birkeh is 0.7° outside. Only the Nigro point is inside.
- From the summit ruin, all four are inside.
- The database point is 148 m south-east of the summit ruin, on the slope below it. The protocol uses the database point. A rule that used the site centre would give the other answer. This is the same lesson as at Tell esh-Sheikh Dhiab: at under 1 km, a point error of 150 m decides the sector.

## el-Muntar

- The audit of 13 November said that the HEXAGON frames do not cover el-Muntar. That is wrong: tile F017 b of 1 December 1974 covers it. Edit log row D11.
- At the database points of el-Muntar (177050 / 134300) and of the reservoir of Qasr ʿAli (177650 / 134900) the tile shows bare desert slopes. I can not identify the tower, the reservoir or any structure. The bearing stays 45.0° on a 50 m grid; the match stays "on the edge, not testable".

## Tell es-Samarat or Tell Abu Hindi (the cut mound of old air photographs 1)

- SWP sheet XVIII names both mounds. After the shift of step 28, the "Tell es Samarat" sign is at 191.777 / 141.377: **15 m from the cut mound** (191.79 / 141.37). The "Tell Abu Hindi" sign is at 192.052 / 141.554, 320 m from it. Tell el-ʿAraïs is 386 m, Tell Abu Zelef 236 m.
- With the database point of Tell es-Samrat (S2430, 114 m), two sources agree. The cut mound is **Probable Tell es-Samarat**, as in note 1. The reading "probably Tell Abu Hindi" in the note of 10 November (from the title of frame 1031) is not supported. Edit log row B72.
- SWP signs are good to about 200 m, so 15 m is partly chance. The decisive point is that the Abu Hindi sign is on the other side, 320 m north-east.
