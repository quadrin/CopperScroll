# Kh. es-Saleh (Koḥlit unit S845): the sources read to the end (14 November 2026)

This note is beside the registered count. It does not change the count, a match, a rating or a candidate.

## Result first

- **S845 stays not decided.** The sources narrow the question but do not settle it.
- Gophna and Porat 1972, site 170, give the grid **2021/1791**. This is the database point. Zertal and Bar 2019 (2021/1790) did not visit the site, so the only grid from a visit is the one that the database uses.
- The spur between Wadi es-Salih and the gully south of it runs from about E 202.10 to 202.33 and from N 179.05 to 179.13 (1974 image, F014b).
  - **On its west third (E 202.09 to about 202.15), both conditions hold**, or at least C3s holds (see the table).
  - **On its east two-thirds (E 202.19 to 202.33), both conditions fail.**
- The written descriptions point to the west end, but they do not prove it:
  - Glueck puts the ruin "on the E side of the modern N-S Ghor road". The 1974 image has a track along the west edge of the spur, and the paved road 120 m further west. Which of the two is Glueck's 1943 road is not known. If it is the track, the ruin is at the west end of the spur. This is Possible only.
  - Gophna and Porat put the ruin "on three hilltops", about 4 dunams. The spur has three rises over about 230 m, which is larger than 4 dunams. So the ruin may be on part of the spur only.
- SWP sheet XII has no Kh. es-Salih. "Kabur ed Delameh" is at PG 201.69/177.34 after the shift, 1.8 km south of the site. It does not help. (Zertal and Bar say that it is "roughly in this place"; on the registered sheet it is not.)

## Test on the spur (measured Gharur camp: centre 201.437/179.826; south-east corner, where the sarcophagus lay, about 201.49/179.79)

- The database point (202.10/179.10) is in the A area.
- If the grid of Gophna and Porat is rounded to the nearest 100 m, the site is in E 202.05–202.15. That is A or c.
- If the grid is cut down to the 100 m below (truncated), the site is in E 202.10–202.20. That is A, c or –.

## Limits

- The image cannot show the ruin. No foundations or graves can be read at this scale, and the ground in 1974 was a minefield.
- The cistern positions inside the camp are not on the survey plan.
- Gophna and Porat (1972) describe Kh. el-Ghirūr as a 2-dunam ruin with a tower. Zertal (2008) describes a 9-dunam Roman camp at the same grid. The camp seen on the image agrees with Zertal.
