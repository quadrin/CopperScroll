## 0. Conventions

- **Tags.** EVIDENCE = the 1964 book says it (or a figure or plate shows it). INFERENCE = my conclusion. BK = background that is not from this book. That includes facts the project brief supplied (Wright, *Biblica* 42, 1961; *Search in the Desert*). Comparisons with 1960 rely on the project's 1960 extraction (`~/tcs/allegro1960_treasure_extracted.md`, cited as "1960 ext."). That file is EVIDENCE for the 1960 text but secondary here.
- **Quotes** are verbatim, 12 words or fewer, in quotation marks. Everything else is paraphrase. The OCR is Cyrillic-contaminated, so wording was checked against page images where it mattered: Items 24, 28 and 40, the totals, and Figs. 5, 6, 15 and 18.
- **Locators.** "p.N/PDF M": printed page / PDF page. Mapping: pp.1–66 = PDF 19–84 (offset +18); Plates 1–17 = PDF 85–100 (Plates 12 and 13 share PDF 96); pp.67–106 = PDF 101–140 (+34); Plates 18–34 = PDF 141–156 (Plates 33 and 34 share PDF 156); pp.107–163 = PDF 157–213 (+50); Figs. 1–18 = pp.164–181 = PDF 214–231; index PDF 232–236; back cover PDF 240. ## 1. The survey of treasure sites (site by site)

### 1.0 The expedition as a whole

- **Date, EVIDENCE.** "In the winter of 1959-60, a party of us visited Mird" (p.55/PDF 73). It took place "during an expedition I had arranged to seek out some" of the treasure sites (p.55). **No month, day or duration is given anywhere.** Elsewhere it is "our expedition", "our little expedition", "our short expedition" (pp.60, 65, 67–68, 75, 105) and "our recent expedition" (p.130).
- **Purpose, EVIDENCE.** "to survey the places for possible future archaeological work". Also, where possible, to "make small soundings which might, without undue disturbance" clarify the topography of the inventory (p.55/PDF 73).
- **Team, EVIDENCE.** "a party of us" (p.55). Named: G. R. H. ("Mick") Wright, "our archaeological surveyor" (p.56/PDF 74); the List of Illustrations (PDF 15) gives his initials as G. R. H. Unnamed: "a scientist from England" who operated the detectors (p.58/PDF 76). No other member is named. The expedition is never called the "Copper Scroll Expedition" (EVIDENCE by absence).
- **Equipment, EVIDENCE.**
  - "metal and cavity detectors of the most advanced type" (p.58).
  - An "electron ladder" and nylon ropes (p.56).
  - The instruments detect metals and cavities at "hardly more than eighteen inches" (p.67/PDF 101).
- **[INF] Is the "recent expedition" the same trip?** Two passages say "our recent expedition" or "our expedition" without a date: the Haram attempt (p.130) and the Kidron "Zadok" excavation (p.105). The book does not say whether they belong to the winter 1959–60 trip. Being singular and undated, they read as one expedition, but this is not stated.

### 1.1 Kh. Mird / Hyrcania: Item 1 (steps) and Item 2 (monument). Ch. 6, pp.50–61/PDF 68–79; Fig. 5; Plates 12–17

**Identification (EVIDENCE).** Unchanged from 1960. The Vale of Achor is the Buqei‘a, with "fair certainty" (p.50). "the fortress" is Hyrcania / Kh. (el-)Mird (p.51).

**What the expedition did. All EVIDENCE, p.55–58/PDF 73–76 unless noted.**
- **Damage since 1953.** There had been much vandalism since "the last survey in 1953", meaning the Louvain party (p.55). Two marble chancel-screen posts were "found thrown down the hillside". A small room with a coloured bird mosaic about 10 ft square had been "largely hacked away" (p.56).
- **Surface survey.** Allegro asked Wright "to make a thorough surface survey of the ruin". The aim was to record it permanently before further damage (p.56).
- **Herodian fabric.** Wright pointed out stretches of Herodian masonry on probable enclosing-wall lines with corner towers. The building platform had been levelled up on underground vaults; two are accessible, running north–south (p.56).
- **The western vault.**
  - The team fixed "our electron ladder around the low stone parapet" and lowered each other on nylon ropes (p.56; Plate 15).
  - Inside was a vaulted cistern "some forty feet long, sixteen feet wide, and twenty-five feet high". It had mortarless roof masonry, likened to the Antonia cisterns, and hard hydraulic plaster on the floor and sides. Whether it was built as a cistern "cannot be certain" (pp.56–57/PDF 74–75).
- **Plan of the court.** Wright thought the Byzantine ranges of rooms followed the Herodian lines, citing Masada (p.57).
- **Item 1 (the steps).** "Where the original eastern entrance lay, we cannot now tell". So they are "unable to define the treasure site" of the forty cubits (p.57/PDF 75).
  - No digging, depth or detector use is reported for Item 1.
  - The 1960 suggestion that the phrase could mean steps on the west side (1960 ext. §3) does **not** reappear (EVIDENCE by absence).
  - The visitor climbs "the zigzag path on the western side of the hill" (p.55), but this is not tied to the item.
- **Item 2 (the "monument").** Allegro turns to it "supposing it to be in the vicinity". He now reads Item 2 as "100 bars of gold" (p.57).
  - **Location:** "some three hundred yards to the southwest a rubble construction". It crowns a small promontory overlooking the plain, as seen from the court's present western entrance (p.57/PDF 75; Fig. 5).
  - **Form:** it looks like a lookout but is "more monumental in character". A platform "juts out on the northwestern side", facing the only approach (pp.57–58).
  - **Damage:** the upper part was damaged by vandals, but "the plan is clear enough" (p.58/PDF 76).
  - **Structure:** "laid out as a square of some thirty-three feet" around "a pinnacle of rock". The retaining walls are keyed into the irregular rock, and the enclosed space was "then filled in with rubble" (p.58; Plate 16).
  - **Date:** "Pottery embedded in the cracks between the boulders" of the retaining walls showed it is "another relic of Herodian times" (p.58).
  - **Function:** Wright thought it "could well have been a funerary monument". Allegro adds there was "no difficulty in determining the “third course of stones.”" (p.58).
  - **Detectors:** they had "metal and cavity detectors of the most advanced type" and "a scientist from England to operate them". But "the rock of the hillside had a natural magnetism", so a natural response could not be told from metal (p.58).
  - **Result:** "Needless to say, there was no thought of destroying the whole monument" on the chance it held the cache (p.58).
  - **[INF] What is missing.** No clearance, trench or sounding at the monument is reported. There is **no depth, no statement that bedrock was reached, and no find** other than the sherds in the wall cracks. The book also never says "no chamber or deposits". BK: Wright (*Biblica* 42, as quoted in the brief) reports clearing "around and through" the monument with a "No chamber or deposits" result; Allegro's account omits the clearance. INF: his "pinnacle of rock … filled in with rubble" may reflect what the clearance exposed, but he does not say so.
  - **Units [INF].** Allegro's distance is "three hundred yards", while Wright (Biblica 42 p. 14) gives about 300 m.
- **Herodian graves.** "we actually found a group of Herodian graves" on a low hillside east of Mird (p.58/PDF 76; Fig. 5, *Herodian graves*). None was distinguished enough to explain the monument (pp.58–59).
- **New speculation (EVIDENCE, pp.59–60/PDF 77–78).** Herod's funeral cortège may have gone not to Herodium but to Hyrcania. If so, "the fabulously rich tomb of the Idumaean king" may lie at Mird, possibly within "a stone's throw" of the monument. In 1960 Item 2 was tentatively linked to Antipater's burial.
- **Stepped tunnels (EVIDENCE, pp.60–61/PDF 78–79; Fig. 5 *Stepped Tunnels*; Plate 17).**
  - North of the fortress, in a narrow wadi only two or three yards wide, "we found the entrance to a tunnel" in the south cliff.
  - It descends in steps "at an angle of about thirty degrees", "a hundred feet or so into the solid rock".
  - The clearance to that point was done by others: "some busy hands had laboriously cleared out the dust and rubble" this far and given up. A second tunnel nearby was cleared only for its first step or two.
  - The entrance is about 6 ft above the wadi bed. The air at depth is "heavy and warm". Clearing it would need forced air.
  - Its date is unknown short of full excavation, but it is hard to "disassociate" it from the fortress. A similar tunnel about six miles north, on the Jerusalem–Jericho road, is known.
  - **INF:** Allegro does **not** link these tunnels to Item 1's "steps entering to the east". The expedition entered at least the first one (Plate 17 shows a man inside), but no clearance by the team is stated.
- **Mird–Qumran link (p.61).** The Byzantine monks' garden near ‘Ain Feshkha, and the archaeologists' Byzantine house there, are the work of others, not the expedition.

### 1.2 Buqei‘a presses: Items 18–20

**EVIDENCE by absence.** No visit, survey or search is reported. Items 18–20 appear only in the translation list (pp.22–23/PDF 40–41). Item 18 is now "two buildings (?)" in the Vale of Achor, not oil-presses. Items 19–20 now involve a "trough" and "KHLT". The 1960 proposal to search for twin olive presses (1960 ext., p.68) is gone.

### 1.3 Kh. Qumran / Secacah: Items 21–25. Ch. 7, pp.63–70/PDF 81–104; Fig. 6; Plates 18–20

**Identification (EVIDENCE).** Secacah = Kh. Qumran; "the only place which at all fits" (p.64/PDF 82). He lists the site's features: a "watchtower", a water "aqueduct", a "conduit", and a "noticeable earthquake “fissure”" through a large "reservoir" (p.64).

**Item 21 (dam in the Valley of Secacah, 1 cubit).**
- EVIDENCE: no fieldwork. The dam-to-cisterns system is described as "dam, rock-hewn conduit, aqueduct, and cisterns—are there today", "corresponding remarkably with the details of the copper scroll" (p.67/PDF 101).
- INF: by implication Item 21's "dam" is the barrage in the gorge, but Allegro does not state this.

**Item 22 (head of the conduit from the north, settling basin).**
- EVIDENCE: no fieldwork or specific placement in the 1964 text. The water passes a "breakwater into a large settling tank" (p.67).
- The 1960 Fig. 4 label *Settling basin (It. 22)* is **absent** from 1964 Fig. 6 (EVIDENCE, image).

**Item 23 (the fissure in the "Solomon" reservoir).** EVIDENCE, pp.65, 68/PDF 83, 102; Plate 18.
- The great cistern on the east side split lengthwise; one side "dropped some two feet". In places the soil fell away under the plaster, leaving a gap beneath the plaster "shell".
- "During our expedition we were able to crawl inside this compartment". There they "found there a few pieces of broken pottery" (p.65).
- He expected little, since Roman soldiers would have searched it after AD 68.
- The scroll lists "tithe vessels" here, which makes the sherds "of some interest". It is "by no means conclusive evidence" that they "had hit upon the very spot" (p.68).
- Fig. 6 labels *Solomon's cistern (It. 23)* and *Earthquake fissure* (EVIDENCE, image).

**Item 24 (60 cubits from the "Solomon" aqueduct toward the watchtower, 3 cubits).** EVIDENCE, pp.67–68/PDF 101–102; Plate 20.
- They "brought instruments to bear on the floors and walls" of the settlement, with little hope. The rocks were magnetic, and the stated depths were "far out of the range of our instruments", which reach "hardly more than eighteen inches".
- They made "one very shallow trench outside the main group of buildings", in an open courtyard that "seemed to be at a point indicated by the scroll". **No result is stated for this trench. No item number and no depth are given.**
- Then, where the instruments gave a strong reaction "at the western end of the Hall of Congregation", "our narrow trench brought to light an oven" and a group of broken pottery, "completely overlooked by former archaeologists" (p.68). Plate 20: "Making a sounding at the western end of the Hall".
- INF: the courtyard trench most likely tested Item 24. The 1960 book had put Item 24's 90-ft fix midway along the south wall of the great courtyard (1960 ext.). The 1964 Fig. 6 drops the 1960 *It. 24* sight-line (EVIDENCE, image).
- INF: the "Again" sentence is ambiguous. It may describe one trench, or two (courtyard, then Hall).

**Sacred-meal deposit (no item).** EVIDENCE, pp.68–69/PDF 102–103.
- The expedition added one to the known animal-bone pot deposits. It was "hidden inside an interior wall of one of the rooms", close to the silver-coin hoard, and "it was one of our scientific instruments that detected" it.

**Item 25 (the tomb in Wadi Kippā’, on the way from Jericho to Secacah, 7 cubits).** EVIDENCE, pp.69–70/PDF 103–104.
- **Candidate routes.** The modern dirt track along the shore, turning inland, follows an ancient track that "could well be" the scroll's road. Alternatively, the road is the route along the cliff foot entering from the NW.
- **Cemeteries.** The main cemetery has about 1,100 graves; the bodies lie in side-niches about 4½ ft down. It is the archaeologists' work (cited to PDSS).
- **No tomb found.** "No tomb has appeared as yet actually in the side" of the wadi "to my knowledge". Allegro has seen "more ancient burials dug into the soft limestone sides" of a watercourse a little to the north.
- **Proposal.** The wadis where the two routes cross "merit a very close inspection".
- **No digging is reported** (EVIDENCE by absence).

**Other Qumran facts (EVIDENCE).**
- The hoard is 558 Tyrian tetradrachmae in three juglets under a floor, about "fifteen pounds" (p.44/PDF 62; p.66/PDF 84; Plate 19). The 1960 figure was about 20 lbs.
- The excavations "usually stopped at the lower floor levels" (p.160/PDF 210).

### 1.4 Jericho "Gerizim" = Cypros (Item 58); Taurus; Alexandrium; Docus (Item 32). Ch. 8, pp.71–78/PDF 105–112; Fig. 7; Plates 21–25

- **Item 58: identification unchanged (EVIDENCE).**
  - The Samaritan Gerizim is rejected again. The directions are too vague for a hilltop "dotted with dozens of pits and cisterns", and hiding Jewish treasure in a Samaritan holy place is "inconceivable" (p.71/PDF 105).
  - Item 58 is the Jericho "Gerizim": Herod's Cypros, with Taurus as "Ebal", at the mouth of the Wadi Kelt (p.72/PDF 106; Fig. 7).
  - The new reading is "under the ascent of its upper ditch" (p.71).
- **Cypros fieldwork (EVIDENCE, p.75/PDF 109; Plates 23–24).**
  - From the upper slopes the fortress outline and a tower "marked by fallen stones" are visible. Just inside the tower is the open mouth of a cistern.
  - "our expedition tried clearing this shaft". "after some twenty feet" it was "only then beginning to bell out". It "could well have extended a further fifty feet below".
  - Where the "upper ditch" or "pit" lay, "only a full excavation" could tell.
  - INF: they stopped at about 20 ft. The cistern was not bottomed and nothing was found.
- **Herodian fortress system (EVIDENCE, pp.73–75).**
  - Alexandrium (Sartaba), Docus, Cypros and Taurus, Hyrcania, Herodium, Masada, Machaerus and a second Herodium make "nine fortresses".
  - He quotes Stewart Perowne at length, his companion when "first visiting some of these sites a few years ago" (p.74/PDF 108). The visit is undated.
  - "None of these fortresses has been fully excavated" (p.75).
- **Docus = Item 32 (new, EVIDENCE).**
  - Docus "appears to be the site of the treasure deposits of Item 32" (p.73/PDF 107). Item 32 now reads "In Dok, under the eastern corner of the guardhouse" (p.24/PDF 42).
  - Mount of Temptation / Quarantana (p.76; Plate 25). "only a full excavation" could show where the guardhouse corner was, and how a deposit "some ten-and-a-half feet beneath the surface" was reached (p.77/PDF 111).
  - Caves on the lower slopes, "recently excavated", produced skeletons possibly of Simon's time. The excavators are not named and it is not said to be Allegro's work (p.77).
  - **No expedition work at Docus is reported** (EVIDENCE by absence).
- **Wadi Kelt.** Plates 21–22 are views: across the Kelt toward Taurus from Cypros, and from the Kelt north toward Alexandrium. No work is described.
- **‘Ain Fara (Item 46).** EVIDENCE by absence: the 1960 identification of Item 46 with ‘Ain Fārah is gone. Item 46 now reads "the cistern which is alongside the irrigation channels" fed from the Great Wadi (p.25/PDF 43), and the book does not discuss where it is. Fig. 7 still shows *‘Ain Farah* and *Kh. Farah*. NB: the "Wadi Farah" on p.73 is the northern one under Alexandrium, not the Kelt.

### 1.5 Kidron gorge, Mar Saba and Christmas Cave (Item 36). Ch. 13, pp.113–115/PDF 163–165; Plates 29, 33

- **Item 36, EVIDENCE.** The Kidron "gorge" (ṣôq) of Item 36 is set beside the scapegoat precipice, about 12 miles out. It must be "some well-defined constriction" (p.114/PDF 164).
  - Mar Saba is the most striking gorge, but only nine or ten miles away measured along the valley. Twelve miles as the crow flies reaches the final gorges near the Dead Sea (p.114).
  - No firm site is chosen. INF: this moves away from 1960's preference for the Kidron–Hinnom confluence near the city (1960 ext., Item 36).
- **Christmas Cave, EVIDENCE.**
  - It lies a few miles short of those final gorges. There "a small scrolls expedition led by myself in recent years" "sought and found evidence of habitation in the early centuries" AD (p.114/PDF 164; see *Search in the Desert*).
  - Jewish warriors and families "must have taken refuge" there in the Second Revolt (p.114).
  - **No date, no season and no name "Mazin".** The Christmas Cave is not tied to any scroll item.
- **Mar Saba.** Plate 33 is "Part of the lower Kidron (“Wadi of Fire”)" with the monastery. No work there is reported. Mar Saba is also discussed historically (St. Saba at Hyrcania, p.54).

### 1.6 Kidron tombs: "Tomb of Zadok" (Item 52), "Garden of Zadok" (Item 53), Absalom (Item 49). Ch. 12, pp.103–112/PDF 137–162; Figs. 12, 15; Plates 30–32

- **Item 52 placement, EVIDENCE.**
  - "the Portico's southern corner" = the Pinnacle, the SE Haram corner (p.104).
  - The tombs line up on that corner: Jehoshaphat, Absalom, St. James and Zechariah (p.105).
  - An unfinished sepulchre south of Zechariah, almost in line with the Haram's south wall, "fits better than the others" as the "tomb of Zadok" (p.105/PDF 139).
  - **Change:** in 1960 this was the Bene Hezir tomb *or* the façade south of it.
- **Excavation, EVIDENCE.** "On our expedition we made an excavation here and around the base" "of the Zechariah monument" (p.105/PDF 139). Findings:
  - The unnamed tomb "was intended to be a replica" of the St. James (Bene Hezir) sepulchre (p.106/PDF 140).
  - They were able to "clear the ground in front of the huge monolith" (Zechariah): "There we did, in fact, discover a courtyard". There had been "a chamber at some time dug into the pedestal of the monument" (p.108/PDF 158; Fig. 15).
  - From the back of that chamber "a crack in the rock exuded a trickle of water". They also found "a roughly hewn conduit from the sill of the little chamber" across the courtyard to the platform edge. It perhaps led to a cistern "still buried beneath many feet of debris" (p.108).
  - The scroll reference plus these findings are "the first indication" that such tombs had gardens or courtyards (p.108).
  - The unfinished tomb "had proceeded no further than a rough hewing out of the pillars" (p.111/PDF 161; Plate 32). Enough rock was cut for a body in the entrance, and "a skeleton was, in fact, found lying there, feet to the sill".
  - Chisel marks show a slab being cut round when work stopped abruptly. The pillars are cracked and the porch roof had collapsed, perhaps in an earthquake. The crack runs across the Zechariah monolith, and "this too had been left unfinished" (p.111).
  - Next door is a single burial chamber with "an entrance only some two feet square" (p.111).
  - **Treasure angle:** below the unfinished "exedra" the cliff is much cut about. The inner end of another small tomb niche was found ending "very nearly “under the pillar.”" (pp.111–112/PDF 161–162).
  - **No depths, trench dimensions, dates or team names** are given for this excavation, and no treasure was found (EVIDENCE by absence).
- **Interpretive additions, EVIDENCE.**
  - The Garden of Zadok might be the Garden of the Agony (pp.108–109). The area might hold Joseph of Arimathea's tomb (p.110).
  - Most strikingly: if the "Tomb of Zadok" had been "the temporary resting place of the Christian Messiah" (p.112), the name Zadduk would fit the title "the Just".
- **Absalom (Item 49).** EVIDENCE by absence: the item is only listed (p.25). Fig. 15 still marks a *cistern* west of Absalom (image). No work is reported.

### 1.7 Jerusalem: Temple area/Haram, the Rock, Antonia, Bethesda, Siloam, Wilson's Arch, Shaveh, Golden Gate (Chs. 9–11, 14–17)

- **Haram underground survey: attempted, not done (EVIDENCE, pp.129–131/PDF 179–181).**
  - Recent works around the Dome of the Rock, including lifting some paving, had partly "neutralized" the area. "I was allowed on one occasion to take photographs of the Rock" (p.130/PDF 180).
  - "This, anyway, is what we proposed to do during our recent expedition." The plan was to get under the platform from an edge, through vaulting. The local authorities, "from the governor of the city to the custodian", were willing. "our surveyor" was to produce the first measurements of the installations under the paving (p.130).
  - Then the army was consulted. The commanding officer "thought it militarily inopportune to make our survey". By then it was "too late in the expedition's timetable" (p.131/PDF 181).
  - Afterwards a journal of the French Biblical School carried an article "openly rejoicing at the miscarriage of our plans". It was written by "the foremost protagonist" of the legend view, the same person who had opposed giving the Arab authorities a translation (p.131).
  - INF: the surveyor is presumably Wright (not stated).
- **Temple items (3, 5–8, 10, 16–17, 26, 31, 34, 40, 60–61).** These are identified from the Mishnah, Josephus and the PEF Survey (Warren, Wilson, Conder), whose work is quoted at length (pp.79–82, 93–95, 99–100, 120–123, 128, 141–145). **No fieldwork of Allegro's.**
  - The Golden Gate (Item 26) rests on Warren's failed tunnelling: 143 ft from the gate, a shaft 25 ft further down, tunnel work stopping more than 55 ft below ground (pp.120–121).
  - The cave under the Rock: "Recent repair work" exposed a drainage canal (p.139). The work is not Allegro's.
- **Antonia (Strouthion) and Bethesda (Item 56), EVIDENCE, pp.83–88/PDF 117–122; Fig. 9; Plates 26–28.**
  - With the Superior's permission, "you may be led below the kitchens" to the double cistern (p.85). The tanks are 165 and 127 ft long and about 20 ft wide (p.86).
  - Plates 27–28 show the cisterns and the north chamber. **No visit by Allegro is stated** (INF: the plates suggest one; photographer not named).
  - Bethesda: "Recent excavations" at St. Anne's (Plate 26) are others' work (p.84).
- **Wilson's Arch (Item 29), Outer Valley (Item 35), Shaveh (Items 37–38), Siloam.** These are argued from Warren and Wilson (pp.89–101). No fieldwork. Siloam enters only through the King's Garden topography (p.98).
- **Item 56: identification shifted (EVIDENCE, pp.83–84/PDF 117–118).** "it may well be that Item 56 of our scroll gives" "at last the contemporary Hebrew underlying the name". The name is House of the Twin Pools, *Beth Eshdathayin*, i.e. Bethesda. The Antonia double cistern is kept only to explain Josephus's "Strouthion" as a misread *yemimah*, "settling basin(s)" (pp.86–87). In 1960 Item 56 was the Antonia Twin Pools, with Bethesda as the alternative.

## 2. Changes from 1960

### 2.1 Format (EVIDENCE)

- **Foreword.** Dated "Prestbury, Cheshire" "September 1963" (PDF 11). The edition is "intended primarily for the non-specialist". "The complete facsimile, transliteration, and notes of the original edition" are omitted. Photographs are kept to a minimum, with readers referred to PDSS.
- **Structure.** 19 chapters (1960: 8 chapters plus notes).
  - The translation is in Ch. 3 (pp.21–27/PDF 39–45). Specialists are sent to "the first edition of this book, pages 32-55" (p.21).
  - Still **61 items**, numbered 1–61.
- **Basis.** Milik's "definitive version has appeared". Allegro was "able to utilize his work in revising my initial decipherment". He follows Milik in many readings but rejects his fiction view (p.37/PDF 55). He also rejects Milik's "essence" for "tithe" (p.68). [BK: DJD III, 1962.]
- **New narrative chapters.**
  - Chs. 3–4 (pp.15–38): Kuhn's study; the "official" fairy-tale release; the 1957 Amman visit; the friction with Milik, de Vaux and the museum; Milik's French-journal translation (pp.29–38).
  - Ch. 5: treasure legends and tithe law.
  - Ch. 6: Mird.
  - Ch. 8: Herodian fortresses, Docus.
  - Ch. 13: lower Kidron, Christmas Cave.
  - Ch. 15: the Haram attempt.
  - Ch. 19: Fund, institute proposal.

## 4. Dates (EVIDENCE unless tagged)

- **Survey season.** Only "In the winter of 1959-60" (p.55/PDF 73). There is no month, day, length or date range anywhere, and no "April".
  - BK (from research/history/wright_milik1961_el_mird_extracted.md): Wright, *Biblica* 42, gives "first half of April 1960"; *Search in the Desert* gives winter 1959–60.
  - INF: Allegro's wording agrees with *Search in the Desert*. Early to mid April is spring, so either "winter" is loose, or the work spanned more than one visit. The book does not decide this.
- **Haram attempt.** "our recent expedition" (p.130). Undated, and not said to be the same trip.
- **Kidron "Zadok" excavation.** "On our expedition" (p.105). Undated.
- **Christmas Cave.** "in recent years" (p.114). Undated, and no name "Mazin".
- **Dead Sea Scrolls Fund.** "In the last year or two a start has been made" (p.161/PDF 211). "a charitable foundation has been organized called the Dead Sea Scrolls Fund". "already two expeditions have been mounted under its auspices". It refers to *Search in the Desert* for "our first campaign in the Kidron Valley". No dates or sites are given for the second expedition.
- **Perowne fortress visit.** "a few years ago" (p.74).
- **Foreword.** September 1963 (PDF 11). Copyright 1960, 1964; Anchor edition 1964 (PDF 8). The blurb lists *Search in the Desert* (Doubleday, 1964) (PDF 6).
- **Mird history.** Monks reoccupied it in 1925 (p.54). Bedouin finds came in 1952 and 1953, followed by the Louvain party (p.55). The "last survey" was in 1953 (p.55).
- **Scroll.**
  - Found "spring of 1952" (p.1) and "on 20 March 1952" (p.2). The 1960 date was 14 March.
  - Smaller roll delivered "In July 1955" (p.6). The 1955 permission came in spring (p.4).
  - Transcript sent in November. The larger roll arrived "on 2 January" and was finished "on the evening of 16 January" (p.20).
  - Allegro was in Jordan in 1957 (p.33).
- **Deposit.** "most probably during the spring of A.D. 68" (p.159). Vespasian reached Jericho in June 68 (p.154).

## 5. What the book does NOT say (EVIDENCE by absence after a full read and keyword checks, unless tagged)

- **Survey dates.** No exact date, month or duration. No "April 1960". The expedition is never called the "Copper Scroll Expedition".
- **Team and backers.** No team list besides Wright and an unnamed English scientist. No Jordanian official or other member is named. No sponsor, newspaper, funder or detector type or make is named.
- **Mird monument (Item 2).**
  - No clearance "around and through" it, no trench, no depth, no "bedrock", and no "No chamber or deposits" statement.
  - The NW platform is described as a visible feature, not as an excavation result.
  - No finds besides sherds in the wall cracks.
  - Allegro explicitly did not dismantle it.
- **Mird steps (Item 1).** No location found; there was no search or depth. The stepped tunnels are not tied to Item 1.
- **Buqei‘a (Items 18–20).** No visit and no search for presses; there is no discussion beyond the translation list.
- **Qumran.**
  - The courtyard trench has no item number, no depth and no stated result.
  - The Hall of Congregation trench has no depth, and no item number beyond "a point indicated by the scroll".
  - No dimensions are given for the cistern compartment.
  - No digging was done for Items 21–22 or 25.
  - No trench locations are marked on Fig. 6.
- **Cypros.** The ~20 ft clearance is the only depth reported for any expedition dig. The cistern was not bottomed and nothing was found.
- **Wadi Kelt / ‘Ain Fara.** No work. Item 46's ‘Ain Farah identification is gone.
- **Samaritan Gerizim.** Not visited.
- **Jerusalem.**
  - No fieldwork at the Temple area: the planned survey was blocked.
  - No fieldwork at Absalom's monument, Bethesda, Siloam, Wilson's Arch or the Golden Gate.
  - No stated visit to the Antonia cisterns, although Plates 27–28 show them.
- **Kidron "Zadok" excavation.** No dates, team, depths or dimensions. No treasure. The skeleton is not dated or described further.
- **Christmas Cave.** No date, no "Mazin", and no tie to a scroll item.
- **Treasure.** No find of treasure anywhere. The book still allows that in the desert sites "it is still there" (p.160).
- **Photographs and plans.** No photographer is named for any plate. No plan is said to be drawn by Allegro: Fig. 5 is credited to Wright's *Biblica* article.
- **Milik.** No citation details for Milik's definitive edition or his French-journal translation [BK: DJD III 1962; RB 66, 1959].
