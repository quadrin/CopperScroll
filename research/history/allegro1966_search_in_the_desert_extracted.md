## 1. Chronology

### 1a. Background dates the book gives
- 1947, "early summer": Muhammad adh-Dhib finds the first cave near Qumran. p. 2 (PDF 28) [E]
- 1952: Ta'amireh find the "Cave of the Wounded Partridge" in the marl plateau, "no more than fifty yards away" from the ruins, p. 101 (PDF 135). The book never calls it Cave 4 [BK: it is 4Q]. Also in 1952 a habitation cave "in the Qumran area" yields "pottery and even tent poles", p. 100 (PDF 134). Bedouin also find documents in an underground cistern at Khirbet Mird in 1952, p. 111 (PDF 145). [E]
- 1953: Allegro is invited to Jerusalem to edit the fragments, p. 4 (PDF 30). He has been involved with the Scrolls "since ... 1953", p. 138 (PDF 172). [E]
- January 1956: Bedouin find Cave Eleven's manuscripts. p. 155 (PDF 189) [E]
- Copper Scroll: it lay "three years" in the Jerusalem museum showcases before being cut open at the College of Science and Technology, Manchester. Allegro supervised the cutting and made "the first decipherment". The book gives no year for this. pp. 92-93 (PDF 126-127) [E]

### 1c. Years: what is stated and what is inferred
- The main expedition's year is **never stated**. Only months are given: November, December/Christmas, January. [E]
- The only explicit year for the expedition story is the film broadcast date, "April 17, 1963", p. 164 (PDF 198). [E]
- Two further clues point the same way: the "wretched winter" with January blizzards along the route (p. 158), and the death of de Langhe in the following October (p. 164). [BK: the European "Big Freeze" was in winter 1962-63; de Langhe died in 1963.]
- So the main expedition = **mid-November 1962 to late January 1963**. [I] This matches the WorldCat blurb's "winter 1962-63". The blurb's Kh. Mazin component is only partly right: the Mazin ruin itself was *not* dug in 1962-63 (p. 146), only the cliff caves near it (see Sites).
- Mazin season: "winter of 1961", ending on Christmas Day. [E]
  - At the expedition's last day Allegro says his mind "went back twelve months" to the Bethlehem roadside, p. 162 (PDF 196). [E]
  - Together these give **about early December to 25/26 December 1961**. [I] Counting back three weeks from Christmas puts the start in early December 1961. [I]
  - Conflicting hint: p. 2 (PDF 28) says the area "had sprung into prominence thirteen years previously" (1947 + 13 = 1960). This would suggest December 1960. I treat it as loose counting, but it is a flagged inconsistency. [I]

## 2. Sites

### 2.1 Mazin (the book never writes "Khirbet Mazin", "Qasr el-Yahud" or "harbour")
- **Location** [E]:
  - "a few miles south of Qumran on the sea shore", p. 3 (PDF 29).
  - It lies below the cliffs where the Kidron (Wadi en-Nar) breaks out, behind the camp, p. 9 (PDF 35). Behind Mazin, "the Kidron Valley ends in a sheer drop of several hundred feet", p. 18 (PDF 46).
  - A headland between Qumran and Mazin "drops into the sea cutting off the coastal track", p. 18. This is the "rocky headland south of Ain Feshkha", p. 77 (PDF 109).
  - "the shoreline sweeps round in a small natural bay" below the ruin. The camp stood "on a plateau overlooking the ruins", p. 77.
  - En-gedi is "About fourteen miles" south (de Langhe's estimate), p. 81 (PDF 113).
- **Earlier work** [E]: de Langhe had only made "a sounding at the gate which was apparently a massive affair", p. 3.
- **Mazin season (winter 1961)** [E]:
  - "we dug trenches in and around the building" over three weeks, using Bethlehem workmen, p. 4 (PDF 30).
  - Result: ruins "probably dating from the time of Herod the Great". Their position links them to the ancient route from Jerusalem across to the east bank and the hot springs of "Calirrhoe" [sic OCR/print], p. 4.
  - They concentrated on the buildings "hoping to find in them a clue" to other habitation nearby, p. 4.
  - No objects from the Mazin trenches are described. [E: silence]
- **Water level and boats** [E]:
  - The water mark round the gateway shows the sea "some twenty feet or more" higher. De Langhe speculates the bay was used for boats "as far back as the time of Herod", p. 80 (PDF 112). This is conversation, not a measured survey.
  - A matching landing for Callirrhoe on the east shore is supposed (p. 81). Fig. 4 shows the Madeba-map boat (p. 81).
- **1962-63 expedition** [E]:
  - Mazin was a camp only. Supplies came by Army landing craft and dinghies (pp. 62, 77-80, 86, 91).
  - De Langhe "loved his little ruin" and, "although we were not digging it", wandered round the walls, p. 146 (PDF 180).
  - **Not dug in 1962-63.** [E]
  - The camp was withdrawn before Christmas because of "Another water emergency", the finished Byzantine cave and the completed cliff exploration, p. 146.
- **Shore south of Mazin** [E]: a few members trekked south and found "some mounds, certainly, but none so big as Mazin". Gray and de Langhe thought them worth investigating, "But not on this trip", p. 123 (PDF 157). Not dug.

### 2.2 Cliff cave near Mazin ("Byzantine cave", later Chalcolithic) - Plate 11
- **Location** [E]: in the cliff face south of the Kidron gorge mouth. Reached by crossing a dangerous scree slope. Ibrahim said it was unexplored. During "the previous year's expedition" they had noticed "one or two promising Byzantine caves in that vicinity", p. 78 (PDF 110).
- **Work** [E]:
  - The entrance was nearly blocked by rockfall; it was widened first.
  - The cave is "fairly shallow" and runs parallel to the cliff.
  - Byzantine pottery near the surface. John Gray estimated "a week or two's hard work to clear the cave", p. 79 (PDF 111).
  - Arab pick, mattock and basket methods are described, p. 79.
- **Result** [E]: it "turned out to go back to chalcolithic". Under a ledge they found "a perfectly preserved small jar of that period", p. 123 (PDF 157) (Plate 11). Clearing was completed before the camp withdrew, p. 146.
- **Other work by the Mazin climbers** [E]: Trueman and Roberts worked "along the cliffs and up this gorge a little way", p. 123. The Mazin climbers also worked the Kidron gorge "from the seaward end", p. 130 (PDF 164).

### 2.3 Kidron gorge (Wadi en-Nar) and Christmas Cave
- **Identification** [E]: "the Wadi en-Nar (the Valley of Fire)" is "the 'Brook Kidron'", p. 9 (PDF 35). Christmas Cave is in this gorge, *not* in Wadi Qumran.
- **Location** [E]:
  - On foot from the Mazin camp: "For a mile or two we followed the dry wadi bed", p. 9.
  - The entrance is "in the cliff face on the other side of the ravine". It is hidden by an overhang and by a right-angled rock wall, p. 11 (PDF 39).
  - The tunnel ends at "a great natural window overlooking the wadi bed hundreds of feet below", p. 12 (PDF 40).
- **Overland route** [E]:
  - Howard's "twenty-thousand scale chart" shows a track from the north crossing the Kidron "about four miles inland", p. 18 (PDF 46).
  - Tested from the Jerusalem-Jericho road crossroads (pp. 51-53):
    - about four miles to the "open plain of the Vale of Achor", which has a north-south track;
    - the Kidron "some six miles ahead", p. 53 (PDF 83);
    - then east down the wadi bed;
    - camp "not more than three miles as the crow flies from Christmas Cave", p. 55 (PDF 85).
  - The route was marked with cairns with Ibn Ubaid help, p. 59 (PDF 89). It was later driven in about 3 hours (p. 74) or 2 hours (p. 85).
  - The camp was on rocky platforms above the gorge: a kitchen cave, a generator ledge, and a "heliport" hill with an "H sign laid out in stones", pp. 72-74, 151.
- **Mazin season, Christmas Day** [E]:
  - A shepherd boy had shown Salim "a cave near here that some men have been working in recently", p. 11.
  - Layout: tunnel, then main chamber "about six yards square", p. 13 (PDF 41). Further tunnel, smaller cave, window, p. 12.
  - Previous diggers had cut a trench "straight in from the tunnel entrance, but only for a few yards". Cloth and reed matting showed in its side under the bat dung, p. 13.
  - Allegro "marked off a small area" at the back of the cave and Salim dug a small trench (p. 13). Finds:
    - Roman-period pottery, cloth, a leather piece (sandal?), p. 13;
    - a bone needle, a flint blade, and a coin with palm tree and "SH-M-'-N" (Simon, Second Revolt), p. 14 (PDF 42).
  - Work stopped for lack of light. Named "Christmas Cave" because the day was Christmas, pp. 15-16.
  - Allegro judged it was probably *not* the source of the Bethlehem cache, p. 16.
- **1962-63, first visit** [E]: three Ibn Ubaid Bedouin were sifting the old spoil with a sardine-tin lamp. Their trophies (cloth, a small coin) were bought for piastres, pp. 57-59 (PDF 87-89).
- **1962-63 excavation** [E], supervised by Doug Miller with Salim's men:
  - Lights were run in from the 3 kW generator on the platform beside the camp (pp. 75, 83, 130).
  - After survey they decided "to concentrate on this lower cave", p. 83 (PDF 115).
  - Stratigraphy: "Below the stamped earth of the Roman-period floor" lay a thick layer of twigs, then "potsherds which looked chalcolithic in date", with no occupation in between, p. 121 (PDF 155).
  - They had to "restrict the level at which we excavated"; workmen were kept "to the upper levels", p. 121.
  - Finds: multicoloured cloth stuck to a leather pouch and rush mat (with a small animal or bird bone), cooking pots, date and olive stones. No waterskins, no cistern, p. 122 (PDF 156).
  - Upper chambers: Byzantine pottery; near the window "a complete jar, probably of a later, Arab date" and "part of a sword hilt", p. 122.
  - Interruptions: generator failure (pp. 129-130); a 350 W Army Signals stand-by set and a diesel 3 kW Army Engineers generator flown from Zerqa (pp. 137-143).
  - End: Doug reported work "as complete as it could be, at least for that year". The gear, including the generator, was removed by helicopter, p. 158 (PDF 192).
  - Not done [E/I]: the chalcolithic levels were deliberately not fully excavated (p. 121). The tunnel widening with the electric hammer was planned (p. 35) but is never reported as done [I: silence].
- **Kidron gorge cave survey (1962-63)** [E]:
  - During the generator outage, all workers led by Salim split into "small groups working systematically along the sides of the valley" below the cave toward the sea, p. 130.
  - A cave with a collapsed front, spotted by Keith with binoculars, needed "three days' work to clear it completely". Mar Saba monks' occupation. Finds: "three small lamps, two of them quite perfect", pear-shaped with grape-cluster moulding, p. 131 (PDF 165).
  - Allegro: "hundreds of such caves as yet untouched along this valley", p. 131. He adds that "the Kidron caves ought to be thoroughly searched", p. 135 (PDF 169).

### 2.4 Vale of Achor (the plain crossed by the overland track)
- [E] Allegro calls the plain the "Vale of Achor" (pp. 52-53) or "Vale of Trouble" (p. 59). He points out "mounds of Iron Age settlements", which he identifies with the "cities of the wilderness", p. 53 (PDF 83).
- [E] The Alsager party walked inland to ancient sites, "not a few" of which "may be seen in the Vale of Achor", p. 103 (PDF 137). No digging is reported.
- [BK] This plain is el-Buqei'a. The book never uses that name.

### 2.5 Khirbet Mird = Kastellion = Hyrcania (Fig. 5, Plate 10)
- **Names** [E]: "Kastellion, or, as it is known today, Khirbet Mird"; "Mird" is from Aramaic *marda* (fortress), p. 111 (PDF 145). Hyrcania was built by John Hyrcanus (134-104 B.C.), p. 112 (PDF 146). Herod captured it in 31 B.C., p. 112. Saba's laura dates to the early sixth century, p. 113.
- **Location** [E]:
  - A "conical mountain" to the right (west) of the north-south track across the Vale. Howard took "a bearing on the fortress" there, p. 53.
  - The "motorway" track is "an ancient road to Bethlehem from the north", p. 112.
  - A row of black basalt stones runs out toward the plain (probably a defensive wall), p. 114 (PDF 148).
  - The path up is on the northwest side, "the last hundred feet or so", p. 114.
  - From the south wall the Vale stretches "to the hills south of the Kidron", p. 115 (PDF 149).
- **Earlier work at Mird** [E/I]:
  - Traces were found on an earlier visit "in connection with our survey of the treasure sites", p. 113 (PDF 147). "Our surveyor charted them and afterward published his plans and description", p. 113.
  - [I] Fig. 5 is credited "after G. R. H. Wright, Biblica 42", p. 109 (PDF 143). So the published plan is very probably Wright's, and "our surveyor" probably = G. R. H. Wright. The text does not name him. [BK: Wright, "The Archaeological Remains at El Mird", *Biblica* 42 (1961).]
  - On that "previous visit" the surveyor "had found parts" of the church's marble screen "scattered down the hillside", p. 114.
  - The cemetery on a low hill to the southeast: "Excavation of one or two of the graves" "showed them to date from the Herodian period" (pottery), p. 115.
  - The SW "monument": "On our previous visit we had excavated around the sides", p. 117 (PDF 151). They found "a platform had been built out on the northwestern side", p. 118 (PDF 152).
  - Metal detection at the monument: "We did try a mine detector here". The rocks were magnetic: "We got signals everywhere!", p. 118.
  - [I] These "previous visit" activities belong to the treasure-site survey. The only such campaign the book dates is winter 1959-60 (p. 93).
- **1962-63 visit** [E]: one day "when returning to Christmas Cave from Jericho". The party was Allegro, Howard, Keith and Anna, p. 111 (PDF 145). The text then has "Anne called us over" on p. 114 (PDF 148). [I: that is probably a slip or OCR error for Anna, since p. 114 also speaks of "Our three architects" (Howard, Keith, Anna).] What they saw and did:
  - **Cisterns and aqueduct**: "two vast cisterns below us hewn out of the rock". Between them "a bridge carrying a wall of masonry surmounted by a plastered aqueduct", bringing water from the hills behind. Mird is "artificially separated by a large ditch". The aqueduct blocks show Herodian marginal drafting (Anna), p. 114.
  - **Church and mosaics**: the church on the north side, with a plain mosaic floor; an enclosure south of the church with a coloured mosaic bird. They brushed and washed it, p. 114.
  - **Courtyard vault ("well")**: Keith found a well-head in the middle of the courtyard. Allegro went down by electron ladder and rope, followed by each of the others (pp. 116-117). Observations:
    - debris pile, then "several feet of silt" (estimated), plastered walls;
    - roof "Arched, squarish blocks and no mortar. This is certainly Herodian.", p. 116 (PDF 150), compared with the Antonia cisterns;
    - size "about forty feet" long, "some sixteen feet high", "twenty-five feet wide", p. 117;
    - the end blocking walls are later; it was plastered as a cistern, but "this may not have been its original use". It could be a palace chamber or passage, p. 117 (this is Plate 10's caption text).
    - Not excavated. [E]
  - **East "steps" test (Copper Scroll item 1)**: Howard went "newly restored compass in hand" to the east side. That side is a sheer bluff, "hardly the place for an entrance". Conclusion: the entrance must be inside the palace, pp. 115-116 (PDF 149-150).
  - **SW monument (item 2 candidate)**: square, "sides of about thirty-five feet", rubble masonry round a rock pinnacle. The "third course" of stones is easily seen and Anna peered into the cracks, pp. 117-118. No 1962-63 digging there is reported.
  - **Stepped tunnels on the north side**: in the gully separating Mird from the hills.
    - Opening "some six feet up from the valley bed", with "about a hundred" rock-cut steps ending in unexcavated fill. Recently cleared by someone (unknown), p. 119 (PDF 153). They descended it.
    - A second tunnel farther along is "blocked to within a foot or two of the entrance", p. 120 (PDF 154).
    - Purpose unknown (water system? escape?). Fig. 5 labels "Stepped tunnels" (OCR of drawing).
- **Why no full excavation** [E]: "nobody has yet attempted a full-scale excavation at Mird". One reason: "the monks of Mar Saba still have their rights" to the Byzantine settlement. They partly rebuilt it between the wars and used it until 1939, p. 113. Allegro thinks the Herodian palace plan lies below, p. 113.
- **1952 finds** [E]: Bedouin "searching in one of the underground cisterns" found Gospel, classical and Christian Palestinian Aramaic fragments, p. 111. Allegro thinks the Ibn Ubaid parchment shown to the lost Alsager student might come from that cache, pp. 109-111.

### 2.6 Mar Saba (Kidron, upstream)
- [E] Byzantine sherds in the Kidron caves are attributed to monks "from the Mar Saba monastery farther up the valley", p. 10 (PDF 36).
- [E] The Bethlehem truck reached the Vale "down by Mar Saba", p. 75 (PDF 107).
- [E] The Alsager party visited and stayed in the guest annexe. One student got lost; Ibn Ubaid Bedouin fed him and showed him "inscribed parchment"; he was guided back via the monastery, pp. 103-109 (PDF 137-143).
- [E] Allegro's ideas: Saint Saba's "pillar of flame" legend may preserve a tradition of the Jewish-Christian flight down the Kidron (c. A.D. 68), pp. 134-135. "the Greek Orthodox Church has in mind" a search of its own, p. 132.
- No digging at Mar Saba itself. [E: silence]

### 2.7 Ain Feshkha (Plates 9, 12)
- **Before the expedition** [E]: "Recent excavations" (by others, unnamed) showed the Essenes used the spring and built "plantation walls and houses and some kind of industrial installation" with "plastered tanks and conduits", p. 17 (PDF 45). The water is too salty to drink. Allegro doubted the cliffs south of the Qumran monastery had been fully explored, "particularly around the headland". Robert had once found a never-searched cave "high up in those cliffs" with very early occupation, p. 17.
- **Camp** [E]:
  - First "a small holding camp until the Alsager party arrives", p. 62 (PDF 92).
  - The tent was pitched "some way from the pool on a level site beneath the cliffs". Camp guard: Isa, p. 94 (PDF 128).
  - The Alsager tents stood "under the cliffs", with a latrine shelter on the plateau below, pp. 98-99 (PDF 132-133).
- **Work** [E]:
  - The Alsager brief was to "work over the cliffs immediately behind the camp" and then northward, p. 99.
  - Trueman and Roberts did the high cliffs; Norman brought sherds daily to John Gray, p. 99.
  - A suspected "filled in" cave proved to be a natural scree formation, pp. 99-100.
  - Later the Alsager group "were working northward towards Qumran", p. 146 (PDF 180).
  - The camp was closed after the Alsager party left, p. 158 (PDF 192).
- **Not done** [E: silence]: no excavation of the Feshkha buildings, tanks, conduits or pool is described.
- King Hussein landed at the camp on Christmas morning and Dick filmed it, p. 150 (PDF 184).

### 2.8 Khirbet Qumran and its caves (general)
- **1959-60** [E]: "Our detectors" gave "a reading at one end of the Essene Hall of Congregation". A "narrow trench" produced much pottery and animal remains, "almost certainly part of an oven" that earlier excavators missed by not sounding below the plaster floor, pp. 93-94 (PDF 127-128).
- **Coin hoard** [E]: "five hundred-odd silver Tyrian tetradrachmae buried in three earthenware pots" under a room floor. Whether it belongs to the Copper Scroll treasure is unknown. Allegro mocks the "thief from Jericho" explanation, p. 93.
- **1962-63** [E]:
  - On a drive, Allegro suggests that if Mazin fails they could "consider mounting a new one here at Qumran" to re-examine "apparently worked-out caves", pp. 91-92 (PDF 125-126). He says every visible cave "will have been looked in", p. 91.
  - Qumran's name: Allegro derives it from Hebrew *qimron* ("an arched box lid"), p. 92.
- **Caves 1 and 4** [E]: Cave 1 is described (jars, wrapped scrolls) at pp. 2-3 and 102. The Cave of the Wounded Partridge lies in the plateau "within a stone's throw of the monastery", in a tributary wadi face south of the monastery, pp. 100-102 (PDF 134-136).
- **1952 search figure** [E]: "two hundred and thirty-odd caves searched in that area"; "very many" showed "signs of habitation", p. 101 (PDF 135). [BK: this matches the 1952 cave survey, but the book does not name the survey, its directors or its dates.]

### 2.9 Cave Eleven and "Ibrahim's caves" (Fig. 6, Plate 13)
- **Location** [E]: from "the plateau north of Qumran". Ibrahim led Allegro to the cliff foot "above us was the gaping hole of Cave Eleven", p. 155 (PDF 189).
- **Ibrahim's earlier trek** [E]: Ibrahim "The year before he had led us on a long trek" to Qumran area caves, circling "one of the best-known scroll caves", p. 155. [I: "the year before" = the Mazin season.]
- **Cave Eleven history** [E]:
  - It "had defied detection until 1956"; Bedouin found the manuscripts in January 1956.
  - The archaeologists' "complete excavation" showed occupation back to Chalcolithic, p. 155.
  - Allegro later learned that the entrance had never been covered. Searchers mistook a roof fall for solid cliff. Ransom of "nearly £50,000" was paid; the Psalms scroll publication was sold to an American family for "some £25,000"; two scrolls were never recovered, pp. 156-157 (PDF 190-191).
- **Ibrahim's first cave** [E]:
  - Position: "about ten yards to its left" of Cave Eleven's mouth, under a ridge of solid cliff. Crevices between boulders mark a blocked entrance, p. 156 (PDF 190).
  - They opened a "long tunnel", but "no signs of human habitation". Probably used only by wild animals.
