**Notes to the table**

- [EV] Typology from 542–543/4–5. The numbers grouped by type are:
  - wide-open caves: 1, 10, 27, 31, 37, 39;
  - partly blocked by rockfalls: 40;
  - large but low, narrow or uneven: 14 (1Q), 8 (3Q), 19 (2Q), 4, 18, 32;
  - very small caves: 3, 7, 22, 26, 28, 29;
  - crevices: 9, 17;
  - clefts: 15, 21, 30;
  - low rock shelter: 12;
  - pottery heap under fallen blocks: 2.

  That makes 26 numbers. De Vaux concludes that most sites were stores or hiding places, not dwellings. Some members lived in huts or tents near the cliff (543/5).
- [EV] Periods. The article never gives a period number by number. Its only blanket statement is: "Tous les dépôts de poterie qui viennent d'être étudiés sont exactement contemporains" with the main phase of Kh. Qumrân (558/20). Elsewhere it notes 25 Qumran-type sites versus others with sherds of various, often very recent, periods (541/3).
- [INF] The 26 numbers named in the type passage, minus no. 14 (1Q, found in 1947), make exactly 25. That matches "25 de ces sites". It suggests that the numbers discussed are the Qumran-type sites and that the unlabelled numbers are the later or shepherd sites. This is suggestive arithmetic only: 40 − 26 = 14 unmentioned numbers, one of which (35) is probably not a cave, against 37 − 25 = 12 other-period caves. The article does not reconcile its 37 caves with 40 map numbers. The project's Reed figure (39) also differs.
- [EV] Quantities across all caves: "plus de 100 jarres et environ 70 couvercles" are represented among the collected sherds, against about twenty other pieces (552/14). Objects other than pottery are "très peu nombreux": tent poles (17), two bronze rings (3), a mat (12). There were no coins anywhere: "Nulle part on n'a recueilli de monnaies" (553/15; again 560–561/22, 29). The Khirbet had already yielded 250 coins (561/29).
- [EV-absence] Bones are not mentioned for any cave. Wood is mentioned only for no. 17.

## 2. Cave 8 (3Q) and its neighbours

**Location [EV]:** no. 8 in "la liste des grottes, pl. XX". The cave lies "environ 2 km au nord de Khirbet Qumrân", "sur le flanc sud d'un vallon" (555/17). Later the copper band is said to have been hidden in this cave, "éloignée de 2 km" from the Khirbet (558/20). **Map [INF]:** the dot is at about E 193.49 / N 129.75, ~2.1 km N of the Kh. Q. square, in a cluster of numbered points (3, 4, 7, 9, 10) that sits on and just east of the hachured lower cliff line. No watercourse is drawn right beside the cluster, so the "vallon" is not shown as a drawn wadi on the plate.

**Discovery and form [EV] (555/17):**
- The cave was found because a few sherds had slid outside: "Son existence fut révélée par quelques tessons qui avaient glissé à l'extérieur".
- It was originally fairly large, but the ceiling collapsed ("le plafond s'est effondré"). What survives intact is "un renfoncement large de 3 m, profond de 2 m". This recess continues as "une étroite galerie ascendante terminée en cul-de-sac".
- Nothing can be said of the front part, which has collapsed. Neither its height nor its original entrance is known.
- The ceiling of the inner chamber is very low above the rubble. Intact jars must almost have touched it. "Cette chambre n'a jamais pu être habitée."

**Distribution of finds [EV]:**
- Under the blocks obstructing the entrance there were few sherds. They were very numerous in the inner chamber, mixed with rock chips, and all the written fragments came from there (555/17).
- Totals collected: one lamp, parts of 2 jugs, 26 lids and about 40 jars (555/17).
- Clearing soon exposed small pieces of leather, then the first inscribed fragments. Humidity, rats and worms had destroyed almost all the manuscripts.
- Fragments (555–557/17–19):
  - the beginning of a beige leather roll with an Isaiah 1:1 lemma followed by a gloss-type commentary (pl. XXIV b), ruled with 9 mm between lines and letters 3 mm high;
  - pieces of about ten other manuscripts, one of which mentions the Angel of the Presence;
  - several pieces from a hymnic composition;
  - no biblical text recognised.

**Copper rolls in situ [EV] (557/19, pl. XXIII):**
- Position: "Près de l'entrée de la chambre intérieure", "contre la paroi rocheuse qui faisait le fond de la chambre écroulée". The rolls had been laid "l'un sur l'autre". "Ils n'étaient enveloppés ni protégés par rien". The roof fall had spared them and left them "isolés dans une sorte de niche".
- [INF] So the rolls lay at the threshold between the collapsed outer chamber and the inner recess, apart from the inner-chamber deposit of jars and fragments. The article does not tie them to any jar.
- Treatment: they were lifted with great care and gently brushed, which showed the reverse of a deeply engraved text in relief. At the Palestine Museum they were coated with celluloid dissolved in acetone.
- Material: nearly pure copper, completely oxidised; the sheet is under 1 mm thick and very brittle.
- Plans for opening: Prof. A. H. Corwin (Johns Hopkins) was testing a regeneration process on an oxidised replica. If that failed, the rolls would be cut into strips.
- Physical description (557–558/19–20):
  - originally three sheets, each 30 × 80 cm, riveted end to end into a band 2.40 m long;
  - the large roll holds two sheets still joined; the third sheet is the small roll (pl. XXIII lower, pl. XXIV a);
  - letters about 1 cm high, in columns, in square script like that of ossuaries.
- Content and interpretation (558/20): the visible part is not biblical. Its paragraphs, numerals, abbreviations and repeated formulas suggest a catalogue or tariff. De Vaux thinks the band was posted on a wall of the central building, then taken down, cut in two, rolled and hidden in the cave in a hasty abandonment. He made no sustained attempt to decipher it.

**How far 3Q was excavated [EV]:** "minutieusement fouillée dans toute son étendue par une petite équipe" working "pendant une dizaine de jours sous la direction d'Henri de Contenson" (555/17). **[INF]** "Toute son étendue" most naturally means the surviving recess and gallery. The article says the collapsed front part cannot be described, and it does not say the fallen roof blocks were cleared beyond the entrance area where "few sherds" were found. No excavation depth is given.

## 3. Method and coverage

**Origin and dates [EV] (540/2):**
- At the end of February 1952, while finishing at Murabbaʿât, de Vaux learned that Bedouin had located a new cave near the first one and were finding fragments there. Two scouts confirmed this.
- A Bethlehem dealer then offered two lots, which the École Archéologique Française and the Palestine Museum bought.
- The expedition joined these two bodies with the American School of Oriental Research in Jerusalem.
- On 10 March camp was pitched at the foot of the Qumrân cliff, Taʿamireh Bedouin were hired, and work went on until 29 March.

**Aims [EV] (540/2):** to check where the purchased fragments came from and finish digging there if needed, though the "clandestins" were not expected to have left much. Above all, to explore the whole Qumrân region, to fix the area inhabited by the "secte des manuscrits" and perhaps find more of its library.

**Team [EV] (540/2):**
- Led by W. Reed (Director, American School) and R. de Vaux (Director, École Française).
- Assisted by three École members, P. D. Barthélemy, abbé J. T. Milik and Henri de Contenson, and by three Arab foremen.
- "Sept équipes volantes furent constituées", each "composée de 3 ou 4 Bédouins" and led by an expedition member.
- "La région fut divisée en secteurs", which several teams prospected in turn.
- [INF] Seven teams against five named members plus three foremen. The article does not say who led which team.

**Search technique [EV] (540–541/2–3):** surface sherds sometimes led to a deposit. More often, traces appeared only after clearing a blocked cave, digging a trench in an open cave, or cleaning out a rock cleft.

**Extent [EV] (541/3):** the cliff was examined "depuis Ḥadjar el-Asbaʿ au nord" "jusqu'à 1 km au sud du Râs Feshkha". That is a band of about 8 km with Kh. Qumrân roughly at its centre (pl. XX). The sites found are spread along the cliff "depuis 2,50 km au nord jusqu'à 4 km au sud" of the Khirbet (559/21).

**Counts [EV] (541/3):**
- "On a fait 230 sondages infructueux, mais 37 grottes ou trous" contained pottery and other remains.
- 25 sites had pottery identical to that of 1Q and Kh. Qumrân.
- A rectangular enclosure in the plain gave sherds of the same type.
- Two ancient roads were traced. One zigzags up opposite the Khirbet, just N of W. Qumrân, to the Buqeʿa; de Vaux credits it to the community. The other reaches ʿAin Feshkha from the SW.
- [INF] Compared with Reed's figures in the project context: de Vaux's 230 are explicitly *unsuccessful* soundings in addition to the 37 positive sites, and he gives 37, not 39. He also extends the survey 1 km south of Râs Feshkha.

**Geology and where sites occur [EV] (542/4):** going up from the shore there are three zones:
1. the littoral plain;
2. the marl terrace, bounded roughly by the −350 contour;
3. the limestone cliff, rising to about −100 m and higher, usually in two steps fronted by steep scree.

The plain and terrace narrow southward until the cliff meets the sea at Râs Feshkha. Qumran-type deposits "se rencontrent toujours dans l'étage inférieur de la falaise". They cluster at the W. Qumrân outlet because of water pools and nearness to the Khirbet. Only natural cavities were used in the cliff.

**Stated limits of coverage [EV]:**
- "Nous ne prétendons pas que notre expédition ait épuisé les possibilités" of the region, because the expedition was too short. At the end of March "nous avons été chassés par la chaleur". Members were exhausted, and Bedouin were falling ill or leaving the work (541/3).
- **Marl terrace excluded:** the team "avions exclu la terrasse marneuse qui s'étend à son pied", thinking it held only sterile water-cut cavities. "C'était une erreur". Six months later the Bedouin found "une chambre artificiellement creusée" there with a large lot of fragments. The footnote reads: "Ce sont les grottes à manuscrits 4Q et 5Q." (541/3). 4Q is "la grotte la plus proche du Khirbet" (559/21). 4Q was very poor in pottery, and 5Q held fragments but "pas un seul tesson" (560/22).
- **Cliff not exhausted:** "il est vraisemblable que certains recoins nous aient échappé", or that other caves are "scellées par des éboulis". After the team left, the Bedouin found a rock hole with a few fragments. The footnote reads "c'est la grotte 6Q" (541–542/3–4).
