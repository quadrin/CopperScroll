- De Vaux's introduction to the Copper Scroll (p. 201) adds details of the 3Q findspot. (EVIDENCE)
- **Ordering and numbering rules, p. 6 (EVIDENCE):**
  - The sites are numbered and plotted geographically, starting from the north.
  - The date after each number is the day the site was recognised.
  - Each description was written by the leader of the team that dug the site.
  - Whether a cave was habitable rests on the layout and the digger's judgement, and is not always certain.
  - Objects are split into catalogued items and uncatalogued fragments.
  - The cliff sites are to be cited as "site (ou grotte) 1 … 40". They are not the 1Q–11Q sigla.
  - The only equivalences given are **grotte 8 = 3Q; grotte 14 = 1Q; grotte 19 = 2Q; grotte 26 = 6Q** (p. 6).
- **Provenance codes (EVIDENCE, p. 18, fig. 2 heading):** excavation numbers read as cave number–item number, "8–11 = Grotte 8 n° 11; 29–3 = Grotte 29 n° 3". The marl caves use a different form, e.g. "8Q9", "7Q6" (fig. 6, p. 28). So **"8-x" means cliff cave 8 (= 3Q), and "8Qx" means cave 8Q**, a different cave. (EVIDENCE; the warning is mine.)

## 3. Cave 8 = 3Q and its neighbours (7, 10, 4, 9, 3, 2, 1, plus 5, 6, 11, A, B)

**Cave 8 = 3Q (EVIDENCE, pp. 6, 7–8, 14–15, 18–24, 33–34, 201)**
- **Date.** "8. 14–25.3.1952 = grotte à manuscrits 3Q (pl. III. 1)" (p. 7). At 12 days, this is the longest span in the list (INFERENCE).
- **Plan.**
  - The cave was originally very large, about 10 m wide.
  - The ceiling has entirely collapsed except at the back, where a **chamber of 3 × 2 m** survives. The text layer reads "4 > 2"; the image clearly shows **3 × 2**.
- **The pottery layer.** In front of this chamber lay a great mass of broken jars and lids. It was mixed with ceiling debris, had no earth fill, and was **30–40 cm thick**.
- **The rear chamber.** It held stratified levels of pebbles with some textile, blackened leather and inscribed fragments, and very few sherds.
- **The rat-nest cavity.** A narrow rising passage leads from the chamber to a cavity almost filled with rat nests. These contained textile pieces, a few bits of leather and one inscribed fragment.
- **The copper rolls.** At the north corner of the chamber, two inscribed copper rolls lay one on top of the other against the rock wall (pl. III.2).
- **The clay deposit.** In front of the pottery heap, a fine clayey deposit with sherds runs for about 10 m from W to E, under the ceiling debris.
- **Entrance and height.** The original entrance is unknown. The cave was very low: intact jars would almost have touched the ceiling (p. 8).
- **Habitability.** Doubtful (p. 8). In p. 33 cave 8 is grouped with 4, 18, 19 (= 2Q), 32 and 1Q as caves too low, narrow or uneven to have been lived in.
- **Finds.**
  - Catalogued jars: 5 (2 t1, 2 t4, 1 t8; pl. V–VI). One jar has the letter **ט** incised twice before firing, on the rim and on the shoulder (pl. VII).
  - Catalogued lids: 21 (1 t1, 4 t4, 4 t5, 1 t6, 1 t7, 2 t8, 1 t12, 1 t13, 1 t14, 5 t18).
  - Other catalogued pottery: 2 jugs (fig. 5.13 = 8-4; fig. 5.15 = 8-5) and a lamp (fig. 5.6 = 8-12).
  - Uncatalogued: about 30 cylindrical jars of various types and at least 5 lids (p. 8).
  - Codes for cave 8 seen in the figures: 8-1, 8-2, 8-3, 8-4, 8-5, 8-6, 8-8, 8-9, 8-11, 8-12, 8-18, 8-19, 8-20.
  - Table totals for cave 8: jars 2 + 2 + 1 + 30 indet. = 35; lids 21 + 5 indet. = 26 (INFERENCE: sums). This makes cave 8 the largest pottery group among the 40 sites (INFERENCE).
- **Period.**
  - Lamps 5 and 6 are of the "Herodian" type characteristic of Kh. Qumran level II (p. 15).
  - The jug tops 13 and 15 are also found at the Khirbeh (p. 15).
  - INFERENCE: these place at least part of the 3Q group in period II, though de Vaux dates no cave to Ib alone (p. 32).
- **Texts.** Skin fragments: Baillet, pp. 94–104. Copper rolls: Milik, pp. 211 ff. (p. 8).
- **De Vaux's introduction to 3Q15, p. 201:**
  - The copper rolls were found **on 20 March 1952** in "la grotte 8 = 3Q (cf. pp. 7–8)" by the team led by **H. de Contenson**.
  - The front part of the chamber had collapsed. A huge block fell from the ceiling just in front of the rolls; it was lucky they survived ("c'est une chance qu'ils aient été préservés", p. 201).
  - The rolls lay a little apart from the mass of broken jars and lids. No skin or papyrus fragment was collected near them.
  - De Vaux says this neither proves nor rules out that the rolls were deposited after the pottery and the other texts.
  - The upper roll had shed many small fragments. It was consolidated with celluloid and the rolls were lifted separately.
- **De Vaux's interpretation, p. 34.** The texts of 2Q, 3Q and 6Q may have been stored or hidden, with the group's dishes, in a cavity near where they camped. He presents this as a hypothesis.
- **How 8 was examined.**
  - No sounding-or-emptying statement is given for cave 8. The only thicknesses recorded are the 30–40 cm pottery layer and the clay deposit, whose depth is not given. Bedrock is not mentioned.
  - INFERENCE: the 12-day span and the layer-by-layer description imply excavation rather than a sounding, but the book does not say whether the collapsed ceiling debris or the 10 m clay deposit were fully cleared.
- **Plates.** Pl. III.1 shows the site of cave 8 = 3Q; pl. III.2 shows the rolls in place (plates list; not read).

## 4. Method and coverage (EVIDENCE unless marked)

- **Organisation (p. 3).**
  - Lankester Harding convened the parties at Jericho on 7 March 1952.
  - Camp was pitched on 10 March at the foot of the cliff near the new cave 2Q.
  - The expedition was led by W. L. Reed (ASOR) and R. de Vaux (EBAF), assisted by D. Barthélemy, J. T. Milik and H. de Contenson.
  - Three Arab foremen (Hassan Awad, Ibrahim Asouly, Azmeh Khalil) and 24 Taʿamireh Bedouin took part.
  - Work ran until 29 March.
  - The aim was to check the findspot of the purchased fragments and, above all, to survey the whole Qumran region to define the area used by the manuscript group.
- **Method (p. 3).**
  - Seven "flying teams" were formed, each of 3–4 Bedouin under one expedition member.
  - The region was divided into sectors, each prospected in turn by several teams.
  - Sites were sometimes signalled by surface sherds. More often traces appeared only when a blocked cave was cleared, a trench was cut in an open cave, or a rock cleft was cleaned out.
- **Coverage (p. 4).**
  - The cliff was examined from Hadjar el-Aṣbaʿ in the north to 1 km south of Râs Feshkha, a band of about 8 km with Kh. Qumran near its centre.
  - "On a fait 230 sondages infructueux" (p. 4).
  - **40** caves, rock holes or deposits held pottery and other remains. In **26** of them the pottery matched 1Q and Kh. Qumran.
  - The search extended north and south of the mapped sites without further result.
  - INFERENCE: the 230 soundings are not listed or located anywhere I read.
- **Limits stated by the book.**
  - The team left at the end of March because of the heat; members were tired, and the Bedouin fell ill or quit (p. 4).
  - The team knew it had not exhausted the possibilities. The Bedouin later found 6Q (Sept. 1952) and 11Q (Jan. 1956, a cave with a blocked entrance) (p. 4).
  - The cliff probably still hides deposits sealed by rockfalls (p. 4).
  - A large cave "qui a été sondée par nous mais n'a pas été entièrement fouillée" (p. 4) might still yield something. No cave numbers are given.
  - The marl terrace was not prospected in 1952, which de Vaux calls an error: 4Q was found there six months later (p. 4).
  - In some sites "des sondages ont seulement été effectués", so the inventory is not always complete (p. 6). No list of which sites these are is given.
  - In large open caves, traces of Qumran use may be lost or "cachées sous des amas d'un dépôt que nous n'avons pas partout enlevé" (p. 16).
  - Cave positions on the map were plotted by eye: "La localisation des grottes sur la carte a été faite à vue" (p. 6).
- **Per-site method statements.** Only scattered ones, all recorded in §2:
  - 1: ash to bedrock.
  - 3: finds at or just below the surface.
  - 14 = 1Q: search for the entrance outline.
  - 19 = 2Q: pottery from the clandestine spoil.
  - 24: pottery in the scree, not in the ash.
  - 26 = 6Q: spoil sifted.
  - 27: earlier Bedouin soundings.
  - 37: surface collection in the side caves; none in the main chamber.
  - 39: earlier Bedouin holes.
- **No coins.** None of these caves produced a single coin: "n'ont pas livré une seule pièce de monnaie" (p. 17). The point is developed on pp. 35–36 (text layer).
- **Later work.**
  - 1953: a return visit to 37 produced Chalcolithic sherds (p. 12).
  - 1955: Hassan Awad's team prospected the whole marl-terrace face around Kh. Qumran and along the Wadi Qumran up to the cliff, 7 Feb – 15 Mar 1955. All visible holes were sounded and the terrace face was stripped so that any cavity would show. This found 7Q–10Q and an unfinished, unused oval artificial cave between 4Q and 5Q (p. 27).
  - 1956: re-examination of the cliff around 11Q produced caves A and B (p. 13).
  - Internal inconsistency: p. 4 says 7–10Q were found "en 1956", but pp. 27–31 date the work and the caves to 1955.
- **Totals and interpretation (pp. 32–34).**
  - Cliff caves plus terrace caves plus 4Q and 11Q give **33 caves** used in the same period (Ib–II) (p. 32).
  - INFERENCE: 26 + 5Q + 7Q–10Q + 4Q + 11Q = 33 fits exactly if the "26" includes site 26 (= 6Q).
  - Ten of the caves held manuscripts, counting 11Q but not 10Q, which yielded only an ostracon (p. 34 n. 1).
  - Classification (p. 33):
    - Open, high caves still used by shepherds: 1, 10, 27, 31, 37.
    - Blocked but once habitable: 39, 40.
    - Doubtfully habitable: 4, 8 (= 3Q), 18, 19 (= 2Q), 32, and 1Q (= 14).
    - Not habitations: small caves 3, 7, 22, 26 (= 6Q), 28, 29; crevices 9, 17; clefts 15, 21, 30; low shelter 12; pottery heap 2.
  - INFERENCE (my count from the entries): clearly Qumran-type material at 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 26, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 37, 39, 40 (24 sites), and doubtfully at 4, 27, 34, 35. The book's figure is 26.
- **Milik appendix (p. 37, text layer only).** Two inscribed jars and juglets were bought in October 1952. The Bedouin attributed them to a rock shelter c. 100 m south of 6Q and slightly higher. This shelter is not in the numbered list.

## 6. What the volume does NOT say (in the parts read)

- It gives no list of which of the 40 sites were only sounded and which were fully excavated. There is only the general warning on p. 6 and a few scattered remarks (§4).
- It gives no excavated depth for cave 8 beyond the 30–40 cm pottery layer. It does not say whether bedrock was reached or whether the ceiling-collapse debris and the 10 m clayey deposit in front were cleared.
- It gives no location, number per cave, or results for the **230 negative soundings**. It does not name the large "sounded but not fully excavated" cave(s) of p. 4.
- It gives no surveyed coordinates, distances, bearings or elevations for individual sites. Positions come only from the eye-plotted fig. 1, and the text locates sites only relative to each other (e.g. 5 above 8; 13 SW of 12; 23 N of 25; 28 above 29).
- It does not name team leaders per site, except H. de Contenson for the 3Q copper rolls (p. 201).
- It reports no other metal objects from cave 8 besides the two copper rolls.
- It does not record what, if anything, was found in the rat-nest cavity of 8 beyond textile, leather and one inscribed fragment.
- It does not mention any search for further hidden deposits on account of the Copper Scroll, in Part I or in the 3Q15 introduction (pp. 201–202).
- It does not explain the cross-reference mismatches noted above: pl. II.4 is given to both 28 and 29; pl. III.3 vs VII.3 for 19; 7–10Q dated 1956 on p. 4 but 1955 on pp. 27–31.
- It does not give uncatalogued items codes. Excavation numbers appear only for drawn or catalogued pieces.
