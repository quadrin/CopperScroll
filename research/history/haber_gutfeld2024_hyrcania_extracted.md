# Haber & Gutfeld 2024 – Ḥorbat Hyrcania, 2023 season: extraction

## Conventions

- **EVIDENCE**: the source states it, or a figure shows it. **INFERENCE**: my reasoning from the source. **BK**: background knowledge, or the project brief, not taken from this source.
- Quotes are verbatim and ≤12 words. Everything else is paraphrase.
- **Page map** (EVIDENCE): PDF 1 is the JSTOR cover. PDF 2–28 are printed pp. 103*–129* (printed page = PDF page + 101). PDF 29 is blank apart from the JSTOR footer (presumably p. 130*).
- The JSTOR cover leaves "Published by" blank. "IAA 2024" is taken from the brief (BK). The latest work cited in the text is Hoyland 2024.
- All 12 figures were viewed as images. Fig. 4 (the plan) and Fig. 1 (the map) were viewed at 300 dpi.

## B1. Season dates, team, permit, method, areas and depths

1. **EVIDENCE (p. 103* / PDF 2).** Quote: "In the inky darkness before dawn in mid-May 2023"
   - A Hebrew University team set out from kibbutz lodgings by the Dead Sea, with three donkeys for the equipment.
   - Quote: "The approach was from the south, via the saddle"
   - The saddle is "traversed by the Hasmonean/Herodian aqueduct". From there the route follows quote: "the snaking path that zigzags sharply up the steep western slope"
2. **EVIDENCE (p. 103* fn. 2).** Quote: "The four-week-long excavation season was carried out in May–June 2023"
   - It was carried out by the authors for the Institute of Archaeology of The Hebrew University of Jerusalem.
   - The partner was Carson-Newman University, Tennessee.
   - The workforce was local volunteers, Carson-Newman students and faculty, and military veterans from American Veterans Archaeological Recovery.
   - The authors thank Pastor W. Hild, and B. Har-Even (Director, Staff Office of Archaeology – Judea and Samaria) for support.
3. **EVIDENCE.** No permit or licence number is given anywhere. The only authority named is the Staff Office of Archaeology – Judea and Samaria.
4. **EVIDENCE (p. 110* / PDF 9).** The pilot season was quote: "focused exclusively on the summit", over quote: "a maximum area of ca. 35 × 85 m" (Figs. 3–4).
   - Deferred to later seasons: the water system, quote: "its necropolis and adjacent Roman encampment", and the "monument".
   - Quote: "the curious 'monument' would have to wait"
   - Aims: separate the Hasmonean phase from the Herodian, understand the layout of the fortress-palace and the Byzantine monastery, and find when the monastery went out of use (pp. 110*–111*).
5. **EVIDENCE (p. 111* / PDF 10).** The areas excavated:

| Area | Location | Depths and levels |
|---|---|---|
| Area A | NW of the summit, below the floor level of the church and monastery compound to the SE. It includes the square opened in 2016 (p. 110*). | Collapse layer, quote: "in some spots descended over two meters until floor level". A probe in the hall's NW corner reached bedrock. Floors at **250.68 m asl** (packed earth) and **250.11 m asl** (plaster) (p. 113*). |
| Area B | Quote: "The southeastern corner of the summit and the early fortifications." The text later says, quote: "The southwestern corner of the summit was chosen for the second" excavation area (p. 117*). | Walls exposed to 4–6 courses (to 1.8 m). The slope wall descends nearly 10 m. Halted for safety (p. 120*). |
| Square C-1 | Inside the raised monastery compound, W of the church. | Cleared to a white mosaic floor; no depth given (p. 123*). |
| Square W-1 | The lower area along the whole W side of the summit (level with Area A). A probe inside a "Hasmonean" tower. | Not completed; no floor reached (p. 125*). |

   - **INFERENCE.** Fig. 4 places Area B at the lower right (SE) of a north-up plan. The Conclusions speak of fortifications "in the southeast" (p. 126*). So "southwestern" on p. 117* looks like a slip.
6. **EVIDENCE (p. 125* / PDF 24).** The authors describe the 2023 season as quote: "the first extensive academic and methodological excavation" at the site. The inscriptions are the first found in properly documented contexts.
7. **EVIDENCE.** The method is open-area excavation of collapse and fill, small probes beneath floors, and photography, including aerial and elevated views by O. Bejarano (Figs. 2, 3, 5, 8, 9). The platform used for these photos is not stated. Pottery reading is used for dating; wood went to the IAA and Tel Aviv University, with C-14 planned (fn. 11, p. 114*). Plaster analysis and pollen extraction are planned for the cisterns (p. 122*).

## B2. Findings by area and period

### B2.1 Site setting

- **EVIDENCE (pp. 103*–105*).** The mount commands the Bukei'a ("Hyrcania Valley"). Quote: "at an elevation of about 255 meters asl". The Dead Sea cliffs close the plateau on the east.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 103*).** Looking west from the summit, the aqueduct remains are seen. Quote: "carried water over a distance of 17 km from Jerusalem's Gihon Spring". The view runs to the el-Muntar ridge.
  - **INFERENCE.** The sentence is syntactically ambiguous. Taken literally, it makes the aqueduct carry water 17 km from the Gihon Spring. I record the claim as printed and do not test it from memory.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 106*).** The Great Laura (Mar Saba) is said to lie "about 3 km to the southwest".

### B2.2 Area A, NW summit (pp. 111*–116* / PDF 10–16; Figs. 5–7)

- **EVIDENCE.**
  - An E–W wall of well-hewn stones lies roughly parallel to the N wall of the central courtyard, but much lower.
  - Under the collapse the team found an elongated hall, quote: "ca. 5.5 m wide and at least 15 m long", lined with at least six pairs of piers (Fig. 5).
  - Its thick W wall is flush with the summit edge. The narrower outer enclosure wall of the monastery phase was built directly above it, following the summit's irregular oval rather than the earlier line.
- **EVIDENCE.** Doorways lead S (unexcavated), N (an elongated chamber along the hall), and E towards a subterranean vaulted space reached only through a looter's pit. A plastered bench with niches lies along the N wall's north face (pp. 111*–112*).
- **EVIDENCE (p. 113*).** The first phase is unresolved, Herodian or Byzantine. The hall was clearly in use under the monastery, and the probe to bedrock was inconclusive.
  - Byzantine/Early Islamic changes: N–S partition walls; packed-earth (250.68 m asl) and plaster (250.11 m asl) floors; small storage units plastered with pinkish mud.
  - The hall is not exactly parallel to the monastery compound's N line, possibly reflecting different phases (Fig. 4).
- **EVIDENCE: finds (pp. 113*–117*).**
  - Byzantine pottery; tri-coloured mosaic fragments; frescoes not typical of Herodian work; wood (cypress) (fn. 11).
  - Nine cornice blocks with ovolo-like moulding, including a keystone with a medallion bearing a defaced cross and traces of blue paint. The authors set out three dating options and lean Byzantine (Fig. 6a/b).
  - Roof tiles, some stamped with one or three circles.
  - Marble altar posts (one marked with a red alpha) and a chancel panel with a hart.
  - A stone (35 × 45 × 80 cm) with a nine-line painted red Greek prayer paraphrasing Ps 85(86):1–2 and Deut 6:13, 6th–7th century, beneath a painted cross graffito (Fig. 7).
  - Over a dozen fragments of floriated Kufic wall inscription.
  - A child's gold ring with turquoise, incised in Umayyad square Kufic ("Allah").

### B2.3 Area B, SE corner fortifications (pp. 117*–121* / PDF 16–20; Figs. 8–10)

- **EVIDENCE.** The supposed "upper line" of the fortification at surface level proved to be monastic, one course high.
  - Beneath it, about 1.5 m further east, the outer face of the earlier fortification wall was found at the summit edge.
  - It is built of tightly fitted, medium-sized ashlars, quote: "preserved to a height of four courses".
  - It is exposed for 2 m and can be traced northward for at least 25 m.
- **EVIDENCE (pp. 119*–120*).** Directly south of it is a chamber, quote: "ca. 12 m long and at least 3 m wide", protruding eastward.
  - Its E wall is preserved up to six courses (1.8 m).
  - Quote: "seems to have constituted a guard post or watchtower" of the original Hasmonean/Herodian phase.
  - It was reused under the monastery, with a raised floor, slabs, a white mosaic (Byzantine pottery) and a Late Byzantine jar. The fill below holds mixed Byzantine and Herodian pottery.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 120*).**
  - At the top of the E wall are two plastered sloping "slides", 20 cm wide each (Fig. 9). The authors compare the windows of Herodium.
  - At the foot of the wall, three or four rows of slabs slope sharply down to the east. The authors compare Herodium's sloping "retaining wall".
  - A thick wall, 1.2 m wide, quote: "descends for a length of nearly 10 m". It is at least six courses (1.8 m) high and turns a corner into a wall running south.
  - Quote: "Due to safety considerations, excavation was halted".
  - Remains of a wooden ladder (two side rails and the bottom rung) and a beam were found in the lower part (Figs. 8, 10).
- **EVIDENCE (p. 121*).** Quote: "we propose attributing the fortifications to Herod". The reasons given:
  1. No Hasmonean pottery.
  2. Smooth-faced masonry without bosses or header-and-stretcher courses, unlike W-1 and the lower courses of the aqueduct.
  3. Gabinius razed the site in 57 BCE.

### B2.4 Square C-1, monastery compound (pp. 122*–125* / PDF 21–24; Fig. 11)

- **EVIDENCE (p. 122*).** Quote: "The spacious courtyard lies above four water cisterns"
  - Quote: "two finely vaulted installations in the eastern side"
  - Quote: "two immense rock-hewn examples in the west"
  - The first phase of all four is "commonly attributed" to the Hasmonean/Herodian period. Plaster and pollen analysis is planned. The cisterns were not excavated in 2023.
- **EVIDENCE (pp. 123*–125*).**
  - A room of 3.75 × 5.8 m on the compound's N line, W of the church.
  - It has a white mosaic floor, two coins, a bronze kohl stick and a wooden beam, with Byzantine pottery.
  - It has a 2.7 m passage to the church, blocked in the 20th century.
  - Reused in its S wall are a Herodian Ionic capital fragment with apsidal depressions over an unfluted column drum.
  - Quote: "the first known case of this style outside of Jerusalem"
  - Fragments of monumental columns and bases are scattered on the summit, including some fallen into the cisterns.

### B2.5 Square W-1, west side (p. 125* / PDF 24; Fig. 12)

- **EVIDENCE.**
  - A probe inside a probable Hasmonean tower, identified by its masonry: bossed blocks in alternating header and stretcher courses. Parallels cited: Alexandrium and Jerusalem's First Wall.
  - Its W wall runs 7 m south and is 2 m thick. No floor was reached.
  - Apart from the lowest courses of the aqueduct, this is quote: "the only (tentative) evidence of the original Hasmonean fortress phase"

### B2.6 Features relevant to the Copper Scroll searches

- **Water system.** Four courtyard cisterns (above). The aqueduct crosses the saddle (p. 103*). Its lower courses are header-and-stretcher masonry (p. 121*). The water system was deferred to future seasons (p. 110*). Plan of the aqueduct or cisterns: none.
- **Stepped passages or tunnels.** EVIDENCE (p. 110*): Gutfeld excavated "both tunnels" in their entirety about forty years after 1960; quote: "Their purpose remains enigmatic" (citing Gutfeld 2006). None were dug in 2023. No stairs on the summit are described; the only means of climbing mentioned is the wooden ladder in Area B.
- **Ditch or moat.** Not mentioned. Only "the saddle" traversed by the aqueduct is named (p. 103*).
- **Structure SW of the summit.** The "monument" (B3 below) was not excavated in 2023.
- **Cemetery.** Quote: "pits seen dug into the necropolis located near the eastern foot" of the mount (p. 110*). Separately, they name "its necropolis and adjacent Roman encampment" (p. 110*). It is unclear whether "adjacent" refers to the necropolis or to the site. Neither was investigated.
- **"Steps" on the east.** None described. On the east and SE side, the only features are Area B's guard post, the sloping slabs and the wall descending the slope.
  - Quote: "The segment of fortifications uncovered descending the steep slope in the southeast" (p. 126*).

### B2.7 Conclusions (pp. 126*–127*)

- **EVIDENCE.** The date of the fortress's founding is unresolved; W-1 may help.
- An Abbasid-period Muslim presence is attested. Whether it coexisted with or replaced the monastery is unknown.
- The absence of glazed ware suggests the site was abandoned by the late 8th century.
- Heavy looting is noted.

## B3. Earlier work as reported by Haber & Gutfeld

- **Conder & Kitchener (SWP).** EVIDENCE (p. 107*): they were the first modern recorders (late 1873, published 1883). They noted the aqueduct, deep cisterns, masonry and vaulted chambers, and called the site "a [Byzantine] town of some importance".
- **Schick/Marti 1880.** Their plan is "forgivably imprecise" and links the site to Euthymius. Furrer (1880) identified it with Sabas and Kastellion. Rhétoré (1897) linked it to Josephus' fortress.
- **Mader.** Five visits, 1913–1927.
- **Mar Saba monks.** EVIDENCE (pp. 107*–109*): monks settled in 1925, cleared the church and rooms, and exposed mosaics, a sundial, a font and a chancel post.
  - They made a chapel in a "tomb-cave" with frescoes of saints. Fn. 7: this is a Byzantine burial cave on the upper SW slope, not a converted cistern.
- **Mandate Department of Antiquities.** Three inspections between 1928 and 1943, including Hamilton in 1935. Husseini (1943) reports devastation by the 'Ubeidiyya Bedouin, quote: "brought to a head in late 1939".
- **Papyri and the Louvain team.** EVIDENCE (pp. 109*–110*).
  - In summer 1952 Bedouin found papyri (fn. 9: named as Ta'amirah, not 'Ubeidiyya).
  - De Vaux urged Louvain to send a team. Quote: "arrived at the site in early 1953, led by Robert de Langhe"
  - Quote: "His unpublished fieldnotes are very vague"
  - The search seems confined to quote: "a few 'caverns' located in the upper part of the southern slope". Nothing is known of their pottery or small finds.
  - Revised papyrus dating: ca. 500–800 CE (Hoyland 2024); Arabic texts mostly second half of the 8th century.
- **Allegro 1960.** EVIDENCE (p. 110*).
  - Quote: "John Allegro's Spring 1960 search for the 'treasures of the Copper Scroll'"
  - The search led him to Naḥal Sekhakha, "identified with the biblical Vale of Achor", below the N edge of the mount (citing Brown 2005:122–125).
  - Weather and water shortages cut it short.
  - The efforts were quote: "concentrated on the two mysterious tunnels hewn into the bottom" of the cliff.
  - Quote: "a survey was also undertaken of the summit and of the 'monument'" (citing Wright 1961).
  - Quote: "No inscriptions or papyri were discovered, nor were they expressly sought."
  - Vandalism was blatant (Brown 2005:125).
- **The monument, credited to Allegro.** EVIDENCE (p. 106* fn. 4). The authors speculate, explicitly "purely conjecture", that Antipater's tomb might be the "funerary monument," or nephesh.
  - Quote: "identified by Allegro on a neighboring peak ca. 300 m" to the SW.
  - They say Allegro dated it Herodian, citing "Wright 1961:14–16, Fig. 4, Pl. IX".
  - **INFERENCE.** They credit the identification to Allegro and the documentation to Wright. Wright's own text does not name Allegro as the identifier; it says Copper Scroll references directed "the expedition".
- **Wright 1961.** Cited as Biblica 42:1–20 (bibliography). Cited for the monastery (pp. 1–14) and for the monument (pp. 14–16, Fig. 4, Pl. IX). Milik 1961 is cited as Biblica 42:21–27.
- **Gutfeld.** Excavated both tunnels "some forty years later" (Gutfeld 2006, *BAR* 32/5). In 2016, quote: "an exploratory square was opened in the northwestern part of the summit", by the Staff Office of Archaeology with Gutfeld. It found deep fill and was folded into Area A.
- **Patrich.** Not mentioned anywhere in the chapter or bibliography (EVIDENCE: text search and reading).
- **Copper Scroll.** Mentioned once, in the Allegro sentence (p. 110*). There is no discussion of its contents or any item.

## What the source does NOT say

- No permit or licence number. No exact start and end dates (only "mid-May 2023" and "May–June 2023", "four-week-long"). No full staff list beyond the authors, the photographers and the plan-maker.
- No coordinates or grid references for the site or any feature. No plan of the slopes, the aqueduct, the cisterns, the tunnels, the necropolis, the "Roman encampment" or the "monument".
- No excavation of the cisterns, the aqueduct, the tunnels, the necropolis or the SW "monument" in 2023.
- No description of the "monument" beyond fn. 4 (Allegro's identification, ca. 300 m SW, Herodian, per Wright).
- No ditch or moat. No stepped passage on the summit. No "steps" on the east side. Nothing on the east beyond the Area B guard post and the wall descending the slope.
- No mention of a mine or metal detector, of Allegro's grave-digging, or of his 1962–63 return or architect.
- No discussion of Copper Scroll items 1 or 2 or of the "Vale of Achor" fortress, beyond reporting (via Brown 2005) that Allegro identified Naḥal Sekhakha with Achor.
- No mention of Patrich.
- No firm Hasmonean-versus-Herodian date for the Area A hall. The fortification dating (Herod) is offered as a proposal.
- No count of steps or dimensions for the tunnels (those are referred to Gutfeld 2006).
- No dimensions for the four courtyard cisterns.
