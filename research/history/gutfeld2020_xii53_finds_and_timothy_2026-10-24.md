## Caves and excavations (EVIDENCE unless marked)

- **XII/53 (renamed 53a).** Excavated January 2017 by the Hebrew University (O. Gutfeld with A. Ovadia; funded by R. Price, Liberty University), p. 71. Middle rock terrace south of Naḥal Qumran, about 293 m below sea level, about 1 km south of the Qumran park. Opening to the north-east, about 4 m wide, split by a built wall. Central chamber about 3.5 × 5.0 m, about 2.5 m high; a niche on each side; a narrow tunnel about 15 m long running south. First probed in the 1993 survey (Cohen and Israel), two 1 × 1 m squares (p. 71, n. 1).
- **XII/53b.** Excavated January 2018 after an upper continuation was noticed from the western niche, p. 72.
- **XII/52.** Excavated January 2019, about 100 m above XII/53 on the upper terrace south of Naḥal Qumran, p. 72. Narrow natural entrance (about 2 m long, 0.5 m wide, 0.9 m high), central chamber about 4.0 × 5.5 m and 3.5 m high, a tunnel about 7 m long to the south-west. Recorded in de Vaux's 1952 survey as QG29 and named **"Timothy's Cave"** (de Vaux 1962: 11), p. 74. Milik described seven broken scroll jars and a stack of 16 lids there, p. 74. All earth from XII/52, 53a, 53b and their dumps was sieved, p. 74.

## How things were hidden in XII/53a (EVIDENCE, pp. 75–76)

- Scroll-jar and lid fragments of the 2nd–1st c. BCE in the central chamber; many sherds of one such jar in the southern niche.
- **Behind a large rock "apparently placed on purpose"** in the north-east corner, sherds of a large jar of the same date, with a rolled piece of leather inside (Fig. 4). Opened in the IAA scrolls laboratory: blank.
- **In niches of the natural tunnel** at the south end: several jars and lids "hidden in niches" (Fig. 5), with about 15 pieces of linen cloth (Fig. 6) and leather straps, compared with the Temple Scroll wrapper (Sukenik 2016).
- **Two complete iron pickaxes** beside the jars, identical to those de Vaux found in Cave 11 and at Kh. Qumran (de Vaux 1956).
- Also cord with deliberate knots, date pits, stones, an acorn.
- XII/53b: a complete Qumran-type lamp of the 2nd c. BCE in a niche at the entrance (Fig. 7); a bronze cooking pot (Fig. 8; compared with KhQ1040 in Moliner and Moliner 2017); seven arrowheads, spearheads, late Hellenistic–Early Roman sherds.
- Author's conclusion (CLAIM): jars, lids, linen and straps show XII/53a was used to hide scrolls, like XII/52 and perhaps cave A4-028 (Klein et al.).

## When the scrolls were taken: the author's proposal (pp. 78–80)

- The usual answer is Bedouin looting after 1947 (p. 78). Gutfeld proposes instead a removal in the 8th c. CE (CLAIM), because **sherds dated to the 8th c. CE were found on the cave's surface** (p. 80; their context and quantity are not given in these pages).
- **Timothy I's letter** (Nestorian patriarch, 727/8–823 CE; Letter 47 to Sergius, metropolitan of Elam; Brock 1997: 59–65), quoted pp. 78–79 via Filson-Floyd 1950. Timothy says he heard from Jews, newly baptised and "worthy of credit", that about ten years earlier (about 790 CE, Gutfeld's gloss) books were found **in a cave in the hills near Jericho**. An Arab's hunting dog followed game into a cave; its owner went in and found a chamber with many books; he told the Jews of Jerusalem, who came in numbers and took Hebrew books, including biblical books and "more than two hundred psalms of David".
- Context given: Qirqisani's "cave sect" (Maghariya), named because their books were found in a cave (p. 79); Milik's naming of XII/52 as Timothy's Cave (p. 79); the 1883 Shapira strips (p. 80). The author suggests the Damascus Document found in the Cairo Genizah might come from such a removal, and says there is no way to know (CLAIM, p. 80).

## What this bears on in the project (INFERENCE)

1. **Salvage records** ([catalogue](salvage_records/README.md)): the catalogue's 27 accounts do not include Timothy's letter. It is a contemporary letter reporting second-hand testimony of a **chance rediscovery of a cave cache about eight centuries after concealment**, followed by organised removal. That is the opposite pattern to the catalogue's main result (every EVIDENCE-grade account describes removal soon after a loss). Proposed addendum row, not entered (the catalogue's coding plan is frozen and its counts were seen): reference Timothy I, Letter 47 (Syriac; Brock 1997 pp. 59–65; original not read), about 800 CE, near Jericho, Hebrew books in jars(?) in a cave, found by chance and removed by Jerusalem Jews; kind TRADITION or EVIDENCE-of-report, to be decided by the coder.
2. **Search-theory map (Method 4) and the dossier's "the deposits may be gone"**: an attested medieval removal episode in the Jericho/Qumran cliffs, and an excavated cave (XII/53a) that held scroll jars but no scrolls, both lower the chance that cliff-cave caches survived to 1947. The 8th-c. sherd evidence is thin in these pages.
3. **Concealment practice (comparanda)**: a cache set behind a deliberately placed rock, jars in tunnel niches, wrappings, and **iron pickaxes left beside the jars**, 2nd–1st c. BCE. Pickaxes with a cache fit hiders who dug, as the scroll's "dig N cubits" instructions assume, but they could equally be later looters' tools; the pages give no stratigraphic tie.
4. **Entry 25 / cave rows:** the dimensions above agree with the 19 Oct record; nothing new on the entrance wall.
