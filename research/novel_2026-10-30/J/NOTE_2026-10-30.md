# Lane J: a landscape date for the Copper Scroll list (note, 30 October 2026 session)

Branch `claude/novel-2026-10-30-J`. Freeze commit `942fbb3`. Protocol SHA-256 `47ea48eff97f34a8ca5ae118599968c8d0ad6ae26aeb75e6f366a5e086ed7f80`. One confirmatory run on 31 October 2026 06:46 UTC.

**Result in one line.** No registered dating scenario separates from the permutation null, and no scenario beats the others. The landscape date of the list is **not identifiable from available evidence**.

## 1. Novelty gate

- Earlier work dates installations one site at a time (`research/measurements/cycle*/`, `registration/*_extracted.md`).
- Branch G (logistics simulation) gives one constraint: a region-wide deposit that starts at the Temple must end before June 68 CE.
- No earlier test compares dating scenarios for the whole list at once.
- This lane does that: it codes the period text of 7,366 inventory features blind, and asks at which date the most entries have an installation of their kind in existence at their proposed places.

## 2. Inputs and bias check

Full log: [bias_check.md](bias_check.md).

- **Used:** HA-ESI merged inventory (3,373 rows of nine installation classes; alternate report versions left out), ASI near places (3,099 feature mentions), caves of the entry 25 lane (665), columbaria (229). These inventories select rows by place, region and feature type. None drops rows by date.
- **Left out:** tombs and water systems (some source lineages were chosen because they publish Second Temple sites, and their other rows duplicate HA-ESI and ASI); Peraea (sites included only with occupation in the window); mandate maps (no dates); RAF 1940s (no feature table).
- **Not read:** `dated_in_window`, `period_in_window`, `date_code`, `candidate_entries`. `candidate_entries` has no date filter (it is place-based), but eligibility was rebuilt so that one class rule applies to every entry.
- **Bias that remains:** the candidate places were proposed mostly by scholars who assume a first-century list. The test compares scenarios on these places only.

## 3. Eligibility rule

- Entry landmark words come from `entry_features.csv` (T02). Each word maps to a feature class: cistern → cistern; pool, reservoir, basin → pool or reservoir; conduit, outlet → channel or dam; tomb, monument → tomb; cave → cave; dovecote → dovecote; steps → stepped pool or ritual bath; pit → cistern or rock-cut; chamber → rock-cut; fortress → fortress. Other words are not used.
- A feature is eligible for an entry when its class matches and it lies within 2.0 km of a candidate place of the entry (W2B `places_v2.csv` points), or the inventory places it by that place's name.
- 42 of 61 entries have at least one eligible feature. There are 7,255 entry–feature pairs.

## 4. Blind period coding and check agreement

Full log: [period_coding/BLIND_LOG.md](period_coding/BLIND_LOG.md).

- 3,161 distinct period strings were coded by 8 separate model instances (Claude Code CLI, `claude-opus-5-5`, no tools, no MCP, no project, empty folder). Each saw only the coding instructions (a neutral period table) and its batch. None was told the scenarios, the hypothesis or the Copper Scroll.
- Output per string: start year, end year, construction event (yes/no), end event (yes/no), kind (feature, site, finds, relative, undated, other), confidence. 3,161 of 3,161 coded; no start after end.
- A ninth instance checked 150 strings (seed 20261030) against the source text: interval agrees 148/150 (98.7 %), events 149/150, kind 145/150; all three agree 143/150 (95.3 %). The checker is the same model family, so this measures consistency, not historical truth.
- Mapping to "in existence": the coded interval is mostly a period of use or a period name. The primary rule takes the start as "built by then" and ends existence only when the text states destruction, abandonment, filling or going out of use.
- Only 51 % of eligible pairs have a dated feature (59 % when site and finds dates count). The rest are undated and decide the bounds.

## 6. Frozen protocol

- [PROTOCOL_FROZEN.md](PROTOCOL_FROZEN.md), SHA-256 `47ea48eff97f34a8ca5ae118599968c8d0ad6ae26aeb75e6f366a5e086ed7f80`, commit `942fbb3` ("J: freeze protocol sha256=…"), pushed before the run.
- Statistic S1 (primary): number of entries with at least one eligible feature in existence at the scenario year. S2 (secondary): mean share of each entry's eligible features in existence.
- Null: the full coded date record of each feature is permuted among all features of the same class and zone (55 strata), 10,000 permutations, seed 20261030.
- Pass: the same winner in the best and worst bounds; winner p ≤ 0.00625 (0.05 / 8) in both; winner beats every other scenario (difference-of-z permutation p ≤ 0.05) in both.
- Code was tested only on fake dates before the freeze (`data/devtests/`).

## 8. Context, not evidence (not used in the test)

- **Palaeographic date of 3Q15.** F. M. Cross dated the script to about 25–75 CE (late Herodian), in "Excursus on the Palaeographic Dating of the Copper Document", DJD III (Baillet, Milik and de Vaux, *Les "petites grottes" de Qumrân*, 1962) pp. 217–221. J. T. Milik, in the same volume, preferred about 100 CE. These are the published values as commonly cited; the pages were not re-read in this session.
- **Cave 3 finds.** The 1952 excavation of Cave 3 found the two copper rolls with fragments of other manuscripts, and Qumran-type cylindrical jars and lids (de Vaux, DJD III, 1962; *RB* 60, 1953). The jar count differs between DJD III and *RB* 60 (`research/schematics/searches/README.md`, sheet 09). Qumran-type pottery does not by itself fix a date after 68 CE or before it.

## 11. What it changes

- The dated inventory record cannot choose between a Hasmonean, Herodian, pre-revolt, First Revolt, Bar Kokhba or later setting for the list. Most eligible installations at the proposed places are either undated (49 % of pairs) or carry long period ranges ("Second Temple", "Roman") that span several scenarios. Coded "Herodian" and "Early Roman" both run 37 BCE–70 CE, so HER, ERP and REV1 cannot separate on that wording.
- A later reading (Bar Kokhba, Late Roman, Byzantine) is not excluded by the installation record at the proposed places. The 66–70 CE reading is not supported over them by this evidence. Branch G's "before June 68" constraint stays a conditional of the logistics model, not a dating result.
- No scenario other than 66–70 CE won, so no new inventory is opened.

## 12. Is a further AI-only session justified?

No: re-coding the same period text or changing the scenario years cannot add dated features, and the undated half of the record decides the bounds.

## 13. Exact reopening condition

Reopen only if (a) a new source gives construction or abandonment dates (not period names) for at least half of the currently undated eligible installations at the proposed places of at least 20 entries, or (b) a documented error is found in `coded_intervals.csv`, `eligibility.csv` or `score.py` that changes an existence value. The fieldwork route that remains is stratigraphic dating of the specific installations (construction fills and abandonment deposits) at the proposed places; it is not started here.
