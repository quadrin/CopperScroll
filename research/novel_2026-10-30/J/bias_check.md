# Lane J bias check: date filters in the inventories

Written 31 October 2026 UTC (30 October session), before any scenario statistic was computed on real dates.

Question for each inventory: did its plan or build drop rows by date, or select its rows by a date-related rule? A row filter by date biases a landscape-date test toward the filter's window. A date *field* that is only coded (not used to drop rows) is harmless if this lane does not read it.

## Entry → feature eligibility rule (no dates)

- Entry landmarks: `research/agent_review_2026-10-07/wave1/T02_hoard_match/entry_features.csv`, column `landmarks` (T02 vocabulary, built from the project lexicon; no date input).
- Candidate places: the same file, column `candidate_places`; coordinates from W2B `places_v2.csv`. These are the published/proposed identifications. They were not chosen by a date rule, but most proposals are by authors who assume a first-century list; this is a selection effect on *places*, not on feature dates, and it applies equally to every scenario.
- Landmark word → feature class: cistern→cistern; pool, reservoir, basin→pool_reservoir; conduit, outlet→channel_aqueduct or dam; tomb, monument→tomb; cave→cave; dovecote→dovecote; steps→steps_ritual_bath; pit→cistern or rockcut; chamber→rockcut; fortress→fortress. Words with no installation class (valley, threshold, building, corner, stone, court, cairn, field, pillar, rock, road, wall, ruin, mound, fissure, spring) are not used.
- A feature is eligible for an entry if its class is one of the entry's classes and its point lies within 2.0 km of one of the entry's candidate places, or (no coordinates) the inventory places it by the name of that candidate place.
- Result: 42 of the 61 entries have at least one eligible feature; 7,255 entry–feature pairs.

## Things this lane cannot remove

- The project places (and so the HA-ESI and ASI inventories, which were searched near them) were proposed by scholars who mostly assume a Second Temple date. The landscape around them is therefore the landscape of first-century proposals. A Bar Kokhba or Byzantine reading of the list could point to other places that no inventory covers. This test compares scenarios *on the proposed places*; it does not test other places.
- The permutation null (date intervals shuffled within class × zone) controls for the regional date mix of each class, so a period that is simply common in the record (Byzantine, Herodian tombs in Jerusalem) does not win by frequency alone.
