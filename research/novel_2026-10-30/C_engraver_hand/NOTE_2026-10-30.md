## Question, and what could change

The question is whether the physical writing of 3Q15 changes along the text, and whether it changes where the formulas change. Sandbox run 13 (branch `sb`, commit 6b3163e) found seams before entry 11 (II 12 | II 13) and before entry 49 (X 14 | X 15). If the hand changed at those seams, the list would look like a compilation of separately written source lists. That would limit how regional and sequence models may treat the list as one ordered document.

## Result (T1, primary, decides)

The drawing features change significantly along the text: p_block = 0.0034 against 10,000 column-order permutations (free line shuffle p = 0.0001). Two change points were detected:
1. **III 6 | III 8**, global line 38 (III 7 has under 3 letters). Letters become taller relative to line pitch, wider, and more tightly spaced within words: h_rel 0.93 → 1.11, asp_rel 0.85 → 1.05, gap 0.25 → 0.18.
2. **XI 7 | XI 8**, global line 159. Letters become shorter again: h_rel 1.11 → 0.88.

**Coincidence with the seams: 0 of 2.** The first change lies 10 lines after S11 (seam at global 28), and the second 10 lines after S49 (global 149). The tolerance was ±3 lines. Without column XII the run finds a single change (III 6 | III 8, p_block = 0.003), again at neither seam.

**Verdict under the frozen rule: not obtained.** A change in the drawn hand was detected, but not at the seams.

## What each source can and cannot show

- **(a) Drawing (W2F boxes on Puech 2006 facsimile).** This is a modern tracing over a photograph of the EDF copy. It can show the drawn proportions and spacing. It cannot separate the engraver from the drawer, the copy or the plate photography. Plate scale differs per column (42–74 px per letter), but the features are normalised by local line pitch and letter class. The change at III 7 also falls inside a single plate, so it is not a plate-scale boundary. It could still be a drawing or tracing artefact, or a change of space or surface on the object. The mean-shift model also turns a gradual trend into steps: h_rel rises from about 0.9 (I–II) to about 1.2 (VI–VIII, X) and falls in XII.
- **(b) Photographs (USC public previews of the WSRP 1988 film).** These are on the object, at about 20–30 px per cm. The scale comes from the frame's ruler. They measure layout only: line pitch and letter advance. Letter height and shape are not resolvable automatically at this size. Frames of a cut include neighbouring columns, and the cut-to-column map comes from radiograph captions. Neither photographic measure shows any change point.
- **(c) Transcription.** Final-form use is a property of the editors' transcription. It is near-uniform and carries little information (5–17 cases per column).
- **Column XII** was exposed on 30 Oct. It is included, and a run without it is reported; it changes nothing in the verdict.
