## 1. Novelty gate
T01 (7 October) matched scroll names against Palmer's 1881 Arabic name lists only. It used name likeness alone, and that likeness was chance-level. Branch D (30 October) matched emended readings against the same nineteenth-century gazetteers. This lane uses a new layer: place records written 1100–1600 (pilgrims, Jewish itineraries and tomb lists, Mujir ed-Din, Arab geographers via Le Strange). A blind extractor drew these records from the texts. The test is a conjunction of name, feature type and position. The chance baseline puts random names through the same conjunction.

## 4. Placement method
- `scripts/place_records.py` uses 29 anchors (`data/anchors.csv`). Coordinates come from the repo: places_v3 (W2B), Mandate 1:20,000 features, the Peraea gazetteer, and T01 SWP grid-square centres.
- A record whose own name matches an anchor goes to that anchor, with radius = anchor sigma.
- A record positioned relative to a named anchor goes to the anchor, with radius = sigma + the stated distance. Units: mile 1.5 km, league 4.5 km, parasang 5 km, day 30 km. If no distance is stated, the radius is sigma + 3 km.
- Other records keep their region only.
- Primary layer: 724 placed by own name, 718 relative, 523 region only.

## 5. Frozen protocol
`PROTOCOL_FROZEN.md`, SHA-256 `f3d1a1c6…7d201`, commit `fd3fbf9`. The protocol has:
- the T01 scorer and sound rules unchanged;
- medieval rendering rules M1–M9 (ph/qu/ch/th/c/j/g/y variants, generic-word removal, Beth- prefix);
- the conjunction: name ≥ 0.75, AND a feature class allowed for the scroll name, AND position within the name's candidate area (radius + sigma + 5 km);
- the chance baseline: 200 random Hebrew-like cores per variant, built as in T01 `chance.py`, run on the same eligible records;
- the pass mark: observed total above the random p99;
- positive controls, named before matching.

## 8. Matches above the pass mark
No test (C1, C2, T) passes, so no match is above the test pass mark. Two single name groups exceed their own random p99 (reported under rule 7; exploratory, 40 name groups were tested):
- **Zered group, variant גיחנ (Gihon), test name**: 31 records against p99 23 (p = 0.01). The records are the Gihon spring and the Valley of Gehenna/Gehinnon at Jerusalem. Examples: Burchard (c. 1280) PPTS pp. 70, 74–75; Marino Sanuto (1321) pp. 43–44, 48; Felix Fabri (1480–1483) II pp. 78, 139–142, 201; Fetellus (c. 1130) p. 40; Ibn Chelo (1334) Adler p. 130 / Carmoly p. 236; Idrisi (1154) via Le Strange "Wadi Jahannum". Four more records come through variant איב: "Bir Eyub / Puits de Job" (Burchard p. 75; Gerson of Scarmela 1561, Carmoly pp. 442, 450; Fetellus p. 40).
- **Gerizim, known-survival name**: 24 records against p99 4 (p = 0.005). Examples: Benjamin of Tudela pp. 33–34; Theoderich p. 62; Fetellus p. 33; Burchard p. 53; Marino Sanuto pp. 19, 21; Jacob of Paris (1238) Adler p. 116; Yaqut (1225) and ʿAli of Herat (1173) via Le Strange pp. 484, 512 ("Kazîrîm").
- Both are biblical names in continuous learned use (Bible, Talmud, church tradition). They show that the sources keep the biblical name at the expected place. They do not show local survival of a scroll name.
- In a post-hoc check (`scripts/posthoc_name_field.py`, EXPLORATORY), 10 of the 31 Zered-group records match only through `other_names`, where the extractor sometimes copied the editor's gloss.

Other matches of interest (below the pass mark; listed with page):
- **Achor**: 6 records. Fetellus (c. 1130) p. 22, "Emecanchor, between Jericho and Galgala". Anonymous Pilgrim (12th c.) p. 47, "Emechachor". Burchard p. 57, "near Gilgal, half a league to the south". Marino Sanuto p. 13. Fabri II p. 40 and Ludolph p. 120 ("Valley of Achan"). Group p = 0.035; p99 not reached. All six place Achor in the Jericho–Gilgal plain, not in the Buqeiʿa. This is the Latin Onomasticon tradition, not local survival.
- **Doq** (known survival): Burchard p. 56 and Marino Sanuto p. 13, "Docus", 1 league from Phasael towards the north. The Arabic form ʿAin Dûk appears only in the editors' gloss (post-hoc check: 0 of 2 survive on name_as_printed alone).
- **Koziba** (known survival): John Phocas (1185) "monastery of Choziba", on the far side of the great ravine.
- **Absalom** (tomb sub-test): 5 records in the Jewish lists, in the Valley of Jehoshaphat (Benjamin p. 37; Jacob b. Nathaniel p. 99; Jacob of Paris p. 118; Meshullam p. 191; Obadiah p. 241). Group p = 0.02 with observed 5 = p99 5, so not above the mark.
- **Zadok** (tomb sub-test): one hit, "cave of Zedekiah" (Jacob of Paris, Adler p. 119). This is a sound-alike, not the same name.
- **Koḥlit, ha-Melaḥ, Sekakah, Kippa, Shaveh, Beth Tamar, Ḥorebbeh**: at or below chance. The Koḥlit hits are Ebal/Gebal, Bala (Zoar), "Hachilah" (Burchard p. 89) and Choziba. Sekakah has 0 hits.

- The tomb controls pass (3 of 3).
- The scroll's named tombs give 8 conjunctive records against a random mean of 3.3 (p99 13, p = 0.10).

## 12. What it changes
- No candidate moves.
- The medieval layer (1100–1600; Latin, Jewish and Arab sources in translation) gives no evidence of local survival for any scroll name without a biblical form.
- The Achor tradition of these sources points to the Jericho plain, not the Buqeiʿa. This is a learned tradition and does not weigh against the Buqeiʿa or Nuweiʿimeh branches.
- With T01 and lane D, name survival is now closed for nineteenth-century gazetteers and for the medieval sources read.

A further AI-only session on medieval name survival is not justified. Only the blocked Hebrew and Ottoman sources could add a different kind of record.
