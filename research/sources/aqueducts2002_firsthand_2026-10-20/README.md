## Findings

All page numbers refer to the 2002 English edition. EVIDENCE = stated in the source. INFERENCE = the project's.

### Cypros (Meshel & Amit, pp. 313–329)

- EVIDENCE: the main source is ʿEin el-Fawwar, a karstic spring with a strongly seasonal flow (p. 313). ʿEin Farah, 6 km west, and ʿEin Fajira (map ref. 1804 1377) were connected into the Fawwar pool for the low season (pp. 313, 317).
- EVIDENCE: Fig. 1a (pp. 314–315) maps six lines (I–VI) and 40 numbered points from ʿEin Farah to Cypros. Line V is a southern branch of the northern aqueduct that crosses to the south bank near St George's monastery and ends at point 40, above an ancient pool (p. 313 n. 6, after Porath & Izdarechet).
- EVIDENCE: the line is about 14 km long (7.25 km as the crow flies). Its gradient is 10–40 cm per km as far as ʿEin Qelt and averages 2.4 cm per km after Wadi Abu Dabʿa (p. 317). Kopel's view, which the authors accept, is that the line was first built for ʿEin Qelt water; Fawwar was connected later (p. 322).
- EVIDENCE: near the hill above St George's monastery (map ref. 1889 1384), the aqueduct leaves the Wadi el-Qelt basin and follows the "Roman road" (p. 319).
- EVIDENCE: point 25 is described as "the only tunnel showing both stages"; its exit was exposed (legend; Fig. 24, p. 327, a section through the tunnel).
- EVIDENCE: at point 23, a Second Temple bridge was destroyed within that period and replaced by a detour (point 24). The pipe aqueduct later built a new bridge on the same spot, "some 650 years later" (p. 326).
- EVIDENCE on dating:
  - The run-off system with four cisterns is Hasmonean, by Porath's plaster analysis and Netzer's excavation (p. 320).
  - The long aqueduct is Herodian. Its western part may be Hasmonean; the authors say this is "not yet conclusively established" (pp. 320–321).
  - The pipe aqueduct is Byzantine "at least in its inception" and was re-used in the Umayyad period (pp. 328–329).
  - p. 328 n. 15 says this reverses the authors' Hebrew-edition view. The 1989 Hebrew chapter (pp. 229–242, read 1 Oct; `entry32_wadi_qelt_plan_review_2026-10-01.md`) gave the 7th–8th c. CE.
- EVIDENCE: the authors identify Beit Jaber el-Fauqani (point 29) with the reservoir "above the monastery and beside the road" in the life of George of Choziba (p. 329, citing Patrich 1990, p. 206). Fig. 6 on p. 319 calls it "early Arab"; the text on pp. 328–329 says Byzantine with later re-use.
- Measurements:
  - Cistern 3: 17 × 7 × 8 m to the fill; at least 500 m³ (p. 320).
  - Bridge no. 32: c. 135 m long, 3.25 m wide, maximum height 14.2 m; one arch spans 12.6 m (p. 321).
  - Abu Dabʿa bridge: c. 130 m long, 25 m above the wadi bed (p. 325).
  - Pipe segments: 60–70 cm long, c. 45 cm in diameter (p. 323).

### Jericho valley (Fig. 1b, p. 316)

- EVIDENCE: Fig. 1b, "Aqueducts to ancient sites in the Jericho valley", shows:
  - Qanat Musa from ʿEin ʿAuja to the ʿEnot Dok / Naʿaran junction;
  - lines to Archelais, to Kh. al-Mafjar and to the Jericho palaces;
  - the three Wadi Qelt lines;
  - a dashed line south of Wadi Qelt to Kh. Mugheifir.
- EVIDENCE: Fig. 1b marks no installation at Doq / Jebel Qarantal.

### Birket Musa (Netzer & Garbrecht, pp. 378–379)

- EVIDENCE: Fig. 15 reproduces Nöldeke & Schultze's 1909 sketch plan. Fig. 16 is a section through the pool wall.
- EVIDENCE: the wall is either Hasmonean and thickened under Herod, or Herodian in both phases. The authors prefer both-Herodian, because known Hasmonean remains south of Wadi Qelt are generally lacking (p. 379).
- EVIDENCE: the pool stored winter floodwater for irrigation and was possibly also a pleasure pool. Local people used it for irrigation until the mid-1970s (p. 379, n. 16).

### Qumran (Ilan & Amit, p. 380)

- EVIDENCE (drawing only, no text): Fig. 1 numbers the line from point 1 (inflow from the west) to 19.
  - Points 3 and 4 are the ends of a heavy dashed line across the east side of basin 2, which is probably the dam.
  - Point 16 is the sharp NE bend at the top of the line.
  - Sections A–C are closed (tunnel), D-D is an open U channel, and E-E is a flat channel.

## Bearing on entries

- **Entry 32**:
  - INFERENCE: the 2002 edition calls point 25 a tunnel showing both stages, with its exit exposed. The 1989 review read point 25 as a two-phase outlet section on the Cypros route. So point 25 is a tunnel exit, not a culvert through a wall. This makes it less likely to be SWP's culvert in the wall opposite the monastery.
  - Nothing in pp. 313–329 describes that wall or its culvert.
  - The pipe-aqueduct date moves from 7th–8th c. CE (1989) to Byzantine at inception (2002). This changes no rating, because entry 32 does not depend on the pipe aqueduct's date.
- **Entry 31**: no aqueduct or cistern serving the Jebel Qarantal fortress is shown or described. No change.
- **Entries 20–22**:
  - Fig. 1 confirms the drawn position of points 4 and 16 in the 2002 numbering. It is consistent with the assumption in `claude/source_access_leads_2026-09-29.md` that the 1989 and 2002 numbers are the same, but it does not prove it, because the text is missing.
  - Stacey's p. 385 claims (Archelaus; a low wall to catch run-off) are still unchecked.
- **Entry 16**: nothing new.

No reading, identification or confidence level changed.
