## Wolters 1987, "Notes on the Copper Scroll (3Q15)", RevQ 12/4 (48), pp. 589–596
EVIDENCE. Four proposals, each preferring ordinary Hebrew over Milik's rare words:
- **I 9 (entry 4) בלגין**: read *baʿal goyin*, "master of nations" (Isa 16:8), i.e. Rome; "vessels of the tribute of the master of nations" (pp. 591–592). Against Milik "sandalwood", Allegro "log measures", Luria "flask", Pixner "breastplate". *Not in readings.json* (no record of בלגין).
- **III 9 (entry 13) לבושי**: "my garments", implying a leading priest wrote the list (pp. 593–594). *Already in the repo* via Wolters 1994 (e13-garments).
- **IX 10 (entry 41)**: divide Allegro's letters as **כסף מנה חרם, "a mina of silver, a consecrated offering"**, against Milik's "much silver deposited" (pp. 594–595); notes the DJD III p. 293 misprint of this line. The repo's e41-silver has Puech and Lefkovits only and says "the exact point of difference is open"; *Wolters' word division is new and bears on that note*.
- **X 11 (entry 47) כופרין**: "expiatory vessels" (from *kōpher* "ransom", Day of Atonement), against Milik "pitch-coated" and Allegro *kĕpôr* "bowls" (p. 596). *Not in the repo* (e47-cups has Puech and Lefkovits).

## Wolters 1988a, "The Fifth Cache of the Copper Scroll: 'The Plastered Cistern of Manos'", RevQ 13 (49–52), pp. 167–176
EVIDENCE. **Entry 5, I 13–14.** Taking Milik's facsimile as authoritative (p. 168), Wolters reads the first word as **שיח / אשיח (*sîaḥ*), "cistern"** (after Luria; Sir 50:3, Mesha line 9, m. B. Bat. 2:1; the same word at V 6, VII 4, X 5, XI 12), and Milik's **מעבא as *mĕʿubbeh*, "waterproofed, i.e. plastered"** (pp. 171–174). Full rendering (p. 176): "In the plastered cistern of Manos, on the way down to the left, three cubits high from the bottom: forty talents of silver." He also prefers Luria's ביריד׳ לשמל "in the descent to the left" and suggests the "descent" is the flight of steps built into many large cisterns, with the cache in a recess near the bottom (p. 176). Also urges imaging the reverse (convex) side of the strips, "since the deeply engraved letters show through" (p. 168 n. 12), as the dossier's Method 7 proposes.
**Repo:** e5-stair lists Puech's spiral stair (המסבא) and Milik's foundry/mine (המעבא) only; e5-manos does not record the cistern reading. *New: a third landmark class for entry 5 (a plastered cistern with an internal stair), on Milik's letters.* INFERENCE: under this reading entry 5 moves from "staircase/foundry" to a water installation, which matters for the landmark-class coding (concealment contexts, water-systems branches).

## Wolters 1988b, "The Last Treasure of the Copper Scroll", JBL 107/3, pp. 419–429
EVIDENCE. On the final entry (XII 10–13; repo entry 60):
- Follows Pixner for the hiding place: "in Sehab north of Koḥlit", in Transjordan near the Yarmuk (p. 420). *Note for the integrator:* the dossier attributes the XII 10 reading שכינה to "Allegro; Wolters"; in 1988 Wolters follows Pixner's place name instead. His later edition (1996) may differ; not checked.
- משנא הכתב הזא = a duplicate of this document (p. 421). **פרושה = the interpretive key**, needed to give the coded numbers their true values and to explain the Greek letters; so the treasures "may very well have been real, but of much less value than the coded numbers seem to indicate" (p. 421).
- **משחותיהם = "their (priestly) consecrated portions"** (Exod 40:15; Lev 7:35; Num 18:8), not "their measurements" (pp. 422–424).
- **פרותכל = Greek *prōtokollon*, the first sheet of a scroll**, not "details of each" (pp. 425–427); it corresponds to the blank 6 cm first margin of 3Q15. So the duplicate was an exact replica plus a "protocol", not a fuller version (p. 427). אחד ואח[ד] = "the one and the other" copy (pp. 428–429).
**Repo:** e60-duplicate cites only a Phase 1 summary; no record of פרושה, משחותיהם or פרותכל. *New.* INFERENCE: bears on Method 9 (what the copy would contain: a replica, so its text would match 3Q15 closely, the case the v2 test measured) and on Method 10 (the "coded values" idea is a third option beside talents and karsh).

## Wolters 1990a, "Apocalyptic and the Copper Scroll", JNES 49/2, pp. 145–154
EVIDENCE. A history of the "folklore/apocalyptic" reading (Milik, de Vaux, Mowinckel 1957, Silberman 1960; Masseket Kelim) against the majority "authentic list" view (Rabin, Kuhn, Bruce, Allegro, Dupont-Sommer, Roth, Luria, Lehmann, Pixner and others) (pp. 145–150). Arguments: the bookkeeping style and the copper medium; no narrative or messianic language; Masseket Kelim is late and its numbers are of another order; its "copper tablet" supports authenticity more than folklore; the Temple Scroll also mentions a *lūaḥ nĕḥōšet* (pp. 150–152). Large sums: perhaps coded with a key (citing his 1988 paper) or not as large as they seem (p. 151).
**Repo:** the Treatise of the Vessels is covered (T08; salvage catalogue C24), but not this historiography. *Partly new (background for T08); no reading.*

## Wolters 1990b, "The 'Copper Scroll' and the Vocabulary of Mishnaic Hebrew", RevQ 14/3 (55), pp. 483–495
EVIDENCE. 30 Mishnaic items accepted from Milik; 10 from other scholars; 10 new. Items that bear on the repo's apparatus:
- **I 3 (entry 1) אריה, "lath, rod": "rod-cubits"** (after Luria), a named standard cubit (m. Kelim) rather than a false start (p. 488). *Not in e1-distance (Puech, Lefkovits, Milik).* INFERENCE: a cubit-length variant, relevant wherever cubit length matters.
- **IX 2 (entry 38) חתין, "tusks" (ivory)**, reading a taw for Milik's חפור "dig" (p. 492). *Not in e38-holes.* INFERENCE: if right, one "dig N cubits" instruction disappears.
- **X 15 (entry 49) בית המים, "privy"** (after Luria), against Milik's בית חמים "baths" (p. 488). *Not in e49-outlet.*
- **XI 17 (entry 56) טיף, "a little"**, a rough distance measure (p. 493); IX 12 מרחב likewise. *e56-platform has only the Phase 2 lexicon.*
- **X 8–9 (entry 47) מעות, *maʿah* coins** (p. 493). *Already in the repo via Lefkovits.*
- **I 11 (entry 4) מפוגל, "second tithe rendered piggul"** (p. 494). Same letters as Puech's "disqualified" in e4-disqualified; Wolters adds an independent attribution.
- **II 3 (entry 7) "Washer's Chamber" of the Temple** (p. 491). *Already in the repo via Høgenhaven 2020.*
- **XII 12 משחה "(anointing) oil"** (p. 493), a variant of his 1988 "portions".

## Silberman 1960, "A Note on the Copper Scroll", VT 10, pp. 77–79
EVIDENCE. Supports the Milik/Mowinckel apocryphal reading with Masseket Kelim (Jellinek, *Bet ha-Midrash* II): Shimmur the Levite writes the treasure lists "on a copper tablet"; the vessels stay hidden until the son of David; sums as fantastic as Milik's (pp. 78–79). *Not new:* the repo covers this text (T08, C24). Only the earliest-pointer attribution is new.

## Puech 1998, "The Necropolises of Khirbet Qumrân and ʿAin el-Ghuweir and the Essene Belief in Afterlife", BASOR 312, pp. 21–36
EVIDENCE.
- Puech names the site "Khirbet Qumran–Sokokah" (p. 21 heading), i.e. Qumran = Sekakah (entries 20–23).
- About 1,100–1,200 tombs: main cemetery, eastern extensions, north and south secondary cemeteries (south of Wadi Qumran, about 30 tombs). 43 opened by de Vaux, about 53 in all with Clermont-Ganneau (1873) and Steckoll (1966–67) (pp. 21–27).
- Standard tomb: a shaft 1.2–2 m deep, with a loculus at the bottom closed by mudbricks or flat stones; north–south, head to the south; individual burials; no family tombs, no ossuaries, no tomb at ʿAin Feshkha (pp. 22–27).
- Dates: fill finds parallel Strata Ib–II (a Hasmonean jar in Tomb 4; a Herodian lamp in Tomb 26; first-century CE sherds in Steckoll's Grave 9). Puech dates the cemeteries 152 BCE–68 CE (about pp. 25–27).
- ʿAin el-Ghuweir: 20 tombs, about 800 m north of the settlement, with coins Herod to Agrippa I and an inscribed jar (pp. 27–28).
- Similar shaft-and-loculus burials at Beit Safafa (about 50) and East Talpiot in Jerusalem (p. 28).
- **Exposure flag, SULTAN-1:** p. 28 summarises about 18 similar tombs at Jericho, "the majority" in the northern part of Kenyon's Trench II at Tell es-Sultan, and others "in the cemetery area to the north", dated to the first century CE (citing Kenyon 1981: 173–174; Bennett 1965: 516–518). This passage was read. INFERENCE: it restates Kenyon's published observation, which the repo already lists as exposed (ACTIVE_TEST, item 2), so it adds no new observation. The integrator should log the exposure anyway.
- pp. 29–30: the Copper Scroll "informs us about treasure deposits in and around Jericho, where the Ha-Qos family was established", citing VII 9 and XI 9. The repo has e30-haqqoz.
**Repo:** no record cites this article or the cemetery counts. *New as context.* INFERENCE: it gives a dated burial type (a shaft with a slab-sealed loculus), which bears on tomb entries placed near Qumran and on "slab" readings such as e14-slab. It is not an identification.

## Gurevich 2020, "The Enigma of the High-Level Aqueduct to Jerusalem and the Mamilla Water System", Tel Aviv 47, pp. 268–281
EVIDENCE.
- **High-Level ("Upper") Aqueduct:** Solomon's Pools to Jerusalem, about 13 km (p. 270). Not built before 41–44 CE, because an Agrippa I coin lay in its foundation core (Billig and Dolinka 2013) (p. 271). Renovated by the Tenth Legion (siphon inscriptions); out of use by about 400 CE (an Arcadius coin in the channel); covered by the 8th century (p. 271).
- **Segments:** 300 m on the western slopes of Ramat Raḥel, with a foundation 2 m wide and up to 2 m high (Billig 2012, Area A1). A 250 m segment along Hebron Road at about 776 m. A short conduit at St Claire's, whose plaster radiocarbon-dates to the 1st–2nd c. CE, but whose attribution to this aqueduct is uncertain (pp. 270–271).
- **Mamilla Pool** (60 × 95 m, walls about 6 m deep): the 1999 trial squares put its earliest floor bedding directly on bedrock, with finds as late as the 6th–8th c. CE. So it was built no earlier than the 6th century (pp. 272–273).
- **Mamilla Street Aqueduct:** to Hezekiah's Pool, Byzantine (4th c. CE or later) (pp. 275–277).
- The short conduit near Mamilla that Amit attributed to Herod and Agrippa I is a different style and need not be linked to the pool (pp. 277–278). The High-Level Aqueduct cannot have reached Mamilla: the valley at the old train station lies at or below 755 m, against a pool rim of at least 774 m (p. 273). Its last stretch is unknown (p. 279).
**Repo:** the Mamilla Street Aqueduct is a scored HA feature (out); no record uses this dating. *New.* INFERENCE: Mamilla Pool and the Mamilla–Hezekiah's Pool system are post-Roman and should fail a first-century period test wherever they appear as candidates. For entry 46 (Beth ha-Kerem = Ramat Raḥel, Milik and Lefkovits), an aqueduct built in the 40s CE ran along Ramat Raḥel's western slope. Gurevich reports no reservoir there, and the site association stays as rated.
