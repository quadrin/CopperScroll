Read: ch. 1 (Wagemakers, Boer biography, pp. 1–21), ch. 7 (J. E. Taylor, "The Archaeology of Khirbet Qumran", pp. 149–165; J. Gunneweg, pp. 166–175) and the photo catalogue (pp. 246–253). The photo catalogue lists a few Jericho titles (p. 248, nos. 8.5–8.9, 5 Jan 1954, including "Refugee camp seen from Tell es-Sultan"; p. 250, nos. 11.12–11.14, 10 Mar 1954). Only their one-line titles were seen; no image and no text about the strip north of the tell.

## What the book settles (EVIDENCE)

- **Two Qumran visits.** The catalogue (p. 248) lists "7.28 Qumran" and "7.29 View from cave (4Qa) in Qumran", both dated **31-12-1953**, after "7.27 Jerusalem and road to Jericho". Boer then joined de Vaux's third campaign (13 Feb–14 Apr 1954) for **20–27 March 1954** (pp. 8, 18, 149). Catalogue dates for that week: 21 Mar (12.7–12.18, "Excavations Qumran"), 22 Mar (Dead Sea views), 23 Mar, 25 Mar (pay day, tea break, "Measurement of the site"), 26 Mar (caves 1 and 2, "Qumran and the Dead Sea").
- **The PEF captions are the book's captions.** Taylor says the photographs were published on the PEF site "with captions illustrating their significance – and have been reproduced here" (p. 149). The book's figure captions repeat them, so it cannot independently correct a PEF caption. Only the catalogue dates and Taylor's text add anything.
- **No aqueduct photograph is reproduced**, and the diary extracts quoted in ch. 1 say nothing about the water system. P06's direction cannot be checked from this book.

## What Taylor's text adds on the water system (EVIDENCE, with her sources)

- **Before excavation, some cisterns were visible on the surface** (p. 153, citing de Vaux 1953a pp. 89–90): "Two small cisterns (later called L117, L118) could be seen in the west", and a large stepped cistern was "plainly visible in the south (L71)", fed by an aqueduct up Wadi Qumran. Isaacs (1857) already noted an aqueduct, the tower, a wall and two small reservoirs west of the tower; his etching (Fig. 7.4, p. 153) is drawn from the SE corner of L71.
- **Correction to my earlier reading.** Finding 4 there ("the north-west pools were still buried in March 1954") is too strong. The NW area was unexcavated, but the tops of L117 and L118 were visible on the surface from at least 1951, and probably 1857. Corrected wording: unexcavated, not invisible.
- The book has nothing on L91's plaster, steps or date beyond its excavation in the week of 21–25 March 1954, nor on L110, L138, the dam or the aqueduct head.
