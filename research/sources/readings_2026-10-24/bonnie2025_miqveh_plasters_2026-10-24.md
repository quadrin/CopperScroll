# Bonnie et al. 2025, miqveh plasters: reading for Method 6 (24 Oct 2026)

**Source.** R. Bonnie, E. Holmqvist, P. Kouki and B. Zissu, "Plasters from Jewish ritual purification baths in Late Hellenistic–Early Roman Palestine: composition, production areas, and anthropogenic residues", *npj Heritage Science* 13:78 (2025), doi:10.1038/s40494-025-01594-1. 26 pp. Owner's Drive copy (04 Articles and chapters). Exploratory reading; no test, rating or outcome count changes.

## What the study is (EVIDENCE)
- 54 samples: plaster from 13 stepped pools at five sites, plus local rock (pp. 1, 3; Table 1). Sites: Ḥorvat ʿEthri (pools XI, XIII, complex XV and its cistern), Sepphoris (SP2, SP4, SP8, SP17), Gamla (L1060 with reservoir L1062 and channel L1024; L1293; L3105), Yodefat (no. 1 with its cistern; no. 2), Keren Naftali (Sq. D).
- **None is on the Method 6 shortlist.** All are in the Judean Foothills, Galilee and Golan; no Jerusalem, Judean Desert or Jericho installation was sampled.
- Methods (p. 5): WD-XRF and ICP-MS at the Washington State University GeoAnalytical Laboratory; petrography and SEM-EDS at UCL Institute of Archaeology (P. S. Quinn); on-site portable XRF; loss on ignition reported (Supplementary Data 2).
- **No radiocarbon, no uranium–thorium, no dates of any kind.** Every pool date in Table 1 comes from stratigraphy or the lowest fills, and the authors say these dates "remain imprecise" (p. 1).
- Sampling (p. 5): about 5 g per spot, chisel and hammer, on well-preserved surfaces, chosen next to existing cracks and holes to limit damage. Permits from the IAA and the Israel Nature and Parks Authority (acknowledgements).

## What bears on Method 6 (EVIDENCE, then INFERENCE)
- **Charcoal and lime lumps are common in these plasters.** Ḥorvat ʿEthri, Yodefat, Keren Naftali and Sepphoris plasters contain "charcoal flakes", lime lumps and argillaceous aggregate (p. 11). The authors take the charcoal's abundance to show "intentional addition", perhaps for the grey colour. Gamla's basalt-tempered plaster lacks charcoal, and lime lumps are rare in it (p. 12).
  - INFERENCE: limestone-region plasters of this period often carry the two things a radiocarbon lab looks for: lime lumps (formed when the lime set) and charcoal. That makes the first test more likely to pass for Judean plasters made from limestone. Deliberately added charcoal can be older than the plaster (the old-wood effect), so lime lumps are the better target, which matches the D-REAMS method.
- **Layers and relining (p. 13).** Several pools carry layers made to different recipes: one for the coat on bedrock, one for the surface, one for floors. In other pools every layer uses one recipe (Ḥorvat ʿEthri XIII; Keren Naftali Sq. D, finer on top). Yodefat no. 1 has five successive layers (p. 4). The authors cannot tell recipe-by-function from replastering: replastering "cannot be ruled out".
  - INFERENCE: a coin in "the plaster" (Wilson's Arch 5060, Western Wall Tunnels L22034) dates the layer it sits in. Sampling must take that same layer, and a thin section should show the layer sequence before any accelerator date is bought.
- **Function warnings (p. 22).** Metal, phosphorus and strontium anomalies show that some pools were reused as workshops, food stores or dumps (especially Sepphoris SP17). Surface layers may be contaminated; a sample should come from inside the coat, not the face.
- **Portable XRF is not enough (p. 21).** It could not tell the recipes apart on site, though it does catch metal enrichment. Screening needs samples taken to a lab.

## Leads this opens (INFERENCE)
- **Shimron's earlier plaster chemistry**, cited as refs 16–18: Qumran water installations (in Humbert & Gunneweg 2003, *Khirbet Qumrân et ʿAïn Feshkha II*, pp. 321–340), **"plasters and carbonate crusts" from Masada miqva'ot** (*Masada VII*, 2007, pp. 127–131), and Sepphoris (2018). The repo mentions Shimron only in one index file. The Qumran chapter is in a volume the repo already holds, and the Masada carbonate crusts are possible uranium–thorium material.
- **People:** Rick Bonnie (Helsinki, corresponding author) and Boaz Zissu (Bar-Ilan) know which miqva'ot have intact, well-dated plaster and how to get sampling permits. Patrick Quinn (UCL) did the petrography and could do the thin-section screening step.
- **Labs:** this paper adds no dating lab. D-REAMS (Weizmann) stays the lab for lime-lump radiocarbon dates.

## Answer
- **The first test: somewhat more likely to pass** (INFERENCE). Charcoal and lime lumps turn up routinely in limestone-region plasters of the period, but this paper did not date anything, so whether the lime lumps are datable is still untested.
- **Which installations: unchanged.** No overlap with the shortlist.
- **Who to approach: two additions.** Bonnie and Zissu, for well-documented pools and sampling practice; Quinn, for petrographic screening of layers before dating. Read Shimron 2003 (Qumran) and Shimron 2007 (Masada crusts) first.
