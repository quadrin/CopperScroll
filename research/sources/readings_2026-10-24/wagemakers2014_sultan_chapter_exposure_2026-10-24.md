## Reading (18:15–18:30 UTC): what the chapter says about the strip north of the tell

Chapter 6, pp. 116–147 (PDF pp. 133–164): M. Sala, "The Archaeological Expeditions to Tell es-Sultan (1868–2012)", and F. Bocquentin and B. Wagemakers, "I. W. Cornwall at Tell es-Sultan". All observations below are **exposed / exploratory**.

- **EVIDENCE, text.** The only statements about ground north of the tell: Kenyon explored "the huge cemetery located to the north and west of the site", more than 600 tombs from EB I to the Roman period (p. 117, citing Kenyon 1960a, 1965); Garstang "discovered the large necropolis west and north of the site" (p. 124); Kenyon's Trench II crossed the northern flank and fixed the settlement's northern boundary (p. 121); in 1993–94 "the necropolis of Tell es-Sultan suffered severe looting" (p. 129, n. 10). No pit, cave, opening, quarry or grave mouth is described in the strip.
- **EVIDENCE, Fig. 6.4 (p. 121; Boer, March 1954).** Camera on the tell's northern slope looking north: the ʿAin es-Sultan refugee camp's houses stand close to the foot of the slope, behind a fence line and a narrow open band. No opening or pit is visible at print size (about 760 px across at 110 dpi).
- **EVIDENCE, Fig. 6.3 (p. 120; Boer 1954, from Jebel Quruntul).** The camp beside the northern side of the mound; too small to show openings.
- **EVIDENCE, Fig. 6.1 (p. 118).** Kenyon's composite plan of 1907–1958 work covers the mound only (grid A–M/1–9, 10–60 m scale); nothing is drawn beyond the north foot.
- **Fig. 6.9 (p. 126; Garstang 1931).** The necropolis with Tomb A, seen from the west; it is west of the tell, not in the strip.

**Outcome for SULTAN-1-precamp-mouth: silent.** The chapter neither locates nor dates an opening in the strip. It confirms the repo's existing statement that the strip has been under the ʿAin es-Sultan camp since 1948 (by 1954 the camp reached the slope foot). No rating, identification or registered outcome changes.
