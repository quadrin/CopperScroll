INFERENCE: the two excavators disagree on whether Feshkha had a Hasmonean phase. Neither dates any water installation by a sealed coin or by plaster.

## 3. Caches, hoards and deposits

- **18-coin hoard (EVIDENCE, de Vaux p. 252, pl. XII c).** "On the top of the retaining wall built east of loc. 21" (a Period III wall) lay 18 bronze coins welded together; the oxide crust kept the impression of the **sack** they were in. **17 coins of Agrippa II, 78–95 CE**, and one worn bronze with two unidentified countermarks. INFERENCE: this is Callegher's locus-160 sack hoard, and it closes after 70 CE. The repo's T02 row **H07 ("18 bronze of Agrippa I, 41–44 CE", from CHRE 8122) is contradicted** by the excavator; the findspot "no floor or depth" in the repo can now read "on top of a Period III retaining wall east of loc. 21". Flag only: check whether H07 entered the Method 10 hoard list or the concealment comparanda as a pre-70 bronze hoard.
- **Antigonus hoard (EVIDENCE, Hirschfeld pp. 42, 53, fig. 15).** **Eight coins of Mattathias Antigonus (40–37 BCE) under the threshold** of the common entrance of the two rooms east of the stable, northern wing. Published by Bijovsky 2003 (not read). Not in any repo hoard or concealment table found. INFERENCE: an "under a threshold, in a building" concealment, 1st c. BCE, inside the concealment-contexts window.
- **Jars set in floors (EVIDENCE, de Vaux pp. 227, 247).** A jar buried to the shoulder in loc. 6; four jars buried in virgin soil at the bottom of locs. 21 and 22, all crushed, one under the 22/22 bis wall (Period I by position).
- **Stone heaps (EVIDENCE, de Vaux p. 237).** "A little over 100 m" north-east of the buildings, **small heaps of stones** suggested tombs. They were excavated "without result", except one that covered a grave: a narrow pit about **1.75 m deep, E–W**, head west facing south, no objects, date uncertain, isolated.
- Three tombs along the outer face of the main building's south wall, late 1st c. CE (Hirschfeld p. 42, citing Donceel 2002).

## 4. Other features (Hirschfeld)

- Area B: a square tower (4.7 × 4.7 m) in the cliff about 50 m above the site, at −336 m, by the Maʿaleh Feshkha path; late Iron Age sherds (pp. 66–68).
- The "long wall": de Vaux's segments make about 500 m running north–south between Feshkha and Qumran (a 200 m segment at Feshkha, 110 m at Qumran). De Vaux proposed an Iron Age date; Hirschfeld dates it Early Roman because both ends start at Early Roman complexes (pp. 66, 69; de Vaux p. 237).
- Between Qumran and Feshkha: the "Israelite building", about 17 × 17 m, 200 m south of Naḥal Qumran; the "isolated building", 12 × 12 m, 25 m north-west of ʿEnot Qumran, a possible columbarium (pp. 68–70). Springs ʿEnot Qumran (ʿEin Ghazal) and Tannur (ʿEin et-Tannur), 800 m apart (p. 68).
- Shore route: the foot of Ras Feshkha is at −393 m; an Early Roman sea at −395 to −400 m would leave the coastal route to En-Gedi open (pp. 38–39).

## 6. Entry by entry: what is new, and whether a repo assessment would change (not changed)

- **Entry 20 (the valley of Sekakah; Milik: the lower valley by Feshkha's cultivated edge).** New: the irrigated strip from Wadi Qumran to Feshkha was enclosed by the long wall, Early Roman by Hirschfeld's argument; ancient springs rose higher and further north than today's. No change: no name and no cairn tie the valley to Feshkha.
- **Entry 23 (sixty cubits from the Trench of Solomon to the great heap of stones).** New: a searched set of **small stone heaps** about 100 m north-east of Feshkha, all empty but one covering an undated grave (de Vaux p. 237). INFERENCE: a failed or silent look at heap-like features near Feshkha for `models/search_effectiveness`; there is no detection scope (number, size and depth of the heaps are not given), and "small heaps" are not "the great heap". No change.
- **Entries 25 and 26 (east-facing caves; the corner of the heap of stones).** Neither report describes a cave at Feshkha; Hirschfeld's cliff feature is an Iron Age tower. **Silent.** No change.
- **Water entries placed at Kh. Qumran (21, 22) and the R01 dating lane.** New comparanda: a Period II (de Vaux) or Herodian (Hirschfeld) system of plastered channels from a now-dry spring, a reservoir of 3.2–3.5 × 8 m and at least 3 m deep without steps, and pools; inflow blocked after 68. None is dated by a sealed contact. No change.
- **Method 10 and the concealment comparanda.** H07 is contradicted (Agrippa II, after 70); the Antigonus threshold hoard is missing from the tables. Both are flags for the integrator; frozen tables are not edited.
- **Method 6 (dating installations).** Basin 24's carbonate deposit and the plastered channels, with coin-dated phases (Period II begins with Archelaus coins), make a possible screening candidate. Feshkha lies in the Einot Tsukim reserve on the West Bank shore; INFERENCE: licensing would go through the Staff Officer for Archaeology (Area C), to be confirmed.
