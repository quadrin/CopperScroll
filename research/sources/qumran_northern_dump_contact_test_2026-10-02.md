# Qumran northern dump and L117 overflow: two bounded tests

2 October 2026 (UTC); 1 October locally in Los Angeles. R01 / Q38, Q41–Q42.

## Results

1. **Date-attribution test: conflicting evidence.** The first-half-of-the-first-century-BCE date on Magen–Peleg 2007 p. 8 concerns the smaller northwestern dump, pictured in Fig. 12. It does not date the northern dump pictured in Fig. 11. The attribution to the northern dump in Stacey's 2009 discussion cannot be supported by that page.
2. **Dated-construction-contact test: inconclusive.** The inspected reports document the slab-covered overflow at the western side of the northern dump and connect it with L117. They do not identify a specific dated, sealed deposit beneath its construction. Photographs alone cannot assign the surrounding assemblage to a construction contact.

These results remove a misassigned dating argument while leaving actual overflow construction, initial aqueduct construction and the gorge features' dates open. They do not rule out a Herodian date or a Qumran candidate.

## Test 1: which dump receives the cited date?

Hypothesis: Magen–Peleg 2007 p. 8 assigns a first-half-of-the-first-century-BCE date to the northern dump beside L117's overflow.

Reading pp. 7–8 together resolves the paragraph:

- On p. 7, the northern dump lies about 10 m north of the site. Its basal material is Iron Age, above which lie five strata and a top burning layer. The covered L117 overflow lies on its western side.
- The final paragraph on p. 7 introduces a fieldstone wall found about 20 m west of that dump.
- The paragraph continuing on p. 8 describes that wall as a possible central-aqueduct retaining wall. North of it is a second, smaller dump. Its finds receive the first-half-of-the-first-century-BCE date.
- Captions distinguish Fig. 11, northern dump, from Fig. 12, northwestern dump.

Result: **the proposed northern-dump date attribution is contradicted by the paragraph's referent and the separate figure captions**. A date from one dump cannot be moved to another because both appear under a joint section heading.

The 2018 pottery discussion separately identifies the northwestern dump as L9501 and gives a Hasmonean–Herodian range (p. 212). Do not treat the two publications' chronological summaries as identical precision.

## Test 2: can the northern dump date the overflow?

Hypothesis: the inspected report coverage ties a dated underlying deposit directly to construction of the L117 overflow.

**Published relationships:** 2007 p. 7 and 2018 p. 68 place the covered channel at the dump's western side. 2018 p. 166 repeats this position and associates the channel area with L900, L522, L2028, L2006, L2018, L2011 and L2017. Fig. 76 (p. 69) is a photograph of the overflow; it supplies no labeled stratigraphic section linking a diagnostic find to a sealed construction interface. “West of” establishes a lateral relationship and does not specify a construction sequence.

**Catalogue limits:** entries for those renewed-excavation loci on pp. 154–155 describe dump finds and relative positions. L2011 includes a coin, but this entry neither identifies its issue nor places it under the channel. The inspected entries do not specify a sealed foundation context. Some other dump loci do have vertical relations; those relations cannot be transferred to the channel without a documented junction.

**Keep excavation labels separate:** Trench A on pp. 166–167 is an east–west de Vaux sounding, excavated in roughly 20 cm increments. Its aggregate list includes a coin assigned to Herod Archelaus (reported 4 BCE–6 CE), with no depth/channel contact supplied there. The separately listed Northern trench has coins assigned to Tiberian governors, Agrippa I and a Neronian governor. Those belong to that trench's aggregate entry. They cannot be reassigned to Trench A or used as a beneath-channel construction assemblage.

**Pottery coverage:** Eisenstadt's pp. 211–212 explicitly discuss material from all northern-dump strata, spanning Iron Age to first-century CE occupation. The proposed first-century CE building-clearance interpretation does not identify when the overflow was built. Dump duration, deposition of a particular layer and construction upon that layer are separate events.

**Earlier interpretation:** 2007 p. 66 note 8 says de Vaux partially excavated the northern dump as “Section A” and associated its material with renovation after the 31 BCE earthquake, citing de Vaux 1973 pp. 25 and 35–36. This is an attributed interpretation; the note does not independently establish the earthquake date or the channel's construction contact. Verify the correspondence of Section A / Trench A in the original records.

Result: **inconclusive to this coverage**. A photograph may suggest that the channel intersects or rests above refuse, but an unlabeled photograph plus a broad assemblage is insufficient to date its construction. The checked descriptions do not supply the necessary find/contact association. This is a coverage result, not proof that no such record exists.

## Consequence and next test

The inspected evidence supports a northern covered overflow associated with L117. It does not select a precise construction year or show that the overflow and initial gorge aqueduct were built together. Retain the site candidate and its unverified upstream phase connections.

Next inspect a plan/section or field record directly connecting the L117 outlet to the raised rim and added steps. Required evidence: construction joint, section/level, underlying locus, dated artifact position and explicit linkage to the internal supply-channel alteration. De Vaux's cited pages are now inspected, with the limited result recorded below. The required measured junction remains unestablished. This follow-up can test relative sequence even if an absolute date remains unavailable. Peleg-map retrieval stays parked.
