## What changes and what remains contextual

**Coastal finds need a formation history.** Demesticha and Polidorou, printed 1507–1537/viewers 1504–1534, report a disturbed pottery concentration at Paralimni-Louma. Survey inside the modern harbour basin was unavailable. Marina construction and repeated relocation by fishing nets remain alternative explanations. The recorded position of stray ceramics does not establish an ancient anchorage there. All ten figures and three tables were checked, including table continuations. The chapter's summary/catalogue vessel numbers and aggregate totals are inconsistent; they were not normalized into a project dataset.

**Route preferences depend on the question.** Byrd and Brandy, printed 1553–1577/viewers 1548–1572, compare travel time and caloric cost, with different preferences depending on passengers and loads. Their model excludes food/water stops, combined land-water trips and acquisition costs for a boat, and lacks craft-specific metabolic measurements. This is a North American method comparison; it supplies no independently observed Copper Scroll itinerary. Gambash's preceding shipbuilding review likewise distinguishes invention from adoption and warns that a shipwreck findspot does not establish its building place.

**Archive integration has a specific route.** Avni, Tendler and Weinblum, printed 1581–1602/viewers 1574–1595, describe DANA's linked locus/basket, elevation, plan and photographic records and its integration ambitions. These are relevant record types for future requests for phase-controlled junctions. Their presence in a national system does not establish that a required historical file survives or is accessible. Figure 1 at printed 1583/viewer 1576 has conflicting dates: its caption says 1990–2010 while the graphic says DANA 2013–2023. The screenshot is not a denominator for a completeness or rarity claim.

**Resolution and accuracy remain separate.** Ridder, Fall and Falconer, printed 1603–1625/viewers 1596–1618, report a fine-resolution orthophoto and DEM alongside a 5.5 m spatial model error (printed 1609/viewer 1602). Printed1611/viewer 1604 calculates a K factor of 0.35895; Equation 4 at printed 1612/viewer 1605 uses 0.86951. Both values were verified against native pages. The erosion quantities require reconciliation before reuse. Their phase-controlled well was capped after the earlier architectural phases; the artistic reconstruction is explicitly separate from the photographed remains. Supplementary Video 1 is named but absent from this PDF.

Later [supplement recovery](supplements_recovery.md) obtained the publisher-served MP4 transcode of Video 1 and inspected views throughout the terrain flythrough. The derivative's identity, sampling scope and silent audio are recorded separately; the original AVI remains unverified.

**A digital reconstruction inherits its reference.** Neil Smith, printed 1657–1681/viewers 1649–1673, reconstructs earlier KIS photographs using virtual control points drawn from a later site model. Those are inherited geometry, not independent survey points. Voxel loci, chosen material textures and hypothetical structural explanations must remain labelled as models. Figure 8's printed 1675/viewer 1667 caption calls the grain room southeast; the adjoining prose calls it northeast. No orientation was silently corrected. Pavlidis's earlier chapter is a general review of digitization and AI; no model was trained or applied to a reserved manuscript image in this pass.

**Interpretation is documented as interpretation.** Vincent's Rekrei account, Richard and Clark's Madaba project, and Sobieralski's *Cult and Copper* describe digital and public-heritage work. The first distinguishes incomplete reconstructed geometry from preserved physical heritage. The Madaba chapter points to raw-data dissemination through OpenHeritage3D; retrieval was not attempted. Sobieralski explicitly marks artistic choices, including unverified drumming and mythic game imagery; its temperature-label correction belongs to a modern prototype. These chapters provide provenance and presentation controls, without supplying new ancient cache observations.

The final boundary section, Tauxe et al., printed 1753–1759/viewers 1744–1750, distinguishes experimental uncertainty from uncertainty in archaeological ages and defines its potentially ambiguous use of “site.” It cautions that one archaeological context can contain materials made decades or centuries apart. The chapter continues with the next reader after viewer 1750; the corrected-publication notice is retained, and chapter-wide conclusions require the joined receipt.
