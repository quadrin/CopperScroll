# Qumran II: inscriptions and their chemical-provenance claims

Source3, Humbert and Gunneweg eds.2003. Lemaire, printed341–388/viewers371–418; Gunneweg and Balla, printed389–394/viewers419–424. These chapters have now received a complete available extracted-text read, including catalogues, indexes, notes1–191, tables and bibliography. Native inspection is recorded separately in [coverage](qumran_inscriptions_coverage.json).

## Context labels require the field sequence

Lemaire p341, KhQ161: the alphabet exercise was registered before the trench opened, and earlier notes describe a Bedouin hole. A recorded depth of one metre does not restore an undisturbed context. At p344, the Joseph stamp KhQ439 was found before the plaster elements of locus30 were exposed; its association with that room cannot automatically assign it to the room's earlier use. At p345, KhQ461's lower-level registration is qualified by disturbance and the following day's Second Revolt coins. These are corrections within the original deVaux observation chain, not additional excavations.

At pp357–358, the Pinhas and Set sherds KhQ2108/2109 came from upper cistern110 fill near the old inlet; weight KhQ2124 came from lower fill. Their find positions do not independently date construction, inlet operation or the plaster repair. Any hydraulic phase claim still needs the relevant physical junction.

## Letter groups, quantities and names retain alternatives

Lemaire p347 prefers an epithet for HTL on KhQ621, disputing Demsky's proposed nearby place-name. At pp348,365–367, proposed liquid-measure readings and personal names remain qualified. At p366, KhQ2538's first letter looks more like K palaeographically, while BT is preferred by comparison; the bath value is not a directly measured capacity of that jar.

At pp374–375, 6Q1/2 were purchased on the antiquities market and their reported origin about100m south of cave6 depends on later Bedouin information. Lemaire explicitly allows a written quantity to describe part of the contents rather than the vessel's entire capacity. This removes a shortcut from jar size to a fixed ancient unit. The provenance and metrology remain edition-based, without independent manuscript or object inspection.

At pp375–376, RWMʾ on jar7Q6 supports a personal-name interpretation, with a Judean place-name still possible; Rome is a competing interpretation. It cannot by itself establish a route from Rome. The item labelled8Q10 is recorded with a March1952 find date at p376. Preserve the complete item, date and photo identity; the cave-code warning in the [earlier review](qumran_ib_changes.md) prevents a global conversion of bare cave numbers.

## The adjacent chapters disagree

Gunneweg and Balla p392 proposes linking the Copper Scroll's origin to Jericho through an Eleazar bowl, a separate deed ostracon and a script comparison. The deed was not analyzed by INAA. Lemaire p356 explicitly says that a popular name without a patronymic cannot establish a particular person's identity. Ceramic manufacture, inscription location, individual identity and scroll origin require separate arguments; the proposed chain supplies no independently identified Copper Scroll landmark.

The chemical chapter's own opening p389 distinguishes pottery manufacture from the location of later writing. Its pp391,393 claims of locally written inscriptions are stronger than that distinction permits for post-firing ink or incision. They also inherit the source and reference-group limits already recorded for the [NAA work](qumran_naa_changes.md).

Gunneweg and Balla p394 says no Hebrew inscription was found at Feshkha, while Lemaire pp378–379 explicitly publishes Fesh207 as Hebrew/Aramaic, with a proposed year-one formula whose era is uncertain. The latter entry also corrects a registration date that precedes excavation of the locus and questions a roof-fall reconstruction. Neither the language summary nor the numeral fixes an absolute phase.

Additional internal crosswalk conflicts require object-level checks: QUM211/Fesh255A is placed in the Jericho group and later described among Beersheba–Arad outliers; descriptions alternate between sherd and inkwell. QUM209's catalogue/description and later locality wording also differ. Preserve these contradictions rather than transferring a chemical result by a shortened number. Lemaire's index at p385 assigns PNHS to KhQ2088, while the catalogue and onomastic discussion assign it to KhQ2108 (pp357,383).

These are newly read source controls and published disagreements. The underlying archaeological objects chiefly remain those of the1951–1958 excavations; repeated later discussion adds no independent field observation.

**Additional quantity conflict, native pp.374/390–393:** Lemaire’s proposed 6Q1 contents are16.03litres within an approximately35litre jar. Gunneweg–Balla Table1 and GroupII print2seah,2log (pp.390–391), while p.393 prints2seah,7log and about16.3litres. These are conflicting printed values, not OCR corrections. Preserve the inscription reading, selected unit equivalents, proposed contents and vessel capacity separately. They do not calibrate a Copper Scroll unit.
