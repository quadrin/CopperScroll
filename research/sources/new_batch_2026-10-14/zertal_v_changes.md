## Evidence added or qualified

1. **The apparent Auja–Archelais connection has a surveyed gap.** Site 111's general account (pp. 314–324, especially 324) assigns the city's supply to ‘Ein ‘Aujah. Appendix D p. 547 explicitly reports that only the western and eastern ends can be followed and that the connecting middle sections were not found. Pp. 549–551 repeat this limitation. P. 552's proposed source join and replacement chronology require excavation. Record the surviving sections as survey observations and the continuous source-to-city route as reconstruction. This resolves the former access gap and qualifies a derivative hydraulic assertion; it does not establish a phase-controlled Neara supply.

2. **Local connections exist around Archelais without a complete chronology.** Appendix D pp. 552–554 and Figs. 4.26–4.27 describe four mapped aqueducts, three connections between the middle pair, two links from middle no. 2 to the upper aqueduct, and a merged lower outlet toward the city/fields. The survey observes connections; their operation as maintenance bypasses and the sequence of replacing the upper channel are interpretations. Several city outlets may be buried. The general site account's “at least five” channels (p. 324) and Appendix D's four labelled courses should retain their respective scopes, rather than be silently harmonized.

3. **The Fasael reservoir provides a measurement and phase caution.** Pp. 533–536, Figs. 4.14–4.16B: the survey gives internal sides of 37.5 m (north, south, west) and 36.25 m (east), exposed depth about 5 m above silt of unknown thickness, a stair and small plastered pool on the west, and an approximately 80 × 80 cm eastern opening. Porath's older account, quoted at p. 538, describes a 15 cm drain. These opening descriptions are not yet a verified identity match. The new work explicitly says construction dates/phases are uninvestigated; nearby Iron II podium finds support only the author's proposed association. A hypothetical Iron Age system is not a dated reservoir wall/floor contact.

4. **Fasael capacity figures depend on assumed depth.** P. 533 distinguishes about 6,800 m³ at the observed 5 m depth from a revised estimate of about 10,550 m³ at Porath's assumed 7.5 m; it disputes the older 15,000 m³ estimate quoted on p. 538. Preserve the dimensions, sediment and assumed depth with each estimate. These are publication estimates, not newly measured project capacities.

5. **The fresh survey is independent only where it reports fresh observations.** Appendix D p. 517 dates detailed mapping to the winters of 2010–2012, including the Musa 2–4 stretch in winter 2011–2012. Pp. 522–526 separate Porath's earlier mill record from the new measurement; the former aqueduct-to-mill connections were no longer visible. The Porath quotations, SWP quotations, and Braunschweig summary remain dependent accounts, rather than additional corroborating campaigns.

6. **Qanat Musa's surviving contacts support a provisional relative sequence.** Pp. 570–573, Figs. 4.43–4.44, distinguish an attached plastered duct, plaster bands beneath later stone construction and a widened support. The author proposes two stages and a raised replacement channel, expressly allowing alternative reconstructions. Approximately 150 surface sherds, mostly Early/Late Roman, do not provide an accurate construction date. The continuation south toward Jericho is referred to earlier sources and future volume VIII work. This supplies a specific section to compare, not the requested dated Jericho opening–wall–floor junction.

7. **Nearby dated settlement finds cannot date the complete hydraulic network.** Site 111 pp. 319–320 recounts Hizmi's excavation of connected plastered pools, an Agrippa I coin and Herodian material, plus a tower whose entrance was not found. These are attributed excavation reports. The survey's remapping (winter 2003–2004) is separate. The source does not establish that each mapped aqueduct belongs to those dated building contexts.

8. **An out-of-question observation has an explicit context limit.** Appendix C pp. 510–516, Figs. 3.1–3.4, records a plundered square tomb on a spur about 2 km north of Wadi ‘Aujah; an inscribed stone lay beside it and a few nearby sherds were Roman. The script/name interpretation and a merchant/shepherd narrative are the authors' interpretations. Neither the loose ceramics nor adjacency securely dates the burial or fixes the stone's original placement. This is an independent exploratory cemetery/context record, with no asserted Scroll association.

9. **Separate installations are intentionally outside the settlement catalogue.** Index 3 p. 576 explains that the same installation may have been used for centuries or millennia and that attribution/dating require separate research. Its rows include destroyed or unrelocated features from previous surveys. “Cisterns: none” in a settlement entry therefore cannot be promoted to a searched regional absence. Examples worth retaining as unscored background are the undated cistern/tumulus pairing (p. 576), plastered cave labelled possibly a cistern (p. 579), and mixed-date cemetery/tumulus records (pp. 581–585). These have neither secure first-century phases nor a frozen eligibility/scoring protocol.

10. **Text completion exposes internal duplicate accounts.** Site 23 p. 140 retains an approximately 45 × 45 m reservoir and 15,000 m³ capacity; Appendix D pp. 533–538 supplies the fresh dimensions and corrected capacity discussed above. Site 15 pp. 108–111 and Appendix D pp. 522–526 recount the same Fasael mill. Site 143 pp. 394–400 explicitly summarizes the separately supplied Zertal et al. 2009 paper. Cross-link these accounts by feature and campaign; do not count them as independent corroboration.

11. **A pool name does not establish an ancient hydraulic feature.** Site 119, “Niran Pool,” pp. 341–342, describes an ancient enclosure; its photograph shows the water pool on top of the hill. The name alone does not identify an ancient hydraulic feature. Site 140 pp. 385–389 gives a large pit of unknown date/function, a later Muslim cemetery, Byzantine water installations and a mosaic inscription invoking a spring. These observations have different chronology and contextual strength; they supply no established ancient pit–grave relationship.

12. **Lithic chronology can depend on ceramic chronology.** Appendix A pp. 445–448 states four identification levels and uses dated sherds when diagnostic flint is lacking. Numerous catalogue assignments explicitly follow that rule. A site's pottery-based flint date consequently supplies no independent chronological confirmation of its pottery date. The mixed surface assemblages also cannot date an individual wall or reservoir. Appendix B pp. 507–508 warns that specialized prehistoric surveys concentrated on Fasael and Salibiya and that general surveyors may miss prehistoric sites. The stated absence of early sites in the main new-survey sample is not a region-wide prehistoric absence.

13. **Surviving and lost controls need separate records.** Pp. 12–13 leave much of the Zor unsurveyed behind fences/minefields; p. 27 gives incomplete coverage in cultivated plains; site 38 pp. 183–185 was inaccessible to the team. Some older sites could not be relocated after destruction. These are observation-process limits. At Phasaelis p. 171 the authors cannot securely reconcile the older bathhouse/reservoir description with their remapping. The unlocated features are neither verified duplicates nor negative excavations.

14. **The tomb account differs within the volume.** Site 117 p. 336 gives a roughly 2 × 1 m grave and a categorical Roman attribution. Appendix C pp. 510–511 describes a roughly 2 m square frame, the displaced stone and loose Roman sherds. Retain the disagreement; the original excavation/survey documentation is needed before treating the size, original setting or burial date as established.

15. **A reconstructed surface plan combines phases.** Fig. 324 (p. 398) is labelled as a reconstruction proposal. Its combined plan is not a surveyed, single-phase building record. The [regional change record](regional_changes.md) supplies the later excavation correction: the former barracks and outlying tower require different dates. That record also preserves the conflicting printed coordinates in the 2009 article, this volume and the 2023 chapter. Fig. 350 (p. 423) marks “Tombs?” tentatively; the diagram does not turn those forms into dated graves.

16. **Quantitative background needs its original denominator and source.** Appendix A Tables 1.1–1.2 (pp. 485, 490) include overlapping period assignments; their totals are not counts of distinct sites. Fig. 1.18 (p. 494) reproduces a popular website's climate chart. The source's climate/migration interpretation remains an attributed hypothesis; this figure is not a newly obtained primary palaeoclimate dataset.

## Copy and extraction corrections

The source text uses a damaged Unicode character map in parts of the PDF. Private text extraction normalizes individual text spans using a character mapping; the source-page render controls the reading. Normalization is not a replacement for checking a disputed measurement or inscription.

The supplied copy's index order is irregular: viewer 595 is printed 587; viewer 596 is printed 585; viewer 597 is printed 586; viewers 598–601 are printed 588–591. The two final viewer pages contain the two halves of the site map listed in the contents at p. 593. Do not extend the ordinary viewer-minus-ten crosswalk blindly through the indexes. No absent printed p. 592 has been independently established as a missing content leaf.

At p. 574 the native page itself prints “3451 cu. m per second” when summarizing Braunschweig's spring discharge. The number/unit needs checking against the original Garbrecht–Netzer report before reuse; this reading is not adopted as a hydraulic datum.
