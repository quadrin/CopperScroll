## Patrich1994: lineage and detection limits

Joseph Patrich, “Khirbet Qumran in Light of New Archaeological Explorations in the Qumran Caves,” *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*722 (1994),73–95. Full supplied text, references and final discussion read; all Figs1–17 inspected.

- **Already captured/repeated field lineage:** pp77,90/viewers5,18 report the same 1986/1988/1991 3Q/11Q work discussed in the later11Q volume. The 11Q four Roman sherds came from excavation dump; the right crevice had sterile aeolian sand. These are excavation-reach-specific negatives, not a second independent confirmation from1994. Fig5 p82/viewer10 is the same Patrich cave plan/cross-section reproduced later.
- **Source-plan distinction:** p76/viewer4 calls Pixner's later3Q plan schematic and its original jar layout hypothetical. Patrich's own Fig2 p80/viewer8 annotates a Copper Scroll find point, dated20March1952, on a later measured plan; this is not the unpublished1952 field plan mentioned in the11Q introduction. Its annotation does not independently authenticate the exact1952 datum. Preserve the original archive lead.
- **Coverage correction for negative surveys:** pp74–75/viewers2–3 explicitly say no soundings were made during the1984–85 survey, older crews collected pottery, and original cave numbering often cannot be recovered confidently. The reported reduction in artifact-bearing caves therefore cannot establish absence of ancient activity. His conclusion that all members lived in the settlement is an author interpretation from access, habitation and survey arguments.
- **Independent field observation, relevance to concealment controls:** p91/viewer19 describes a newly excavated1991 crevice in Cave13: more than3m deep, lower1.5m mixed with pottery/dates, with lime-and-straw stoppers. Dates and the oil juglet's emergency hiding are interpretations; the juglet's balsam identification retains its question mark. A crevice can preserve stored food and mixed archaeological fill without establishing a treasure pit.
- **Explicit dating uncertainty:** p92/viewer20 cannot securely date FQ37's mud-and-palm floor because earlier excavation disturbed the area; a Roman attribution follows the predominance of Roman finds. Do not treat it as a stratigraphically established Roman floor. Fig16 p88/viewer16 shows actual FQ37 arrowheads, distinct from the later misreported11Q mattock/arrowhead discussed in the previous sweep.

## Yellin/Broshi/Eshel2001: chemical observations and provenance limits

“Pottery of Qumran and Ein Ghuweir: The First Chemical Exploration of Provenience,” *BASOR*321,65–78. Full supplied text/references read; tables1–7 and Figs1–7 checked natively.

- **Independent laboratory observation:** pp65–66,70,75–77/viewers2–3,7,12–14 report neutron-activation measurements on selected existing excavation objects. The analytical campaign is independent of the excavations; object provenances remain inherited field assignments. This is not a new cave excavation or an independent check of an object's precise find context.
- **Constraint on interpretation:** p73/viewer10 explicitly says local Qumran clays were not measured. KHQ-QRN is presumed local from workshop context; treating that label as experimentally established local manufacture overstates the paper. Jerusalem comparison uses an existing reference group. Their finding of matching composition supports a provenance argument; Essene makers, commodity transport and religious connections are author interpretations.
- **Method limits retained:** pp66,70–72/viewers3,7–9 discuss drill contamination, excluded measurements, detector changes, very small samples and heterogeneous temper. pp75–77/viewers12–14 warn that small sample size cannot determine vessel-type preferences or population proportions. The two plotted guide lines in Fig6 are explicitly not fitted regressions.
- **Unexpected source inconsistency:** p66/viewer3 describes KHQ21 as a double-mouthed inscribed vessel from the settlement; Table1/viewer6 agrees. The conclusion p77/viewer14 calls KHQ21,22,24,25 four cylindrical jars. Preserve the inventory definitions instead of silently treating the concluding phrase as four independently verified scroll jars.

## Eshel/Greenhut1993: a cemetery comparator with undated graves

“Ḥiam el-Sagha, a Cemetery of the Qumran Type, Judaean Desert,” *Revue Biblique*100/2,252–259. All supplied pages and Figs1–3 read/inspected. Cited PlAa/Ab is absent from this supplied9-page PDF, so publication completeness remains open.

- **Independent field observation:** May1990 excavations examined graves17–18. p254/viewer4 reports a child with glass beads and an adult without datable objects, explicitly saying the adult grave cannot be dated. Fig3 p257/viewer7 supplies schematic grave sections with a0–100cm bar and orientation arrows. It establishes burial construction geometry, not a dated pit/grave contact for Koḥlit.
- **Avoid circular dating and identity:** lateSecondTemple date and Essene affiliation are comparative interpretations using Qumran/EinGhuweir form, nearby documents and historical arguments. The authors expressly retain a nomad alternative (pp258–259/viewers8–9). Reports of Qumran and EinGhuweir tombs cite earlier excavations and do not independently repeat them.
- **Survey lead with unresolved function:** p258n16/viewer8 reports an oval tumulus120m east of the cemetery, a northern expansion that might be an entrance, and stone groups80m farther east with unknown function. These are exploratory field descriptions; they supply neither a dated building nor a candidate identification.

## Puech1998: cemetery labels and interpretation disagreements

“The Necropolises of Khirbet Qumrân and ʿAin el-Ghuweir and the Essene Belief in Afterlife,” *BASOR*312,21–36. Full supplied article including notes/references read; all four figures checked natively.

- **Correction traced to an earlier author, not independent excavation:** p31n3/viewer12 distinguishes the1955 secondary northern cemetery with TombsA/B from the1951 eastern headland extension with Tombs9/10. He identifies conflation in the1994 synthesis p347 and Fig32, and separates photographs444 and448. Norton's2003 discussion repeats this correction with credit to Puech; count the field campaign once and retain the publication-error lineage. Puech's Fig1 p22/viewer3 separately draws the north, main/eastern and south cemetery zones, with50m bar and north arrow.
- **Contradiction between authors:** p33n28/viewer14 rejects an Essene attribution of Ḥiam el-Sagha without further evidence and proposes pre-Islamic nomads; compare the1993 article's retained nomad alternative above. Burial form alone cannot establish sect, date or Koḥlit identity.
- **Already familiar site hypothesis, now earlier citation:** p33n34/viewer14 suggests TellKoḥlit=Telles-Sultan from tell/tombs/pools. It supplies no newly observed dated pit-mouth/grave relationship; the proposal must not count as corroboration independent of the existing Puech model. p28/viewer9's Jericho tomb description cites Kenyon1981/Bennett1965; no new Jericho field section is provided.
- **Author interpretation:** cemetery affiliation, religious meaning of orientation, transport of bodies/coffins and estimates of female proportions are arguments from selectively excavated burials, with uncertain identifications retained. The same author expressly cautions against generalizing from the excavated sample at pp25–27/viewers6–8. Keep his stronger theological conclusions separate from those observations.

## Bar-Adon1977: original report, translated publication lineage

“Another Settlement of the Judean Desert Sect at ʿEn el-Ghuweir on the Shores of the Dead Sea,” *BASOR*227,1–25. Complete supplied text, notes, catalogue tables and bibliography read; all24 figures inspected. The opening explicitly identifies a1971 Hebrew original, *Eretz-Israel*10,72–89. The Hebrew capture is a second publication copy of this campaign. Its independent coverage ledger confirms18 text pages72–89 and four photographic platesXLVII–L across31 capture slices; its fuller textual reading remains separately recorded. It cannot supply independent archaeological corroboration.

- **Independent excavation observations, later repeated by Puech:** pp12,16–18/viewers13,17–19 describe tomb disruption by earlier treasure/scroll searches, pit/loculus construction, pottery above covering stones and the jar inscription. Pottery in upper grave fill dates that assemblage; it does not supply a sealed construction date or an adjacent pool/pit-mouth contact. The later Puech cemetery account inherits these observations.
- **Hydraulic uncertainty as a control:** p4/viewer5 records a1m threshold beside a15cm-wide channel of two stone rows, with clay deposit, but explicitly cannot establish whether it sloped inward or outward or determine its function. A channel beside an entrance is therefore not automatically a dated inlet or outlet. The report's room/courtyard sections (Figs2,6–8, viewers4,7) do not resolve that particular gradient.
- **Construction-phase limits:** flash floods removed large parts of walls and floors; two rooms were only partly excavated (pp1–5/viewers2–6). Synchronizing the two ash layers with Qumran's destructions is the excavator's comparative model, with alternate historical mechanisms retained at pp18–20/viewers19–21.
- **A false writing cue explicitly investigated:** p24n1/viewer25 reports UV and botanical/geological checks of dark grave-stone spots. The observed stains provided no foundation for reading letters. This is an original negative identification with described methods; it does not negate the real jar inscription or an unrelated manuscript reading.

## DeVaux1959: Feshkha hydraulics and qualified functions

“Fouilles de Feshkha: Rapport préliminaire,” *Revue Biblique*66/2,225–255 plus inserted plates. Complete supplied text, notes, object catalogues and coin lists read; all3 body figures and all12 inserted plates inspected. Native validation of every printed page resolves the clipped extracted-text line endings. Split foldouts remain separate native capture parts, with north arrows, captions and scale bars preserved.

- **Observed hydraulic contacts:** pp230–233/viewers7–10 describe two openings beneath the northern enclosure wall feeding basin23; an overflow returns north through that wall; branches serve basin24, pits25/27 and basin26. Basin24's low southeast aperture begins in a drilled stone and drains through a pavement channel to pit25. Two branch paths and the stepped channel into27 remain distinct. Pits27 and basin26 were not fully bottomed because of groundwater. Flow descriptions and reconstructed uses must remain separate.
- **Elevation/datum limit:** p233/viewer10 puts channel origin29 almost5m above the then-current main spring and3m above the highest nearby spring; it reports stabilized excavation water levels at source-plan cotes2.32/2.04. These are1958 observations and an inferred former higher spring; no modern geographic datum registration was established in this pass.
- **Phase evidence:** p247/viewer24 says the northern enclosure wall cut an earlier drain and assigns the basin complex toPeriodII; its two superposed exterior surfaces are not two independently built basin phases. p251/viewer48 reports blocked twin feed apertures after industrial use ceased, with later water diverted along the enclosure's north side. A modern visible opening/blocking can therefore represent a later state.
- **Function retained as a hypothesis:** pp235–237/viewers12–14 acknowledge difficulties with tannery comparanda. The p255/viewer52 postscript reports final negative chemical tests for expected tanning residues and weakens the tannery attribution. Bath exclusion in the initial description and later ritual-bath proposals are author arguments requiring comparison of surviving steps and reconstructions.
- **Apparent reporting discrepancy, scope unresolved:** p237/viewer14 describes an isolated grave a little more than100m northeast of Feshkha's buildings, about1.75m deep, east–west, no objects; its relation to ancient occupation is expressly uncertain. Puech1998p27/viewer8 says no tomb has yet been discovered at Feshkha while discussing community cemeteries. Retain both wordings and check whether Puech intended that narrower burial category before calling it an error. Neither account establishes a dated cemetery association.
- **Concealment context:** p252/viewer49 records an oxidized bronze-coin group on top of a later retaining wall, with a bag impression preserved in corrosion. Placement, bag evidence and coin dates are observations reported by the excavator; intentional concealment and its exact purpose need their own argument.

## Hirschfeld2004: new campaign and incompatible readings of old fabric

- **Independent2001 observations:** pp53–54/viewers18–19 locate an eight-coin Antigonus group below a northern-wing threshold and an iron arrowhead embedded inW7. The coin group provides a lower chronological constraint for that threshold context; selecting a specific construction year or historical destruction needs additional argument. The group is distinct from deVaux's later oxidized bag group on a retaining wall.
- **Reobserved hydraulics with old lineage retained:** pp56–63/viewers21–28/Figs20,24 describe channels, distribution poolL133, reservoir, large poolL142, western vatL127 and eastern poolL119. Flow throughW29's basal opening is reported; the eastern small pool has no connection through the dividing wall to the western vat. Depths reported in2001 differ from the earlier groundwater-limited exposure; compare excavation reach before calling a measurement contradictory.
- **Chronology disagreement:** pp39,42,44/viewers4,7,9 argue for one initial construction enterprise and later modifications against deVaux's two main phases; p42 makes the author’s negative probes explicit. DeVaux1959's cut earlier drain, superposed thresholds and jar beneath a wall remain actual reported contacts to reconcile, not erased by broad plan resemblance. - **Function disagreement and failed search:** pp62–65/viewers27–30 compare tannery, fish, date-wine, date-honey and perfume proposals. No ovens/hearths were found in the2001 search; a two-site production chain withQumran is a proposed explanation, not an observed connection. Fig26 is a reconstruction. Fig9/viewer13 also labels its lower stone-object images reconstructions of tables, while the surrounding prose argues from opus-sectile tiles for a luxurious floor; distinguish object survival from functional reconstruction.
- **Source checks still needed:** p42/viewer7 reports three tombs beside the main building's southern wall, citing Donceel2002pp109–114/Fig17. This differs from deVaux1959's isolated northeast grave and requires that original catalogue. It cannot be silently merged with it. The same printed page pairs a second-half-first-century destruction with “Second Revolt”; retain the internal chronological mismatch. p44/viewer9 gives59 identifiable coins from deVaux, while the original1959p245/viewer22 says87; count should follow a reconciled inventory, not a repeated overview.
- **Unexpected regional lead, clearly interpreted:** p70/viewer35 calls the isolated12m-square building a possible columbarium by plan comparison; Fig31 caption retains its question mark. No pigeonhole evidence is reported. It remains a function hypothesis rather than an additional confirmed columbarium.

## Caves2016 chronology and ceramic synthesis: inherited observations, competing explanations

Popović, “When and Why Were Caves Near Qumran and in the Judaean Desert Used?”,177–183/viewers194–200, and Magness, “The Connection between the Site of Qumran and the Scroll Caves in Light of the Ceramic Evidence,”184–194/viewers201–211: complete text/notes read; Magness's Table12.1 inspected natively.

- **Deposition versus object age:** Popović pp178–180/viewers195–197 separates internally dated documents, manufacture/palaeography and deposition. The proposed lower limit for Babatha's archive uses its reported physical relationship above Eliezer's archive, not just an old document's date; his wider cave conclusions remain comparative inferences. The caution applies directly to a broad3Q lamp type or an old coin.
- **Negative-evidence reach:** pp181–182/viewers198–199 distinguish coins on cave terraces/intervening ground from coins inside caves. Apparent absence and short-term/long-term use arguments inherit older survey/excavation records; they do not independently establish complete detection. Family-refuge, seasonal use and manuscript-only safekeeping remain competing functions.
- **Already published criticism rather than a new discovery here:** Magness p188/viewer205 already points out that Yellin's four purported cylindrical jars include differently shaped vessels and that the supposed local clay assignment lacks a measured local clay source. The classification inconsistency identified in this pass belongs to that earlier criticism lineage.
- **Provenance correction proposed from records:** p188n43/viewer205 attributes to Fidanzio the suggestion that the inscribed jar labelled6Q1 actually came from a dealer's cave about100m south of6Q, rather than the single uninscribed cylindrical jar catalogued for6Q. It expressly identifies the label as Chambon's handwriting. This is a proposed reassignment requiring original custody/label verification; chemical testing does not validate the cave findspot.
- **Same objects, differing measurements and interpretations:** pp189–193/viewers206–210/Table12.1 cross-identify objects across laboratory teams. The same Johanan Hatla/Roma jars and some bowls acquire conflicting geographical group labels; those labels are not independent proof of manufacture at either site. Petrographic group numbers also do not mean the same thing as INAA group numbers. - **Hydraulic function versus sediment composition:** pp184–185,193–194/viewers201–202,210–211 distinguish material caught by floodwater from a pool's original design. Pottery/pool-silt similarity would not alone prove that the entire water system was designed to obtain potters' clay.
