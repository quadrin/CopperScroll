Exact coverage is in `copper_coverage.json`; private source copies and images remain outside the public repository.

## Changes established so far

- **Correction of interpretation history: Wolters 1988 → 1990 → Mandel 1993.** Wolters, *The Last Treasure*, JBL107 (1988) pp419–429 (viewer2–12), proposes a separate interpretative key, priestly portions, a Greek *protokollon*, and a near-identical duplicate. His1990 vocabulary paper, RdQ14 pp492–493 (viewer11–12), expressly retracts the portions interpretation: the singular verb in Exodus40:15 invalidates his proposed plural. He replaces it with anointing oil, prompted by the1989 oil-juglet press report, and corrects the newspaper's claim of23talents of oil. Mandel, RdQ16 (1993) pp71–76 (viewer4–9), disputes oil as an attested Hebrew sense, the syntax of the protocol proposal, and the late explanatory sense of *prwš*. He proposes specification of locations, measurements and amounts, with an almost exact copy rather than an expanded guide. These are conflicting linguistic interpretations, not independently observed objects. **Our inference:** the existing duplicate-plus-key proposal needs Mandel's alternative alongside it; discovery of another copy would not necessarily add missing geographic precision.
- **Already captured, now direct: Ullendorff1961.** VT11 pp227–228 (viewer2–3) verifies the original numeral proposal cited secondhand in the Greek-letter record: ΔΙ10, ΞΕ65 instead of Allegro's ΘΕ, Ν−Η42, ΤΡ400 versus40, ΚΕΝ170 versus17, ΧΑΓ3000 without a Hebrew amount. The author explicitly allows inconsistent cryptographic treatment and speculative decimal relationships. He bases the discussion on Allegro1960, not a claimed original-metal examination. No new arithmetic corroboration or reopened test follows.
- **New direct lexical-source coverage: Wolters1987.** RdQ12 pp589–596 (viewer2–9) argues from Milik's facsimile and earlier editions for I9 *baʿal goyin*, III9 'my garments', IX10 a mina of silver plus a consecrated offering, and X11 expiatory vessels. Priest/high-priest ownership is his inference from those choices. His p594n30 identifies a typesetting displacement of the samekh in Milik DJDIII p293, checked against Milik's other printed quotations rather than fresh metal. None authenticates the manuscript or independently establishes ownership.
- **New direct lexical-source coverage: Wolters1990.** RdQ14 pp483–495 (viewer2–14) enumerates50 proposed Mishnaic lexical items, expressly including contested readings and semantics and defining Mishnaic to include Tannaitic and Amoraic material. P485 states that this work uses Milik's facsimile and previous interpretations. P488 contrasts a privy interpretation at X15 with Milik's baths; pp489–490 distinguish an author's *kwk* tomb-chamber reading from the narrower modern archaeological niche term; pp491–492 propose notches rather than feet and cistern sediment rather than a pierced stone. These are textual branches. A glossary label must not silently become an archaeological feature requirement. The claimed pre68CE date on p483n3 depends on Pixner, not independent dating evidence.
- **New direct source, literary control: Silberman1960.** VT10 pp77–79 (viewer2–4) compares Massekhet Kelim in Jellinek1853, from the1648 *ʿEmeq ha-Melekh*, including its copper-tablet treasure record and restoration expectation. This is a later literary parallel. It supplies no independently dated ancient hiding place and cannot date the Copper Scroll or prove its contents historical/fictional.
- **Already captured debate with direct citations: Wolters1990, 'Apocalyptic'.** JNES49 pp145–154 (viewer2–11) traces the initial press release, Milik, Mowinckel and Silberman and argues against a folkloric reading. P147 notes a change from600 bars in the1956 announcement to6 in Milik's edition. P149 calls independent scholarly agreement 'confirmation'; the underlying evidence still overlaps. P152 explicitly separates the late *Massekhet Kelim* from earlier hidden-vessel legends; p153 compares object types, retrieval instructions and eschatology. Scholarly preference and repeated literary comparisons cannot identify a deposit or authenticate ownership.

## Source chains and changed constraints

**New direct source / archaeological function dependency — Wolters1988, Fifth Cache**, RdQ13 pp167–176 (viewer2–11). P169/viewer4 prints three facsimiles of I13, after Allegro, Baker and Milik; these are separate drawings of the same inscription. P168n13 explains their dependency: Durra drew Allegro’s copy; Baker traced photographs; Milik revised Baker against the original. Pp172–175 argue for an elongated, waterproofed/plastered cistern of Manos rather than an escape staircase/foundry. P176 distinguishes three cubits above the bottom from depth below a present surface; a descending stair and wall recess are the author’s reconstruction. This improves the original textual argument for a candidate’s required function but does not independently survey a cistern or choose Manos. Its I13 figure was safely inspected and archived privately.

**New direct source / later disposal model — Lehmann1964**, RdQ5 pp97–105 (viewer2–10). P98 distinguishes vessels holding offerings from vessels inherently consecrated; their emptying and reuse mean that a vessel’s cultic label need not identify sacred ownership of the container itself. Pp99–105 propose a post70CE accumulation of redeemed offerings; the argument depends on reading *ʾpwdt* as redemption, applying later legal texts, and inference from absent sacrificial pledges. P101 explicitly acknowledges that ordinary heave offerings cannot normally be redeemed and offers historical/legal explanations. P104 infers water-filled hiding places and defaced coins under that model, even where the scroll does not independently specify water or defacement. These inferred features must not populate an observation ledger. The p105n6 ΧΑΓ/Hebrew feast suggestion is an interpretive predecessor already reported in the Greek-letter record.

**Already captured, now direct / independent comparanda require their own provenance — Richey2012**, DSD19 pp177–197 (viewer2–22). Pp182–186 analyze the grain account4Q350; its disputed origin, recto/verso order and uncertain association with excavated fragments prevent automatic assignment of every documentary item to pre68CE Qumran. Pp186–189 draw on Lemaire2003’s inscriptions and Hamidović2009; these are not additional epigraphic finds. P192n54 says Koḥlit occurs with and without Greek groups; pp193–195 review numerals and personal initials without fixing bearers. The name-matching exercise is expressly potentially endless (p194). This authenticates the cited author’s argument and supplies a useful comparative accounting system, without a new decipherment.

**Already captured, now direct / inventory-genre correction — Weitzman2015**, HTR108 pp423–447 (viewer2–26). P431 introduces letter-labels through Tod1954, not a recovered Copper Scroll labeling key. P430n19 says Weitzman could not access Wilmot’s unfinished dissertation and relies on Wise’s summary. Pp434–435 distinguish routine handover, special audit and removal/recasting inventories. Pp436–442 compare the Lindian Chronicle, whose record of absent offerings shows why inventory form alone does not authenticate present deposits. P432 explicitly conditions the public-display/archival-copy analogy on the original’s intended display; p433 cites deVaux1953. Taylor2023 p306 disputes display using Baker’s mechanical account. These are competing uses of the same object reports. No inference that a specific Copper Scroll deposit was invented follows from the comparative genre.

**New direct source / competing chronology model — Taylor2023**, chapter8 pp293–334 (viewer2–43). Pp318–325 dispute a compulsory pre68CE cave deposit. The3Q lamp is reportedly missing; p319 points to Młynarczyk2016 p115/Fig7.6p120, type036.2 with a range extending to mid2ndCE. The type dates a lamp tradition, not the scroll. Pp321–323 preserve disagreement over the Roman-era cavity and collapse, explicitly criticizing Patrich1994’s reconstruction rather than supplying new excavated stratigraphy. P324n120 records a1952 newspaper report of coins but also deVaux’s later denial: no published coin assemblage/date is established. P324n122 cites anIAAcurator’s2019email saying no3Qorganicremains held; this is historical custody testimony, not a current inventory. P322n113 withdraws Taylor’s earlier acceptance of bitumen-impregnated jar wrapping; clay stoppers are now proposed. Pp303–310’s material-cost, haste, manufacture and intended-secrecy arguments remain author interpretations; no new metallurgy was undertaken here. P311’s post68Roman attribution of4Q350 conflicts with Richey2012’s sectarian-context argument and depends on the same Cotton/Larson2003 discussion.

**Copy-level direction discrepancy — Taylor Figure8.2**, p295/viewer4. Native inspection shows a FreeText PDF annotation saying “west” over a caption whose extracted underlying text says “east.” The annotation creation/modification date is20February2023; its author cannot be inferred from its account label. Preserve both layers; do not use extracted “east” as a verified orientation. Figure8.7 p320/viewer29 is Taylor’s drawing after Patrich1994Fig2, not another independent survey. Figure8.1p294/viewer3 reproduces a Ziegler overview rather than the unlocated1952findspot plan.

**New direct source / geographic selection and exposure — Pixner1983**, RdQ11 pp323–365 (viewer2–44). P328n7 reports Puech’s1981conversation with Contenson and DoronChen’s cave measurement; PlanI/viewer41 combines extant ground plan with inferred original entrance/collapse. Pp334–335 argue that the rolls must share pre68deposition with manuscript jars; the timing and exclusive access conclusion exceed an independently dated sealed context and are disputed by Taylor. P341 explicitly says the regional grouping is the author’s, while the translation largely adapts Milik. P335n32 offers Josephus names as possible initial matches, expressly disclaiming identification except a suggested Thebuthi connection. P337’s multiple-Koḥlit/monastic-centre explanation is the author’s conjecture. P347n16’s rock-pillared cistern east of the Dormition pool is a reported modern feature used in a proposed match; it has no ancient phase/junction proof here. PlansII–IV are explicitly suggested localizations/reconstruction. Their map symbols cannot independently corroborate the text that selected them. Entry25 is Pixner27, entry29 is Pixner31, entry49 is Pixner51 and entry60 is Pixner64; preserve the canonical concordance rather than copying his numbering blindly.

**Correction of derivative calculations — Clark2022**, *The Copper Scrolls: A Modern Look From a Cold Eyes Perspective*, title+printedpp1–60/viewer1–61. The source compiles translations, historical summaries, old maps and an author's scenario; it supplies no newly excavated deposit. P15/viewer16 chooses112–125lb per talent by analogy, rather than resolving the scroll's unit branches. P16/viewer17 computes2.3ft³ for17talents of gold, then gives a1×1×1.5ft box (1.5ft³). Its native tablespp25–30/viewer26–31 include600 pitchers and609 vessels alongside talents; the location-weighted total4562 and77%Jerusalem allocation therefore mix object counts with mass units and model-based geographic assignments. The repeated/sorted tables are one derivative dataset. Pp44–46/viewer45–47 select Qumran's eastern cistern steps as entry1, but give no surveyed ancient threshold, construction-phase join, calibrated distance or independent detection result. P48/viewer49 explicitly says photographs and aerials are omitted from this redacted copy. Its unlabelled image pages10/12 were withheld pending safe content screening; no blanket visual-completion claim is made. The old-map reproductionspp54–60/viewer55–61 retain reconstructed identifications and modern annotations; they do not independently identify Secacah or date the scroll's features.

**New direct interpretive source — Eshel/Safrai2002**, *Cathedra*103 pp7–20. Publisher text was recovered at https://files.ybz.org.il/periodicals/Cathedra/103/Article_103.1.pdf and read end to end, including notes. Pp10–13 argue for a literary/ritual inventory, citing imprecise locations, difficult hiding volumes and the duplicate reference; p11 explicitly conjectures that entry25's unspecified scroll is another copy. P13n23's asserted lack of post68cave finds conflicts with Taylor's later discussion and requires original-context checking. Pp13–17 compare hidden First Temple vessel traditions; pp17–20 place the list in sectarian competition over cultic legitimacy. These are interpretations, not a recovered First Temple cache. Their assumed duplicate with extra detail must be compared with Mandel's lexical argument. The supplied browser capture has no extractable text; complete text reading here refers to the publisher copy. Unknown and whole-scroll image pages remain withheld, with native coverage separately recorded.
