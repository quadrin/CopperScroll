## Findings

### Setting and dates
- EVIDENCE (p. 367): the excavations ran 1973–1981 near where Wadi el Qelt enters the western plain. They found water systems linked with a royal estate next to the Hasmonean winter palaces, which "covered an area of c.2.5 ha" and lie "about half a kilometer northeast of the gorge".
- EVIDENCE (p. 367 n. 1): study of the water systems was done mainly in 1976 (with G. Kopel) and 1981 (with D. Stacey).
- EVIDENCE (p. 367): there were two separate systems (Fig. 6), from the Wadi el Qelt springs and from Naʿaran and ʿEin el ʿAuja. The earliest originated in Wadi el Qelt. They were "roughly surveyed by the British Survey of Palestine" (n. 3: Conder & Kitchener, *SWP* III, 190, 222, 227–28).

### Springs (n. 4–6, p. 367)
- EVIDENCE: Wadi el Qelt flow = ʿEin Farah, ʿEin el-Fawwar and ʿEin el Qelt. Average combined discharge "174 litres per second (78 l/s minimum, 354 l/s maximum)".
- EVIDENCE: channel capacity "420 l/s between ʿEin el Qelt and St. George, and 250 l/s between St. George and the area of the estate". The slope is S = 0.01 above St George and 0.006 below it.
- EVIDENCE: allowing 20% loss, flow reaching the palaces and estate was "in the order of 140 l/s or 12,000 m³ per day".
- EVIDENCE (n. 5): "The three springs of Naʿaran lie close together at 120 below sea level". Annual yields:

  | Spring | Annual yield |
  |---|---|
  | ʿEin ed Duyuk | "5,300,000 m³" |
  | ʿEin a Nuʿeima | "2,300,000 m³" |
  | ʿEin Shusha | "500,000 m³" |
  | Total | "8,100,000 m³" |

- EVIDENCE (n. 6): ʿEin el ʿAuja is "the largest in the Jericho plain (at 20 m asl)" and more rainfall-dependent; average "11,000,000 m³".
- EVIDENCE (n. 7): for the Qelt channel upstream of the plain see Meshel & Amit, *Qadmoniot* 46–47 (1979) 67–77, and Amit pp. 313–29 in this volume. See also Garbrecht & Netzer, *Leichtweiss-Inst. Mitt.* 115 (1991).

### Qelt channel and the royal estate of Hyrkanos (pp. 367–370)
- EVIDENCE (p. 367), construction:
  - The channel is "50 cm wide and 50-60 cm deep", with two side walls "both 60 cm wide" and a foundation "at least 15-20 cm deep".
  - It is built of small fieldstones in lime mortar, with "2-3 cm" of lime plaster.
- EVIDENCE (p. 368): a lime kiln ("3 m diameter") was found close to the channel west of the royal estate.
- EVIDENCE (p. 368), course:
  - Where the channel leaves the gorge it is "about 170 bsl (or 180 below the spring)". There it turns north along the edge of the plain.
  - "Some 250 m to the north" it hugs the cliff of a dry waterfall on the southern tributary of Wadi Shaq ed-Dabi.
  - "50 m further on" it enters the royal estate, which is marked by long boundary walls.
- EVIDENCE (p. 368), boundary walls:
  - mostly straight, the longest known "300 m";
  - "70 to 80 cm" wide, of fieldstone foundations with probably mudbrick walls "some 2 m high".
- EVIDENCE (p. 369), estate size: N–S "600 to 700 m", E–W "probably about 700 m", "a minimum area of 45 ha".
- EVIDENCE (p. 369), course inside the estate:
  - The channel runs "more than 700 m" inside the western boundary.
  - It leaves the boundary only at the northern tributary of Wadi Shaq ed-Dabi, running "75 m into the wadi".
  - It ends "some 100 m south of the NW corner of the estate, at c.180 bsl" (Fig. 5; found 1981).
- EVIDENCE (p. 369), outlets:
  - Six outlets are known (Fig. 6, I–VI).
  - All except no. II have short plastered side channels "(80-100 cm)" at right angles.
  - Near no. IV there are two or three parallel channels.
- EVIDENCE (p. 369), boundary walls by side:
  - The N and S walls are regular and straight.
  - The W wall follows the slope contours. Stretches were "recently uncovered when the Tarik abu Hindi road was widened".
  - Near the SW corner, the steep cliffs serve as the boundary.
  - n. 9: the crops were probably date palms and balsam, the latter probably in the southern part near the palace; soil tests showed the area south of the Tarik abu Hindi road is best.
- EVIDENCE (pp. 369–372), wine press:
  - A large wine-press ("perhaps for date wine") was uncovered in 1981 at the W edge of the estate. It was probably built together with the Qelt channel (Figs 10–11).
  - First phase: two juice-extracting tanks "(5.5 m square and close to 1 m deep)", two settling pools "(1.1 m square, 1.3 m deep)", and a main collecting tank "(2.8 m square, 2.2 m deep)" with steep steps.
  - It was later expanded.
  - A drainage channel runs below the Qelt channel without breaking it, so the two were built together.
- EVIDENCE (Fig. 11 caption): beside the Qelt channel near the wine press are a "stepped immersion pool (ritual bath)" and a partly exposed pool.

### Hasmonean winter palace and the Naʿaran channel (pp. 372–376)
- EVIDENCE (p. 372): the first Hasmonean palace was built "some time after the royal estate was built under John Hyrkanos (or perhaps his father Shimʿon)".
- EVIDENCE (p. 372), terracotta pipe:
  - A pipe "(8.5 cm inner diameter)" branches from the Qelt channel near point I (Fig. 6).
  - It "would have carried about 9 l/s or 12,000 m³ per day" (as printed, checked on a crop). INFERENCE: the two figures disagree; 9 l/s is about 780 m³ per day. 12,000 m³ per day equals the p. 367 n. 4 total, so it is probably a carry-over.
  - "Remains of this pipe can be followed for 380 m from the Qelt channel to within 10 m of the central building (on its NW side)". It was buried "about 40 cm" below the surface, south of and parallel to the S boundary wall, and protected by small stones in mortar.
- EVIDENCE (p. 372 n. 10): a tower "(c.9 m square)" preceded the estate. It was later incorporated into the SW part of the palace.
- EVIDENCE (pp. 372–373), Naʿaran channel:
  - It brought ʿEin el ʿAuja water and was "12.3 km long, from ʿEin el ʿAuja to Naʿaran". It is "1.15 m wide and 0.60 m deep", with slope "0.00210 and 0.00247".
  - It is traced from Wadi Mafjar "(about 1 km north of the estate)" southward.
  - "maximum hydraulic capacity of 725 l/s" against ʿAuja's average "345 l/s" (max "555 l/s").
  - It crosses Wadi Nuʿeima "about 200 m north of the Naʿaran" springs and adds ʿEin el Duyuk "(171 l/s)" and ʿEin Nuʿeima "(77 l/s)" (a further "182 l/s").
  - After diversions, about "270 l/s"; after 20% losses about "200 l/s" at the estate's N edge.
  - It reaches the estate higher than the Qelt channel ("150 bsl versus 175 bsl"), running near the top of the slope.
- EVIDENCE (p. 374), branches:
  - "Some 120 m west of the NW corner of the estate (at 152 bsl)" the channel splits.
  - The N branch drops "25 m in 110" to a small pool "(2 x 2.2 m)" near the NW corner. It then runs east along the N side of the N boundary wall for "about 800 m" (Fig. 9). It irrigated fields east of the estate (n. 13: now intensively cultivated, remains not visible).
  - The S branch runs inside the estate, "2.3 m" from the S wall, crossing the Qelt channel on a slab bridge "35 m from the SW corner". The 2.3 m strip was probably a service route for wagons.
- EVIDENCE (p. 375): "Four hundred meters east of the junction" the S branch passes the main palace and the swimming-pool complex. It is destroyed "c.200 m east of the palace" and is assumed to continue east.
- EVIDENCE (pp. 375–376), junction (Figs 13–14):
  - The Naʿaran channel crosses over the Qelt channel on a large flat stone. "Naʿaran could not predate the Qelt channel."
  - Two short interconnecting channels were added later.
- EVIDENCE (p. 376), pool complex:
  - Added "in the early days of Alexander Jannaeus": two pools "(18 x 13 m each)", each holding "800 m³"; a Doric pavilion; a peristyle garden "(c.70 x 60 m)".
  - The pools and the channel were built together.
  - n. 14: later, "probably under Herod", the pools were combined into one of "32 x 18.3 m" by lowering the common wall "(6 m wide, 3 m high) by about 2 m".
- EVIDENCE (p. 376), Fortified Palace:
  - "some 10-15 years after" the pool complex, the central building was buried under an artificial mound with a moat "c.7 m deep". This changed the main channel's course north of the mound and removed one of two small pools west of the palace.
  - A new ceramic pipe ran from the Qelt channel (near the bridge) to the elevated palace. Its first half was set into the side wall of the conduit, then alongside it.

### General hydraulics (pp. 376–377)
- EVIDENCE: the channels follow contours; bridges were avoided and channels ran up into side wadis.
- EVIDENCE: aqueduct widths are uniform at "40 to 50 cm".
- EVIDENCE: outlets are either simple openings or flanked by short walls (Fig. 7). There are no sluice-gate grooves.
- EVIDENCE: there were no reservoirs; water went straight to the fields.
- EVIDENCE: "The estate appears to have been in constant use from its foundation until the destruction of the Temple in 70."

### Appendix: Birket Musa (p. 377, start only)
- EVIDENCE: the pool lies "south of Wadi el Qelt". It was surveyed and probed in 1976.
- EVIDENCE: it is sunk "c.3 m" below its surroundings and measures "c.180 x 150 m", with depth "at least 3 m" and capacity "at least 80,000 m³".
- EVIDENCE: probes at the E wall centre and NE corner showed two phases: a fieldstone wall with lime plaster, later thickened by a 50 cm wall with ash-lime plaster (Fig. 16).
- The text continues on p. 378, which was not photographed.
