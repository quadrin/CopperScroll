## Source observations, author interpretations and project implications

### Carmel Cave / Bir el-Hammam — supplied p. 1; atlas pointer 56

Source: https://www.malham.info/carmelcave . By Boaz Langford. Cave mapped during the May 2012 visit following Yigal Amihai's report. A local youth supplied the alternative name Bir el-Hammam; that is reported oral naming evidence.

**Reported observations.** A 1 × 1 m entrance lies at the base of a rocky slope on the northern bank of a tributary of Wadi Abu Shaban, the upper Nahal Hever drainage. It is about 3 m above the channel and 15 m east of a broad drainage junction. An initially vertical passage opens about 1.5 m below the entrance into the roof of the southwest part of a 47 × 10 m chamber, usually 3.5–6 m high. Dark sediment slopes east and south at the southern end; large collapse blocks cover its central and northern parts and create small subordinate voids. The northern ceiling has deep solution cupolas. Bedding is reported as dip 11.5 at azimuth 330. The access note gives about 6 m total descent. Source coordinate `16621/09306`; stated elevation 700 m above sea level.

**Author interpretation.** Hypogenic/phreatic formation in Turonian Shivta or Bina limestone; a sediment cone probably entered through the opening. The source gives no archaeological dating or human installation.

**Effect.** Adds a documented vertical-access/collapse morphology, with no dated mouth. Obtain the May 2012 survey plan through the article/Malham records if geometry matters. No plan is in this PDF.

### Bir el-Kair — supplied pp. 1–2; atlas pointer 62

Source: https://www.malham.info/birelkair . The supplied copy gives no writer credit. The article refers to a visit but gives no dated survey campaign.

**Reported observations (p. 1).** A roof opening 4 × 3.5 m lies on a low spur about 30 m above a large tributary of Nahal Hever, east of Umm Daraj. A 20 × 16 m chamber is about 8 m high and covered in a thick pigeon-dropping layer. Rock strata dip 6.5 at azimuth 55. Two passages run northeast and northwest. The northwest passage is low and continues about 2 m through near-blocking organic material to an elliptical chamber with a blind chimney rising about 2 m above its ceiling. A roughly 2 × 1 m small area outlined by several fieldstones lies beside this passage entrance; a single potsherd was found nearby. Further sherds beneath the droppings are a possibility, not an observation.

**Author interpretation.** The roof opening followed collapse after phreatic development. Dead animals are interpreted through a natural fall-trap history; limited human activity is inferred from the stones and sherd. Neither the enclosure nor the sherd is dated. P. 2 gives an approximately 8 m descent, source coordinate `175646/094237`, and elevation 388 m.

**Effect.** Adds an undated interior stone arrangement with explicitly obscured archaeological visibility. It does not establish a built entrance, a dated deposit or an ancient floor. Original survey/photograph and find-context data would be needed before archaeological coding beyond this descriptive fact.

### Chariton Cave — supplied pp. 1–4; atlas pointer 90

Source URL is printed as `https://www.malham.info/ןוטירחתרעמ` in the copy; preserve this exact source string rather than silently correcting its Hebrew path. Authors: Boaz Langford, Amos Frumkin, Roi Porat and Shmulik Avidan.

**Reported observations.** P. 1 gives 4,216 m total passage length, about 400 m maximum straight-line reach from the entrance, and three openings on Nahal Tekoa's western bank. P. 4 distinguishes the nearby mouths: a high inaccessible northern opening, the middle opening used for entry, and the southern opening closed by a grille. Modern iron stairs/rails modify access. Pp. 1–2 describe an interconnected multilevel labyrinth, two principal levels around the Great Pit feeder, high blind chimneys above chambers 43–44 and wet/muddy lower sections. In chamber 49, standing water was observed in a depression 40 cm deep in the mud. This is a modern internal water observation with no ancient pool construction context.

**Survey lineage (p. 1).** Tobler visited in 1853; Conder/Drake's 1873 survey covered entrance/main passages. A 1965 Strobel-led survey is described by the present authors as partial and directionally erroneous. Gideon Mann's 1969–1971 thread-and-compass traverse recorded 55 numbered chambers; loop errors were corrected graphically. Malham's 2006–2008 survey by A. Amihai, M. Avital and S. Avidan used Mann's framework and added walls/selected profiles. The source also reports 2020–2021 remapping by Malham and the cave enthusiasts' club. These are distinguishable survey generations with shared numbering; the later description does not establish independent measurement of every earlier traverse.

**Human-use reports and interpretations (p. 2).** Pottery from inner areas is assigned to the Intermediate Bronze Age, approximately 2200–2000 BCE. Byzantine familiarity and a refuge episode in 1139 are historical reports, with no excavated architectural contact here. Monastic biography, the nearby hanging cell and its identification with Chariton are historical/modern identifications. The cell lies about half a kilometre south of the cave; its 3.5 m elevated opening, steps, plaster and three-level complex must not be transferred into the main cave's geometry. Formation under confined phreatic conditions and the small spring's possible geological relationship are author explanations.

**Route instructions (p. 3), read in full.** Entrance to T-junction about 5 m; chamber 3 to 6 involves a roughly 3 m drop, then a roughly 1 m rise into chamber 7. Loop 1 goes through 8/9, the Fissure and the main passage, returning through 22 with an approximately 10 m slippery slope. Loop 2 uses shaft 21 and returns to 7. Loop 3 passes 14–18 and reaches 19, whose roof has a shaft rising about 6 m, then returns through 21. These are modern navigation descriptions with relative turns, not surveyed mouth bearings. P. 4 describes the approach past Bir el-Aniziya, a pool attributed to monastery use, and an approximately 50 m ledge approach. Neither this water feature nor the chamber-49 puddle supplies a Copper Scroll pool association.

Source coordinate exactly `172541/11658`, explicitly labelled old Israel grid; components have unequal digit lengths. Preserve without repair or conversion. Elevation 543 m. Obtain the actual 2020–2021 map, its north/scale/section conventions and predecessor crosswalk from Malham; the main text says a map is attached, but the supplied copy omits it.

**Effect.** Documents a three-mouth modern cave and several distinct mapping generations. No phase-controlled ancient mouth count or northern-threshold datum is supplied. The previously read CIIP3397 hanging-cell drawing and this narrative share the monastery identification; neither independently dates the main cave entrances.

### Mushroom Cliff Cave 10 — supplied pp. 1–2; atlas pointer 98

Source: https://www.malham.info/pitriya10 . By Boaz Langford. Discovered in the 1984 Malham youth survey led by Frumkin; original designations cave 10 and openings ז1+ז2. Fully remapped February 2013 by Shemesh Yaaran, Boaz Langford and Nevo Fishbein.

**Reported observations (p. 1).** Cumulative length 63 m, vertical extent 10 m; two mouths about 10 m apart. The main northern opening is an elliptical slit 9.7 × 2.2 m facing northwest. The cave passage level lies in the opening's upper part, about 6 m above the cliff base, with a natural lighted balcony. A shallow conical carved cupmark on its northern side is about 6 cm in diameter and similarly deep. The entrance chamber is 2.5 × 3 m. A passage about 5 m long leads west into a 5 × 5 m northern chamber, usually about 2 m high, with solution domes up to 5 m above the floor; very few sherds occur on its surface. The southern passage connects to the secondary opening, 1.7 × 1.55 m, with a comparable shallow carved cupmark on its inner side. An eastern room has a blind chimney reaching about 7 m above the floor; beyond a roughly 3 m sloping passage is another chimney room. Calcite covers parts of the internal surfaces.

**Author interpretation.** Hypogenic/phreatic origin in Shivta limestone. Cupmarks and surface sherds supply no dates for mouths or anthropogenic features. The northwest-facing statement is a modern textual observation, without a north convention or instrument bearing; no model test is performed.

P. 2 gives source coordinate `172822/117402`, elevation 522 m, and the exact original publication route: A. Frumkin, 1986, "The formation of the caves of Nahal Tekoa," *Niqrot Tsurim* 13, pp. 33–41. Retrieve that article and the February 2013 plan, keeping their shared survey lineage explicit.

### Wadi Qelt Cave — supplied pp. 1–2; atlas pointer 118

Source: https://www.malham.info/wadikelt . By Boaz Langford. Frumkin first reported the cave after a 1975 visit; relocated October 2011.

**Reported observations (p. 1).** Total passage length 146 m; elongated main passage on an east–west axis, average slope about 10 degrees. Bedding dips about 5 degrees at azimuth 295. Arched mouth 4 × 2.3 m on the northern bank, about 25 m above the channel. A small terrace and natural descending rock steps lie in front; three further openings below it lead into small cavities. Near the highest of these, about 5 m below the main entrance, is a large carved cupmark now filled with earth. Passage A has soot-darkened walls/ceiling and a compact goat-dropping/soil floor. About 15 m west of the entrance, passage B runs north about 6 m to a high step and an inaccessible narrow continuation. About 40 m west of the entrance, A splits into parallel branches rejoining after about 5 m; the northern is obstructed by stones in dark earth. Beyond the reconnection a low step and 3 m more lead into chamber C, 6 × 4 m and 4.5 m high. Its surface contains many ceramic fragments. From its west side, about 2 m above the chamber floor, passage D continues west and narrows to impassability after about 50 m. Short lateral passages have natural or stone/soil blockages.

**Author interpretations.** Phreatic development in Bina limestone; sherds probably Bronze Age, an identification by Uri Davidovich from a photograph. Suspected looting affects chamber C and short subsidiary passages. The ceramic assignment is qualified and photograph-based, without a sealed floor or architectural date. Stone obstruction does not establish built blocking. The three lower openings are described as access to small cavities; connectivity with the main cave is not demonstrated.

P. 2 gives source coordinate `1812/1377`, elevation 189 m. This is a cave locator, not a registered outlet point.

**Effect on entry32.** The current entry32 notes concern an aqueduct outlet and a separate SWP monastery-opposite wall/lower culvert. This article gives no water outlet, aqueduct-to-cave connection, buried stone course or phase relation. A carved cupmark is not evidence for the entry32 hydraulic feature. Keep the same-valley name separate from feature correspondence. Retrieve the 2011 cave map only for the cave's own geometry; entry32 still needs the wall-specific plan and construction context already specified.

### Steps Cave complex — supplied p. 1; atlas pointer 136

Source: https://www.malham.info/madregot . By Boaz Langford. Malham recorded nine separate cavities during its March 1982 survey, on the south bank of upper Wadi Makkuk. The cavities are distributed both along and up the cliff. Some connect by roughly hewn exterior steps and vertical shafts inside the cliff. Simple one/two-room spaces have niches, a formed threshold, stairs and shafts. The rough and weathered workmanship is compared with the nearby carefully finished Khallet ed-Danabiya laura, about 150 m away. Cave 91 differs, containing a carefully hewn hanging cell.

**Author interpretation.** Monastic activity probably affected the complex; similarities to cliff refuges motivate a proposed Jewish-refuge function. Roughness and similarity do not supply a date, ethnic attribution or construction sequence. No dateable assemblage, mouth bearing or levelled ancient threshold is reported. The 20 m rope in the access note is equipment advice, not the vertical separation of the cavities.

Source coordinate `18085/14426`; elevation 410 m. Exact retrieval routes: A. Frumkin, 1988, "Survey of the caves of upper Wadi Makkuk," *Niqrot Tsurim* 14; **Malham archive aerial 28A, caves 83,84,85,86,87,88,89,90,91**; Haim Goldfus, 1987, "Another monastery in the chain," *Teva va-Aretz* XXIX(2). The copy supplies no page span for these references. The nine stated cavities and the nine archive numbers correspond within this article; their precise geometry requires the source map.

**Deduplication caution.** The current regional inventory has `A41:II/42`, also called "Steps Cave," at `18095/14414`, described as a two-storey cave with windows and coins. This web article's name, nine-cavity complex and different locator do not establish identity. Preserve a possible correspondence pending the original maps, not a merge. If both grids share their assumed old-grid convention, their printed points differ by about 100 m east and 120 m south; this arithmetic cannot measure physical separation or source accuracy.

### Arak en-Naasane — supplied pp. 1–3; atlas pointer 158

Source: https://www.malham.info/araknasane . By Boaz Langford. No dated mapping campaign is identified in the supplied text.

**Reported observations (p. 1).** Total length 2,238 m; maximum depth below the mouth 52 m. Most developed chambers follow northwest–southeast joints; one enormous hall is stated as 200 × 12 m. The entrance, 10 × 3.5 m, is on Wadi Daliya's north bank about 5 m above the channel. A broad forecourt rests on bedrock and is partly held by a poor stone wall, with thick accumulation over it. From the mouth a broad passage runs north about 45 m to chamber B, 15 × 8 m, elongated east–west. A narrow eastern route reaches chamber C, 16 × 14 m, about 1.5 m high, covered in collapse blocks. West of B is chamber D, 26 × 6 m, east–west; its western branch is a 240 m labyrinth. Its eastern continuation runs north about 35 m then northwest into chamber E, 30 m long, about 12 m wide and up to 3 m high. Collapse dominates the onward F chamber.

**Lower level (p. 2).** At F, a narrow vertical link descends to passage G, 85 m long and 1.8–10 m wide; height varies from 1.1 m south to 5 m north. A small southern opening links G with the large hall complex whose branches total 375 m. The text calls that approximately a third of the whole cave; this fraction conflicts with 2,238 m overall length and must be preserved as a source inconsistency. The literal values are retained separately. Eastern H1 and western H2 branches are distinguished; the latter leads to the Cathedral, with its ceiling roughly 10 m above the floor. The central hall I links to northwest M, southeast N, and P/Q passages parallel to the general northwest–southeast axis. P. 3 contains attribution and locator/elevation only.

**Dating and interpretation.** The article reports Bronze Age and Early Roman finds, with Early Roman use attributed to Jewish Bar Kokhba refugees; it gives no catalogue, stratified contexts or construction dates in this copy. Accumulation in front is probably human-related; C and H1 may once have connected before collapse; pits in E may result from looting. Each is author interpretation. Soot, surface flint/bones and disturbance show affected deposits rather than a recoverable ancient threshold. The source also attributes 2004 wall graffiti to a modern organizational gathering, a conjecture unnecessary for archaeological dating.

Source coordinate `188708/155702`, elevation 134 m. Retrieve the mapped cave plan and archaeological reports independently; the web narrative has no bibliography identifying those excavation publications. Its singular main-mouth description does not prove absence of other exterior apertures. Never equate a 52 m cave depth with depth under a northern threshold.

### Daliya Waterfall Cave — supplied p. 1; atlas pointer 162

Source: https://www.malham.info/mapaldlaya . By Boaz Langford. Surveyed in 1980; fully mapped again in June 2013 by B. Langford, M. Ullman and A. Lev Ari (initials in the body match the names supplied by the associated cave articles).

**Reported observations.** Total length 39 m; two openings at the top and bottom of the central Wadi Daliya waterfall. Upper opening is rectangular, 0.6 × 0.5 m, in the streambed beside the lip. Floodwater traverses the cavity and exits the lower opening, 6 × 10 m. The intervening chamber is broad and lighted; its bedrock floor slopes gently northeast. Its north/west margins form a lower dark crawlspace; eastward a narrow route leads over a high vertical step to the lower opening. No dateable artefact or artificial installation is reported.

**Author interpretation.** Original phreatic formation in Shivta limestone followed by exposure at the waterfall. The two openings are at different levels, with a present floodwater route; this is no report of two ancient horizontal facade mouths. Source coordinate `18870/15565`, elevation 115 m. Obtain June 2013 plan/section before measuring aperture separation or flood levels. Equipment wording gives no ancient-access datum.

### Abu Kati — supplied pp. 1–2; atlas pointer 168

Source: https://www.malham.info/abukati . By Boaz Langford. Schematically surveyed in 1981; fully remapped June 2013 by B. Langford, M. Ullman and A. Lev Ari.

**Reported observations (p. 1).** Length 63 m; main passage runs east-southeast–west-northwest, perpendicular to the bedding-dip axis stated as 55 degrees at azimuth 230. Four exterior openings occur along the north-bank cliff of Wadi Daliya: three horizontal and one vertical. Main easternmost mouth A is 7 × 4.5 m, broadly triangular/arched. The main floor rises inward at about 25 degrees and has at least 0.5 m of organic/goat-dropping cover exposed in a cross-section. About 10 m inward is a 12 m transverse passage with a southern mouth B of 2.5 × 3 m. Mouth C is west of B, several metres above the channel, joining through a low broad passage about 24 m from A. At that junction, chimney mouth D opens vertically to the surface. A neighbouring blind chimney is separate. Beyond the vertical mouth the main passage turns north and continues about 15 m to its narrowing end; p. 2 adds that the innermost floor/ceiling are horizontal.

**Archaeological limits.** The author observes orderly transverse trenches near the entrance and infers archaeological investigation. A possible connection to the early-1960s American expedition is conjectural; the article explicitly says Abu Kati is not mentioned in those expedition reports. Do not cite that expedition as the trench excavator without a field record. The roof aperture is interpreted as a truncated/collapsed karstic chimney, with collapse blocks beneath. Neither the trenches nor the half-metre floor cover supplies a construction date or ancient mouth threshold. Length and passage connections are modern survey observations; ancient opening count remains undated.

Source coordinate `18890/15575`, elevation 100 m. Obtain June 2013 plan/sections and any trench record tied explicitly to Abu Kati. Nearby Naasane, Abu Shinji and Yogev caves are distinct sites; adjacency does not merge their finds or excavation histories.

### Arak Ti — supplied p. 1; atlas pointer 170

Source: https://www.malham.info/arakti . By Boaz Langford. Frumkin first reported it in the early 1980s; Vladimir Boslov updated the report after April 2006; Mika Ullman, Amit Lev Ari and Boaz Langford mapped it June 2013.

**Reported observations.** Total length 49 m. Main mouth 2.5 × 1.7 m at a cliff base on the north bank of lower Wadi Abu Mahmoud. The passage initially runs northeast, then northwest and rises progressively inward; two short branches run north. Floor combines exposed bedrock and dry sediment; the latter reaches into a narrowing crawl route. Layered calcite rafts occur centrally. Several nearby openings lead to small voids, including a subvertical chimney; the source does not demonstrate that they join the main cave. A single sherd was found in the inner passage, without type or date.

**Author interpretation.** Hypogenic formation in Turonian Shivta limestone. The water borehole about 100 m below the cave in the modern access note is a separate modern feature. It supplies no cave-water installation or ancient relation. Source coordinate `189039/155832`, elevation 77 m. June 2013 map is the precise survey target. No verified entrance bearing, ancient floor or cave-level occupation date is supplied.

### Wadi an-Nuweiris Cave — supplied p. 1; atlas pointer 176

Source: https://www.malham.info/nuviris . By Boaz Langford. Discovered in 2011 by Vladimir Boslov, Boaz Langford and Stas Primak while searching for Amal el-Watwat Cave; discovery context does not establish that identification.

**Reported observations.** Length 25 m; two levels. A rounded 2 × 2.2 m mouth lies about 5 m below the top of a low cliff on the south bank. Several large stones on the mouth's east side form an improvised access step; the text gives no age. Main passage runs south about 15 m, with heavily mixed dark dry sediment, numerous sherds and several flints. A central chimney rises about 4.5 m to an upper passage parallel to the entrance passage. Its northern part descends back into the main passage about 4 m south of the mouth. A low constriction about 10 m south of the mouth opens into approximately 5 m more passage with conglomerate layers overhead; sherds and charred twigs occur in the inclined floor. Bedding reported as +25.5-degree dip at azimuth 292, generally NNW–SSE.

**Author interpretation.** Phreatic formation in Shivta limestone. No ceramic date, stratified charcoal date or date for the access stones is given. The internal upper loop is not a second demonstrated exterior entrance. Source coordinate `188152/151852`, elevation 70 m. Obtain the 2011 survey/map if connectivity or external aperture inventory matters; no plan reference/call number occurs in the copy.
