## Published observations

**P. 212 / viewer 1.** The complex comprises three caves: West, East and North. Source-rounded coordinates, marked נצ״מ, are West `243600/629260`, East `243615/629268`, North `243600/629325`; reported elevation −270 m. The entry does not specify a full CRS/vertical-datum definition or a surveyed mouth reference point. Preserve these strings as approximate locators, without deriving precise inter-mouth distances from them. The methods chapter's warning about rounded coordinates applies.

Reported passage lengths are West 128 m, East 35 m and North 35 m. Length totals must remain separate from straight-line depth or distance. The two southern caves, East and West, are explicitly identified as the pair Bar-Adon called Twin Cave; their mouths are described as approximately 25 m apart. This establishes the atlas author's cave-complex association with Bar-Adon. It does not individually register Bar-Adon's northern/southern cavity terminology against atlas survey stations.

West has a roughly 50 m main passage extending approximately north, a front opening and a roof opening, plus a lower passage joining the main passage at both ends. Its present overall height reaches 23 m. Approximately 8 m of sediment had accumulated; excavation removed most of it, greatly enlarging the cavity. Some secondary passages were opened/deepened during intensive excavation. East is a broad, high passage extending north-northwest, narrowing inward, with no branches described. North lies farther north and higher on the slope; its entrance faces northeast and is inconspicuous from afar. It has a tall passage, roof opening and small branch.

The research account tentatively attributes the first West excavation to de Vaux in 1952, Bar-Adon's 1970s work mainly to East, and Jones's 1980s–1990s work to West. It reports the earlier identification hypothesis based on the East/West pair and intervening rock partition or “pillar”, and says the searches failed to yield the expected results. That historical interpretation supplies neither a target-volume negative excavation nor independent identification evidence. The chapter cites Bar-Adon 1989 and Jones 2005.

**P. 213 / viewer 2.** Sparse pottery spans Late Chalcolithic/Early Bronze I, Late Hellenistic/Early Roman, and probably later Byzantine/Early Arab/medieval use. These are cave-use assignments, without a dated target threshold or wall contact. The photograph viewed from the east labels West at left, East centrally and North as hidden farther right. It helps distinguish the separate northern cave from the East/West pair.

**P. 214 / viewer 3.** Three photographs show North, West viewed inward from its entrance, and Vladimir Buslov in West after Jones's excavation. Captions and complete images are preserved in the native PDF. Photographed surfaces receive no ancient-floor or wall-date assignment from this inspection.

**P. 215 / viewer 4.** The plan is credited to Shmulik Avidan, A. Kleinberg, B. Langford and A. Cohen, Malham 2011. It maps all three caves, with a 5 m scale bar, an N arrow, labelled profiles B–B′ (West), D–D′ (North), A–A′ (East), and cross-sections C–C′, E–E′ and F–F′. West's upper/lower levels and roof opening are differentiated; East has its own mouth; North's mouth and roof opening are separate labels. The published plan draws no connecting passage between East and West or between the pair and North. This is a plan observation, not proof that every possible buried connection was excluded.

## Historical-name crosswalk — 15 October 2026

The original Bar-Adon chapter now supports a bounded cavity-level correspondence: **his northern cavity is atlas East; his southern cavity is atlas West**. This is an exploratory inference from the atlas author's explicit association of the pair, their distinctive morphology and the landscape photographs. It does not register a historical mouth or threshold to a survey station. [Machine-readable source crosswalk](twin_crosswalk.json).

Bar-Adon, printed p. [15], describes two east-facing cavities separated by a thick rock pillar. The southern cavity has multiple levels, a higher innermost part open to the sky, and a narrow tall shaft continuing inward. The northern cavity is closed and single-level, approximately 35 m long and 4 m wide on average. The atlas p. 212 explicitly identifies East/West as Bar-Adon's pair; East is 35 m and unbranched, while West is multilevel with a roof opening and a lower passage joining the main passage at both ends. Bar-Adon's B1 landscape photograph and the labelled atlas p. 213 photograph show the paired-mouth outcrop. The comparative lengths refer to the particular source's passage or survey total; West's 128 m total does not contradict the older northern cavity's 35 m length because they concern different cavities and definitions.

This resolves the earlier individual-name ambiguity at cavity level. **Atlas North is a third cave and cannot stand for Bar-Adon's northern member of the pair.** Bar-Adon's named northern cavity should therefore lead an entry-25 retrieval to atlas East. A source item still needs its cave name, season, mouth and level tie before any measured depth or finds locus can transfer. The original warns that finds collected by excavation areas lacked precise boundaries between different loci; the B2 table's Cave A/B labels do not independently establish a northern-threshold datum.

The VJRI North/Qetorit cave is **probably atlas North**, on a separate source-visual comparison. Banks and Downing's April 1992 report, reproduced in the 2026 compilation at pp. 114–116 and 119, describes a northeastern-facing northern cave with a passage extending southward. Its p. 116 interior photograph and the atlas p. 214 North photograph have a corresponding tall, jagged aperture and stepped roof outline. The p. 119 plan's long southwest passage and northeastern mouth agree with the broad atlas North layout. These correspondences support cave identity; they do not provide an explicit VE/VW-to-Malham station join. The reproduced coordinate header is degraded or typographically ambiguous, so no normalized point or absolute elevation is accepted. The reported possible connection to the Cave of the Column remains hypothetical; neither plan demonstrates an internal connection.

The opening labels also need their source precision. Atlas p. 212 gives West a front opening and an additional roof opening; p. 215 labels its roof opening `פתח בתקרה`, its front access `כניסה`, and East's front mouth `פתח`. The East/West aggregate therefore has at least three labelled exterior apertures in the published survey state, including two front mouths. The lower West passage connects internally and adds no demonstrated surface aperture. Bar-Adon already described the southern cavity's sky-open upper part, so the atlas clarifies the opening labels rather than supplying independent new field corroboration. Whether the roof opening existed in the relevant ancient phase, and whether an exact-two requirement counts roof apertures, require separate archaeological and textual arguments.

No exact bearing, ancient-floor elevation, three-cubit fit or candidate score follows from this crosswalk. Preserve the atlas N-arrow convention as unspecified, distinguish developed profiles from true 3D geometry, and keep the atlas rounded locators separate from historical mouth coordinates. Most of West's roughly 8 m of sediment was excavated; the VJRI North mouth was also altered. Neither present floor supplies Bar-Adon's ancient northern threshold.
