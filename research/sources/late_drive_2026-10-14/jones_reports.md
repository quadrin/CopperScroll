# Northern cave and claimed incense: source comparison

14 October2026 UTC. Exploratory source audit of the supplied Browning1996, Amar1998 and Project Qumran2026/VJRI1995 copies. Observation, reported laboratory result, reconstruction and later testimony are distinguished below. No authenticated deposit identification or confirmatory score is derived.

## Separate cave and excavation datum

Browning, *Biblical Archaeologist*59(2),74–89 (1996), pp.76–78/viewers4–6, distinguishes the twin openings excavated1977–89 from the smaller cave farther north used in1992. Its caption says the twin openings give access to separate cavities and were enlarged during excavation. The northern-cave photographs dated2/4/10April1992 and the June1993 re-filled pit are retrieval evidence, without a surveyed ancient threshold.

The2026 compilation reproduces the1995 April account at pp.115–122, with the site-locus plan on119. Native pp.115–116/119/121 were checked. P.115 reports3–4m of pre-season fill removal, a reinforcing-rod datum at the west edge and painted control points whose accurate survey was intended later. P.116 reports the outer4m cave axis as N30°E, with an unspecified north convention; possible connection to the southern Cave of the Column remains hypothetical because their levels might differ. The original mouth is reconstructed roughly10m from the present cliff face. These are reported present geometry and reconstruction, not two registered ancient mouth bearings.

P.119 supplies metre axes and VE/VW points; the coordinate header is degraded/ambiguous, so no normalized geographic point or absolute elevation is transcribed. Preserve the complete page for the original survey request. A horizontal plan does not supply a vertical stratigraphic section.

The April account at p.121 describes a roughly2m-long bedrock cavity, average **8cm depth** and25cm width. Automated OCR read the depth as28cm; native inspection corrects it to8cm. Pp.120–121 report a10cm red-material lens. The May account at pp.124–126 instead describes30–50cm material, a150cm ascent, a14m by2.5–3m distribution and a coin at VW7. These are separately authored/located statements pending survey/locus joins, not interchangeable measurements. The May narrative quotes XII10 while combining multiple scroll entries; this derivative text is exposed and supplies no independent manuscript reading. AppendixF images remain unopened.

The April account claims clearance to bedrock. Its sentence about the qalal is grammatically ambiguous in the received copy, so it is not transcribed as a definite find or absence statement. Its equipment/control/screening limitations and altered mouth datum prevent treating the clearance narrative as a scored negative for the registered three-cubit volume. Original survey, section, finds register and ancient mouth phase are required.

## Chemistry and alternatives

Antelman's reproduced preliminary report, pp.100–101, begins with an explicitly suspected Ketoret sample and a recipe-based interpretation. It reports bulk density, suspension pH, heated residue and acid reaction, then calls for mass-spectrometric verification. These tests alone do not identify individual spices or demonstrate archaeological age.

Browning p.85/viewer13 prints McMurtrey's actual sample/data review. He distinguishes powder bulk density from particle density, repeats sinking/acid observations, and interprets the data as mainly calcite/dolomite soil with trace solvent-extractable organics. This is a competing published analysis, beyond the2026 narrative's account of Amar. It refers to the29March1993 Luvak analytical report; the original report, spectra and sample-to-locus custody records are still needed. It does not establish that all presently circulating samples have the same source.

Amar, *Dead Sea Discoveries*5(1),1–15 (1998), explicitly received no requested sample and relies on Jones's1995 description. His proposed borit/alkali-production explanation uses comparative historical processes and reported cave channels/grinding stones. It is a hypothesis, without a new excavated kiln context or independent sample assay. His Salsola reference p.8/n17 is Segal–Carmi1996 p.90 and remains a separate source retrieval; do not relocate those plants into this cave by inference. The paper's p.1 prints68–69BCE for the revolt and mis-cites Browning as volume52; preserve those source errors instead of using them as dates/bibliography.

Hutter's reproduced report pp.103–104 does provide a processing description, chemicals and sieve fractions. It identifies nine organic ingredients, separately invokes two inorganic materials and records laboratory ragweed contamination. Its historical identification, comparative specimens, raw images/counts and contamination controls still require audit. Amar's critique and Hutter's claims should be assessed against those originals rather than treating either conclusion as established.
