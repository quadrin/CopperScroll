## What site 143 actually provides

Hebrew p. 142 reports most remains concentrated in the village core, with reused building stones in houses and terraces. Caves occur at the southern edge of the core; the authors suggest a burial function for some. One contains an olive press. This function label cannot classify every southern cave as a tomb.

**West of the village core, down the spur's slope, four bench-type tombs are cut into rock. One has a repository.** The description locates the group relative to the settlement. It supplies no measured individual tomb positions, entrance directions, dimensions, shaft depths, connecting channels or internal plan. The repository belongs to one member of the group, whose individual identity is unstated.

**On the saddle east of the village core stands a roofed, plastered reservoir, 16.5 m long; its width is unclear.** The authors call its foundations ancient without specifying a period or dating deposit. They do not connect it to a tomb. The p. 28* summary lists a reservoir and burial caves without adding those directional or structural details.

Other site features include steps north of the road, interpreted as part of the Roman road; a large terrace beside the wadi west of the road; and building remains to the northwest, tentatively interpreted as a fort. None supplies a published connection between the four western tombs and a water channel. These are source interpretations of separate remains, with no inferred composite structure here.

The printed reference point is `16085 14360` under the source's Israel-grid label; it also prints abbreviated `UTM 69995 52990`. These are settlement references. The page does not specify the UTM zone, full coordinate expansion, geodetic datum or a tomb survey station. No coordinate conversion, tomb point or bearing was generated. Source metadata gives an 18-dunam site extent, elevation 600 m, and a 400 m distance to perennial water without naming a water point or path convention in this entry.

Site ceramics comprise 322 sherds, assigned to Iron II, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine and later periods. These are settlement-survey collections, with no stated tomb or reservoir assemblage. They cannot date the tomb cuts, reservoir foundations, roof or plaster. The structured extraction retains the publication's original period categories separately from feature chronology.

## Illustration check and observation ancestry

Site 143 occupies text on p. 142. Its full entry ends before site 144. Adjacent pp. 141-144 contain no site-143 plan or tomb photograph. The illustration on p. 144 is explicitly site-146 pottery. Site 28 on p. 49 supplies an aerial view of the village core rather than a measured tomb drawing. This bounded inspection does not establish that an uncited drawing cannot occur elsewhere in the volume or in unpublished field records.

The four tombs, repository and eastern reservoir repeat the field observations already transmitted by [IAA online record 9199](https://survey.iaa.org.il/#/MapSurvey/65/site/9199). Reading their original publication supplies provenance and resolves the page-access uncertainty; it does not create another archaeological witness. Site 28 and site 143 are parallel survey descriptions of the same settlement, with different collected assemblages and stated areas (15? versus 18 dunams). They should not be deduplicated as two independently observed tomb groups. Site 28 adds no tomb description.

## Exposure and limitations

The evidence was selected using existing Entry 40 notes, so every extraction here is exploratory. No untouched tomb observation was registered or used as an unseen prediction.

An initial broad repository search returned snippets from existing Kh. 'Almit extraction notes. Those were not used for a prediction comparison, and no 'Almit page in this book was opened. During English-pagination location, p. 27* (viewer 530) and p. 37* (viewer 520) were incidentally displayed. Page 37* includes short entries for Kh. Those printed observations are now exposed to this worker. They were neither scored nor added to an inventory. This notice prevents treating these pages as unseen observations later. No reserved gorge or cave-record file was opened.

Front photographs and unrelated pottery drawings were also encountered while locating pages. Their retrieval and rights status appears in [figures_access.csv](figures_access.csv). The copyright page says all rights reserved, IAA, 1993; no publication licence or separate permission for these images was supplied. The full PDF and page images remain outside the public repository. The ledger provides exact retrieval pointers instead of reproductions. It makes no assertion about rights on unrelated IAA publications.
