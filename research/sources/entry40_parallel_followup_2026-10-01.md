# Entry 40: Upper Beth Horon survey and regional tomb follow-up

Research date: 2026-10-01. Project-authored findings from public sources and local files. Scope: original Upper Beth Horon site 143, tomb geometry and drainage comparisons. No candidate, coordinate, log, mirrored-note or registration edits were made.

## Result

The original 1993 site-143 pages and any associated tomb plans remain unread. Printed p. 142 now has a traceable supporting citation in Hawari 2004, p. 254, but this does **not** verify the original page itself. The newly accessed 1997 survey provides the cross-reference recorded in §2, without resolving the missing original drawings.

The most useful new architectural comparison is Beit Iksa Tomb 101: a published plan and report document a burial courtyard with drainage. Its west-facing opening is a single loculus mouth, not a demonstrated west-facing entrance to a multiroom chamber. This establishes a regional tomb-and-drainage combination, without identifying the Copper Scroll feature or improving the geographical case for Upper Beth Horon.

The evidence still does not justify moving or reranking either Beth Horon candidate. A western district or slope, a west-wall burial recess, a south-facing external entrance, and a channel attached to an eastern reservoir are different observations.

## 1. Original Upper Beth Horon: what is now located

### 1993 chapter and page locator

The bibliography of Aharonovich's primary Beit Iksa report supplies the chapter containing site 143:

Feldstein, A., Kidron, G., Hanin, N., Kamaisky, Y., and Eitam, D. 1993. Southern part of the Maps of Ramallah and el-Bireh and northern part of the Map of ‘Ein Kerem (Sites 141–321). In I. Finkelstein and Y. Magen, eds., *Archaeological Survey of the Hill Country of Benjamin*. Jerusalem. Hebrew pp. **133–264**; English summary pp. **28*–47***.

This is a more specific retrieval target than the whole volume. Site 143 belongs to this chapter. Hawari 2004, printed p. **254** as labelled in indexed text, cites the 1993 survey at **49, 142, 16*, 28*, sites 28 and 143**. The grouped citation supports retaining p. 142/28* as a locator; it does not expose the original description, assign every feature to one page, or prove the presence of a tomb illustration.

Access limits: the author's Academia volume entry exposes a one-page item with no available preview; the author's book page supplies a cover, not a readable volume. The NLI catalogue request returned 403. No full 1993 scan was obtained. The original p. 142, p. 28*, illustration numbers and possible tomb plans therefore remain **unverified**.

### IAA record 9199: revalidation, not independent evidence

The public survey API was successfully read anonymously using its JSON content header. Record 9199 identifies Upper Beth Horon with original number **143**; the online map's displayed site number is **48**, not a replacement for the original numbering.

Its description retains four bench tombs west of the settlement core, a repository in one, possible burial caves south of the core, and a separate roofed/plastered pool on the eastern saddle, 16.5 m long. It does not give a tomb entrance bearing, measured tomb plan, tomb-specific pottery association or construction date. The returned record contained no archaeological plan image. This repeats the existing source, rather than independently corroborating its field observations.

**Project inference:** “west of the core” locates a group; it cannot supply the direction an entrance faces. The eastern pool cannot be joined to a western tomb without a documented connection. Site-wide ceramics remain settlement evidence, not a date for these tombs.

## 3. Upper Beth Horon reservoir channels: separate feature

Hawari 2004 reports a measured architectural survey conducted in September 1998 and 1999. Indexed text labelled printed **253** describes an eastern reservoir below the road, supplied by rainwater-collection channels. This is a site-specific channel observation, but not a tomb channel. Equating this reservoir with the 1993 eastern pool is possible, **not demonstrated**.

The indexed caption for **Fig. 3**, printed **252**, identifies a village plan of medieval/Ottoman buildings; no image of it was inspected here. Printed **255** points to **PAM File 775**, Baramki, with an unresolved date discrepancy: narrative November 1937 versus citation 16.11.48. The archival original was not accessed; the cited passage concerns reservoir, walls and foundations, not a tomb plan. Hawari is primary for his architectural survey, secondary for the 1993 and Baramki material. Access here was indexed article text with embedded page labels, not visually verified page images.

## 4. Specific regional tomb comparisons

### Beit Iksa, Tomb 101 — substantive drainage comparison

Aharonovich 2018, *Hadashot Arkheologiyot* **130**, unpaginated online report; excavation September 2011, licence **15-3-2011**. **Fig. 7**, plan and sections, was visually inspected; Fig. 8's caption identifies a northwest-looking photograph, but that photograph was not inspected.

**Reported remains:** courtyard L101, 2.1 × 2.3 m, maximum depth 1.45 m; eastern-wall loculus L107, 2 × 0.7 × 0.8 m; unfinished northern-wall cavity. A southeastern oval perforation feeds drainage channel L109, 2 m long, 0.18–0.37 m wide, maximum height 0.83 m. The excavator interprets it as preventing rainwater flooding. Courtyard pottery is first-century CE, not a sealed construction date.

**Project plan inference:** the north arrow makes the L107 mouth west-facing and the northern cavity mouth south-facing; L109 extends southeast. These are plan-derived directions, not published measured azimuths. L107 is a loculus, not an external multiroom-chamber entrance. Dashed section contours do not establish another underground passage.

No sixteen-cubit feature is established. The separately described late Byzantine–Umayyad winepress channel must not supply this tomb's date. This is a comparison, not a proposed Entry 40 candidate.

### Khirbat ‘Addasa — explicit entrance direction

Baruch and Ganor 2008, *Hadashot Arkheologiyot* **120**, unpaginated; January 2005 documentation after robbery, permit **A-4438**. The cave was documented, not excavated. **Fig. 1**, original plan, was visually inspected.

**Reported remains:** southern courtyard, entrance in its northern wall, chamber 2.5 × 3 m, standing pit, four completed kokhim—two west, one north, one east—and an unfinished eastern recess. **Project inference from text and north arrow:** the exterior entrance faces south; west-wall kokhim do not constitute a western exterior entrance. Dashed plan outlines are not treated as fully exposed boundaries.

Pottery, glass and ossuary evidence supports first–second-century CE use; lamps support use after 70 CE. The robbed context does not seal a construction date. This tomb report gives no drainage channel. A channel associated with a cistern in a separate 2015 report cannot be transferred to this tomb.

**Citation repair:** a hyperlink in the 2015 report led to report 2316, a 2013 terrace report, instead of the 2008 tomb report. The correct original is **report 752**, linked below. The misdirected DOI is not used as this tomb's citation.

### Tel Shilat — axis is not an entrance bearing

Tendler 2014, *Hadashot Arkheologiyot* **126**, unpaginated; excavation December 2013, permit **A-6961**. **Fig. 7**, original plan, was visually inspected. Two rectangular tomb cuts run north–south; clearance reached 0.6/0.7 m and exposed an arcosolium in the west wall of the western tomb. The inspected evidence supplies neither an exterior entrance bearing nor a direct tomb date. Nearby Persian/Hellenistic ceramics and site chronology cannot fill those gaps. Stop condition: adequate evidence for a limited geometric comparison, without an Entry 40 identification.

## 5. Horite / Beit Guvrin alternative

No newly inspected primary Horite plan or original 1958 page image was obtained. The existing notes' source-specific distinction remains necessary: Jeremias's northern Beit Guvrin shaft-grave group is not automatically the Maresha northern cemetery adjoining Bet Guvrin's southern cemetery. Their district labels cannot supply an entrance direction, and one cemetery's trough or shaft cannot be transferred to another.

A targeted search for Jeremias, *Moreseth-Gath, die Heimat des Propheten Micha*, *Palästinajahrbuch* **29** (1933), **42–53**, located bibliographic references but no readable original. This is an unfulfilled source lead, not new architectural evidence. Previously blocked lending/publisher routes were not reopened. The existing 1958 OCR conflicts in depth and trough length remain unresolved; no preferred number is manufactured here.

## Remaining discriminating evidence

The decisive missing source remains the **original 1993 site-143 description and adjacent figure/caption coverage**, especially Hebrew p. 142 and English p. 28*. A measured plan must identify an actual tomb opening and its relationship to any channel; a settlement pottery table cannot substitute. The 1971 Lower Beth Horon p. 24 is a precise secondary retrieval target, but its cave must first be distinguished from the already checked Peleg monument. Neither lead currently warrants a coordinate or candidate change.
