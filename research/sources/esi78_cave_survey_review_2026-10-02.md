## Page-specific findings

**Wadi Qilt, printed pp. 92–93 / PDF pp. 103–104.** Cave 20 lies high in an inward-sloping cliff. Its reported dimensions are about 8 m long and 2.5 m wide; a white-lime-plastered cistern occupies its west end. Part of the thin rock at the entrance has collapsed, enlarging the opening. Fallen stones partly cover an approximately 3.2 m forked tree trunk with axe-trimmed branch ends. The proposed former climbing aid is the authors' interpretation.

The continuation on p. 93 reports jug fragments and an almost intact round lamp assigned to the second century CE from Cave 20. These support period-compatible cave use. The article suggests possible association with the Bar Kokhba revolt; no event-specific dating evidence establishes that association. It gives no numbered sealed lamp context or construction contact dating the cistern, entrance, collapse or proposed aqueduct wall. A similar cistern in Cave 1 had yielded Byzantine pottery in the preceding season. Architectural similarity therefore cannot supply a shared construction date.

P. 93 attributes five of twenty numbered caves to the Roman period, with the other caves used for monastic retreat. Cave 14, above a burial complex, yielded Roman–Byzantine pottery and newspaper fragments interpreted as evidence for monastic habitation into the early twentieth century. These are published use/assemblage assessments, not dated construction sequences.

Complexes 13, 16 and 18 are described as monastic cells of Choziba, 1.5–2 km from the monastery. A Roman iron arrowhead was found on the path from the monastery toward Jericho. The path find has no stated stratigraphic link to the opposite-monastery wall.

**Illustration identity and report limits.** The cave-survey article has no Wadi Qilt wall plan, outlet section or measured ground datum in pp. 92–95. Fig. 78 at the top of p. 92 belongs to the preceding **Ramot burial-cave** report. Fig. 79 on p. 93 shows a gilded clay disk from Cave 22 in the **Na'aran–Jericho cliff** subsection. Fig. 80 on p. 95 shows a leather sandal from **lower Wadi Makkuk**. None can be used as a Wadi Qilt culvert drawing.

**Lower Wadi Makkuk, pp. 94–95.** Its separate cave numbering and architectural descriptions must remain separate from Wadi Qilt. Cave 1's rock column and two descending natural burial tunnels are not evidence about Wadi Qilt Cave 1 or Twin Cave. The niche near lower-Makkuk Cave 1 has a reported blocking wall, deposit and sandal in a fissure; the authors propose Roman sealing while also considering an originally connected cave and subsequent collapse. Preserve those alternative interpretations. The sandal comparison and inferred concealment do not independently date the proposed Choziba wall.

The survey article ends in the upper part of p. 95 after Fig. 80 and its author credit. The following heading begins **Hirschfeld's separate 1987 monastery survey**. Its Pharan discussion concerns upper Wadi Far'ah, not the wall opposite Choziba. This boundary was inspected to prevent transferring the following survey's plans to the cave report; the remaining monastery article was not studied in full.
