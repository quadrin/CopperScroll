## Entry 49: the original outlet drawing

Guthe plate II is titled “Ausfluss des Siloahkanals.” It depicts the tunnel-mouth Siloam pool, an adjacent buried basin GHJK and a broad channel running beside that basin. The section distinguishes the basin from the channel. Printed pp. 65–68 describe GHJK as approximately 9 m long and 2.70–3.20 m wide, with red cement lining; the adjoining channel is approximately 1 m wide. Page 67 identifies later masonry inserted into a gap in the basin's older western wall.

**Inference:** this supplies a source-plan-relative hydraulic comparison at the tunnel outlet. It does not establish that the channel corresponds to entry 49's trough/gutter, nor supply a secure calendar date in the checked pages. Keep it separate from the small grooved tank in Bliss–Dickie pp. 134–135, which lies in the southern drain-exit sector. No modern registered footprint or geographic coordinates were calculated.

## Later outlet excavation: dating remains limited

The 2022 report documents L408, a short rock-hewn drainage-channel segment south of Siloam near the Tyropoeon outlet (exposed length 3 m, width 0.80–0.85 m; Fig. 7). The excavators probably associate it with the previously recorded municipal drainage channel, but explicitly report no finds that date this segment.

W50 delimits part of the separate stepped pool L124, assigned to the late Second Temple period (2014 report and later references). W40/W51 probably formed a dam; diagnostic dating evidence remains uncertain, and the report records sampling of bonding material for radiocarbon work without publishing a result.

Neither L408 nor W50 identifies the small grooved tank. Their locations, proposed functions and dating evidence must remain separate. Entry 49's exact feature stays unresolved.

## Entry 40: Beth Horon routes

The existing [entry 40 review](../sites/entry40_bethhoron_review.md) supplies the textual alternatives: Horon/Horites, sea/west versus south, and channel/gutter versus other senses of זרב. The burial setting and sixteen-cubit instruction remain necessary feature constraints.

Tepper–Tepper pp. 101–105 describe stepped routes in the Beth Horon region. Page 102 reports a stepped segment near Beit ʿUr el-Foqa under 2 m wide, slightly separated from Roman paving; it also describes a roughly 600 m stepped route, up to about 2 m wide, in modern Nahal Sanhedrin. Pages 102–103 distinguish proposed earlier pilgrimage paths from later imperial Roman road construction. Page 104 records additional stepped approaches near Khirbet Harmasha, Khirbet Titura and Kafr Rut. The authors interpret their chronology and pilgrimage function; those interpretations are not a fresh stratigraphic date for every step.

**Result:** these are approach-route observations. The checked passages provide no burial chamber, channel adjoining a tomb, matched water source or sixteen-cubit feature for entry 40. No specific candidate emerged that could be dated through an excavation report. The existing lower-village tomb report remains prior evidence, without a demonstrated relation to these road segments. Upper Beth Horon, Lower Beth Horon, Beit Guvrin and Naṭuf alternatives retain their existing treatment.

## Jericho approaches

Pages 151–153 describe Adummim and Tariq el-Hindi approaches, with stepped sections and later Roman road reuse. Figures 55–56 show the Adummim route. These are route-comparison leads for the Jericho group; they establish no Doq drying-floor or entry 32 outlet-wall correspondence. The authors acknowledge missing evidence for reconstructing some Jordan Valley ascents.
