## Exact-page findings

- Kh. Hamad, printed 285 / PDF 304: cisterns, reservoir, presses and burial caves are reported alongside a dressed-stone building. Its chapel identification comes from earlier travelers; water installations have no individual dates or dimensions here.
- Kh. Tannura, printed 294 / PDF 313: deep plastered reservoirs on the north slope, cisterns, a south-slope loculi tomb and rock-cut olive press, eastern wine press and square building. Dating is a mixed surface chronology, not construction dating.
- Qarawat Bani Hasan, printed 299 / PDF 318: northern building approximately 10 × 10 m, surviving 4–5 m, tentatively Herodian. A pool with pilasters is a DOA-file report. Dar's excavated Abbasid mosque and earlier structure have distinct evidence ancestry.
- el-Wili Shabbuni, printed 308 / PDF 327: building 24 × 30.5 m, central portion 24 × 19.5 m, western entrance, southern halls, cisterns and burial cave with cut forecourt. The monastery function is the authors' inference.
- Meiyita, printed 318 / PDF 337: church apse 3.5 m wide reused in a later agricultural tower; column rows 4.45 m apart. Reuse and architectural measurements are separate from proposed historical names.
- Jaryut, printed 325 / PDF 344: a slab-roofed spring tunnel leads to a plastered reservoir. Nearby vaulted building and opposite-wadi settlement are described, but dimensions and construction phases are absent.
- el-Latatin, printed 345 / PDF 364: courtyard cistern and a rock-cut reservoir approximately 100 m east. To Ennaton is a proposed road-station identification; DOA ossuary fragments belong to a derivative cave report.
- Ras Alam, printed 372 / PDF 391: southeast rock-cut reservoir 7 × 11 m; subterranean ashlar vaults with cut-rock lower portion and basins. Crypt and monastery labels remain interpretations.
- Hablata, printed 384 / PDF 403: nine shallow circular cuttings approximately 2 m across and 5 cm deep, each channel-connected to a 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.3 m basin; square installations retain mosaics. The authors call them probably wine presses. The IAA photograph is Fig. 8.131, printed 385 / PDF 404.
- Bir Zeit ruin, printed 417 / PDF 436: medieval building 25 × 34 m, vaulted rooms around a courtyard on three sides, walls 1.5–2.2 m thick. The assigned Iron and medieval distributions are survey interpretations.
- Kurqush, printed 439 / PDF 458: decorated rock-cut tombs and quarries about 300 m east of the ruin. Figs. 8.158–159, printed 441 / PDF 460, show a cut courtyard and facade; contents, measured plan and stratigraphic dates are absent.
- Kurnet Bir et-Tell, printed 447 / PDF 466: surrounding wall 1.4 m thick and surviving 1.5 m; ten southern-cliff installations labeled Iron Age olive presses. Fig. 8.162, printed 449 / PDF 468, shows the cuttings. Their dating basis is not developed here.
- Cemetery 16-16/61/02, printed 465 / PDF 484: five shaft tombs, possible additional depressions, no pottery; Intermediate Bronze assignment explicitly tentative.
- 16-16/76/02, printed 478 / PDF 497: southern wall 1.7–2 m thick traced about 100 m, upper collapse downslope and a lower wall. Fortification is tentative. The two neighbouring sites were separated because intervening remains are sparse and assemblages differ.

## Coverage and interpretation limits

Ein Arik (printed 330), Ramallah (353) and Haris (454) lack proper survey coverage. Tira (353) was restricted by a school compound; Beituniya (347) was only partly surveyed; vegetation blocked collection at Kufriya (349). Several other entries reclassify Porat or Kallai collections instead of reporting a new visit. These are coverage limitations, not evidence of ancient absence.

The source explicitly permits transport of early sherds between neighbouring sites at el-Janiya (314), Tibna/Balluta (367–371), and 16-16/35/02 (446). It warns that a 20 × 20 m fence need not date with nearby Middle Bronze pottery (336), and that Islamic pottery is underrepresented at Matwi (436). Burak's area describes the Byzantine ruin rather than the adjacent Iron ceramic spread (433). Najjara's catalogue area, clearance heaps and pottery distribution use different denominators (469). These cautions prevent simple comparisons of sherd percentages or site sizes as occupation intensity.

Function labels such as fort, monastery, crypt, farmhouse and wine press are preserved as published interpretations. Medieval sacred-tomb traditions and proposed biblical or classical names are separated from field observations and inscriptions. No identification score, regional rarity inference or treasure/deposit claim was made.
