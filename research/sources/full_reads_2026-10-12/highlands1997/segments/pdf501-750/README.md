## Findings that require care

Many cisterns, burial caves, presses, towers and terrace walls lack individual construction dates. Surface pottery and a site's overall period table cannot be assigned automatically to each feature. Burial material reported in robbery spoil is distinguished from intact tomb assemblages.

Useful measured features include Ein es-Sawiya's vaulted spring passage (printed 637/PDF 672), Beit el-Khirbeh's 23×20 m building with 1.5 m walls (685/720), and Shurrab's 25×15 m central structure (712/747). The water features and buildings retain the authors' dating and functional limits. Relevant cemetery entries include round shaft-tomb groups at 17-17/51/01 (689/724), fifteen robbed shafts/caves at 17-17/76/01 (707/742), and disturbed Middle Bronze vessels with bones near agricultural towers at 17-17/52/01 (690/725).

The early-bronze table at printed 668/PDF 703 separates 61 Early BronzeI and 74 Early BronzeII–III sherds, while the following narrative says the latter figure includes both categories. This inconsistency is retained as unresolved; the counts should not be pooled without checking the publication or underlying records.

Entries for villages or ruins not surveyed by the authors' team—including Makhna et-Tahta, 'Awarta and Rujeib—derive chronology or size from earlier publications or documents. Those rows explicitly preserve the secondary ancestry. Historical name proposals and burial traditions are author reports, separate from observed architecture. Cited earlier studies and plans were not independently fetched during this segment.
