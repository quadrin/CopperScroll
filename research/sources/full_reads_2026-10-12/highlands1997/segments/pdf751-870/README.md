## Findings and limits

The catalogue provides many measured buildings, courts, cisterns, caves, shaft cemeteries, terraces and reused masonry. Most associated pottery is mixed surface material. These entries support descriptive comparison and further source retrieval; they generally do not establish a feature's construction date or a sealed use assemblage.

Several records require particular care:

- PDF 765 / printed 730, site 18-15/12/01: an Intermediate Bronze scatter is distinguished from pottery beside a 20×10 m building, where only later periods were found. Two cisterns lie southeast. The earlier scatter does not date the building or cisterns.
- PDF 770 / printed 735, Samiya: repeated visits yielded no pottery. Occupation periods and burial-cave reports derive from earlier investigators and Mandate records. A Justinianic inscription's provenance is uncertain between Samiya and a church at Marjama's western foot.
- PDF 779 / printed 744, el-Qasr: the catalogue describes a 16.8×13.7 m building, a northern entrance into a vaulted space beneath it, and a plastered wall with two slab-covered arches. Recorded dimensions and relationships remain descriptive; no separate construction phase or secure function is supplied for each feature.
- PDF 789 / printed 754, Bir el-Huwar: eight cisterns, some in use, are recorded. The visit field is blank and staffing/time fields are zero; the ledger does not invent a visit.
- PDF 813 / printed 778, site 18-16/25/03: the catalogue explicitly leaves openings ambiguous between cisterns and shaft tombs. That alternative remains unresolved.
- PDF 819 / printed 784, site 18-16/41/02: the author cannot securely estimate the area or choose between shepherd activity near Ein Duma and permanent settlement. A stone heap is only a possible building.
- PDF 828–829 / printed 793–794, Muntar: the table's MB sherd-count field is blank. MB pottery is separately attributed to Spanier (1992), while the counted assemblage contains Byzantine/Umayyad and unidentified material.
- PDF 829 / printed 794, Sheikh Mazar: the count of 102 EB II–III sherds includes EB I. Those rows cannot be added as independent totals. The questionable Mamluk material is interpreted as shrine activity.
- PDF 834 / printed 799, Tuweiyil: reservoir construction damaged the site after Porat's earlier survey. This is a reported later disturbance, not a dated ancient reservoir.
- PDF 837 / printed 802, site 18-16/78/01: the catalogue explicitly doubts whether buildings and pottery are contemporary. Nearby modern and other pens remain distinct.
- PDF 848–849 / printed 813–814, Kafr Beita and Beit Furik: the former was not checked by the team and the latter is described as never surveyed. Literary occupation fields and blank sherd counts remain distinct from a fresh field assemblage.
- PDF 853 / printed 818, Aqraba: the team did not survey the village. Eleven Islamic sherds from an earlier total of 28 were rechecked; descriptions of fort, church reuse, columns and inscriptions come from named earlier sources.
- PDF 856 / printed 821, Kurum: a questioned Crusader/Ayyubid sherd appears in the table, while the author explicitly denies occupation in that period. The row is not an accepted settlement phase.
- PDF 858 / printed 823, Ghannam: fort function is possible; both MB and Persian identifications are questioned. The single MB sherd may derive from the neighboring site.
- PDF 862 / printed 827, Risa and Nabi Nun: Risa's fort description is inherited from Porat, with rechecked pottery but blank counts. Nabi Nun's possible Iron Age high-place function is an inference; the inscription dated 1358 AD is an archival attribution.
- PDF 866 / printed 831, site 18-17/44/01: native inspection recovered the description lost in OCR—a courtyard, building remains beneath a heap and a cistern. The surface table contains IA I 19 and IA I–II 3; the cistern itself remains undated.
