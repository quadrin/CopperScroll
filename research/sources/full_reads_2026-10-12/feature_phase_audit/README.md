# Applied feature-phase and relationship audit

This exploratory overlay applies the whole-book observations to particular cave, tomb and water claims. It supplies separate construction, use, fill and alteration fields, preserves the physical relationship actually reported, and names the observation needed to resolve each remaining gap. It is an additive evidence join; it does not rewrite the frozen regional scores.

## Applied consequences

- **Herodium B101 (FP20):** the existing pool unit `G-LHERODIUM-POOL` has source-attributed channel supply and Herodian period eligibility. Netzer 1981 printed 11/viewer 19 explicitly leaves the supply-channel/pool-inlet connection undiscovered; printed 70/viewer 78 transfers chronology typologically from other buildings. Preserve the period label while qualifying its construction basis and the unlocated inlet. A channel-feed code is not an observed same-phase aperture. The closed entry-29 claim requires its registered new section before reopening.
- **Atara (FP05):** join the exact site-176 pool description to `ASI-83/2/34-reservoir_pool`, whose current row is an aggregate of two reservoirs and is already undated. The source adds western/eastern dimensions and a bottom conduit, but no junction chronology. Keep the aggregate distinct from an individual feature; retain unknown construction and conduit/wall/floor contemporaneity.
- **Upper Beth-Horon (FP01–02):** the eastern covered pool and western bench-tomb group have descriptive locators. The pool's ancient-foundation label does not establish construction within the scroll's window. West of the village does not establish a west-facing tomb entrance; one reported repository and a separate count of cisterns do not establish one tomb–cistern complex.
- **Absalom (FP17):** Kloner–Zissu printed 142/viewer 156 differentiates original southern access from the later western breach. Attach orientation to the corresponding access event. Stylistic dating and the traditional monument name remain separate from construction context and ancient name attestation.
- **Ein Fara/Pharan (FP07):** separate later/modern stairs, Byzantine foundations, initial cavity/cistern cutting and water-collection operation. Benjamin and Hirschfeld partly transmit shared Hirschfeld/Bagatti observations; their descriptions do not supply independent confirmations of ancient access. Existing Almit survey facts (FP08) receive the same separation and remain exploratory, with no protected-source scoring.
- **Local phase controls:** Jaba's lamp belongs to a separate tomb from the converted collecting pit (FP06); Herodium B40/B58 was built above fill over B52's original floor (FP21); Rock of Crosses lining pottery attaches to lining rather than initial cave cutting (FP14). These are specific prohibitions on date/datum transfer, not blanket exclusions of the sites.
- **Function controls:** Qalandiya retains bath/winepress alternatives (FP04); Tana's stepped cistern lacks a bath-function argument (FP12); Herodium C11/2 retains frigidarium/ritual-bath alternatives (FP22). The archaeological label does not choose the textual meaning of entry 46.

## Evidence join and boundaries

Baseline: CopperScroll PR34 head `09e045de120c4954fc0bd2ab14f475d9106e6de4`. The source reading CSVs, coverage ledgers, figure registers and source manifests are the inspected evidence. No new page image, archived arrival, manuscript close-up or original excavation publication was opened in this audit. In particular, Dalman plate25/viewer116 fine detail stays reserved; FP25 uses already-read ordinary plates22–23 only. Missing source leaves are excluded.

Each source reference preserves its existing observation row verbatim, a canonical row hash, exact selected viewer pages, the source's printed locator, and the corresponding page/figure access records. These are project-authored reading records. Source construction labels, measured remains, published reconstruction and this audit's inference are kept in separate fields. The original source PDF hash identifies the supplied scan; it does not imply new inspection or public reproduction permission.

The overlay keeps unknown dates as unknown, including unresolved original use, fill and alteration events. It does not convert surface pottery, cemetery activity, floor deposits, robbery spoil, lining ceramics, a photographed state or a monastery foundation date into an installation construction date. No numeric cubit, coordinate, dimension tolerance or candidate ranking is introduced. Printed grid coordinates remain the source's unconverted locators.

The terminal assessment for any requested construction or simultaneous relationship that remains undocumented here is **not identifiable from available evidence**. The row's proposed observation defines what could change that limited assessment. This audit supplies no unseen prediction and makes no confirmatory identification claim.

## Rebuild and verification

`input_manifest.json` pins the source and inventory input bytes and SHA-256 values. Markdown context pins record the inspected baseline; the validator allows the integrator to add links to those context files. The source CSVs/manifests and joined inventory tables must remain byte-identical for validation.

Run from the repository root:

```bash
python3 -I -B research/sources/full_reads_2026-10-12/feature_phase_audit/build_outputs.py
python3 -I -B research/sources/full_reads_2026-10-12/feature_phase_audit/validate.py
```

The builder enriches the authored judgments in `audit.json` from the local pinned source records and regenerates the CSV and input manifest. The validator checks exact source IDs and row hashes, selected page containment, actual read/reviewed coverage, figure access and coverage, missing/reserved-page exclusion, inventory-row identity, CSV/JSON agreement, and specific date-transfer/access-proxy regressions. A pass verifies provenance and data discipline; it does not independently prove the source's archaeological interpretations.

**Obtained** — an applied feature-phase/relationship join, with exact source and inventory links and separate construction, use, fill and alteration fields.
