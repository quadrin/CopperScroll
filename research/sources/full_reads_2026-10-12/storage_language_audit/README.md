## Entry 46: lexical permission and archaeological function

Puech's ancient-text transcription at printed 85/viewer 96 has `באשוח שיבית הכרמ בבואך`, followed by `לסמול וג(א)מות עסר כסף` (X 5–6). The gimel/alef notation is editorial; it is not a new manuscript observation. Lefkovits printed 331/viewer 352 gives the pool reading and spelling alternatives `אשוח`/`אשיח`, and explicitly allows a further reading with taw, `באשית`, a deep pit. The repository's displayed corpus instead has `באשיח` and inserts `חפור` in X 6. These sources therefore require separate reading branches. The old `text/readings.json` note saying all editions agree does not exhaust Lefkovits's alternatives.

Neither inspected edition makes immersion use a necessary or disqualifying meaning. Puech printed 55–56/viewer 66–67 discusses `אשוח` as a pool/basin, comparing Mesha lines 9/23 and Sirach 50:3; printed 103/viewer 114 discusses the plural/dual form at entry 55 and smaller basins that can be entered. Lefkovits printed 394/viewer 415 discusses pool/cistern spellings, and cites `מקוה` alongside `אשיח` in Ben Sira, translating the former as a ritual bath. That contextual comparison supports compatibility with collected water; it does not prove that every miqveh is called an `אשוח`, or that entry 46 demands ritual immersion. The original Mesha/Sirach witnesses were not independently inspected here. Lefkovits prints the Ben Sira locator as 3:3; Puech gives 50:3. The disagreement is retained as printed.

The inherited Milik C70 claim that the word requires a large built reservoir rather than a household cistern remains unverified. Lefkovits printed 191 n. 6 points to DJD printed 244 C70. The indexed DJD file in Drive contains 88 pages of plates/front matter rather than that commentary; no protected manuscript plate was opened. A size, open-air or built-masonry requirement cannot be derived from the two inspected editions. No candidate's dimensions or excavator label supplies the missing lexical premise.

The equally applicable exploratory rules are in [function_rules.json](function_rules.json):

- **Contained-water basin branch:** admit a pool, cistern, bath or other physical water receptacle when evidence places its water-retaining configuration in the tested phase. Immersion, swimming and utilitarian labels do not alone distinguish membership. Generic settlement pottery cannot date that configuration. A press vat is not a demonstrated water receptacle merely because it retains liquid.
- **Storage/service sensitivity:** additionally require evidence of water storage for later withdrawal or supply/service use in the same phase. This is a narrower operational assumption, not the settled meaning of `אשוח`. A supply channel into a bath proves supply to that basin; it does not independently prove that basin supplied other users. Silence about storage gives UNKNOWN. A positive contradiction gives FAIL only for that phase and rule.
- **Deep-pit reading:** keep Lefkovits's alternative separately. A screen limited to water installations cannot evaluate or reject that reading.

For the three selected baths, the native-source audit establishes physical basins, but it supplies no independent ancient storage/service use beyond bath containment. Thus their functional storage/service gate remains UNKNOWN. A later cistern conversion cannot supply an earlier bath's storage function. Ramat Sharet's medieval reservoir and Rogem's later rainwater phase must have their own date/access states. These are applications of a common rule, not new identifications.

Full entry 46 claims also require the Beth ha-Kerem association, a contemporaneous entry origin and orientation, and the selected numerical reading. Lefkovits printed 333/viewer 354 permits distance, depth, or ten small pits; Puech printed 87–88/viewer 98–99 argues for cubits and relative left. `בבואך` does not select the leg of a turning stairway, prove that steps are the relevant entrance, or impose a compass bearing. A bath's eligibility therefore cannot fill the unknown leftward datum or ten-unit relation. Frozen earlier scoring is preserved.

## Herodium: numbered referents and actual scroll vocabulary

The authoritative prior native reading is Netzer 1981 printed 71/viewer 79, Joseph Naveh's discussion and Ill. 99, recorded as N014 in [the observation ledger](../netzer1981/observations.csv). It records two damaged ink labels on bell-shaped jar fragments from B20, with tentative numbered hiding-place interpretations 40 and 35 and the word `מטמר`/root `טמר`. The first proposed interpretation includes earth/mud. Full label spellings, individual uncertain letters, restorations and numeral encoding were not preserved in N014; [lexical_evidence.csv](lexical_evidence.csv) leaves them unresolved. The exact syntax cannot be reconstructed from the English paraphrase.

Under that proposed interpretation the numbered referent is a hiding/storage place. The inscription's carrier is a jar fragment. Those facts do not establish that 40/35 count jars, measure their contents, or identify a numbered physical jar. Nor do they locate any storage place. Grain contents derive from storeroom context; the jar-salt analysis was inconclusive (Netzer printed 134/viewer 141, note III13; prior N020). The B41 unreadable marks in Ill. 98 are a different observation.

[corpus_occurrences.json](corpus_occurrences.json) records a reproducible check of the displayed ETCBC-derived `data/scroll-text.js`. No rendered word or morphologically segmented surface form contains `טמר` or `מטמר`. The five `אשיח`/`אשוח` forms occur in entries 22, 29, 46 and 55, with exact column/line locators. This absence is limited to that derivative transcription and the explicit search; it is not an independent manuscript reading or a claim about every edition variant. Beth-Tamar's `תמר` has tav, and supplies no tet-root match.

The displayed scroll's symbol 40 occurs at I 15 beside `ככר` (entry 5) and III 7 beside `ככ` (entry 12), in quantity clauses. It has no symbol 35 token. Numbered entries 35 and 40 in modern editions are editorial locators, not ancient storage identifiers. Entry 46's `עסר` is part of the disputed unit/pit phrase and `ששין ושנין` gives the silver amount; neither is a label equivalent to the proposed Herodium storage identifier. Number resemblance consequently supplies no historical association, administrative-system identity, deposit date or geographic link.
