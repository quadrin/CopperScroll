# Dalman Institute volume: reading to the final page

Obtained: all 148 supplied PDF pages accounted for,147 fully read. Plate 25/PDF 116 was read only as a caption and an uncalibrated coarse overview; its fine detail remains reserved. There are no other unread or pending supplied pages. Plate 32/PDF 123 is visually read but lacks a visible source caption. Its number follows from its position between plates 31 and 33, not from a printed label.

The source is Christof Hardmeier and Thomas Neumann, eds., *Palästinawissenschaft in Deutschland: das Gustaf-Dalman-Institut Greifswald 1920–1995* (de Gruyter,1995). It is an institutional history and collection guide, not Dalman's 1925 *Hundert deutsche Fliegerbilder aus Palästina*. The corrected full source has 33,946,346 bytes and 148 pages. The earlier local 33,554,432-byte prefix was an incomplete transfer; the Drive original was not damaged. The recovered tail, exact hash and chronology are in `source_manifest.json`.

Hardmeier's foreword occupies PDF 9–14/printedVII–XII. Zobel's institutional history is PDF 15–25/printed 1–11; Männchen's account of Dalman's theological teaching is PDF 27–41/printed 13–27; Willi's library study is PDF 43–74/printed 29–60; Neumann's photographic-collection account is PDF 75–85/printed 61–71. All prose and footnotes were consumed sequentially. The continuation inspected blank pages and all 56 plates individually, including their captions, then read the complete illustration-provenance list at PDF 145 and the advertisement/blank end pages 146–148. `page_coverage.csv` records every supplied page and its actual method.

The book's practical value is provenance and retrieval. The collection description at printed 71/PDF 85 estimates about 7000 originals but only about 2000 labelled/framed items. A missing catalogue label is therefore a metadata limit, not evidence that a landscape feature was absent. The institutional chapters also describe restrictions, dispersed or lost teaching records and later restoration/access work; these explain why the available collection cannot simply be treated as a fully described field archive.

The project-relevant visual records retain source limits:

- Plates 22–23/PDF 113–114 show Wadi Kelt in 1905. The first shows a gorge, vegetation/water and a small masonry structure; the second shows a narrow water surface and rock-wall bands/recesses. Blind factual extraction supplied no measured scale, direction, exact reach, construction date or demonstrated ancient cave function. PDF 145 attributes the glass negatives to an unknown photographer. These images remain descriptive source records, not scored candidate observations.
- Plate 25/PDF 116 is captioned Jericho and the Nabi Musa road, 27 March 1918,7:30. The provenance list connects plates 25–26 to Bavarian Fliegerstaffel 304 (1917/18), glass positives from the Reichsarchiv and Greifswald slide identifiers 745/844. The source names those two slide identifiers collectively for plates 25–26 without explicitly pairing each ID to a plate. The declaration’s single 745 value was an ordering inference, not an authenticated plate-to-slide assignment. Neither is an established original flight/frame number. The coarse view shows broad hills and a road; its 360 px short side (535 × 360 after rotation) is not a calibrated ground resolution. An oblique photograph with unknown footprint cannot support a planar registration or a measured pit/tomb relation. Fine detail remains reserved under committed declaration `e5fada2d3fc08b06509b584e9050c64911c92ea4` at `research/assessments/kohlit_chain/registrations/dalman1995-plate25.declaration.json`.
- Plate 32/PDF 123 has no visible caption. Its domed-mosque form was recorded as a tentative visual identification only; date, photographer and direction remain unknown. No missing caption was reconstructed from the neighbouring sequence.
- Plate 42/PDF 133 shows a dry Birket izzain/Bethesda in an eastward view captioned before 1914, Hentschel. Plate 44/PDF 135 shows partly water-filled Birket ebkia beside the Yemenite colony north of Jerusalem, captioned 1911, Graf. These are photographic states at their caption dates, not measurements or dates of ancient construction.
