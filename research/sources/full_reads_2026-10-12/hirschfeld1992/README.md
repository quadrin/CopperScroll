## Exact-page findings

- **Earlier caves and monastic reuse:** Pharan chambers apparently precede monastic settlement (p 119/PDF 143; fig 56 at p 120/PDF 144). Note 16 at p 255/PDF 279 reports nineteen surveyed caves, mostly Second Temple hiding places, and rejects an older unqualified count of fifty Byzantine monk caves. Masada/Marda reused earlier fortress buildings and cisterns (p 50/PDF 74). Firminus cave refuge and later monastic evidence are separately described (p 54/PDF 78).
- **Water systems:** Heptastomos rectangular cistern 7.5×2.8 m, about 5 m high, estimated at least 100 m³; three-layer lining (p 150/PDF 174; fig 78 at p 153/PDF 177). Ed-Deir's central cistern has successive settling pools and a garden overflow channel (p 153/PDF 177; figs 79–80). Its dammed open pool is approximately 11×15 m, average 2 m deep, estimated 330 m³, with dam 14×1.5×2.5 m (p 159/PDF 183; fig 83).
- **Reservoir estimates:** Theodosius El-Birkeh is described about 800 m from the monastery,30×14×8.5 m, estimated 4000 m³; dating comes from fabric/plaster and the cover is hypothetical (pp 159–162/PDF 183–186; figs 84–85). Martyrius's 20,000 m³ total in p 156/PDF 180 is explicitly called probably exaggerated in note 10, p 276/PDF 300.
- **Wadi Qilt/Choziba:** Earlier blind extraction records the historical outer passage, beam sockets and approximate reconstruction (pp 37–38/PDF 61–62), three tomb caves with one unentered and hearsay reports (p 135/PDF 159), and modern secondary burial (pp 135–138/PDF 159–162; figs 67–69). Resume notes distinguish modern gates (pp 163–167/PDF 187–191), a cell/cave/garden unit (pp 184–185/PDF 208–209; fig 103), and the modern monastery bridge from the separate surviving Cell bridge pillars. Those pillars are about 1 m thick and 3 m preserved across an 8 m gorge gap; arch and timber alternatives remain open (pp 208–209/PDF 232–233; fig 115).
- **Fawwar/Choziba burial functions:** Note 48, p 275/PDF 299, proposes a Choziba bone-filled cave church as a crypt. It describes the Fawwar eastern hall 8.5×14.5 m with two cells, beam sockets, thresholds and in-situ mosaic; burial use is surmised from eastern placement. The note supplies no independent initial construction date.
- **Phase and identity corrections:** Note 19, p 273/PDF 297, dates the surviving Euthymius basilica no earlier than 660 and retracts the author's earlier monastic classification of Umm el-Amed. Note 42, p 257/PDF 281, says ed-Deir's identification as Severianus lacked proof. Note 37, p 278/PDF 302, records complete destruction of Qasr el-Khan and lack of a descriptive record.
