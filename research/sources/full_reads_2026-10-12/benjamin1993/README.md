## What the volume covers

The regional chapters range from northern and southern hill-country sites through the western Jerusalem surroundings, eastern desert/Wadi Makkuk survey and eastern Jerusalem catalogue. They describe settlements, isolated buildings, cemeteries, caves, cisterns, presses, channels, pools and roads. The chapter authors and survey methods differ. Part IV records small desert structures individually, while other sections group installations with settlements; Part II states a 500 m proximity rule. The book does not provide one uniform inventory of individually dated structures.

Introductions describe visibility, access and ceramic limits (printed 21–22, 28, 101, 137–138, 268). Vegetation, cultivation and built village cores affect collection. Hellenistic/early Roman and several later ceramic transitions cannot reliably be separated. Earlier 1967 collections are reexamined and reused; they are not new independent finds. Part IV’s cave survey was not a systematic archaeological cave excavation, and some cave analogies lack confirming pottery (PDF 353–354).

## Exact-page findings

- Upper Beth-Horon’s eastern roofed reservoir is 16.5 m long, with width unclear; its ancient foundations lack a specified construction date. The four west-slope bench tombs remain a group description without individual surveyed geometry (printed 142/PDF 164). The earlier [targeted reading](../../benjamin1993_targeted_2026-10-12/README.md) preserves that question’s scope.
- The additional-installation tables contain a box grave beside a 2.60 m square pool without a shared chronology (printed 123/PDF 145), and a winepress explicitly converted to a cistern (127/149). Coordinate-only rows remain source silence.
- Qalandiya retains competing bath and elaborate-winepress interpretations despite plaster, pipes and rooms (printed 157/PDF 179). The English summary’s shorter bath label does not resolve the alternative. Atara’s two pools and their supply account have dimensions but no controlled construction or junction phases (162/184).
- Reuse matters: a grave becomes a winepress collecting basin at Jaba (178/200); elsewhere tombs, quarries and collecting pits are reused for storage or water. The physical sequence is recorded without inventing an absolute date.
- Khirbet Almit site 496 has west-slope tomb access, a nearby columbarium, cisterns, underground industrial rooms and a possible pool (printed 380–381/PDF 400–401). Mixed settlement pottery does not date every one of those features. Ein Fara site 527 describes crypt access, linked cave rooms, water collection and approach paths (PDF 420–426); courtesy Hirschfeld plans and descriptions share ancestry with the separate monastery volume.

Internal source problems remain visible. Maqatir’s prose denies Byzantine sherds while pottery profiles 6–7 are captioned Byzantine (printed 81/PDF 103). A central-pit measurement at printed 368/PDF 388 is inconsistent with its surrounding cutting. Map labels and several ceramic summaries also retain native-print/OCR distinctions; errors are not silently corrected by archaeological expectation. Native reading corrected numerous OCR substitutions of Iron I for Iron II.

## Source limits and exposure

English PDF 522/printed 35* contains abridged entries 208–214, including cisterns, conduits, caves, presses and a cemetery/ossuary report. It restores some descriptive content, not the complete Hebrew pages or any omitted detail. A bounded alternate-copy check did not recover the full missing pages.

Hebrew printed labels are generally PDF minus 22 through PDF 201 and minus 20 after the gap. Blank/title leaves and the reverse English starred sequence were checked individually. A defaced library stamp on PDF 556 refers to an accompanying map, whose separate availability is unresolved; it supplies no readable map. The English foreword also says complete southern Benjamin maps were not presented.
