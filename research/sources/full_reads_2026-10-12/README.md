## What the reading adds

**Construction and use dates need separate records.** Benjamin and Highlands frequently describe cisterns, tombs, presses and channels beside mixed surface pottery. Some remains were not surveyed at all; their descriptions come from Mandate files or earlier collections. A settlement period does not directly date every cut feature. The records preserve explicit conversions, uncertain functions and transported sherds instead of assigning one date to an entire site.

**Endnotes materially qualify the main prose.** Hirschfeld calls the Martyrius reservoir-capacity estimate probably exaggerated (printed 276/PDF 300, note 10). Fawwar’s eastern hall has recorded dimensions and architectural remains, but its burial function is inferred from placement (275/299, note 48). The surviving Euthymius basilica is dated no earlier than 660 in the note, and Umm el-Amed’s earlier monastery classification is withdrawn (273/297, note 19).

**Herodium supplies a useful language comparison.** Naveh tentatively reads two Hebrew jar labels as numbered hiding places, 40 and 35 (Netzer printed 71/viewer 79, Ill. 99), using מטמר and the root טמר. Grain contents are inferred from context; jar-salt chemistry was inconclusive. This is a comparison for storage language, with no established Copper Scroll association. Pool supply, reconstructed architecture and local excavated contacts also remain separate in the source notes.

**Repeated publications share ancestry.** The Benjamin chapters, Highlands catalogue, earlier survey collections and later online summaries often transmit the same field observations. Bibliography and index entries are retrieval pointers. Neither a second summary nor a newly acquired copy creates independent confirmation. The new synthesis is exploratory; reader-specific extraction procedures do not certify an unused holdout.

## Access gaps and reserved material

Benjamin’s missing Hebrew pages concern the continuation of site 208 and sites 209–213. PDF 522/printed 35* contains abridged English descriptions; it does not restore the missing Hebrew prose or illustrations. The bounded replacement check found no accessible independent complete copy. Kloner–Zissu’s printed illustration list identifies missing Fig. 161 as the tomb 7-49 plan and section at printed 165; an online contents/preview route did not recover that page.

The Dalman source is an intact 148-page Drive file. Its earlier local download was truncated; the full bytes were recovered and validated. This institutional-history volume is distinct from Dalman’s 1925 aerial-photo book. Plate 25’s Jericho/Nabi Musa road caption and collection provenance were read, but its oblique footprint and ground scale are unresolved. Its fine detail remains under the [committed intake declaration](../../assessments/kohlit_chain/registrations/dalman1995-plate25.declaration.json) and [registration protocol](../../assessments/kohlit_chain/registration_protocol.md).

Public reproduction permission was not established for these books. The repository contains original paraphrases, exact printed/viewer locators, source hashes and retrieval/render provenance. PDFs, DjVu, full OCR, scan images, private preparation scripts and model inputs are excluded. Useful figures can be retrieved from the listed source file and exact page without publishing the copyrighted scan.
