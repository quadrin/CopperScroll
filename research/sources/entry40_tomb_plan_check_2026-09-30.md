# Entry 40: tomb-plan access and Lower Beth Horon orientation

30 September 2026 (America/Los_Angeles).

## Upper Beth Horon: site 143

Reopened the IAA Hebrew record for Beit ʿUr el-Foqa, map 83/1, local site 48 = printed Benjamin survey no. 143, online record 9199. The live page repeats the four bench tombs on the slope west of the village core and a repository in one tomb. It reports a separate plastered roofed pool, 16.5 m long, east of the core. The checked record contains no tomb drawing, entrance bearing or tomb dimensions.

The National Library catalogue identifies the 1993 Magen–Finkelstein volume. Searches did not recover a readable digital copy of its site 143 pages or plans. The earlier project's suggested p. 142 remains unverified. No statement is made here that the printed volume actually contains plans for these tombs.

**Result:** site 143 remains a group-level burial lead. Westward slope position does not establish the direction of a tomb entrance. The eastern pool is a separate installation without a published connection to a tomb in this record. The online village coordinate is a site anchor, not a tomb location. Existing site-pottery percentages do not date the individual tombs.

## Lower Beth Horon: Fig. 1 directly inspected

Read Yuval Peleg's 2004 report and directly inspected its Fig. 1, “Burial cave, plan and sections.” This revisits prior excavation evidence; it is not an independent new excavation witness.

The plan's north arrow points toward the right of the image. The external entrance lies at the left, on the southern side of the fore chamber; the rear chamber lies north of it. The two arched niches A/B occupy the west wall. Thus the south-facing entrance is now supported by the plan, replacing the earlier inference from southern-slope location.

The report gives a 0.25 m entrance step, two chambers, fore-chamber height 1.06–1.84 m and rear-chamber height 1.89 m. It identifies no channel and no sixteen-cubit shaft. Pottery in the cave accumulations belongs to the first century BCE–first century CE; this is context evidence from a robbed cave, not a sealed construction deposit.

**Entry constraint:** under a west-facing-entrance model this particular tomb does not fit. Under ברוח, “on the side/direction,” the direction may refer to a different spatial relationship; the plan alone cannot reject the whole Beth Horon identification. A/B's location in a west wall does not establish a west-facing exterior entrance. No exact-feature identification, confidence change or new atlas geometry follows.
