## Meaningful deltas

**Chronological positions need dates attached to their authors.** The extraction reports Magness’s 1995 short abandonment following 9/8 BCE as an earlier interpretation (pp.67–69), superseded by the Mizzi–Magness 2016 continuity model adopted here (pp.69–72). De Vaux’s Ia/Ib/II scheme is first described and then reconsidered. The book’s descriptive attribution of a feature to II therefore cannot automatically become construction in 4–1 BCE. The extraction reports different local upper-floor dates, including coins giving a terminus after the 40s CE in L1–L2 (p.72). The attributed chronology is reconciled in [research/sources/qumran_construction_phase_review_2026-10-02.md](https://github.com/quadrin/CopperScroll/blob/research/resource-sweep-2026-10-14/research/sources/qumran_construction_phase_review_2026-10-02.md), preserving its already-established requirement for component-specific contacts and dates. These are reinterpretations of the same excavation records, not a new stratigraphic campaign.

**L71’s construction history has a documentary sequence.** Extraction-reported pp.76–77 distinguish de Vaux’s 3 March1954 later-channel interpretation from his 1 April1954 observation of earthquake damage, followed by final plans showing L71/channel before that earthquake. Magness says the evidence for repair was not stated. This is a useful original-notebook retrieval specification; it does not independently date the upper gorge or resolve the project’s existing L117 joint test. Likewise, the extraction-reported terracotta drain beneath a L48–L49 step (pp.74–76) is a particular pipe within Magness’s L34 reconstruction, with the proposed supply connection unlocated. It supplies no identification of a scroll cistern beneath steps.

**L120 needs parallel count/readings and separate depositional claims.** Extraction-reported p.228 gives three pots totaling561 coins, with153/185/223; p.233 refers to192 coins in HoardA. The existing `qumran_changes.md` already records Callegher2016 pp.225–229/Fig15.1’s222/186/153 and post-discovery redistribution into153 Jerusalem/408 Amman coins. Preserve these source-specific figures rather than converting them into interchangeable original containers. The extraction’s 9/8 BCE latest coin is a burial terminus after which deposition occurred, not an exact year (pp.59–60,67–72). Her preference for accumulated savings and possible temple tax is interpretation (pp.231–233); the alternative community-wealth explanation and call for fuller publication remain explicit. None independently establishes an emergency burial or a68CE deposit. The supplied Callegher report and these accounts inherit the same1955 discovery, though later archive/object checks may add distinct observations. Do not recode frozen hoard comparisons from this derivative report alone.

**Feshkha and Ghuweir remain separately dated comparators.** The extraction reports a Herodian foundation for each, with Feshkha perhaps after31BCE and its revolt-year2 coin supporting the68CE destruction model (pp.259–260). Ghuweir’s seven-coin sample ends with AgrippaI42CE; that sample cannot establish a pre-revolt abandonment or a shared68CE destruction (pp.260–261). These are Magness’s revisions of de Vaux/Bar-Adon, already directly read in `qumran_changes.md`; they add a later attributed synthesis, not another excavation. Feshkha’s basins/pit steps concern work access and competing industries (pp.255–257), whereas Ghuweir’s north-wall channel lacks a resolved gradient in the original report. Neither supplies the required cave/heap/trench or dated pit-mouth/grave relationship for a scroll identification.

**Later cemetery labels cannot overwrite an uncertain original context.** Extraction-reported pp.205–206 classify Feshkha graves in L2 and about100m northeast as Bedouin, with ornaments/Turkish-coin details. The directly read de Vaux1959 account, summarized in `qumran_changes.md`, describes an isolated northeast grave without objects and explicitly leaves its ancient association uncertain. The existing Hirschfeld2004 note describes yet another south-wall tomb report requiring Donceel’s original catalogue. Keep these descriptions separate until grave IDs, locations and finds align; this sweep has not verified a contradiction between identical graves. At Qumran, Magness’s reported30–40m eastern cemetery separation and marl shaft/loculus graves (pp.200–207) are broad contextual comparisons. They do not establish one named tomb, its date or its required relation to a pit. Ghuweir’s cemetery lies roughly800m north and has no demonstrated settlement connection (pp.258,264).
