## 2. What pp. 425–432 say about the aqueduct's date and phases

The elements discussed are:
- aqueduct/drain **500**: the main channel, and the latest state;
- drain/aqueduct **673**: earlier and more modest, feeding basin L. 173;
- overflow **706** of 500;
- reinforcement **598**;
- the dismantled wall **599**;
- bath **L. 138**, with its overflow outlet 669 and drain 670;
- sluice **658**, feed **700** and basin **650** at the inlet.

### 2a. Statements tied to absolute dates or historical events (all EVIDENCE)

- **p. 425**: in its final form, aqueduct 500 was "une restauration assez grossière, menée après 68". It matches the terminal diversion that goes round L. 77 to the south (Plan période III – Niv. 4).
- **p. 425**: earlier, it was probably "conçu d'une hauteur moindre avant l'abandon qui a suivi 68" (Niv. 3).
- **p. 425**: the north enclosure was "partagé tardivement par un aqueduc encombrant". Before that, the enclosure was laid out more harmoniously.
- **p. 425**: the aqueduct's sides look high in the photographs only because the excavators over-dug the ground around it.
- **p. 432**: in L. 135, the aqueduct 500 drawn on Coüasnon Plans Ib and II is the "lourdement reconstruit, du Niv. 4 (Période III, post 68)" version.
- **p. 432**: "de Vaux a conçu à tort un système hydraulique complexe et ramifié", meaning one present from the start of the settlement.
- **p. 432**: L. 135 in its original state is "assez ancien". It is linked to the buried deposits of L. 130, some of whose pots date from before the turn of the era. Humbert adds that one cannot "trop descendre le développement du système hydraulique", meaning the hydraulic system's development cannot be placed too late. He infers that L. 135 fell out of use gradually or declined fairly fast.
- **pp. 425–432 contain no "31 av. J.-C." and no "séisme".** The only earthquake reference is de Vaux's Journal (2/4/55), quoted on p. 427: "Donc le bain 138 serait antérieur au tremblement de terre".
  - Humbert finds it surprising that de Vaux had the bath abandoned at the end of Period Ib.
  - He rejects the Coüasnon Plan II option that the bath lasted from the start of Period Ib to 68 (p. 427).

### 2b. Relative sequence

- **p. 427 (EVIDENCE)**: the bath was a late installation that depended on the eruvim.
  - Its south-west enclosure wall 656 leans on reinforcement 598, which "scelle le trop-plein 706 de l'aqueduc principal 500".
  - "Le L. 138 ferme la séquence."
  - "Il semble difficile de faire remonter sa fondation avant le Niv. 3C."
  - L. 138 "certainement" stayed in use until 68 (the EN text says "probably").
  - **INFERENCE**: in this argument, the 500/706 system already existed before the bath was founded (Niv. 3C or later). So at least the first state of the channel is older than Niv. 3C.
- **p. 431 (EVIDENCE)**: L. 135 was reworked several times, to reach the ravine, to build bath 138, and "enfin pour fonder l'aqueduc 500". The wall-599 complex was dismantled to build L. 138.
- **p. 432 (EVIDENCE)**: the new water-supply works pushed L. 135 out of its original use.
  - An earlier, lower and more modest transverse drain, **673**, fed only basin L. 173, upstream of cistern 110.
  - Digging the large reservoirs "a nécessité un aqueduc plus ample". This larger aqueduct bypassed the small basin L. 173 and entered through "le mur 512 qui est une reprise de maçonnerie".
  - These works led to bath 138 being built and wall 599 being demolished.
- **Stratigraphic tables** (EVIDENCE; rows aligned by bounding boxes). "Éléments restitués" are reconstructed phases.

- **What the levels mean (EVIDENCE, outside the cited range)**:
  - p. 119: Niv. 3 is continuous occupation from the second half of the 1st c. BCE to 68. 3A = the site adapted; 3B = progressive development; 3C = greatest development in the 1st c. CE. Niv. 4 = restoration after 68.
  - fig. 50, p. 114, Humbert's date column: Niv. 3 Phase B is dated "−30", with "établissement du système hydraulique" (and loci 77, 86, 135). Phase C is "1 à 50", with "compléments du système hydraulique".
  - A "−31" line in that column is aligned with de Vaux's row "Séisme et incendie". Niv. 3A has no date of its own there.
  - **INFERENCE**: on Humbert's scheme, Niv. 3B/3C starts about 30 BCE and later.

### 2c. Inlet and the "decantation basin"

- **p. 427 (EVIDENCE)**:
  - Humbert writes that bath 138 "est incompatible avec un L. 132 bassin de décantation". L. 132 was not a basin. Drain 670, on the floor of L. 132, would have been drowned.
  - He examined outlet 669 himself before the recent restorations. It widens on the L. 132 side and is the overflow of L. 138.
  - At the inlet, runoff water damaged the outlet from sluice 658 in L. 137. Feed 700 was reconstructed "sans vraisemblance" (without plausibility).
  - The bath could only be fed from upstream, probably from basin 650 above sluice 658. Either way, its water came from the channel that starts at the dam in the cliff gorge.
- **Not in the cited range (EVIDENCE)**:
  - p. 387: if there was a decantation basin in the north enclosure, it was L. 173, not L. 132.
  - p. 434: L. 137 "n'a plus de place dans la liste des loci" (it no longer belongs in the list of loci).

### 2d. Kinds of evidence used on pp. 425–432

- **Stratigraphy**: the level tables on pp. 428 and 430 are reconstructions. For L. 138: "Aucune stratification n'est repérable dans le L. 138" (p. 427). EVIDENCE.
- **Construction relationships**: this is the main argument, and it is relative, not absolute (EVIDENCE):
  - 656 leans on 598, which seals 706 (p. 427);
  - mass 668 is caught inside buttress 667, so 668 is older than the bath (pp. 425, 431);
  - wall 599 was built and later dismantled (pp. 425, 431);
  - wall 512 is a later rebuild (p. 432).
- **Coins**: listed in the tables, but none is used to date the aqueduct. The L. 138 coin KhQ 2359 "ne signifie rien" (p. 427). EVIDENCE.
- **Pottery**:
  - Vessels came only from the narrow space north of the bath. De Vaux's attribution of them to an early phase is "arbitraire" (p. 425). Planche 88 is on p. 429.
  - The L. 130 deposit pottery "d'avant le tournant de l'ère" is used for L. 135, not for the aqueduct (p. 432).
- **Plaster and mortar**: outlet 669 was plugged with very hard cement (Journal 2/4/55, p. 427), and the feed pipe was possibly coated with mortar (p. 427). There is no analysis of the aqueduct's plaster layers. EVIDENCE.
- **Architectural parallels**: none on these pages. EVIDENCE.

## 3. Where the volume dates the aqueduct more explicitly (outside pp. 425–432)

All entries below are EVIDENCE.

- **p. 114, fig. 50**: Niv. 3 Phase B (−30) carries "établissement du système hydraulique". This is the only place where the hydraulic system is put on an absolute date scale.
- **p. 119**: defines the levels. "Le tremblement de terre n'a pas eu lieu", i.e. the earthquake did not happen as an event during the occupation. Compare p. 25: "le séisme n'a pas affecté Qumrân avant son abandon".
- **p. 373**: the layout of the aqueduct ended the enclosure's original function. The foundation of 500, "au moins dans son tracé final, serait donc plus récente".
- **p. 375**:
  - The wide drain 500 "est venu tardivement paralyser l'espace", perhaps in its last state at Niv. 4.
  - In the west sector it had already been radically reworked when the large pools L. 91, 56–58 and 71 were filled. This may correspond to the dam.
  - Building the aqueduct belonged to the same project as the large pools: "Nous ne pouvons en préciser la chronologie, Niv. 3A et B."
- **p. 379**, "La chronologie de l'enclos nord":
  - Humbert states that de Vaux's chronology here was locked by the 31 BCE earthquake date.
  - Provisional sequence: (3) building of the north enclosure: Niv. 3A/B?; (4) extension westward with L. 138: Niv. 3A/B; (5) "Établissement de l'aqueduc 500 et abandon de l'enclos : Niv. 3(B/C ?)".
- **pp. 415–419 (L. 132)**:
  - p. 415: de Vaux saw L. 132 as a decantation basin from the first phase, put out of use in Period II in favour of 500. The aqueduct on Plan II is the latest restoration (Niv. 4).
  - p. 417: 500 is the latest restoration, at Niv. 4, "d'un drain plus ancien, Niv. 3B/C (132.2 ?)".
  - p. 419 table: 132.1 = Niv. 4, aqueduct 500. 132.5 = **Niv. 3A/B**: buried deposits, drain 673, overflow 706.
- **pp. 434–435 (L. 136–137; fig. 174 a–d on p. 435)**: the inlet developed in four phases.
  1. Before the dam, runoff was gathered by low walls 701 and 708 into collector 709 and led to drain 673 (later sealed by the Niv. 4 drain 500). p. 434 says "l'aqueduc 673, qui précède sa restauration 500".
  2. With L. 135 and wall 599, collector 707 and sluice 658 were added: "l'élaboration du système hydraulique a commencé ici".
  3. Bath 138 was built; the sluice was widened and the incoming channel doubled.
  4. At Niv. 4 the aqueduct was restored and the new sluice blocked.
- **Inconsistencies inside the volume (EVIDENCE)**:
  - Drain 673 is placed at Niv. 3A/B (p. 419), 3B/C (p. 428) and 3C (p. 430).
  - Bath 138 is placed "not before 3C" (p. 427), at 3B/C (p. 428) and at 3A/B (p. 379).
  - Aqueduct 500 is placed at Niv. 4 (p. 419), 3C–4 (pp. 428, 430) and 3(B/C ?) (p. 379).

## 5. Verdict on the Taylor & Gibson citation

**PARTLY: the pages are on topic, but the specific claim is not on them.**

- **Supported (EVIDENCE)**: pp. 425–432 do discuss the phases of aqueduct 500/673. They treat it as late and multi-phase within the north enclosure, and they reject the idea that the hydraulic system existed from the start (p. 432). This is consistent with **not** placing it in de Vaux's Period Ib.
  - The final form of 500 is post-68 (pp. 425, 432).
  - The bath, which postdates the 500/706 overflow, is "not before Niv. 3C" (p. 427).
- **Not supported on these pages (EVIDENCE)**:
  - No absolute date of about 31 BCE.
  - No earthquake used as a terminus post quem.
  - No coin, pottery or plaster evidence dating the channel.
- **The earthquake gloss (EVIDENCE + INFERENCE)**: "after 31 BCE, i.e. after the earthquake" is **not Humbert's reasoning**. He holds that the earthquake struck Qumran only after it was abandoned (pp. 25, 31, 119, 379).
- **Where "after c. 31/30 BCE" can be derived (INFERENCE)**: it can be derived for the main aqueduct 500 by combining the level assignments with fig. 50 (p. 114: Niv. 3B = −30, "établissement du système hydraulique") and p. 379 ("Niv. 3(B/C ?)"). Even then the volume is not uniform:
  - p. 375 dates the aqueduct's construction only to "Niv. 3A et B";
  - p. 419 puts drain 673 at Niv. 3A/B;
  - in fig. 50, Niv. 3A sits before the "−31" line.
  So the earliest channel could fall before 31 BCE on Humbert's own scheme.
- **Suggested pinpoint** for the claim: Humbert & Chambon 2016, pp. 114 (fig. 50), 373, 375, 379, 417, and 432, plus pp. 434–435 (fig. 174) for the phases. The EN translation has the same printed pages.
- Taylor & Gibson 2020 was not re-read for this check. Their wording is taken from [qumran_construction_phase_review_2026-10-02.md](qumran_construction_phase_review_2026-10-02.md).

## 6. What pp. 425–432 do NOT say

- They give no date of "31 av. J.-C."/31 BCE. The earthquake appears only in de Vaux's Journal quote on p. 427. EVIDENCE.
- They make no comparison with Hasmonean architecture and do not mention Magen–Peleg or de Vaux 1973, p. 8. EVIDENCE.
- They give no absolute date for drain 673, and no coin or sherd from in or under the channel. EVIDENCE.
- They give no sequence of plaster layers and no description of construction joints within the aqueduct itself; only abutment and sealing relations are given. EVIDENCE.
- They do not discuss L. 137 as a decantation basin. L. 137 and the inlet are treated on pp. 434–435. EVIDENCE.
- They do not describe the dam, beyond saying that the bath's water came from the channel that starts at the dam in the cliff gorge (p. 427). EVIDENCE.
