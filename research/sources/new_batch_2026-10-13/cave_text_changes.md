14 October 2026 UTC / 13 October Los Angeles. Lane owner: cave_text_changes. Repository baseline inspected at `CopperScroll/research/ACTIVE_TEST.md`, `regional/caves_entry25/README.md`, cave coding, and source/access records. Root integrator owns repository mutations.

## What changes first

The useful new observation is the 2017 reinspection of a **specific proposed northern second entrance to 11Q**. Humbert reports that the crevasse could not have been a passage (2019 p.52, viewer77). This corrects an earlier author hypothesis. It is an exploratory, source-reported exclusion of that opening model. It does not reject every possible two-mouth history of 11Q or identify entry25. No independent prediction was registered here; the evidence is now exposed.

The same volume supplies a competing account of the cave's masonry. Humbert considers the dividing structures ancient and reconstructs Chalcolithic partitions (pp.63–64, viewers88–89; Fig.6 p.50). Fidanzio et al. observe that part of a wall was rebuilt during the 1956 excavation, then suggest the supports may have been excavation safety structures (pp.82–85, viewers107–110; Figs19–20 p.84). The rebuilding observation and the possible origin of all structures have different certainty. Record the authors' disagreement; do not date the walls by choosing the preferred narrative.

CIIP IV.2's availability adds direct access to inscription3428 and exposes a unit discrepancy: CIIP p.843/viewer97 gives letters **5–10 cm**; the excavators' PEQ2013 p.36/viewer8 gives **5–10 mm**. The original Fig.7 p.38/viewer10 includes a 1cm scale and was visually checked. Retain the discrepancy and use the original excavator measurement with provenance. CIIP also prefers `bt` over `rm` in line3 while acknowledging difficult readings. This is a Burgin comparandum correction, with no new Copper Scroll manuscript reading.

## Evidence and implications

### Cave11Q2019

- **Published observations:** the recognized entrance faces southeast (Humbert pp.46/48, viewers71/73). De Vaux widened its opening for excavation; the reported 1.60m width and 1.35m height therefore do not establish untouched ancient dimensions (p.52/viewer77; appendix pp.73/79, viewers98/104).
- **Specific hypothesis and revision:** a higher crevasse north of the inner chamber had been proposed as another entrance. March2017 reinspection found it could not have been a passage (p.52/viewer77). An adjacent void accessible internally remains separate from an exterior entrance.
- **Pillar relation:** the natural pillar divides the entrance and inner chambers (Massonnat p.67/viewer92, Fig.7 p.70; Humbert Fig.6 p.50/viewer75; 2017 appendix p.82/viewer107, Figs16–18). It is internal. A natural pillar's existence supplies no pair of facade mouths.
- **New stratigraphic documentation:** 2017 OperationB exposed SU203 yellow sediment around0.50m deep over SU204=205 and SU206 (pp.78–81/viewers103–106, Fig.15). Its upper part and the material above had already been removed. This is lower-sequence evidence, not a surviving first-century entrance threshold.
- **Upper chamber:** newly documented internally connected chamber approx2×6m; narrow vertical passage2.5m long; height3.5m decreasing to1.2m northward. Surface inspection found no human activity; hazardous boulders prevented further investigation (pp.85–86/viewers110–111, Figs21–23). This cannot establish an ancient northern exterior entrance.
- **Disputed reconstructions:** Humbert interprets the entrance blockage as deliberate ancient sealing (p.52); the 2017 appendix explains blockage through runoff and sediment accumulation, while allowing human contribution (p.73 and n.7). Their masonry interpretations also differ, as above.
- **Effect on project:** add a source-linked cave morphology/phase note and explicit exclusion of the named northern-crevasse entrance model. Do not replace the frozen regional scores wholesale. No IV/17 or TwinCave ancient threshold, entrance bearing or wall date was obtained.
- **Dependency now made concrete:** the published 2017 plan was derived from a 3D scan; survey by Gabriel Levi/Mabat3D and Alessandro Maifredi, processing by Gilad Lidor and Francesco Pusterla (p.71 and Fig.1 p.72/viewers96–97). Native scan/section data are a possible follow-up source; no outreach authorized or sent in this lane.

### Frumkin2023

Complete chapter text read, printedpp.75–85; viewer2–12; all six figures inspected natively.

- **Published synthesis:** hard-rock escarpment caves and artificial/natural/mixed Lisan caves follow distinct formation histories (pp.79–83). Hard-rock cavities have undergone collapse, fracturing, deposition and human/animal activity; Lisan caves remain subject to fluvial erosion and collapse.
- **Author observation/interpretation:** CavesC/F likely began as piping caves, based partly on his observations during Broshi/Eshel excavations; runoff caused their recent collapse (pp.82–83). SiteA3-013 appears to have developed after anthropogenic cutting concentrated runoff, within roughly the last2000years (p.83). Neither statement dates another cave by association.
- **TwinCave:** p.80 calls it much larger than11Q and sparsely used by humans. It gives no new mouth bearing, threshold datum or dated entrance installation. Earlier archaeological evidence remains necessary.
- **Effect:** improve preservation/detection and ancient-geometry caveats. Modern opening geometry cannot automatically stand for first-century geometry. The chapter provides a specific source dependency, Frumkin2015's detailed Qumran cave surveys, rather than the required IV/17 datum itself.

### Burgin2013 and CIIPIV.2

- **Observed archaeological relationships:** floor-level double entrance and separate stepped shaft were uncovered in a room above ComplexH; finds date the room stratum to the BarKokhba period (PEQ p.33/viewer5). StageIII raised the floor and sealed the earlier double entrance, shaft and dromos. The complex's present two slope openings are separately described, with one about15m west and higher than the other (p.36/viewer8).
- **Inference:** the metre-thick quarry wall probably supported the roof during later building construction; publication uses tentative wording. A shaft predates burrowB13 based on their junction; the earlier rooms' storage use is the excavators' functional interpretation (p.36).
- **Inscription:** graffiti at RoomB7's northern jamb, PEQ pp.36–39, Figs6–7; CIIP3428 pp.843–844/viewers97–98. Both give uncertain personal-name reconstruction; CIIP's preference for `bt` is edition-based. Source photograph/tracing cannot serve as new independent manuscript confirmation.
- **Potential naming trap:** Burgin's **Cave of the Columns**, CaveB, has two columns inside a burial chamber and is dated by architecture/decoration to the Byzantine period (PEQ p.42/viewer14; Fig.9 p.43). A modern pillar-cave name alone supplies no entry25 phase or mouth relation.
- **Effect:** a useful methodological control for access changes and doorway terminology; no direct entry25 candidate promotion. Burgin lies outside the frozen Judean-Desert inventory box. CIIP and PEQ share original observations; do not count them as independent corroboration.

### Midras JAHA11.4

Drive filename says2025; the supplied cover and running headers say issue11–4/2024. Record both rather than silently choosing a publication year. Article begins printedp.86/viewer3.

- **Complex20:** existing cavities were connected by burrows. Bath7's original northern-wall entrance was sealed; its wall was breached. Bath7 measures2.8×1.8m; bath1 measures3.6×3.3m and predates the hiding complex (p.90/viewer7, Fig.5 p.91/viewer8). Finds left by looters include a year2 FirstRevolt coin and later Domitian issues; these date objects, with disturbed collection context.
- **Complex30:** older quarries and cisterns were joined through later hiding passages; an internally supported storage chamber contains two columns (pp.90–91; Fig.6 p.92/viewer9 inspected natively). Neither internal columns nor present access establish two contemporaneous facade entrances.
- **Effect:** phase and function controls for rock-cut facilities. Midras lies outside the frozen desert-cave box. No IV/17 or TwinCave gap closes.

### Abud2017

Hebrew morphology and conclusion checked; not a full article reading claim.

- **Morphology:** approx450m total cave length, a concealed entrance around4m wide and1.5m high, leading into HallA about25×35m (printedp.164/viewer6, natively inspected). Singular entrance description establishes the described opening; it does not prove the absence of other entrances or supply its bearing.
- **Dating dispute resolved narrowly by authors:** FirstRevolt coins and earlier objects permit consideration of earlier refuge use, but the ceramic assemblage can be explained in a BarKokhba context; the authors explicitly leave FirstRevolt refuge uncertain (pp.163–164,172–173/viewers5–6,14–15).
- **Absence caveat:** no human bones/valuable deposits cannot establish safe departure; looting, later activity and survey limits provide other explanations (p.173/viewer15).
- **Effect:** useful chronological/detection comparandum; no direct entry25 datum. Abud's western location lies outside the frozen desert inventory.
