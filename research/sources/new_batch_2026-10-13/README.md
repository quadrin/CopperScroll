# New PDF batch: changes and source coverage

Reviewed 14 October 2026 UTC / 13 October Los Angeles against repository commit 23e2b74. The latest acquisition batch contains 72 PDF file identities; four additional earlier-timestamp cave/epigraphy sources were selected to assess the same questions. [Coverage](coverage.json) records the actual inspection scope for every batch file. Large volumes received targeted reading; selected short articles were read completely. Missing leaves, clipped captures and inaccessible native images remain explicit. Source possession, OCR generation and page rendering do not establish full reading.

## Findings that change the evidence

**A particular proposed second entrance to 11Q is ruled out by the author’s reinspection.** Humbert 2019 p. 52 reports that the northern crevasse previously proposed as a passage could not have served as one. The natural pillar is internal. The same volume preserves disagreement over ancient partitions and possible excavation supports; surviving walls do not have a settled common date. This is an exploratory source correction to that named mouth model, with no registered unseen prediction. [Cave/epigraphy evidence](cave_text_changes.md) retains the alternatives, exact pages and the separate CIIP/Burgin inscription-unit discrepancy. IV/17 and Twin Cave require their own ancient access records.

**Primary reports add concealment contexts and qualify existing records.** Zissu–Ganor 2009 gives separate ʿEthri corner-coin groups, a system-XIV single coin and a chamber-floor-niche single coin. Their identity with the unread 2012 hoard title remains unresolved. The native preliminary Qiryat Sefer article verifies juglet/room and natural-crevice contexts for groups already catalogued; its central-room wording differs from the later catalogue’s doorway-threshold description. These are separately sourced accounts, with no duplicate hoard or transferred concealment date. [Synagogue/coin-context review](synagogue_changes.md), [native capture review](capture_evidence.md), and [observation proposals](ethri_observation_proposals.csv) preserve the distinction. Frozen numerical results were not regenerated.

**New hydraulic sources supply observed contacts and expose unverified joins.** Kh. el-Hamam has a sampled reservoir plaster date and plans; Arumah has an observed feed-channel segment; Deir Sam’an has a surveyed connected reservoir group alongside independently supplied pools. The proposed inter-hill siphons and several aqueduct continuities remain inferred. Amit–Gibson’s author draft distinguishes dated channel segments, later modifications and missing infrastructure; its archival drawings have precise file routes. [Water evidence](water_evidence.md) and [dissertation scope](root_water_scope.md) preserve phase, function and numerical discrepancies. [Dray’s dam review](dray_evidence.md) separates the observed late-antique dam from a port-flushing hypothesis whose terminal outlet is unlocated.

**Construction, use, blockage and excavation reach have separate observations.** Enot Shoim, Huqoq and Maskana retain different construction/reuse branches. Ramat Rahel offers surveyed bath relationships, while its known hoard’s association with hiding modifications remains problematic. Merot retained rear access after some mouths were sealed beneath a building. Wadi Hamam supplies a reported sealed, empty chamber and incompletely excavated alternatives; native figures remain unavailable. [Regional review](regional_changes.md), [remaining-source review](remaining_changes.md) and [Maon native review](maon_evidence.md) give the underlying contexts. These comparisons cannot supply another candidate’s missing datum or support an absence-of-deposit claim beyond actual excavation coverage.
