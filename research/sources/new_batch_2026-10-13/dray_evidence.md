Source: [Drive PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1xF9-MqqOgvAWuUHYx0mhrhI87RNrdHOW/view), Yehoshua (Yeshu) Dray, translated by Miriam Webber and edited by Ilana Gonen, in *Cura Aquarum in Israel II* (2014), printed 81–91 plus blank92. Complete available text, captions, footnotes and bibliography read; all16pages visually screened; Figs6/8/9/12 and associated prose inspected at full page scale. This is a complete individual article plus preliminaries, not a full proceedings reading. Exposure is exploratory.

## What changes

The article supplies dimensions, heights and photographs of a dam-regulated water system. It proposes seasonal port flushing as the low-level aqueduct's function. The port-side outlet and southern terminus remain unidentified; consequently the article enables a testable hydraulic-function hypothesis but does not verify its decisive connection. The dam's late antique phase excludes this installation from an identification requiring pre-Byzantine infrastructure. Its construction dates and functional debate are useful controls for the project's practice of separating surviving masonry from reconstructed purpose.

## Chronology and functional interpretations

Printed82/PDF6 reports archaeological dams dated fourth centuryCE and built in stages, citingSa'id/Ad 2004 andPorath/Gendleman/Arnon 2007. Printed88/PDF12 footnote5 specifies a contact-based limit: dam northwest end blocks tomb cave dated late third/early-fourth centuryCE byAd/Sa'id 2011. The cave date supplies a terminus after that existing burial feature; verify original excavation relationship before reducing to an exact construction year.

Printed84–85/PDF8–9 proposes port-flushing through seasonally opened sluices, with mills as secondary use. Printed88/PDF12 then proposes establishment late fifth/early sixth centuryCE using the literary account of harbour rehabilitation around 500 CE. The harbour account does not name or directly date this dam/aqueduct. Preserve this proposed project date separately from the reported fourth century archaeology and the cave-contact terminus; the article does not reconcile the difference.

The port flushing hypothesis needs: a phase-controlled outlet, connected channel to port, reservoir head/sluice profile and reconstructed discharge sufficient to move observed sediment through harbour entrance. These requirements are project inference from the proposed mechanism. No such terminal or contact or discharge demonstration is supplied here. Apparent household supply inadequacy does not by itself prove port flushing as the sole use.

## Lineage and project consequences

Earlier Frumkin 2014article in this lane studies shafted collecting tunnels serving Caesarea's high-level aqueduct at upperTaninim/Jisr ez-Zarqa. Dray's dam-fed low-level aqueduct is a different installation and later phase. Do not transfer Frumkin's probableRoman tunnel date or shaft survey values to this dam/low aqueduct. Shared Caesarea toponym does not make the observations interchangeable.

Several Dray inputs derive from sources already used in broader aqueduct literature: Peleg 1989crosssections, Porath 2002 water supply and later excavations, historicalSWP mapping, Raban harbour records. The new PDF is a new accessible synthesis and functional proposal; repeated older drawings/descriptions do not create independent corroboration. The2011 sediment view is a separately identified observation, but its annual-cycle interpretation and evaporation estimate need original methods.

For an entry requiring pre-Byzantine construction, this dam/low aqueductsystem cannot supply the requisite phase under the reported late antique dating. That is a conditional phase exclusion; it does not exclude Caesarea as a region or earlier aqueducts. The article provides no Atara western pool gradient, entry 25 cave threshold, Hyrcania measuring origin or Siloam L103 section.
