# Kloner and Zissu 2003: targeted tomb-plan check

**Obtained:** the supplied Hebrew edition's plans, catalogue descriptions and published dating arguments for the Tombs of the Kings, the Kidron monuments, Jason's Tomb and a Sanhedrin control. The bounded pages add an ancient queen-title report and separate dated tomb structures from traditional monument names. They also show that west-facing, multi-chamber kokhim tombs recur among the inspected structures.

This is exploratory enrichment of already exposed leads. The 2003 Hebrew book is a different edition from the still-unread 2007 English edition. Published plans here frequently reproduce earlier drawings; they do not supply independent field corroboration. The selected examples cannot establish regional rarity. The existing regional tables retain their frozen rules and inputs.

## Findings tied to the entry constraints

### Queen's tomb, entry 27: KZ 5-1

**EVIDENCE, pp. 133-136 / PDF pp. 147-150; Figs. 115-117, also Fig. 33 at p. 53.** The book identifies the complex conventionally called Tombs of the Kings as the tomb of Helena's royal family. It cites Josephus's three pyramids and reports reused pyramid stones. Its courtyard has a columned façade on its western wall, a vestibule, a rolling closing stone and a branching complex of burial rooms with kokhim and arcosolia. The catalogue reports Aramaic inscriptions on one sarcophagus that include the title **queen** (p. 136). Figure 33 reproduces that sarcophagus from de Saulcy 1865; the inscription was not independently read from the stone or a manuscript-quality photograph in this session.

**Date basis.** The authors date construction/use to the middle of the first century CE and use until 70 CE from architectural comparison and finds. They expressly distinguish later bone-filled coffins from original use (p. 136). The catalogue's name Helena is an attribution supported through the literary/archaeological association; the reported sarcophagus wording does not itself name Helena.

**Constraint consequence.** The ancient queen-title report supplies a source-backed funerary naming observation for 27-T. It supplies neither an independently discriminated Copper Scroll location nor the entry's digging datum. Keep the title, the person's identification and the complex attribution in separate fields. The façade is on the west wall of the courtyard and therefore faces east; that statement cannot substitute for the orientation of the lower cave's actual doorway when scoring entry 56.

**Ancestry.** Figs. 115-116 credit Cohen; Fig. 117 credits de Saulcy 1882; Fig. 33 credits de Saulcy 1865. The catalogue cites the earlier Cohen, de Saulcy, Vincent and Steve and other studies. Those original publications were not opened here.

### Absalom and Zadok, entries 48 and 51: KZ 6-1 and 6-2

**EVIDENCE, pp. 140-143 / PDF pp. 154-157; Figs. 123-126.** KZ separates the Jehoshaphat cave (6-1) from the freestanding Absalom monument (6-2). The cave has a west-facing decorated façade and a branching group of eight chambers with kokhim, ossuary spaces and arcosolia. Its first-century CE date is an architectural-comparison argument. The book describes the adjacent monument as a rock-cut lower body and a built upper section, with a small internal burial chamber and arcosolia. The monument's entrance is in its southern wall; later breaches are described separately. Its construction/original use is dated to the first century CE by architectural and stylistic comparison.

**Constraint consequence.** A dated monument and actual chamber plan are documented. The catalogue's conventional label Absalom does not provide an inspected ancient naming observation. Its engaged exterior columns also require separate coding from an entrance porch or an exedra pillar. Neither the cave nor the monument supplies a source-reported Zadok name, dated garden, or measured relationship to the southern stoa corner in this bounded check. Reading branches 48-Y/L and 51-S/O/J therefore retain their naming, function or location gates.

The cave's west-facing opening, multiple rooms and kokhim meet the same coarse 56-P feature triad used for the controls. That observation cannot identify entry 56; threshold identity, ancient floor/phase and the depth instruction remain unmeasured.

**Ancestry.** The catalogue describes clearance/survey campaigns, including 1924 and Avigad's 1945-1947 work. Figs. 124-126 credit Avigad 1954. They are republished evidence from that ancestry. Fig. 123 is a July 2000 overview.

### Bene Hezir and Zechariah, entries 51, 52, 54 and 56: KZ 6-3 and 6-4

**EVIDENCE, pp. 143-146 / PDF pp. 157-160; Figs. 127-131.** Bene Hezir (6-3) has an explicitly west-facing, two-column *distylos in antis* entrance, a multi-chamber cave with kokhim and arcosolia, and a Hebrew façade inscription naming the priestly Hezir family. A stepped passage links it to the adjacent monument's enclosure. The authors distinguish a proposed reconstruction of its funerary superstructure from preserved rock-cut remains.

**Names.** The description reports eight people from two generations, but does not transcribe their individual names. Figure 131 reproduces Avigad's drawing of the inscription; its lettering was insufficient for a secure independent Obed reading. This pass therefore adds no source-verified Obed or Jerichoite attribution. The ancient Hezir-family naming report is distinct from the conventional labels Zechariah and Absalom and from Allegro's Copper Scroll naming.

**Date basis, p. 144.** The book records competing datings: Vincent's much earlier proposal, Avigad's first-century BCE architectural/palaeographic dating (with a later adjustment of the inscription), and Barag's mid-second-century BCE date for the kokhim. Kloner and Zissu favour first-half first-century BCE work for the kokhim rooms/façade on a fresh construction-stage analysis. These are scholarly chronological arguments. They supply no sealed construction deposit or dated threshold contact. The retained dates for the original tomb work are compatible with the lane's window, while exact phases remain disputed.

**EVIDENCE, p. 146.** The separate Zechariah monument (6-4) is a pyramidal nefesh with a west-facing principal façade. Avigad treats it as a monument without a tomb; a proposed uncompleted cave remains conjectural. Its published architectural date is the second half of the first century BCE. A nefesh alone cannot supply an observed burial-chamber doorway, kokhim, cistern or tomb-threshold datum.

**Constraint consequence.** Bene Hezir meets the coarse 56-P triad of west-facing opening, multiple rooms and kokhim. Its columns are observed entrance columns, so they provide a functional comparator for 51's exedra/porch branch. Name Zadok, ancient garden, Jerichoite association, entry-specific origin and digging datum remain unknown. The retained interpretations of 51, 52, 54 and 56 remain separate. An Obed occurrence, even if independently verified later, would still need a Jerichoite/family association and discrimination against controls.

**Ancestry.** Figs. 128-131 credit Avigad 1954. Fig. 127 is a July 2000 overview. The modern synthesis is not a second independent observation of the older plan/inscription. The inspected Fig. 13 (p. 20) reproduces de Saulcy 1853 before recognition of the inscription; it cannot be used as a post-discovery name reading.

### Jason's Tomb: KZ 23-3 as a column/phase control

**EVIDENCE, pp. 241-243 / PDF pp. 255-257; Figs. 250-252.** The complex has successive courts, a single-column entrance of *stylos in antis* form, an antechamber, a burial room with eight kokhim and a separate bone-collection room. The authors base its modern catalogue name on the wall inscription mentioning Jason. Its columned court opening is in the court's north side; the burial-room doorway is in the antechamber's west wall. Position of a wall and facing direction of an opening must be kept distinct.

**Date basis.** The catalogue gives Rahmani's two use periods, beginning under Alexander Jannaeus, interruption around 37 BCE and earthquake damage in 31 BCE, followed by later bone collecting until approximately 30 CE. It also records Foerster's earlier beginning in the first half of the second century BCE, supported by ceramics. Use dates, alteration and initial cutting need separate fields.

**Constraint consequence.** A single-column tomb porch and a multi-room kokhim layout have a documented control. These physical features alone supply no Zadok, queen, Obed, ancient garden or exact stoa relation. The known openings described here do not supply a west-facing main cave entrance under the adopted facing convention; a geometrical west-wall mention must not become a west-facing hit.

**Ancestry.** Figure 250 and the catalogue cite Rahmani's 1956 excavation and 1967 publication. Figs. 251-252 reproduce his drawings of graffiti. Original field sheets and the 1967 report were not opened.

### Sanhedrin tomb: KZ 25-14 as a west-facing kokhim control

**EVIDENCE, pp. 264-266 / PDF pp. 278-280; Fig. 274.** The book describes multiple burial rooms on several levels, a decorated entrance, kokhim, arcosolia, ossuaries and bone-collection features. Its cave doorway is in the vestibule's eastern wall (p. 265); the plan's north arrow supports a west-facing opening under the same convention used for the leads (INFERENCE). The authors describe a large family tomb used in the first century CE until 70 CE. A reported Isaac name on an ossuary fragment comes from de Saulcy's earlier observation (p. 266); this session did not independently inspect that fragment. Sanhedrin is the conventional complex name.

**Constraint consequence.** The control meets the same west-facing + multiple-room + kokhim triad as Bene Hezir and the Jehoshaphat cave. An entrance bearing, threshold survey, phase contact and deposited-target observation would be needed to test the rest of 56-P. A cistern is not established by the selected description, so 56-M remains unknown. The control's permitted feature checks are recorded alongside the leads in [tomb_observations.csv](tomb_observations.csv); silence stays unknown.

**Ancestry.** Figure 274 reproduces Rahmani 1961; the catalogue also summarises 1902, 1949-1954 and 1975 work. It therefore overlaps the survey/excavation lineage already represented in the regional lane. Probable crosswalk: ASI 102/221 (T0103), from the named complex and matching local position; this is not an exact newly verified tomb-level join.

## What this evidence can decide

The selected source recovers physical layouts and attribution/dating arguments. The missing names, phase-specific reference surfaces and independent discrimination prevent an identification claim.

## Avigad 1954 read — 22 October 2026

Avigad's original edition (the ancestry of Figs. 124–131) was read from the owner's NLI photos ([record](../nli_photos_2026-10-21/avigad1954.md)). It answers next step 1 above.

- **Facing (EVIDENCE, p. 37).** "החזית פונה לרוח דרומית-מערבית": the façade faces **south-west**. From p. 39 Avigad says he will give only the main cardinal directions, so his "west" is this south-west face. INFERENCE: the "explicitly west-facing" wording above (KZ pp. 143–146) is a rounded convention. The coarse 56-P triad, which takes any west-facing opening, is still met; a surveyed-bearing model should use south-west. The earlier text is kept as written.
- **Names (EVIDENCE, p. 61, crop-checked).** "זה קבר והנפש שלאלעזר חניה יועזר יהודה שמעון יוחנן / בני יוספ בן עובד יוספ ואלעזר בני חניה / כהנים מבני חזיר". Avigad reads עובד ("certainly" short for Obadiah) and weighs and rejects עודד. Eight people: six sons of Joseph son of ʿObed, and Joseph and Eleazar sons of Ḥaniah. **No Zadok.** Nothing links the family to Jericho.
- **Dates (EVIDENCE).** Tomb and Doric façade about the middle of the 2nd c. BCE (pp. 47, 58–59, 66; the English summary says the latter half of that century); the inscription first half of the 1st c. BCE, about two generations after burials began (pp. 65–66). Avigad rejects Albright's 20–10 BCE (p. 64). Architecture and palaeography only, no sealed deposit.
- **Unexplored parts (EVIDENCE).** Corridor steps under earth (pp. 39, 49–50); burial cells in the scarp below the façade and toward Absalom, probably earlier (p. 38 n. 1; Fig. 48, not photographed); an "unfinished tomb" south of Zechariah (Fig. 25); Zechariah's buried base and possible tombs "hidden in the rock to the East" (p. 79; summary); the nefesh base under tombstones (p. 76). A cistern mouth is drawn in the valley bed west of Absalom (Fig. 25).
- **Constraint consequence (INFERENCE).** Entry 51: the porch is still the observed pillared porch, but the inscription names no Zadok, so the name clause is unmet at Bene Ḥezir, not merely unknown. Entry 54: an ʿObed is now verified in a priestly family tomb in Jerusalem, without a Jericho link; the scroll's owner word remains an edition question. The 51, 52, 54 and 56 interpretations stay separate.
