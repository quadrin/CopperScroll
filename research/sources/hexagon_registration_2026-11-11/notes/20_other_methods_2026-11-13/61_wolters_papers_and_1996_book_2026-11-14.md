# Wolters: four papers read in full (1987, 1988 JBL, 1990 JNES, 1990 RevQ) and the 1996 book read on the library photographs (pp. [6]–23, 32–53). Wolters reads VII 9 as "the end house", not Bet ha-Qos; no Cypros at VII 3; Pottery Ravine at VIII 8; no Kefar Nebo at IX 11; no Qobeʿah at IX 10. No change — 14 November 2026 (night)

Follows: note 60 (Wolters 1988 "Fifth Cache" was read there). The owner said "continue" to "read the four other Wolters papers and his 1996 book".

## Result first

1. **All five Wolters papers are now read in full** (text layer). The 1996 book is read **in part**, on the page photographs: the overview and bibliography (pp. [6]–23) and the text and translation of columns I–XI (pp. 32–53). Pages 24–31 and column XII were not photographed.
2. **Entry 30 (Bet ha-Qos, VII 9).** Wolters translates the words as a common noun: a cave next to the fountain "opposite the end house" (1996, p. 45). He does not read a place name or the priestly family Hakkoz here. **So the Tell el-Qos lead (B128) depends on the Milik reading of VII 9.** No change: the lead was already only Possible.
3. **VII 3–4 (Eshel's Cy[pros] or Qi[dron]).** Wolters gives no restoration: an aqueduct of a lost name, and "the northern reservoir". He does not support Eshel's Cypros. No change.
4. **VIII 8.** Wolters reads qdrwt, "the Pottery Ravine" (RevQ 1990; 1996 p. 47), against Milik's Kidron. This is against Eshel's place (a dam of the Hyrcania aqueduct in the Kidron). The project already had this reading through Lefkovits and Puech. No change.
5. **IX 11 and IX 10 lose two place names in Wolters's text.** At IX 11 he reads lkp hbyb, "to the edge of the gutter", against Milik's lkpr nbw (Kefar Nebo). At IX 10 he reads "in its funnel: a mina of silver, a consecrated offering", against Milik's place "Qobeʿah". For the name register: Kefar Nebo and Qobeʿah are Milik readings that Wolters does not accept. No change.
6. **Kohlit.** Wolters translates "the ruin-heap (tel) of Kohlit" at I 9, "the pond east of Kohlit" at II 13 and "the eastern cavern north of Kohlit" at IV 11 (1996). In JBL 1988 he follows Pixner and puts the last cache (XII 10, "north of Kohlit") in Transjordan near the Yarmuk. In JNES 1990 he says that the ʿEn Kohal of Masseket Kelim is on the west side of Mount Nebo, not on Carmel, and that Milik's link of Kohlit to a Carmel spring ("Fons Eliae") has no basis. This agrees with the project's earlier view that the Carmel link is weak. No change to the Koḥlit count.
7. **Other readings of place words in the 1996 translation:** IV 6 "the Valley of Achon" (he keeps Achon, where Puech corrects to Achor); IV 9 the red reservoir at the edge of the (Wadi) ʿAṣla; IV 13 and V 2 Secacah; V 6–8 "Solomon's Reservoir" and "Solomon's Canal"; V 12–13 a tomb in "the Wadi Kippa", on the way from Jericho to Secacah; VII 11 (Mount) Duq; VII 14 the water outlet of the Koziba; VIII 10 and 14 the Shaveh; IX 1 the Natuf; IX 7 (Beth) Horon; IX 14–15 Beth Tamar and Garpela; X 3 the Great Wadi; X 5 Beth Kerem; X 8 the Zered Gorge; X 12 Absalom's monument; XI 3 and 6 Zadok's Tomb and Garden; XI 12 Beth Eshdatain. II 3 is "the old Washer's Chamber", which he puts in the Temple (RevQ 1990).
8. **Wolters's view of the whole scroll (1996, pp. 16–18; JNES 1990):** the treasure is real, not folklore. It most probably came from the Jerusalem Temple and was hidden before 68–70 CE, in the war with Rome. He gives the bookkeeping style and the copper as the main reasons.
9. **No rating, candidate or match strength was changed.** No edit-log row: no status or position changed.
