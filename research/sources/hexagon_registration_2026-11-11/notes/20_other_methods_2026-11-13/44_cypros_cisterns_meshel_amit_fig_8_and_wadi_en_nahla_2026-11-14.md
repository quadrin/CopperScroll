# Cypros: the two pits east of the summit are cisterns of Meshel and Amit's plan (1989, fig. 8); the preserved one is 17 × 7 m and 8 m deep. Two candidates at Wadi en-Nahla looked at on the 1974 pair — 14 November 2026

## Result first

- **The two pits at PG 190.527/139.153 are water cisterns of the fortress.** Meshel and Amit 1989 (*The Aqueducts of Ancient Palestine*, Hebrew, pp. 229–242; held as `research/assets/plans/aqueducts1989/jericho-cypros-original-pages.pdf`), fig. 8 on p. 234, is a plan of the channels near the fortress with a 200 m scale and a north arrow. Its legend has a sign for "water cistern". It shows **four cisterns east of the summit**: a north pair (dashed) about 100 m east and 105 m north of the summit sign, and a south pair (solid) about 125 m due east of the summit sign.
- The text on the same page (my translation from the Hebrew): of the four cisterns only the third from the north is preserved well enough to restore its form and volume (their fig. 10); of the south cistern only parts are left; the two north cisterns were fully destroyed in the development works. The preserved cistern is triangular in section, with narrow steps that go down into it; **length 17 m, width 7 m, depth today 8 m**; it is full of silt, so it was deeper; volume at least about 500 cubic metres. The cisterns took the run-off water of the Hasmonean channel.
- On the images the two pits are due east of the summit (87 m east, 12 m south of my summit point), each about 15 m long, 13 m apart, on 1 December 1974 (forward and aft frames) and on 5 May 1978. Direction and size agree with the south pair; the distance on the plan is 125 m against 87 m on the image (the plan is a sketch on form lines; my summit point and their summit sign are not the same point).
- **Status: Checked as cisterns of the east group; Probable as the south pair (cisterns 3 and 4).** Earlier: Probable pools or cisterns, "can be excavation trenches" (B103, B118). Edit log B119.
- The depth is published (8 m), so the hand measurement on the stereo pair is not necessary and was not done.
- Netzer 2006, pp. 207–209 with fig. 46 (plan of the fortress): the excavation uncovered nearly all of the summit and large parts of the lower shoulder to the south-east; on the summit about 20 rooms and two cisterns, and a bath house. Final report: Netzer and Damati, "Cypros", in Netzer and Laureys-Chachy 2004, pp. 233–285 (not held). The grid of small cells west of my summit point on the 1974 frames is Probable the excavated rooms of the summit building; then my summit point is the east edge of the building, not its centre.
- Entry and count: the registered Koḥlit count is not changed. Kypros (S2589) holds from the summit and fails from the unit point (third pass); this note does not change that.

## Wadi en-Nahla, candidates C13 and C14 of image sweep pilot 2

- Places: C14 at PG 188.295/140.499; C13 at 188.229/140.310 (from the positions of the pilot note).
- On the forward and aft frames of 1 December 1974 (same light):
  - **C13:** a dark oval of about 35 × 15 m with a sharp north edge, and a light patch of about 25 m on its north-east side. The sun is in the south on this date. A mound has its shadow on the north side; here the dark part is on the south side of the light part. So a hollow with a shaded south wall and a lit north side (or spoil) is the better reading. A patch of dark plants beside bare ground is not excluded.
  - **C14:** a round area of about 40 m with a faint texture, and one black spot of about 8 m at its south-east edge. No clear form.
- Stereo: 13 % of the blocks match in this window; none on the two features. No depth.
- **Status: Possible for both (no change).** For C13 the reading "hollow" is a little stronger than before.
