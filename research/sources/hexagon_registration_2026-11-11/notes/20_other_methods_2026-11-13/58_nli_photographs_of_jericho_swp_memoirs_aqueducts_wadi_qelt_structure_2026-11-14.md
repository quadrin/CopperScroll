# NLI photographs of Jericho before 1950 (35 records); the SWP Memoirs describe the Jericho aqueducts; a raised structure across Wadi Qelt on the 1945 and 1978 images, Possible as an aqueduct bridge — 14 November 2026 (evening, PT)

**Correction and update, same night:** see note 59. (1) The Australian War Memorial photograph of April 1918 named in result 3 is a high bridge in the rocky gorge of Wadi Qelt, not a bridge in the plain (edit log D25). (2) Wilson 1881 puts his eleven pointed arches near the mouth of the pass and Birket Musa, about 1.2 km west of the structure of result 4. (3) The structure of result 4 shows arcade openings on the December 1974 frames and still stands on the modern image: Probable as an arched bridge (B126). (4) Note 60: a blind second reader confirmed the structure; SWP sheet XVIII does not support its identity with the bridge of the fourth aqueduct (B127).

Follows: note 57.

## Result first

1. **35 NLI photograph records of the Jericho area dated before 1950** are in the bucket (`open-sources/nli/photos/`). I looked at all of them (80 images, with the pages of four Palphot albums). Most are 400-pixel copies of 19th-century prints. Two give something for the water works:
   - **ʿAin es-Sultan, a coloured lantern slide** (NLI 997012619193005171; the record dates it "ca. 1880–1920"). The spring pool lies against a built wall with a large vault. Two built channels leave the pool: one through a sluice in the foreground and one to the right. **Status: Seen** (the head works of the channels at the spring). The other views of the spring (Good 1868; Bain 1894) show the same pool and a channel.
   - **Isaacs 1856, "Aqueduct leading from the Spring of Elisha to Jericho"** (NLI 997009563171805171): an aqueduct on six or more arches. The arches are pointed. This photograph is not of the spring itself: the spring has one vault, not an arcade.
2. **The SWP Memoirs give a written account of the aqueducts, and it answers part of B122** (vol. III, 1883, sheet XVIII, "Kanat Musa", printed pp. 206–207; read from the text layer in the bucket). The survey describes four aqueducts north and west of Jericho, all with pointed arches, which it took to be Crusader work repaired later:
   - from ʿAin el-ʿAujah south to ʿAin Duk and ʿAin Nueiʿameh (a bridge of many pointed arches below the springs), then to Kh. el-Mefjir and to Bir Heider;
   - **"a shorter aqueduct from ʿAin es Sultan joins the above at Khurbet el Mefjir"**; its water ran **in pipes**, like the high-level aqueduct of Wadi Qelt. This is the SWP line of B122. **Status: Probable (no change); a written source added (B124).** The Memoirs and the sheet are one survey, so this is not an independent second source;
   - from ʿAin Duk along Jebel Quruntul to Tawahin es-Sukkar, then across Wadi Qelt **by a bridge "now broken down"** with pointed arches, to ruins east of Birket Musa;
   - **from ʿAin es-Sultan south to near Rujm el-Mogheifir, across Wadi Qelt "by a bridge still perfect"**, with pointed arches and starlings of dressed stone.
3. **The Isaacs arcade is Possible as one of the two SWP bridges over Wadi Qelt.** Its caption ("from the Spring of Elisha to Jericho") fits the fourth aqueduct, which runs from ʿAin es-Sultan to the south. Other early sources show an arched aqueduct over Wadi Qelt in the plain: F. M. Good (1860s), "Jericho. Ruined aqueduct over the Brook Cherith" (Qatar National Library); Wilson, *Picturesque Palestine* I (1881): an aqueduct across the plain from Wadi Qelt with eleven pointed arches over the dry bed, and the road under it; and an Australian War Memorial photograph of April 1918 of the Wadi Qelt aqueduct with a steel pipe on the Jericho main road (**corrected: this one is the gorge bridge, see the top of this note**). I saw only the catalogue texts and search snippets of these three, not the images.
4. **A raised straight structure crosses the bed of Wadi Qelt**, about 150 m west of the 1945 road bridge. Its ends are at about PG 192.87/140.07 and 192.92/139.99: about 100 m long, running north-north-west to south-south-east. **It is Seen at the same place on the 1945 mosaic (full-size copy) and on HEXAGON 1978, to about 20 m. On 1978 it casts a shadow, so it stands above the bed.** The 1945 key gives no. 11 as the "Wadi Kelt Bridge" (the road bridge), not this structure. The 1941 sheet has no separate sign for it. **Status: Possible = the bridge of the fourth SWP aqueduct (B125).** It can also be a later pipe bridge (compare the 1918 photograph with a steel pipe).
5. No rating or candidate was changed.

## The 1945 sheet key

The bottom-right corner of the 1945 mosaic has a key to 13 numbered places: 1 Winter Palace Hotel, 2 Coptic Church, 3 Agricultural Station, 4 Jordan Hotel, 5 Municipality, 6 Russian Compound, 7 Mosque, 8 Church, 9 Deir Latin Church, 10 Belle Vue Hotel, 11 Wadi Kelt Bridge, 12 Police Station, 13 German Garden. The sheet says that its grid is distorted, but references taken from it agree with standard maps.

## Limits

- One reader; not blind.
- The NLI copies are 400 px for most records. The place of the Isaacs arcade is not fixed: I compared the photograph with written descriptions, not with a second image of the same bridge.
- The structure across the wadi is read by eye on two images. Its length and its ends are good to about 20 m. I did not measure its height.
- The SWP text was read from the OCR text layer; page images were not checked. Short quotations only.
- Two web pages could not be opened at first: the Qatar National Library image page (the request for approval got no answer), and a page of *Picturesque Palestine* on the Heidelberg site (bot protection). Both were opened later in the owner's Chrome, with his approval.
