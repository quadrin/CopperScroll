# The structure across Wadi Qelt is an arched bridge (Probable, four dates); Isaacs's book calls his print the "Roman Aqueduct at Jericho"; the comparison images opened — 14 November 2026 (night, PT)

Follows: note 58. Followed by note 60 (blind second reading; SWP sheet XVIII). The owner approved the web steps ("try", "I approve"). The pages were opened in the owner's Chrome and the tab was closed.

## Result first

1. **The raised structure across the bed of Wadi Qelt (B125) is Probable as an arched bridge (B126).** On the two frames of 1 December 1974 (forward F017b and aft A017f; low winter sun) it shows a row of regular light openings, as an arcade does. It is at the same place on four dates: March 1945, December 1974, May 1978, and the modern image (where it still crosses the canalised bed). On the 1974 frames it is about 40 m east of its 1978 place; the road and the houses move with it, so this is the fit error of the 1974 tiles here. **The identity with the bridge of the fourth SWP aqueduct (from ʿAin es-Sultan, "still perfect") stays Possible:** the place fits, but no plan or measured position of that bridge is held. (Note 60, B127: the SWP sheet does not support the identity.)
2. **Isaacs, *A Pictorial Tour in the Holy Land* (1862; Internet Archive `apictorialtouri00isaagoog`).** The frontispiece is an engraving of the NLI 1856 photograph: the same tall arch and the same arcade, with figures and a camel added. Its title is "Roman Aqueduct at Jericho". In the text (p. 83) Isaacs looks down on the plain from the road: the Spring of Elisha lies under the hills, and "the fine old Roman aqueduct" carries its water down to the village of Jericho. So the caption of the 1856 print is the author's own, and he means an aqueduct from ʿAin es-Sultan to the village. **Status of the place of the print: not identified (no change).** The text does not say that this aqueduct crosses Wadi Qelt.
3. **Comparison images (one image sheet on Drive):**
   - F. M. Good, 1860s, "Jericho. Ruined aqueduct over the Brook Cherith" (QNL; only the 200-px thumbnail opens; the item viewer did not load): two large pointed arches and a broken end, over a bed with plants.
   - Wilson, *Picturesque Palestine* I (1881), p. 169: engraving "One of the arches of an aqueduct over the Wady Kelt, plains of Jericho" (one pointed arch over water). Text p. 174: below the towers at the mouth of the pass, near Birket Musa, an aqueduct runs across the plain from Wadi Qelt, the road passes under it, and eleven pointed arches span the dry bed. This place is near the mouth of the pass, about 1.2 km west of the B125 structure. So it is a different bridge, or Wilson's place is loose.
   - Australian War Memorial H02519 (about 1917, "an aqueduct outside the city"): a long, low bridge with at least four arches and cutwaters across a wide dry bed, with a peaked mountain behind. **This is the photograph that best fits the B125 structure**, but the AWM record gives no place.
   - **Correction (D25):** AWM B00045 (April 1918, "the Wady Kelt aqueduct, with steel pipe, on the Jericho main road") is a high bridge with round arches in the rocky gorge, probably Jisr ed-Deir. In my last note I listed it with the bridges in the plain, from its title only.
4. **What the sources together say:** in the 19th century there were at least two bridges with pointed arches in the plain at Jericho. One was ruined (Good; SWP "broken down", on the ʿAin Duk–Tawahin es-Sukkar aqueduct near Birket Musa). One stood complete (SWP "still perfect", on the ʿAin es-Sultan aqueduct to the south; AWM H02519 is a candidate photograph). The B125 structure is an arched bridge that still stands, and it is a candidate for the second. **No rating or candidate was changed.**
