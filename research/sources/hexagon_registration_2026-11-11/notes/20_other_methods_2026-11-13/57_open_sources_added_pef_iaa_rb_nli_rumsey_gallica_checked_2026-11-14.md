# Open sources added on 15 November (PEF, IAA Reports, Revue Biblique, NLI photographs, Rumsey, Gallica, Met, SoilGrids, GEBCO, Smithsonian): a check against what the project already holds — 14 November 2026 (evening, PT)

The other session reported these sets. I checked them in the bucket and compared them with the Drive holdings and the project notes.

## Result first

1. **All ten sets are in the bucket.** The file counts agree with the report (table below).
2. **The three IAA monographs named as "most directly relevant" were already held.** Akeldama (IAA Reports 1), the Jericho cemetery (IAA Reports 7) and the Cave of the Warrior (IAA Reports 5) were in Drive from October and early November, with the same file sizes. The Hachlili and Killebrew volume was already OCRed and read (reader file of 9 November). The new text files for Akeldama and the Cave of the Warrior are about 2 KB each: abstracts only, not the book text.
3. **The PEF photographs that matter are the Leo Boer set, and we already hold it.** 25 of the 61 Flickr photographs are from the Boer set (1953–54, with comparison photographs of 2009), including the "Plan of Qumran with position marks". Drive `06 Imagery` already has the full set of 48 photographs and the plan. It was read on 11 November (`claude/leo-boer-1953-qumran-photos-first-reading-2026-11-11.md`). The Flickr copies are smaller (about 640 px). There is nothing new for the scroll entries.
4. **The Revue Biblique set stops at 1930.** It does not have de Vaux's Qumran reports (1949–1959) or Milik's work on 3Q15. The project holds those separately in Drive (for example de Vaux 1953 RB 60, de Vaux 1959 RB 66, Kuhn 1954 RB 61). The new set is useful for older Jerusalem and Jericho reports (Vincent, Abel). I did not search it.
5. **One new lead for B122/B123: A. A. Isaacs, 1856, "Aqueduct leading from the Spring of Elisha to Jericho"** (NLI 997009563171805171). The photograph shows an aqueduct on arches: at least six arches, with a tall pier and arch in front, on flat ground with trees. **Status: Seen. Place not identified.** The NLI copy is only 400 × 276 px, too small to identify the place. The caption is the photographer's, and captions of this period are often wrong. If the arches are on the line from ʿAin es-Sultan, they are the oldest image of a built channel there. **No status change.**
6. **NLI photographs: 495 of the 866 records name a project place** (Jericho, Siloam, Kidron, Mar Saba, Bethany, the Jordan, the Dead Sea). Most are 19th-century views. I looked at only one (point 5).
7. Not found: the report names `open-sources/blocked-2026-11-15.md`. It is not at the top of `open-sources/` in the bucket.
