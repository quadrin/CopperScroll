# Entry 57, Tell er-Ras: three older records of the ditch, and steps on the four sides of the mound (Wilson 1873 in the SWP Memoirs; SWP sheet XI "Moat"; Clermont-Ganneau 1896) (14 November 2026)

This note records sources. It does not change the rating or the candidate of entry 57; that is the owner's decision.

## Result first

1. **The SWP Memoirs, volume II, pp. 191–192, print a description of the mound of Tell er-Ras with a ditch and with steps.** It is a quotation from C. W. Wilson, *Quarterly Statement* 1873, p. 67, on the excavations of Lieutenant Anderson (1866). The mound at the north end of the arm that runs north from the castle is "partly artificial, and isolated from the ridge by a deep ditch"; there are "traces of steps on the four sides leading to the summit of the mound"; the top had a building 53 feet square with very thick walls; the excavation gave "a few Roman coins"; and north of and below the mound are rock cuttings "apparently for holding water".
2. **SWP sheet XI (1880) has the word "Moat" at this place**, with a short east–west mark directly south of a ring-shaped mound sign, between that mound and the summit ruins. I registered the sheet today (see the maps note): the mound sign is at PG 175.95/179.26 after the shift, 110 m from the platform on the images (176.06/179.23); the Moat mark is 190 to 220 m south of the mound sign, on the ridge side.
3. **So the ditch is in three records that do not depend on each other's wording:** Wilson and Anderson (seen in 1866, "deep"), the SWP surveyors (map, 1870s), and Bull 1968 ("a wide east–west fosse" at the south limit of the mound). All three put it on the south side, between the mound and the ridge.
4. **Effect on the image status.** This morning the blind reading lowered the dark band south of the platform to "Possible as the fosse", because depth cannot be seen on an image. Three written records now say that a deep ditch is at that topographic place. **The band goes back to Probable as the ditch** (edit log B96). It is still not Checked: no plan gives its line, and tree shadows fall on it in December.
5. **What is new for the text of entry 57.** The entry has a step (or steps) and an "upper" pit or ditch on Mount Gerizim. Wilson's passage has both features at one place: a ditch and steps on the mound. Bull 1968 already asked if his fosse is the scroll's "upper fosse". The 1866 record is older, is independent of the scroll (found 1952), and adds the steps and the word "deep". **It does not date the ditch.** The only finds named are Roman coins, and the temple above is of the 2nd century CE (JSP 8). A ditch of the Hasmonean citadel under the temple staircase (JSP 8 p. 245) is possible; it is not shown.
6. **Other details from the same sources** (data, not evidence for the entry):
   - Clermont-Ganneau 1896, vol. II, pp. 325–326 (visit of 1874): on the summit, "in front of the birkeh is a well called Bir er Resas", which legend joins to Jacob's Well; local people also told of an underground conduit from Jacob's Well to the sanctuary of Sheikh Ghanem. He names the north mound Tahunet el-Hawa and thinks that one of the temples stood there.
   - SWP Memoirs II, p. 192: "North of the castle is a large pool"; south of the castle "several cisterns half-filled with rubbish".
   - Avi-Yonah 1936 (QDAP 5/4, p. 161): "Temple of Zeus Hypsistus, fort and staircase of three hundred steps on the east summit".

## Limits

- I read the Memoirs passage in the recognised text of the bucket copy (PDF pages 221–223), not on the page image. The words quoted are short and clear in the text.
- The SWP sheet is good to about 200 m after the shift; the place of the word "Moat" is not a survey of the ditch.
- I did not read Wilson's article of 1873 itself, or Anderson's plan. The Memoirs quote it.
- A search of the repository and the project found no earlier record of this passage. If an October note has it under other words, this note repeats it.
