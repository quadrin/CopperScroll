# Magen 2000 (Qadmoniot 33) read in full: nothing new for entry 57; no fosse, no Tell er-Ras plan (14 November 2026)

Source: Y. Magen, "Har Gerizim ba-tequfah ha-romit-bizantit" (Mount Gerizim in the Roman-Byzantine period), *Qadmoniot* 33 no. 2 (120), 2000, pp. 133–143. Read: all 11 printed pages on the page images (owner's JSTOR capture, 16 screen pages), with all captions and the bibliography. The machine text was used as a help only.

## Result first

1. **The article has no fosse, no description of Tell er-Ras and no plan of it.** Ten of the eleven pages are about the main summit in the 4th to 6th centuries CE (the Samaritan sacred place, Zeno's church and enclosure, Justinian's outer wall) and about the Crusader period.
2. **For the Roman period the article gives three sentences only** (p. 133). All three are already in the project record from Magen's later books:
   - After John Hyrcanus the mountain was not settled again. A temple of Zeus of Neapolis was built on the north of the mountain in the Roman period.
   - *Antiquities* 14.100: Magen reads the place of refuge as the Hasmonean fortress "whose remains were found in the excavation", beside the Roman temple, which controlled the road up the mountain. This is the "northern citadel" of JSP 8 p. 245 (record of 29 October).
   - The two Samaritan gatherings: under Pilate (*Antiquities* 18.85–89) and on 27 Sivan 67 CE, which Cerealis ended (*War* 3.307–315). Both are in the record (Magen 2021, coin chapter).
3. **The Samaritan tradition of hidden vessels** (p. 133): Magen says that the tradition is alive today: after the Tabernacle on Mount Gerizim disappeared, the holy vessels were hidden **in a cave on the mountain**, and they will be found when the Taheb comes. He gives no source. The outside-witnesses record has this tradition from Montgomery 1907 (pp. 241, 243). The words "in a cave" are the only addition. It is a tradition, not evidence.
4. **Cisterns** (p. 138): three large cisterns inside the Byzantine enclosure "began in the Hellenistic period". JSP 8 has the same (Byzantine use of the precinct cisterns; record of 29 October).
5. Entry 57 does not change. The missing record is still Magen 2005, *Flavia Neapolis* (Hebrew), pp. 220–242.
