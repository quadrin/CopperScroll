# Old air photographs 10: frame BS Pal. 838 (8 April 1918, south-east Jerusalem) beside HEXAGON 1974. Hand fit only; the automatic fit is not stable here. The cemetery on the Mount of Olives is complete in 1918. Stability test of the Jericho fits passed (14 November 2026)

## Result first

- Frame BS Pal. 838 (title strip: unit 304, 8 April 1918, 5 o'clock, Jerusalem, height 4,000, focal length 50) is a vertical photograph. It has the south half of the Temple platform with the Dome of the Rock and the Aqsa at its north-west corner, the Kidron valley, Silwan village, the Jericho road and the west slope of the Mount of Olives.
- **Hand fit** to the HEXAGON image of 25 June 1974 with two tie points (Dome of the Rock; south-east corner of the platform): 0.140 m for each pixel. The Aqsa and Silwan village then agree to about 15 m. East of the Kidron bed the fit is about 40 m off (the two rock-cut monuments in the valley are 42 m apart on the two images). The cause is the relief: the valley floor is about 100 m below the platform.
- **The automatic fit of note 8 is not stable on this frame.** Two runs from two starts each gave 23 agreeing points and 1.4 to 1.5 m of remaining error, and they differ from each other by 130 to 240 m in the middle of the frame. A small error with few points is not a proof of a good fit.
- **Stability test, new rule.** I ran the automatic fit of the Jericho frames again from a start that was moved by 23 m. Frame 1041: 53 points, the pool corners and the mound move by 1 to 3 m. Frame 1037: 32 points, pool A(B)101 moves by 0.2 m. These fits are stable. From now on a fit is used only if a second run from a moved start gives the same positions.
- **Absolute position.** The 1974 tile puts the Dome of the Rock at 172.448 / 131.674. The value I remember for the Dome is 31.7780 N, 35.2354 E, which is 172.387 / 131.706. If that value is right, the tile is about 70 m off in Jerusalem, which is at its west edge. I did not check the value against a map. For this reason this note gives no map positions for Jerusalem features.

## What the frame shows

| Feature | 1918 | 1974 |
|---|---|---|
| West slope of the Mount of Olives, east of the Kidron bed | Dense rows of graves over the whole slope, on the two sides of the Jericho road | Most of the slope is bare ground; a wide road with trees is on it |
| Two rock-cut monuments in the Kidron (by their place the monuments called the tombs of Absalom and Zechariah) | A square block with a deep shadow, and a second structure 70 m north of it at a bend of the path | A round-topped structure and a square block in a dark scarp |
| Temple platform, south part | Open paved court; the Aqsa with a dark roof; an open walled field south of the south wall | The same; excavated walls south of the south wall |
| Silwan | A compact village on the east slope; terraces and garden plots in the valley floor | The village is much larger; the valley floor is built over |

- The record of the cemetery in 1918 is the main value of the frame: it shows the ground east of the Kidron before the changes of 1948 to 1967.
- No entry is linked. Entry 48 names a monument of Absalom; the frame shows the monuments that carry this name today, as they were in 1918. It does not say more.

## One more check: the cut mound of note 1

- Note 1 gave the cut mound south-west of Tell es-Sultan (191.79 / 141.37, good to 30 m) as Probable Tell es-Samarat. A project note of 10 November reads the same mound as probably Tell Abu Hindi, from the title of frame 1031.
- The database of the Koḥlit count has Tell es-Samrat (unit S2430) at 191700 / 141300, a grid value to 100 m. The cut mound is 114 m from it. This supports Tell es-Samarat. I have no position for Tell Abu Hindi.
- Status stays Probable Tell es-Samarat. The two notes do not agree; SWP sheet XVIII, which names the two mounds, can decide.

## Dead Sea shore frames (1060 to 1062)

- These were read on 10 November (note "bavarian frames, remaining 98"): nothing for Qumran or ʿAin Feshkha beyond the terrain; frame 1061 can show Masada as a distant block. They are high oblique views and can not be put on the map grid with this method. I did not repeat that work.
