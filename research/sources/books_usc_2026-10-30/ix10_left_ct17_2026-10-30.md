## 2. Row identification

**Evidence.**

- Two IX rows fall inside the crops:
  - Row A: RB y 2680–2820, RT 2575–2760; crop rows RB 380–520, RT 475–660.
  - Row B: RB y 2830–3020, RT 2760–2945; crop rows RB 530–720, RT 660–845.
- Neither crop shows IX strokes above row A or below row B.

**Counting (blind).**
- On the CT17 preview, the IX traces on the right part sit at baselines RB ≈885, 2085, 2815 (A), 3005 (B), 3480 and 4000.
- The column-IX pitch on CT16 is ≈238 px (17 rows).
- Registering CT16 to CT17 by the scroll's top edge maps A to CT16's 10th row and B to its 11th.

- Blind grade: **A = IX 10, B = IX 11, probable.** The alternative (A = IX 9, B = IX 10) was graded possible.

**After comparison (inference, not blind).**
- Row B's three traces, read blind as box-with-base / short stroke from top to mid-height / bet, match IX 11's ending ב-י-ב (Puech *lkpt byb*). Milik's hand copy also draws ...לכפתביב at the end of copy line 11.

- **Row A = IX 10: probable, now close to certain. The alternative is withdrawn.**

## 3. Blind observations (summary of blind_log.md)

### Row A (IX 10)

One letter, A1, at RB x 1615–1735 (RT 1700–1820), about 1.2 cm left of the CT17 cut edge.

- **Top stroke:** RB y 2700–2715, with a small tick rising up and to the left at its left end.
- **Right side:** a vertical at RB x≈1728.
- **Base:** RB y 2805–2820. It runs ≈30–45 px further left than the top stroke.
- **Left side:** none in either lighting, so the box is open on the left (probable).
- **Identification:** bet, possible to probable; kaf possible. A closed square (ם) is not supported.
- Nothing lies to the left of A1 in either lighting, so A1 is the last letter of the line (probable).
- Between A1 and the cut edge there are only pits and corrosion. No word space can be judged there.

### Row B (IX 11), right to left

- **B1** (RB x 1620–1770): a box with a strong base that projects ≈40 px right of the vertical. Bet, possible to probable.
- **B2** (RB x 1585–1612): a short stroke from the top of the line to mid-height, ending in a pit. Unresolved: a separate yod, B1's left limb, or a corrosion ridge. In RT its top seems to join B1.
- **Gap:** ≈60 px of clean surface. A word space is possible but unresolved.
- **B3** (RB x 1395–1525): top stroke with serif, right vertical, and a longer base with a small right projection; open on the left. **Bet, probable.** It is the last letter of the line (probable).

### Long vertical

At RB x≈1725 (RT ≈1803) a near-continuous thin line runs from A1's top to B1's base, ≈300 px. It forms the right sides of A1 and B1.
- Blind: unresolved whether this is two aligned letter sides, one long stroke, or a crack.
- After comparison: Milik's copy puts IX 11's lamed ascender (of לכפת) four letters further RIGHT, between נ and ח of IX 10. The ascender the CT16 pass found belongs to the START of the line (בקול). **Neither ascender is this line.** The CT17 vertical is best read as the right sides of A1 and B1, aligned by chance (probable). Part of it could still be a crack (possible).

### Lighting

- RB gives the clearest base and top shadows: A1 base, B3 top stroke and vertical, B2 as a ridge.
- RT gives the vertical's continuity, A1's top-left tick and the B2–B1 join.
- Both lightings agree that A1 and B3 are open on the left and that nothing follows them.
- The bright rounded blob at RT ≈1855, 2930 is a corrosion nodule, not a stroke.

| Reading | Who |

The table tags the *ḥrm* readings "[emended]". It records Puech's view that they assume bet corrected to mem and ḥet to he.

**Milik's division.** No transcription of Milik's for IX 10 is in the repo. The variant table has no Milik row for IX 10 w2–3, and readings.json gives none. What I could check is his hand copy (pl. LXIV). After כסף it draws, continuously and with no clear space: מ, נ, (the IX 11 lamed ascender), ח, ה, ר, ב. The last letter is a ב with a top-left serif. I cannot say how Milik divided these letters in his edition. That needs DJD III's text volume (not opened).

**Which letters decide.**
- The division turns on two points:
  - the LAST letter: ם (needed by מנה חרם / מן החרם) against ב (מנחה רב, and Milik's copy);
  - the order ח-ה against ה-ח in the middle.
- On CT17 only the last letter survives (A1). The ר and the ח/ה pair must lie to the right of A1: on CT16's left edge (curled flange, not examined), lost at the saw cut, or in the 1.2 cm of pitted surface between A1 and the cut. **They are not visible on these crops.**

**Result.**
- **EVIDENCE.** IX 10 ends on CT17 with one box letter that is open on the left. It has a longer base and a top-left serif (probable). No left wall is visible in either lighting, and no closed square is visible.
- **INFERENCE 1.** The engraved last letter is ב, not ם: probable. This agrees with Milik's copy and with Puech's reading. It also agrees with Puech's statement that the חרם readers emend.
- **INFERENCE 2.** The image therefore **supports the engraved letters ...רב** (Puech מנחה רב; Milik's copy). It **does not support** an engraved מנה חרם / מן החרם, which needs ב→ם as an emendation. Grade: probable.
  - This weighs only against reading חרם *as engraved*. Wolters and the others already read it as an emendation. The image does not settle whether that emendation is justified, which is a question of sense.
- **INFERENCE 3.** The word division as such (where the spaces fall among מנח/ה/רב, and ח-ה versus ה-ח) is **not decided** by CT17: unresolved. The letters that decide it are not on these crops.
- **Against Milik.** Not testable: his edition's division is not in the repo. His copy's letters agree with the image at the one letter visible here.

**CT16 vs CT17 overlap.** None for these letters. The CT16 pass saw IX 10 only up to about כסף and did not examine CT16's left flange. A1 lies wholly on CT17, 1.2 cm from the cut. The two views therefore cannot be compared for the decisive letters. The CT16 pass's predictions do hold on CT17:
- IX 10 continues onto CT17 (confirmed: A1);
- the IX 11 letters at that height are not מנה חרם (confirmed: ...ביב).

The long vertical on CT17 is not the lamed ascender found on CT16 (see §3).

**Side result for IX 11 (entry 42, e42-kephar-nebo).** Row B reads (box)-(short stroke)-(gap)-ב.
- B2 stops at mid-height. That suits Puech's י (*byb*) better than Milik's ו (*nbw b*); grade possible.
- The ≈60 px gap before the last ב is where Milik puts a word break. Whether it is a word space is unresolved.

## 6. Limits

- One reader. The blind phase was blind to edition texts but not to the candidate readings.
- The row count was blind and probable. It is near-certain only after the edition comparison, which is non-blind.
- A1 is a single letter. Its open left side depends on not seeing a stroke in corroded, pitted metal. A very faint left wall cannot be fully excluded, but it is absent in both lightings.
- Kaf versus bet for A1 is not fully separable. Either one excludes ם.
- Previews (¼ size, JPEG) limit the counting of rows. The CT16↔CT17 top-edge registration is approximate, and CT17 is ≈3 cm shorter than CT16 (I assume bottom loss).
- Milik's own transcription of IX 10 and Wolters's own text were not opened. Their readings are known here only through Puech via the variant table, plus Milik's copy.
- No measurement of normal inter-letter spacing in this hand, so word spaces stay unresolved.
