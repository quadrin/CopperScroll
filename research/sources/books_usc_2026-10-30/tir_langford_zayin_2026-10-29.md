### p. 197 and Kh. Abu Ṭabaq

**p. 197 is photographed:** IMG_2224 (`1Oz7Jka5JitB_Vxqa32OE94LDlLTLMUTb`), pp. 196–197, running heads NEELCERABA / NOB … H. ʿOfrat.

**What the repo wanted from p. 197.** Entry 1 (Achor / "the ruin in the Valley of Achor"; Q13 and R07; `research/assessments/evidence_components/ratings.csv` row 1 nuweimeh, "for P, TIR p. 197 (Noorath / Achor Vallis), still unread"). The target was the Noorath / Neara / Naʿaran entry, to show how TIR places its Achor Vallis label near Noorath.

**Held-area handling.** The Noorath entry (ʿEin Duyuk, NW of Jericho) is held-area material on the Jericho plain/escarpment. It is a gazetteer entry with no measurements. Its "five Roman miles" is an ancient distance, not a survey measurement. Under the rule I record only that it exists: *TIR* p. 197, IMG_2224, entry "NOORATH, NEARA, NAʿARAN". I did not read or paraphrase its text from this photo.

**The repo has already read it.** `research/sources/tir_gazetteer_choziba_followup_2026-10-01.md` §"Supplied TIR gazetteer and guidelines — 6 October 2026" records a full reading of p. 197 (and of Achor Vallis p. 56) from the Clark Library scans (KIC Document 475, PDF 9). The ratings.csv note "TIR p. 197 … still unread" is therefore **stale**; the integrator should correct it. The photo is a second copy of an observation the repo already has. ### Cave of Letters: dimensions (pp. 130–131, Figs. 23–24)

- Total length about **540 m** (citing Reeder 2018). Three large halls (A, B, C) and several elongated passages make it the largest cave on the eastern margin of the Judean Desert south of Jericho (p. 130).
- **Two openings** (a "pair of openings"), **70–80 m below the rim** of the north bank and **about 200 m above** the channel (Fig. 24, p. 130: the openings seen from the south bank, the two leftmost of three dark mouths in the photo).
- Openings rectangular in outline, **5–7 m wide**, **2–2.5 m present height**. The **western opening** was probably the main entrance in antiquity too; it leads to Hall A and was widened and deepened by cutting in the past (p. 130).
- Short stooping is needed in the passages from both openings into Hall A, and from Hall A to Hall B. The passage from Hall B to Hall C (the largest hall) is wide and high. Passages branch off the east end of Hall B and several points in Hall C (p. 130).
- Geology: formed by confined (hypogene) karst dissolution in Shivta Formation limestone. Lacustrine and fluvial fills lie in the outer part. A cosmogenic burial age of **3.39 ± 0.17 Ma** for the top of the in-cave section comes from Matmon et al. 2014 (p. 130).
- Archaeology (p. 131, summary only): use began in the Late Chalcolithic / EB Ib. The main use is a Bar Kokhba refuge for En-Gedi people. Two archives were found in rock-fall crevices (Babatha's archive and a Bar Kokhba letter archive). There was a metal-vessel cache, textiles, sandals, wood, glass and keys. Human remains, some in baskets, were reburied by the Chief Rabbinate. Cited: Yadin 1963, 1971; Yadin et al. 2002.
- **Fig. 23 (p. 129):** plan and profiles. Mapped by the Yadin expedition 1962 and by A. Frumkin, Malham 1998, and taken from the *Atlas of the Judean Desert Caves* (Frumkin 2015). It has a 20 m scale bar and a north arrow. The two openings are labelled כניסה (entrance) and פתח (opening) at the SW end; halls A–B–C; branches a/a′, b/b′, D, E.
  - **My measurements from the figure (inference, ± a few m):** 20 m = 137 px at 300 dpi. The plan spans about 170 m end to end in a straight line. The north arrow points about 71° clockwise from image-up, so the cave's long axis runs from the openings toward about **NNE (bearing about 20°)**, and the openings face about **SSW**. The rock mass between the two openings is about 9–10 m across on the plan. The opening widths scale at about 5–6 m, which agrees with the text.
