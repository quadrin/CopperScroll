## 3. Pass 2a: sites outside the held areas (unrestricted)

These are the features that matter to the repo.

**Northern unit (Kohlit models and the Sartaba chain)**
- **5 Sartaba / Alexandrium** (1938 1670, p. 103; plans with 0–8 and 0–10 m bars):
  - Fort about 35×30 m, walls standing up to 5 m, with Herodian drafted masonry.
  - Enclosure wall on the south; two water pools to the NW.
  - Eight rock-cut caves on the slope, some plastered as pools. One held a column fragment and a marble capital.
  - An earlier fortification lies to the SW.
  - **No graves or tombs are mentioned.**
- **13 T. esh-Sheikh Dhiab** (1908 1615, p. 105):
  - Tell top 15×25 m; extent about 50×50 m. An Arab cemetery lies *on* the tell.
  - At the SW foot, a building of large dressed stones, 45×45 m. About 20 m west of it, water-channel remains.
  - Periods: Chalcolithic to Arab, including Roman.
  - **No graves or tombs north of the tell, and none at a pit mouth, are described.**
- 7 (1970 1653): plastered pool 50×100 m. Roman–Arab.
- 9 el-Marmale: round pool 52 m across and an elliptical pool 28×13 m. Roman–Byzantine.
- 10 Rujm Abu Muheir: round fort 19 m across; pool 8 m across.
- 15 Kh. Fasayil: pool 60×50 m, channels, aqueduct, bath. A plastered enclosure/reservoir lies 250 m south, 75×65 m. Hellenistic–Arab.
- 17 Kh. ed-Dashe: pool and feeder channel. Roman–Byzantine.
- 21 el-Masqara: channels of 60 m, 300 m and 2.5 km (about 0.8 m wide).

**Plateau west of Jericho (outside the held areas)**
- **35 Wady Dar el-Jerir caves** (1846 1480, p. 110):
  - Cave with natural pillars and vaults, steps beside it.
  - Cave with a high opening, 24 m long and about 12 m high.
  - Cave with a rock-cut plastered cistern, 3 m across and 5 m deep.
  - Open cave 10×4 m with an oven, a vaulted plastered cistern and a trough.
  - Five caves fully dug out.
  - Periods: Roman, Byzantine, Arab.
- **42** (1838 1474, p. 112): caves on the north slope of Wadi el-Waheita.
  - **One cave has two openings.** A stepped corridor and a narrow passage lead to a hall 15×12 m.
  - In the hall is a pool 3×3 m and 2.5 m deep, with plaster remains. The cave is wholly dug out.
  - Periods: Iron II, Roman, Byzantine.
- 28 (1803 1495): plastered caves and water cisterns, and a fort with a cistern at 1806 1492. Iron II, Roman–Byzantine.
- 38 Rujm er-Refeif: a rock-cut pool (1811 1475) and a cistern (1815 1474). Roman–Byzantine.
- 41 (1831 1476): round enclosure 22 m across, four rock-cut basins, possibly a high place. Iron II and Byzantine.
- 51 (1859 1463): building, cistern and cave.

**Buqeia, Wadi Qumran, Feshkha**
- **96** (1873 1260, p. 122):
  - Roman camp 106×76 m, opening on the west, walls 1.20 m.
  - To the south, a rock-cut cistern with a round mouth and troughs. **Paved channels reach the mouth; one is visible for about 500 m** (1868–1873 1260).
  - **Built pools** in the wadi to the south.
- **100** (187 125, p. 123): **two stone-built pools side by side west of the road.**
  - One square, 34×32 m, walls 1.8 m. One round, 34 m across, walls 0.8 m.
  - Round building east of the road. Pottery (item c only): Iron I, Iron II, Roman.
- **99** (1868 1252): pear-shaped enclosure 140×130 m with a blocked cistern, 2.2 m across at the top. Iron, Arab, Crusader.
- **104** Maghayir et-Tabaqa (1862 1231): Iron fort 33×32 m. East of it, a pool with an earth bank, 25 m across and 2 m deep. Iron–Arab, including Hellenistic and Roman.
- 102 Karm es-Samra: Iron fort 69×39.5 m with several water-catchment dams. Iron II, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine.
- 92 Kh. Abu Tabaq: Iron fort with a cistern on the north, and a wall about 1.2 km long. Iron, Hellenistic, Roman.
- **93–94** (1921–1929 1277, p. 121): buildings and a boulder wall on the **north bank of Wadi Qumran**, a possible watchtower. Iron. **No cairn or dam is described.**
- 98 (193 126):
  - Settlement, walls and a fort 40×60 m at 1933 1268, about 1 km south of Kh. Qumran. The p. 122 photo shows its south wall, a tower and steps.
  - Building 11.2×11.4 m; a wall about 350 m long. Iron and Roman.

**Wadi en-Nar (lower Kidron) to the Dead Sea**
- 105 (1858 1220): elliptical and round buildings (6×5.5, 2×1.6, 7×6.5 m) on the Wadi en-Nar bank. Roman.
- 110 Qasr er-Rubai': fort 42×28 m, standing up to 6 m. Roman and Byzantine.
- **113–115** (1907–1915 1207–1208):
  - 113: three roofed caves on the south bank of Wadi en-Nar, with built terraces in front. Iron, Roman–Byzantine, Arab.
  - 114 Kafr Wadi en-Nar: village with houses, caves, paths and built steps. Roman–Byzantine.
  - 115: three round stone buildings 2–2.5 m across on the cliff top at the Wadi Mazin gorge. Roman–Byzantine.
  - Bar-Adon calls these "round buildings", **not** tumuli.

**Dead Sea cliffs and the Tekoa–Rujm en-Naqa road (Puech's Tekoa–Herodium sector, entries 39–45)**
- **140 Kh. el-Miniyye** (170 114, about 2 km SSE of Tekoa, pp. 129–130): an ancient agricultural settlement.
  - Rock-cut winepress (treading floor 3×2.5 m; vat 1.3×0.8×1.3 m).
  - A second winepress complex about 450 m west, with a mosaic treading floor and four rock-cut channels feeding a pool.
  - Hilltop ruin inside a wall 0.9 m thick.
  - **18 + 14 farming terraces** on the two wadi slopes.
  - **Four cisterns:** about 5.2 m across × 4 m deep, rock-cut below and built above; 3.3 × 2.5 m; 3.5 × 1.3 m; 4 × 1 m.
  - **Water-channel network** running from 1704 1144 to 1714 1136.
  - Pottery: mainly Roman, Roman–Byzantine.
- 142, 146, 148: Roman camps and forts on the road (for example 'Urqub Sahba, 23×23.5 m). Kh. Sahba fort 19×18 m with rock-cut cup-holes.
- 144: rectangular building with a vaulted roof.
- Tumuli in 147b, 148b, 151a/e, 152, 154f. Mostly Roman; some Iron.
- 151 Kh. Deir 'Alla: cisterns and channels in the wadi bed.
- 154 Kh. Umm ez-Zuweitine: settlement of 13 buildings, rock-cut cup-holes, caves.
- **161b "Birket Dafura"** (1756 1084): plastered pool 15×7×6 m. Iron–Arab, including Roman.
- 182 (1761 1048): plastered cistern 10×3.5×3.5 m. Roman–Byzantine.
- 202 (182 099): rock-cut cistern 4×4 m, visible depth 1.5 m, with feeder channels.
- **Caves:**
  - 118: cliff cave above the Dead Sea, 30×20×15 m, with a long side branch and a pit.
  - 128a/b: four caves, one with an entrance 40 m wide. Iron, Roman, Byzantine.
  - **157**: cave in Wadi ed-Daraja, 50 m deep, with side niches and tunnels, dug by Bedouin. Probably the Murabba'at caves.
  - 164: five caves 10×8, 12×7, 13×5, 8×4 and 1.8×1 m. Roman.
  - 206c: Nahal David caves with organic finds.

**Peleg, Deir Hajleh** (EXPOSED; pp. 180–189 and 40*–41*):
- **Excavation north of the monastery:** a 4.00×2.00 m sounding. Wall W200, 0.80 m wide, survives one course high. Five layers, 0.15–0.40 m thick, include a burnt layer 1.80 m long.
- **Cemetery:** 2002 salvage excavation of about 28.50×22.50 m, NW of the monastery, reaching 1.80 m below the surface.
  - 42 graves with stone covers were exposed, in E–W rows. Graves in a row lie 1.10–2.50 m apart; rows lie 1.50–2.10 m apart.
  - 11 graves were excavated. They are 1.50–2.20 m long, about 0.50 m wide and about 0.30 m deep, each covered by 4–5 slabs. Dimensions for 11 individual graves are given on pp. 182–183.
  - The cemetery covers at least about 500 m².
- **Scaled figures:** Fig. 1 (0–20 m), Fig. 2 (0–5 m), Fig. 3 (0–10 m), and pottery plates.
- **Date:** Byzantine to Early Islamic, 6th–8th c. CE. **No Second Temple material is reported.**
