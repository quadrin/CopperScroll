## 3. Patrich, "The aqueducts of Hyrcania–Kastellion" (pp. 336–352), feature by feature

### 3.1 Setting, history and sources (p. 336)

- EVIDENCE, history:
  - Founded in the Hasmonean period by Alexander Jannaeus (103–74 BC), or possibly by John Hyrcanus (135–103).
  - One of three fortresses Salome Alexandra kept, with treasures. Destroyed in 57 by Gabinius on Pompey's orders. Retaken after a siege in 31 by Herod, who rebuilt it.
  - Not mentioned after Herod's death and not used in the Great Revolt, so it "seems to have been abandoned".
  - St Sabas founded a monastery in 492. It was deserted in the mid-14th c.
- EVIDENCE: the aqueduct remains represent three phases: Hasmonean, Herodian and Byzantine.
- EVIDENCE, research history:
  - Conder & Kitchener placed the source at Bir el Amara, east of Jebel Muntar.
  - Mader mentioned two channels. Wright thought there was one aqueduct, from the direction of Jebel Muntar.
  - Feldman (1971) first traced the S aqueduct from the Qidron and dug test pits. With the Survey Department he mapped its course and the cisterns and pools on the E and S slopes. He found that the N aqueduct starts at the Wadi Abu Shula dam, not at Bir el Amara.
  - Patrich re-examined the system in 1983 for the Mar Saba survey map.
- EVIDENCE, notes:
  - n. 1: NEAEHL 2, 639–41.
  - n. 2: SWP III 212.
  - n. 3: Mader, *JPOS* 9 (1929) 122.
  - n. 4: Wright, el-Mird, *Biblica* 42 (1961).
  - n. 5: Feldman, "The water system at Hyrcania fortress", in Ilan (ed.), 1974, 316–35 [Hebrew].
  - n. 6: Patrich, *Map of Deir Mar Saba (109/7)* (Jerusalem 1994) 13*, and sites 48, 62, 67–69, 83, 86, 87. B. Levenstein (surveyor) prepared the sections and plans; the photographs are the author's.

### 3.2 The two lines (pp. 336–338; Fig. 1)

- EVIDENCE: two aqueducts served the fortress in the Second Temple period.
  - The **N aqueduct is "the shorter and almost certainly the earlier"**: **1950 m**.
  - The **S aqueduct is about 9 km**.
  - Both are plastered throughout with white lime-and-fine-gravel plaster. They are rock-cut or dug according to the terrain (p. 336).
- EVIDENCE, S aqueduct route and geology:
  - It runs mostly through Senonian (Mishash/Menuha) limestone and has vanished where that rock has eroded.
  - It survives best at wadi crossings in hard Turonian Bina rock, which needed rock-cutting and high supporting walls.
  - **Channel c. 50 cm wide** (p. 337).
- EVIDENCE, N aqueduct geology: it runs mostly through gentle Menuha hills, and through Bina rock near the streambeds (p. 337).
- EVIDENCE: the lines **merge c. 750 m W of the fortress**.
  - At c. 500 m W, the friable Hatrurim strata needed a masonry-built channel.
  - **Three saddles** are crossed on thick walls acting as three bridges.
  - For most of this stretch the line runs near the **240 m asl** contour. It then drops sharply to the E saddle, **48 m below the summit (248 m asl)** (p. 337).
- EVIDENCE, the "Kidron and Muntar channels":
  - Fig. 1 labels the N line "W. Abu Shaaleh", with dam no. 44 at its head, coming from the Jebel el Muntar side (NW).
  - The S line starts on the "N. Qidron" W of Mar Saba (nos. 1–3) and winds E then NE (nos. 4–43).
  - The text spells the wadi "Abu Shula".

### 3.3 Phasing (pp. 337–338)

- EVIDENCE: the elevation of the E bridge set where the cisterns could be.
  - Its **lower courses (no. 48) are Hasmonean**. At that height the **four lower S-slope cisterns** could be filled from the N aqueduct.
  - The Hasmonean bridge was "apparently destroyed in 57".
- EVIDENCE: under Herod the **S aqueduct was built**, the E bridge was raised, and new cisterns were cut higher on the S and E slopes.
- EVIDENCE: when the fortress was abandoned, the Herodian E bridge partly collapsed, and the Byzantine level could not fill the upper cisterns.
  - The Byzantine work reactivated only the N aqueduct, without repairing the plaster.
  - It also reactivated a short S stretch "from no. 42".
  - The Byzantine channel is attached to the N face of the W bridge (no. 46) and at the head of the E bridge (no. 48). In between it ran lower than the earlier line and S of it.

### 3.5 N (Wadi Abu Shula / Muntar side) aqueduct (p. 343)

- EVIDENCE: it runs mostly through Menuha rock, rock-cut or built, and is visible for most of its length.
- EVIDENCE: it reached the junction (pt 43) much higher than the S line and lost height down to the bridge. Patrich infers a ***castellum*** at the junction; no remains are visible.
- **Pt 44, dam at the starting point**, EVIDENCE:
  - **17.5 m long, 4–5 m wide**; large stones, on a diagonal across Wadi Abu Shula.
  - It diverts water into the channel on the S bank. One course is visible.
  - It stands at the edge of a **waterfall c. 8 m high**; to the E the wadi deepens into a canyon.
- EVIDENCE: a cross is engraved c. 50 m from the dam, above the aqueduct, near a "black trail-mark painted on the rock". A second cross is lower down.
  - These may show Byzantine re-use of a line "apparently first built in the Hasmonean period".
  - No Byzantine repairs were seen.
- **Pt 45**, sd. c. 300 m from the dam, EVIDENCE:
  - Plastered bottom; lime-and-gravel plaster of Second Temple type.
  - **Channel c. 1 m wide**, almost double the S line, to take large brief floods.
  - Fig. 14: cross-section (0–2 m).

### 3.6 The three saddle bridges (pp. 343–348)

- **W bridge, pt 46**, EVIDENCE:
  - On the saddle at the 240 m asl contour.
  - Massive wall **2.35–2.75 m thick** of large, partly dressed stones (>50 cm), full thickness.
  - The upper (channel) part is missing, so the original height cannot be reconstructed.
  - **Overall length c. 150 m**. It bends slightly S at a point c. 80 m from the E end.
  - Remains of a **lime kiln** downstream N of the saddle, probably linked to building the aqueducts.
  - A **Byzantine channel c. 40 cm wide** is attached to the N side. It has reddish sherd-tempered plaster on stone-chip bedding. Its S side is brick-like flat stones; its N side reuses large stones from the earlier bridge top.
  - Its position on the N side shows its water came from the **Wadi Abu Shula** line.
  - From here the earlier line runs higher; the lower (Byzantine) one is marked by the footpath.
  - Figures: Fig. 15 photo (looking N); **Fig. 16** cross-sections (0–3 m; details 0–1 m).
- **Central bridge**, EVIDENCE (pp. 344–345):
  - **c. 50 m long**; Bedouin sheepfolds abut it; the footpath runs lower.
  - The Byzantine channel is not on its N face.
  - The centre is preserved **1.4 m** above the surface; the E end is **2.3 m** higher than the centre. So the supporting wall was **more than 3.7 m** high at its centre.
- **E bridge, pt 48**, EVIDENCE (pp. 345–348):
  - The saddle was **narrowed by quarrying** to a rock strip, which the path to the fortress also uses (Fig. 17).
  - The upper section is built throughout. The lower E part is a **rock-cut wall** (Fig. 18).
  - **Total length 112 m**. The W end (Fig. 19) lies W of the modern path crossing.
  - Superior construction near the fortress. In a few places (n. 7: mainly on the N side) the lower courses use a different technique, which may be **Hasmonean**. The rest is **Herodian**, with minor Byzantine repairs above.
  - **Hasmonean courses** (above the pools): the 4 lowest courses alternate headers and stretchers, courses c. 40 cm high.
    - Stretchers c. 75 cm, headers c. 30 cm.
    - Drafted margins 8–10 cm; central boss projecting 6 cm.
    - Also at the E and W ends of the built section on the N face (Fig. 20).
    - n. 8: a **50 cm opening** on this side allowed N–S passage during and after construction.
  - **Herodian courses**: almost isodomic, c. 40 cm high; stones 25–80 cm long, in three groups.
    - Margins with a smooth boss. Slight inward tilt every 2–3 courses.
    - Less precise jointing, with small flat stones as levellers. Mud-and-lime mortar.
    - Hatrurim-formation stone, hard to cut. Both dressings also occur at the fortress (Fig. 21).
  - **Quarrying**, EVIDENCE: the saddle and the area W were quarries for the aqueduct and the hilltop buildings. the pools north and south of the bridge were also quarry voids (p. 347, paraphrase).
  - **Levels**, EVIDENCE:
    - The Herodian channel lay above the present bridge top and is **not preserved**.
    - At the W end, above the pools, the bottom of the **Byzantine** channel (reddish plaster) is visible. The rock-cut Byzantine channel also survives at the E end.
    - There the rock wall is >3 m wide. Further E is a higher rock projection, **1.8 m wide**, whose top is **53 cm above** the W end.
    - N of it, remains of the earlier channel lie **70 cm higher** than the projection. So the Herodian channel base was **1.20 m above the present W end**.
    - Herodian bridge height to channel base originally **c. 12.5 m**. At the W cross-section (Fig. 18) it is now **c. 6 m** high, **2.5 m** at the base and **1.45 m** at the top.
    - Patrich suggests arches above, or alternatively a **metal siphon**.
    - Fig. 17 reconstruction (L. Ritmeyer) shows arches; it is a **published reconstruction**, not observed fabric.
  - Figures: Fig. 17 (aerial from the E plus Ritmeyer reconstruction); **Fig. 18** two sections (each 0–3 m); Fig. 19 (W end, looking N); Fig. 20 (headers and stretchers, with ranging pole); Fig. 21 (photo).

### 3.7 The pools (pp. 348, 351; Figs. 17, 22–24)

- **Northern (double) pool**, EVIDENCE, p. 348:
  - **Two pools N of the bridge, separated by a partition wall 4.8–5.4 m wide** (Fig. 22). They were created by quarrying stone for the bridge and the summit buildings.
  - Roughly square, with unequal sides. Walls not fully perpendicular; floors not level. The E side of both and the S side of the S pool slope inward.
  - The N pool is deeper and more regular. **W side of the S pool: max depth 2.6 m. W side of the N pool: 5 m** (the N pool lies lower on the saddle).
  - **N pool: 19 (S) × 15 (W) × 18.2 (N) × 16 m (E).**
  - Three parallel rows of rock-cut **benches** on the slope above suggest it was also a swimming pool. n. 9 compares Netzer's Hasmonean palace pools at Jericho: "Perhaps the pools at Hyrcania also date to the Hasmonean period".
  - Feldman's sondages found large patches of lime-and-gravel plaster, "typical of the Second Temple period", on the rock walls and floor. **Crosses are engraved** in a few places.
  - They served as supplementary reservoirs for the slope and summit cisterns, with water carried up **by pack animals**.
- EVIDENCE: a **round pit** NE of the N pool. Vertical walls, **diam. 3.05 m, depth 4.3 m**; purpose uncertain (Fig. 23 photo).
- EVIDENCE: Feldman found **3 lime kilns** near the fortress.
- EVIDENCE: on the E side of the pools, a footpath crosses the aqueduct's rock wall by a **rock-cut passage**. n. 10 compares the King's Tombs in Jerusalem.
- **Southern pool**, EVIDENCE (pp. 348, 351):
  - A marked depression bounded on its W by a vertical rock-cut wall.
  - No plaster visible on the wall; some may survive lower, beneath the modern ground surface.
  - "This area with the N pools served as a kind of moat" against approach from the W ridge.
- **Second built pool** SW of cistern O, EVIDENCE (p. 351, n. 12; Fig. 24):
  - Rectangular, with stairs on its S wall; possibly a swimming pool like Jericho's. Later opening in the NW corner.
  - To its W are a channel with Byzantine plaster and a second, wider channel with almost sherd-free plaster; purpose uncertain.
  - Fig. 24 is a plan with a north mark; **no scale bar was seen** in the photo.
- Conclusion, EVIDENCE (p. 352): the flow under Herod let the N pools work as swimming pools, "with seats for spectators nearby".

### 3.8 Cisterns and the built tunnel (pp. 351–352; Figs. 22, 25–28)

- EVIDENCE: after the bridge, the line splits into **N and S branches** encircling the hill.
  - The N branch supplied the **E cisterns**.
  - On the S side Feldman found **two aqueducts at different levels**, but only one could cross the bridge at a time.
  - **Lower = Hasmonean bridge level; upper = Herodian.** Patrich assumes a secondary channel (not yet located) carried water from the upper S branch down to the lower cisterns in Herodian times.
- EVIDENCE, totals (after Feldman): **20 rock-cut cisterns + 2 built pools; 20,000 m³**.
  - Thick hydraulic lime-and-gravel plaster; cracks filled with small stones.
  - Distribution: **5 on the hilltop**, 8 on the upper S line, 4 on the lower S line, 3 on the E slope.
- EVIDENCE, hilltop cisterns:
  - The hilltop five (**K1, X, P, L, H**) were filled by drawn water or roof and courtyard run-off, not by the aqueducts.
  - **P** has a fine built vault and steps, perhaps a miqveh.
  - **K1** has its upper opening on the SW of the hilltop. n. 11: Arabic, Greek and Syriac papyri of the 7th–9th c. were found in it in 1952–53.
  - **Cave K** (Feldman's "cistern no. 21") has a low entrance, no draw opening and no channel. It is a Byzantine funerary chapel, probably not a cistern.
- EVIDENCE: the **lower S branch → J, E, O, N** ("presumably Hasmonean"). J lies much lower than the others.
- EVIDENCE: the **upper S branch → A, A1, M, B, C, D, F, G**. It ends at an open pool with plastered sides and a thick E wall, now filled with débris.
- **Point 49, built tunnel**, EVIDENCE (p. 351):
  - "Between cisterns A and N the aqueduct passes through a built tunnel".
  - Débris-free part **c. 11.5 m long, 1.25 m high, 0.75 m wide**; long **barrel vault**.
  - **Square opening at the N end; S end blocked by débris.**
  - Fig. 25a: cross-section, **scale 0–1 m** (labelled "מ'"), captioned in the upper channel on the S slope. Fig. 25b: photo with a scale bar.
  - Fig. 22 marks "tunnel" W of cisterns A/A1 on the channel line.

### 3.9 Conclusion (pp. 351–352)

- EVIDENCE: the system is among the longest and most complex at the desert fortresses. It is Hasmonean in origin and peaked under Herod.
- EVIDENCE: there are **six high bridges**: three over deep streambeds on the S line (pts 29, 34, 40) and three on the shared saddles (46, central, 48).
- EVIDENCE: the bridges were the "Achilles' heel". Upkeep difficulties were possibly a factor in abandonment after Herod.
- EVIDENCE: the author's affiliation is "Department of Archaeology, University of Haifa".
