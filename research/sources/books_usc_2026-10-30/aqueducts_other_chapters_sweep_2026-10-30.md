## 2. Most consequential findings (summary)

1. **Entry 31 (Doq), EXPOSED.**
   - The 2002 English Fig. 2 (p. 331) labels the double line **"Siege wall"**. The repo's Hebrew 1989 extraction calls the same feature a "dike" (דייק) and treats it, with its two "towers", as undiscussed.
   - The 2002 n. 5 (p. 332) names the 1982 excavation team: E. Netzer with R. Bar Natan, R. Birger and the author.
   - Every cistern-table value on p. 335 matches the repo's 1989 table.
2. **Entry 42 (Tekoa–Herodium sector): a dam next to a conduit bend now exists in the sector, but it is dated outside the window.**
   - Amit pp. 262–266 describe "Aqueduct C" from ʿEin Urtas down Wadi et-Tawahin toward Nahal Thecoa.
   - The mill pool's N wall "was in fact a dam" (3.5 m high, 2.5 m thick, 27.5 m long; map ref. 1697 1205).
   - A sharp bend of the valley follows at point 38 (1701 1204).
   - Date: Byzantine by plaster, with a Mamluk mill inscription of 1306 debated.
   - The ʿArrub line also has dams at wadi crossings (p. 215). The aqueduct is Pilate-dated, but the dams are undated or "perhaps later".
3. **Entry 22 ("Solomon" reservoir)**: p. 230 n. 32 confirms that the name Solomon's Pools is a western travellers' identification and that "Burak Suleiman" is secondary. The full pool dimensions are now first-hand. No fissure is recorded.
4. **Entry 32 (ʿArrub/ʿEin Kuweiziba lead)**: p. 213 confirms that the ʿArrub begins next to ʿEin Kuweiziba (point 1, 830 m asl). **No outlet, wall or installation at the head is described.** The lead is not advanced.
5. **Entry 45 / water lane correction**: Chariton's large reservoir (Bir el-ʿUneiziya, 14 × 19.6 m) was filled by a channel c. 60 m long diverting floodwater from the ravine (p. 431). The water lane's statement that the Chariton channels collect only slope or roof run-off is wrong for this reservoir, but the reservoir is Byzantine.
6. **Dating of the Jerusalem system (bears on Temple/Jerusalem entries)**:
   - Low-level aqueduct: Jannaeus–Herod. Coins of Antiochus IV, Herod and Jannaeus ×3; a 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE bowl sealed under the bedding (Billig pp. 248–249).
   - High-level: Herodian arcade, then a Tenth Legion stone pipe 150–200 years later (Amit p. 254).
   - Biyar: Herodian (Amit p. 256).
   - ʿArrub: Pilate (p. 17; Mazar p. 237).
   - The Herodion aqueduct starts at Solomon's Pools, not ʿEin Urtas (Amit pp. 258–260).

### 3.2 Mazar, "Appendix: the aqueduct to Herodion", pp. 243–244 (IMG_2383–84), read from the photo

- EVIDENCE:
  - Begins at the Urtas spring (map ref. 1678 1217). Schick claimed a link with the Low-level aqueduct; there are no remains of it today.
  - First remains at the village exit, 690 m (point 60). Runs along the N slope of Wadi et-Tawahin to Dhar Khalet el-Jammaʿ (point 64).
  - Rock-cut channel **85 cm wide, 1.25 m deep** at 1680 1218. Mostly like the Low-level aqueduct (**45 × 75 cm**). Rock cuttings c. 1 m wide for built sections.
  - Point 62 (1697 1221) preserved entire, like the early ʿArrub.
  - A gorge E of Khalet el-Jammaʿ (1706 1215) was crossed on a bridge or high terrace; nothing remains.
  - Beyond: narrow rock-cut sections ≤20 cm. Built sections at small-gorge crossings (point 66) and W of Kh. a-Natesh to Khalet umm ed-Duyuf.
  - Disappears at **1726 1198, c. 670 m**, c. 800 m from Herodion.
  - Final destination: "the built square pool at the foot of the fortress". Water then carried uphill on animals to fill **wells (cisterns) in the side of the mountain** (n. 71 Netzer 1981).
- **Fig. 25**, p. 244: sections at point 65, between points 66 and 67c, point 67 and point 68. Scale **0–0.5 m**, N/S and W/E labels.
- Entries 42, 44 and 45 (Tekoa–Herodium sector). INFERENCE: the mountain-side cisterns at Herodium were filled by hand, not by channels. That is weak for entry 45's "cistern fed by channels". ### 3.4 Amit, "New data for dating the High-level aqueduct and the Wadi el-Biyar aqueduct, and the Herodion aqueduct", pp. 253–266 (IMG_2388–95), read from the photo

**High-level (pp. 253–256):**
- A stone wall c. 3 m wide (OCR p. 253) runs c. 100 m parallel to the stone pipeline.
- It is built in alternating sections, c. 3 m solid mortared and c. 4 m dry. These are the **bases of arcade piers**; the arcade may have run c. 2 km along the valley.
- The original Herodian solution was the arcade. The **Tenth Legion stone pipeline came 150–200 years later** (Fig. 4, schematic, north arrow, no scale).
- n. 6: excavated by H. Hizmi; the pier bases were **destroyed in April 1994**. Dinur's 1986 probe is unpublished.
- Mamilla (Maeir 1989): a Herodian aqueduct "rides" on a Hasmonean structure, apparently a tower, with Byzantine repairs.
- Conclusion: High-level = Herodian.

**Biyar (p. 256):**
- 2.8 km mostly underground; drained an aquifer; collected floodwater with **a dam and shafts**.
- Dated via its link to the Herodion aqueduct.

**Herodion aqueduct source (pp. 256–260):**
- Fig. 5 (p. 257): ʿEin Urtas plan by Z. D. Ron, **scale 0–4 m, north arrow**. Labels: 1 entrance pit; 2 tunnel outlet into the pool; 3 modern reservoir; 4 exit to the irrigation channels.
- Fig. 6: the large pool at the foot of Herodion (Netzer).
- The Herodion aqueduct is appreciably **higher than the spring**. The original difference was **5.9 m**; even after the spring was raised c. 4 m it remains **c. 1.5 m higher**. ʿEin Urtas is 6 km from Herodion as the crow flies; the aqueduct is 8–9 km.
- The 1970–71 survey found **15 short ruined sections over c. 1.5 km** between the Lower Pool of Solomon's Pools and ʿEin Urtas, **0.2–0.5 m wide**.
- So the Herodion line began at Solomon's Pools. It is a dual-purpose Herodian system, as at Cypros/Jericho (held; mention only).

**Aqueduct C from ʿEin Urtas (pp. 261–266):**
- Leaves ʿEin Urtas on a lower level than the Herodion line (Fig. 8). Its first goal was **Ḥammam Suliman**, a Herodian bath-house dug by E. Finn in 1861.
- The continuation east has plaster on both sides of the channel, "characteristic of the Byzantine period" (Porath).
- PEF: the line ran to et-Tahuneh, a flour mill with a **pool 12.2 × 27.5 m**.
- The pool's **N wall "was in fact a dam"**: **3.5 m high, 2.5 m thick, 27.5 m long** (PEF); only c. 20 m survives. Built of large roughly dressed blocks bonded with mortar; inner arch; breach in the centre.
- NW corner preserved; W wall visible c. 8 m; at its S end a supporting wall arrives from the west, so the aqueduct emptied into the pool here.
- Inner face of the N wall: plaster c. 5 cm thick. Map ref. **1697 1205**, where a minor tributary from the west meets Wadi Urtas/et-Tahuneh (Fig. 9).
- Mosque plaque: the mill was built in **1306** (706 AH) for the Hebron Waqf. Avitsur ties the plaque to this mill; Amit says the plaque is 3 km away and the aqueduct and mill are "more likely" Byzantine.
- The aqueduct continues E: a supporting wall a few hundred m long; remains 1 km on; a built section 2 km further, E of the Herodion–Thecoa road near the start of the Nahal Thecoa (Wadi Khureitun) gorge (found by N. Krispil).
- Amit's suggestion: it went c. 3 km on to the **Chariton monastery**, but no remains were found within the monastery. Alternative: Byzantine Herodion (a boundary wall; churches).
