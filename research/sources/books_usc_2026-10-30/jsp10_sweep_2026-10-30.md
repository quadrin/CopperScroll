## 1. B6: Four graves in the Hyrcania Valley (Peleg), Hebrew pp. 259–260, English 29*

Photos IMG_1274 (pp. 258–259) and IMG_1275 (pp. 260–261); English IMG_1296 (28*–29*).

- **EVIDENCE (p. 259).** Exposed during IDF earthworks c. 1 km north of Kh. Karm Abu Ṭabaq; ITM 23757/62886, IOG 18757/12886; March 2004; licence 996 (p. 260 n. 1). Four pit graves cut in the bedrock, aligned E–W, each for one person, covered with stone slabs. Nothing marked them on the surface. Tombs 1–2 survived whole; only the eastern parts of 3–4 survived (29*).
- **Grave 1** (southernmost): 0.70 m below the surface; 1.85 (or 1.89; the last digit is unclear on the photo) × 0.35 m, 0.35 m deep; three capstones in situ. Child aged 8–9, articulated, on the back, head at the W end, hands crossed. No finds.
- **Grave 2**: c. 3 m NW of Grave 1; 0.70 m below the surface; 1.50 × 0.40 m, 0.35 m deep; six capstones (Fig. 3). Male aged 50–60, same position. No finds.
- **Grave 3** (p. 260): 0.80 m below the surface; surviving length 0.50 m, width 0.40 m, depth 0.40 m. Male aged c. 40.
- **Grave 4**: c. 0.40 m N of Grave 3; 0.90 m below the surface; surviving 0.35 × 0.45 m, 0.35 m deep. Male aged c. 20.
- **Dating (p. 260; 29*).** There are no finds. The body position (on the back, head west, hands crossed) leads the author to suggest Christian burials, Byzantine *if* that is right. This dating is conditional, not stratified.
- **Fig. 1 (p. 259)**: plan and section 1–1 of Graves 1–4, with a scale bar 0–5 m and a north arrow. Figs. 2–3 are photos; Fig. 3 has a scale rod.

**Candidate check.**
- INFERENCE: the printed point converts (EPSG:2039) to 31.75223 N, 35.39562 E. That is 1.54 km NNW of the repo's Kh. Abu Ṭabaq (ASI 109/7-35, 31.74153/35.40589): 1.19 km N and 0.97 km W. "c. 1 km north of Kh. Karm Abu Ṭabaq" is consistent with Karm/Kh. Abu Ṭabaq being the same place or adjacent; the SWP "Kurm Abu Tubk" small cave is already in the repo (features_all SWP3-213).
- The site is 3.84 km from the `buqeia` anchor (area c. 5 km), inside the plateau area, and 4.64 km from the `hyrcania` point. Repo records within 1 km: ASI 109/7/9 / WBADB S3771 Wadi Khashna (dam, cistern, reservoir, aqueduct) at 0.59 km. That record is not in JSP 10.
- **Entries 1 and 17 (buqeia).** The site is not a ruin with steps, a pair of ruins, cavities or buildings. It is undated, or Byzantine by inference. **The report does not mention Kh. Abu Ṭabaq's reservoir, its steps or any finds there.** It does not meet entry 1's `decisive_next` (a dated record of Kh. Abu Ṭabaq Site 35). Proposed effect: none. Record it as a negative or irrelevant record in the plateau, with nothing in the 1st c. BCE–1st c. CE window.
- **Tomb entries.** Entry 24 (`kuteif`) is 5.7 km away, outside its c. 3 km area. No Buqeiʿa tomb entry is scored in the ratings. No effect.

## 2. B5: Early Islamic graves at Wadi Ekteif (Peleg), Hebrew pp. 257–258, English 29*

Photos IMG_1273 (pp. 256–257) and IMG_1274 (pp. 258–259); English IMG_1296.

- **EVIDENCE (p. 257).** Found while Mekorot laid a pipeline north of the Jerusalem–Jericho road; ITM 23915/63415, IOG 18915/13425; March 2003; licence 923. The trench crosses a saddle c. 30 m above the north bank of Wadi Ekteif (Qaṭif). Four pit graves under stone covering slabs, with no surface markers. Grave I is on the SW side of the trench and Graves II–IV are on the NE side.
- **Grave I**: 0.30 m below the surface; 1.70 × 0.40 m, 0.35 m deep. Male over 50, articulated, head at the SW end, face to the S. No finds.
- **Grave II** (p. 258): 0.15 m below the surface; surviving 1.25 × 0.50 m, 0.40–0.50 m deep; the S part was damaged in the dig. Adult male over 60 and a child aged 1.5–2. Offerings: a cooking pot and 27 beads (glass and faience in Hebrew; "27 glass beads" in English).
- **Grave III**: c. 3.20 m SE of II; 0.70 m below the surface; surviving 0.95 × 0.30 m, 0.30 m deep. Child aged 2–3, with a glass bracelet of 3.5 cm diameter.
- **Grave IV**: c. 2.50 m SE of III (the southernmost); 0.50 m below the surface; 1.55 × 0.30 m, 0.30 m deep. Two children, aged about ½ and 5–7.
- **Dating (29*).** The orientation (heads SW, faces S) is read as Muslim, Early Islamic (7th–11th c. CE), and as part of a larger cemetery.
- **Fig. 1 (p. 257)**: plan of the excavated trench and sections 1-1 to 4-4, with a scale bar and a north arrow.

**Candidate check.**
- INFERENCE: the site is at 31.79991/35.41240, 0.27 km from the `kuteif` anchor (entry 24, area c. 3 km) and 0.10 km from WBADB E508 "Wadi Ekteif" (tombs.csv T0532). E508 is this same excavation, so it is the same observation and not new corroboration.
- **Entry 24.** This is the primary publication behind the D_basis statement that the only dated tombs in the wadi are Early Islamic. Proposed effect: add the citation "JSP 10 pp. 257–258, 29*" to D_basis for 24/kuteif and to record_conflicts C22. No level change. The report describes no stepped chamber and does not touch the SWP/Milik "enigmatical" chamber.

## 3. A10: Hiding complex at Kh. el-Ḥanzir (Batz), Hebrew pp. 128–132, English 12*–13*

Photos IMG_1210 (pp. 128–129), IMG_1211 (pp. 130–131) and IMG_1212 (pp. 132–133); English IMG_1304 (12*–13*).

- **EVIDENCE.** ITM 19788/61194, IOG 14788/11194. The site covers 7.5 acres with columbarium-type caves, bell caves used as cisterns, and underground caves and tunnels. Surveyed in Nov 2003 after looting. Pottery runs from the Hellenistic to the Byzantine period, mostly Early Roman to the 2nd c. CE.
- **Layout.** The complex is entered from a basement cut under a 10.00 × 6.50 m field-stone building. It is about 55 m long overall.
- **Phases (p. 129; 12*).** Phase 1, apparently Hellenistic–Early Roman: the cisterns and some cavities. Phase 2, late Early Roman to the end of the Bar Kokhba revolt: the tunnels and extra cavities.
- **Entry Cavity I.** In phase 1 a reservoir of 11.00 × 4.00 m, c. 9.00 m estimated depth. It was reached from a doorway in the building floor by a rock-cut staircase of 2.00 × 0.85 m with four steps, which reached only the middle of the wall's height. The walls carry hydraulic plaster. Three ceiling shafts (c. 0.95 m diameter each) served for drawing water. In phase 2 it became the entrance hall: the shafts were sealed with slabs and an entrance to the complex was cut in its wall.
- **Cistern III (pp. 129, 131; 13*).** In phase 1 a reservoir c. 9.00 m in diameter and 8.00 m estimated depth, with hydraulic plaster and a **spiral staircase of at least 27 steps**. The plaster patches show renewal and continued use in the Early Roman period. In phase 2, tunnel openings were cut in the cistern wall and at a hidden point under the staircase (Fig. 2 section 3-3; Fig. 6). The author compares Hellenistic cisterns at Maresha (Kloner 2003, Complexes 70 and 21).
- **Other cavities.**
  - Cavity IV: c. 7.00 m diameter, with a pillar c. 0.90 m in diameter.
  - Cavity VI: 3.00 × 2.40 m.
  - Cistern VII: the pit bottom reaches the water so it could be drawn without going outside, with a spiral stair around the exit (13*).
  - Cistern X: karstic, 14.50 × 12.50 m, plastered.
  - Tunnel 2 is the longest: 9.00 × 0.60 m, c. 0.70 m high.
- **Fittings.** Stone-door closures with rebates and bolt holes. Triangular or round lamp niches.
- **Finds.** Second half of the 1st c. CE to the 2nd c. CE (13*).
- **Figures.**
  - Fig. 1 (p. 128): plan with a scale bar 0–5 m and a north arrow.
  - Fig. 2 (p. 130): sections 1-1, 2-2 and 3-3, with a scale bar 0–3 m. Section 3-3 draws Cistern III's spiral stair.
  - Figs. 3–6: photos.

**Candidate check.**
- INFERENCE: the site is at 31.59958/34.97709, 25 km from the nearest candidate (`natuf`). It is not a candidate and is outside every search radius.
- **Entry 5 (jer_temple, Puech/Lefkovits "staircase").** This is a dated regional comparandum for a cistern with a spiral stair cut round its wall. The type is Hellenistic and in use in the Early Roman period, with Maresha parallels; the same family as the Tell Sandahannah Fig. 102 noted in the entry 5 F_basis. Proposed effect: append it to F_basis as a type comparandum outside the enclosure. It shows that a spiral stair into a cistern is a regional Hellenistic type and not unique. No level change. It gives no placement under the Temple Mount.
- **Entries 39–45 (cisterns, chambers or tunnels in the Tekoa–Herodium sector).** Outside the sector. No effect.
- **Data.** columbaria.csv W-S5981 (Kh. el-Ḥanzir, "Batz 2009b") is this report. JSP 10 does not date the columbarium caves themselves, so `dated_in_window` stays unknown.

## 4. A9: Kh. Umm el-ʿAṣafir (Batz and Greenfeld), Hebrew pp. 84–127, English 10*–11*

Photos opened: IMG_1188–IMG_1206 (pp. 84–121); English IMG_1305 (10*–11*). Key pages: columbarium pp. 86–87 (IMG_1189), Cave 12 pp. 90–91 (IMG_1191), miqveh pp. 99–100 (IMG_1195, IMG_1196), sections p. 103 (IMG_1197). Pp. 106–127 are finds catalogues and plates, scanned for features only; pp. 124–127 were not opened.

- **EVIDENCE.** ITM 22114/62506, IOG 17114/12506; licence 956; Sept–Oct 2002.
- **Bathhouse, Early Roman phase (pp. 99–100; 11*): ritual bath I.**
  - Entrance 2.50 × 4.00 m. 13 rock-cut, plastered steps: 7 in the entrance and the rest inside the bath (Fig. 17). Most steps are c. 0.50 m wide (tread) and c. 0.30 m high; steps 2, 8, 10 and 13 are c. 0.80 m.
  - At the 7th step a partition c. 0.40 m wide was built, of which only its plaster negatives survive (p. 100). The authors read it as an entry/exit divider.
  - The immersion chamber is trapezoidal, with parallel sides of 4.60 and 5.00 m, 4.80 m apart. Its doorway (c. 2.20 m wide, 2.00 m high) is in the SW side. Two steps in the N corner measure 1.20 × 4.10 m, c. 0.80 m deep. Grey hydraulic plaster (F185).
  - The authors class it with the Jerusalem miqveh type (Mt Zion, Tombs of the Kings; Reich).
  - Late Roman: a bathhouse with hypocaust was built partly over the ritual bath. Byzantine: a 16.50 × 7.00 m pedestal over it.
- **Bathhouse figures.**
  - Fig. 14 (p. 100): phase plan with a scale bar 0–5 m, a north arrow and a phase key.
  - Fig. 15 (p. 103): sections 1-1 to 5-5 with an absolute level ladder of c. 657–660 m and a scale bar 0–5 m.
  - Fig. 16 (p. 104): isometric reconstruction.
  - Figs. 17–22: photos.
- **Cave 3, the columbarium (pp. 86–87; 10*).** A natural cave enlarged to 15.00 × 14.00 m maximum, with two chambers and two entrances (one for people, one probably for the doves).
  - The dove entrance (L162) was deliberately blocked in the 4th c. CE.
  - The roof collapsed in the Byzantine period, with columbarium niches found in the debris lying directly on the floor (F182).
  - The authors cite Hellenistic–Early Roman parallels from the Shephelah and Jerusalem (Kloner) but give no cutting date for Cave 3 in the pages read. INFERENCE: its use in the window is possible but not shown.
  - Fig. 4 (p. 86): plan and section 1-1, with a scale bar 0–5 m and a north arrow.
- **Cave 12 (pp. 90–91; 11*).** Karstic, 14.50 × 5.00 m.
  - Cistern III in its western part is 6.00 m in diameter and 6.00 m in estimated depth; its quarrying looks unfinished.
  - The authors date the hiding use to the Early Roman period because the cistern's plaster matches the grey hydraulic plaster of the ER miqveh, and because a cistern mouth was closed with a slab when the cave became a hiding place.
  - A storage installation of 1.20 × 1.60 m and 1.30 m high, with a Hiding-Complex parallel (Kloner and Tepper 1987).
  - Fig. 6 (p. 88): plans and sections of Caves 12–16, with a scale bar 0–5 m.
- **Burial Cave 2 (10*).** Iron IIB–III. Kokhim were cut under the ledges in a Hellenistic–Early Roman second phase.

**Candidate check.**
- INFERENCE: the site is at 31.7181/35.2222. It is 2.45 km from `ramat_rahel` (entry 46), 4.57 km from `jer_baqa` (36–37) and 6.8 km from `jer_temple`.
- **Entry 46.** The water lane's B-RamatRahel branch matches within 1 km (sensitivity 2 km). At 2.45 km the ER miqveh and the plastered Cave 12 cistern fall **outside both radii**, so they cannot serve as a candidate. They can serve as a dated control and comparandum: a pool with a recorded way in (13 steps and a doorway), in the window, 2.45 km away.
- PROPOSAL: no rating change. Optionally note under 46 U_basis that the nearest dated stepped pool found in this sweep lies 2.45 km away, outside the 2 km sensitivity radius. The existing exploratory lead is Armon ha-Naẓiv at 1.94 km.
- **Data correction (proposal).** columbaria.csv W-S4114 (Umm el-ʿAṣafir) carries `date_code U`. JSP 10 adds the 4th c. CE blocking and the Byzantine collapse, but no cutting date. `dated_in_window` stays unknown, citing pp. 86–87.

## 5. B12: Kh. Gharib, Early Roman burial cave and ritual bath (Peleg), Hebrew pp. 270–272, English 32*–33*

Photos IMG_1280 (pp. 270–271) and IMG_1281 (pp. 272–273); English IMG_1294 (32*–33*).

- **EVIDENCE.** The site is at ITM 21485/62122, IOG 16485/12111. It lies c. 1.2 km NE of Neve Daniel and c. 1.5 km SW of Kafr al-Khadr. Recorded in March 2000.
- **Burial cave (p. 270): ITM 214820/621208.** Cut E–W.
  - Corridor II: 2.60 × 1.30 m, 0.70 m high at its walls.
  - Courtyard I: 3.90 × 2.90 m, with a narrow bench 0.30 m wide and 0.25 m high on the N side.
  - Opening of 1.20 × 2.10 m into the vestibule III, which is 3.40 × 3.40 m and 2.35 m high.
  - A framed entrance of 0.50 × 0.45 m, with three steps of 1.00 × 1.25 m.
  - Burial chamber IV: floor 1.10 m below the threshold; 3.40 × 3.40 × 1.80 m; three ledges 1.00 m wide and 0.90 m high, with a repository.
  - Fig. 1 (p. 271): plan and section with a scale bar and a north arrow. Fig. 2 is a photo.
- **Ritual bath (p. 272): ITM 214740/621215.** In the centre of the site, by the SE corner of the terraced building.
  - Opening of 0.70 × 0.75 m. A narrow corridor of 1.80 × 0.35 m roofed with large slabs. Five steps (0.20 m high) are cut along the corridor.
  - The immersion chamber (1.90 × 1.90 m, 1.95 m maximum height) is cut beyond the corridor, with rounded corners and a flat ceiling. Two steps run across the chamber's width: 0.20 m high, with the upper one 0.40 m high.
  - All of it is coated with hydraulic plaster.
  - Fig. 3 (p. 272): plan and section 1-1, with a scale bar 0–5 m and a north arrow.
- **Dating (p. 272).** Early Roman, from the comparison with Gush Etzion miqvaʾot and 1st c. CE tombs (Kh. ʿAlya; Amit 1999; Peleg and Amit 2004).

**Candidate check.**
- INFERENCE: the site is at 31.68346/35.15586. It is 8.67 km from `ramat_rahel` and 8.7 km from `natuf`. Not a candidate and outside every radius.
- Use: a small-miqveh control (stepped corridor plus chamber) for stepped-installation entries. Proposed effect: none.
