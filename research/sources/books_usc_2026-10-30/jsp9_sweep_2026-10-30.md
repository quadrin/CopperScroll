**INFERENCE (date quality).** The Early Roman date of L112/L113 rests on one 1st-c. BCE cooking pot. The text does not place it in the channel; the material from the channel floor is unpublished and the coin is unidentified. There is no sealed context. A firmer stratigraphic fact is that W8 (7th–8th c.) overlies the channel, so the channel went out of use before then. The Area B aqueduct is explicitly undated.

The report is by A. Reuben and Y. Peleg. Hebrew pp. 170–179 (IMG_1403–1407; pp. 170–175 read); English p. 39* (IMG_1448).

| Page | EVIDENCE |
|---|---|
| p. 170 | A test excavation in June 2000 on four plots west of the road, along a planned water pipe. The site is at the end of a spur sloping NE, **south of Wadi Samiya**. Several springs flow **to the NW**; the road through the site leads to them. |
| p. 170, history | Lyon 1908 p. 66 reported **kokhim tombs**. The Mandate Department surveyed the site in 1929 and noted caves and building remains. Dajani (1947) excavated briefly and found a mosaic floor, probably a Byzantine church. Kallai 1972 p. 173 (site 66) recorded column remains, bases and ashlars. **Zissu proposed identifying the site with Koḥlit** (Zissu 2001). Kh. el-Marjama, across the wadi to the NW, is an Iron Age site with remains of a Byzantine church at its foot (Mazar 1992). Dhahr Mirzbaneh, **north of ʿEin Samiya and about 0.5 km east of Kh. el-Marjama**, is a large MB I cemetery excavated by Lapp (1966). Another cemetery of that period lies SE of the site (Yeivin 1992). |
| pp. 172–174 | **Area A:** poor wall stubs (W1 is 0.30 m wide, preserved 1.40 m long and 0.50 m high). **Area B:** part of a structure; **L108** is a hearth 1.00 m across, lined with ashlars, with a game board nearby. It is Mamluk (13th–14th c.), possibly still used in the Ottoman period. **Area C:** a Byzantine (6th c.) building with wall W4 measuring 9.00 × 0.70 m; it was reused in the Early Islamic period up to the 8th c. **Area D:** stones from one sounding (L109), with no architectural context. |
| p. 174 | Pottery is Byzantine, Umayyad, Mamluk and Ottoman. **Nothing earlier than the Byzantine period is reported from the excavation.** |

**EVIDENCE:** no pool, cistern, pit or water installation was found in the four excavated areas.

**INFERENCE:**
1. The report gives the springs' bearing from the site as **NW**. Any spring-fed basin would therefore lie NW of Kh. Samiya, not east. This bears on Entry 11 ("pool to the east") for a Samiya-centred model. It does not touch the `samiya-kallai` assignment, which was already contradicted on 19 Oct (Kallai's pool lies west of the Marjama tell).
2. "Graves to the north" of ʿEin Samiya are confirmed only as **MB I** (Dhahr Mirzbaneh), which is outside the window. The Early Roman kokhim rest on Lyon 1908 alone, with no position given.
3. Excavation reach: a narrow road strip only. Absence of window-period remains is **not** evidence that the site lacks them.

**PROPOSAL:** no change to the Koḥlit chain or the rarity count. Optionally add the source to `kohlit_chain` records for Kh. Samiyye with the EXPOSED tag. The Lyon 1908 kokhim and Zissu 2001 are the citations to chase.
