### Priority sites
- **3 Chariton / Souka (Kh. Khureitun), pp. 8-12, grid (1727/1172).**
  - EVIDENCE: Vita: 14 stadia (about 2.6 km) from Tekoa.
  - EVIDENCE: Guérin described mainly the large reservoir "Bir el-ʿUneizieh". PEF judged most remains Crusader "except for the reservoir, which they dated to the Byzantine period" (p. 10).
  - EVIDENCE: the author's survey made "precise measurements" (Fig. 9, scale 0-50 m: two towers, garden, cells; inset with Reservoir and the Hanging Cave). Ceramics show the core and garden buildings are Byzantine (p. 11).
  - EVIDENCE: the Hanging Cave is c. 800 m S of the laura's kernel, has crosses and Greek monograms, and can be reached only by ladder (p. 11). It later became a memorial site (p. 12).
  - EVIDENCE: ʿAin en-Naṭuf is not named, and no dovecote, columbarium or regular rock opening is described.
- **16 Sabas (Mar Saba), pp. 31-33, (1815/1236).**
  - EVIDENCE: Cyril calls the site "a gorge descending from the Siloam". The Kidron canyon section is Wadi er-Rahib.
  - EVIDENCE: the cells are on the E bank, and the churches and community buildings on the W bank (Norris). Patrich documented 45 dwelling complexes.
  - EVIDENCE: Fig. 36 is Vailhé's plan of the churches with "Sabas' tomb" (no scale seen). Fig. 37 is the Great Laura sketch plan (0-500 m): Sabas' cave at spot height 268, Hostel, Small Coenobium.
  - EVIDENCE: no cistern, channel, cairn, dam or pre-Byzantine phase is mentioned. Nothing covers the lower Kidron or Wadi en-Nar below the monastery.
- **19 Mikron, pp. 34-35, (1821/1250).** EVIDENCE: 10 stadia (approximately 1.8 km) from the Great Laura's hospice. Corbo places it on a hill approximately 2 km NE, with a "square structure, measuring 40 m. on each side" containing "a water cistern", tesserae and Byzantine sherds. On PEF Sheet XVIII the cistern is marked "vault".
- **6 Euthymius (Khan el-Ahmar), pp. 15-18.**
  - EVIDENCE: 3 miles (approximately 4.4 km) from Theoctistus. Euthymius first lived "in a small cave in the center of the plain".
  - EVIDENCE: Fig. 18 (0-100 m) shows a rectangular "Reservoir" E of the monastery, several cisterns, cells, a garden and terraces.
  - EVIDENCE: most remains, including the church, are of the 8th-12th c. Only the crypt "might well be dated to the Byzantine period" (p. 18).
- **4 Theoctistus (Deir Muqallik), pp. 12-13, (1859/1318).**
  - EVIDENCE: Cyril: "a ravine south of the Jericho road", 14.8 km E of Jerusalem. The founders first lived in "a steep cave".
  - EVIDENCE: Chitty's cave-church plan includes a "vertical shaft leading to a cave at a higher level".
  - EVIDENCE: the 1987 survey recorded a retaining wall 95 m long, 1-1.2 m thick and up to 2.5 m high, and a two-storey tower called "a late, Medieval structure".
  - EVIDENCE: Fig. 13 (0-15 m; section 0-40 m) shows a cave, a gate, living quarters, a circular cistern beside the cave-church, and a tower.
- **9 Martyrius (Kh. el-Murassas), pp. 20-22, (1785/1318).**
  - EVIDENCE: Martyrius found a cave 15 stadia (c. 2.8 km) W of the laura of Euthymius. van Kasteren described mainly the water system.
  - EVIDENCE: Magen-Hizmi excavated the main building, 70 x 80 m, with a water system "(including enormous cisterns)" and a burial cave (p. 22).
  - EVIDENCE: Fig. 25 (0-25 m) labels only the Refectory, Church and Hostel.
- **18 Castellion (El-Mird), pp. 33-36, (1848/1252).**
  - EVIDENCE: 20 stadia (approximately 3.7 km) from the Great Laura. "El-Mird was identified with Hyrcania of the Second Temple period" (p. 33).
  - EVIDENCE: Conder-Kitchener's description "refers mainly to the water supply system". Schick excavated a burial cave on the W slope (van Kasteren).
  - EVIDENCE: Mader recorded a sundial, a font and a reliquary lid, and dated the burial-cave paintings first to the Byzantine and then to the Medieval period. Wright's survey reconstructed part of the coenobium (Fig. 40, 0-10 m).
  - EVIDENCE: no measurement of the Hyrcania cisterns or aqueducts is given.
- **35 Marda, pp. 53-55 (Masada).**
  - EVIDENCE: Cyril: Euthymius and Domitian found "a collapsed well". The author thinks it "reasonable to assume" this was a reservoir of the site's earlier fortress phase.
  - EVIDENCE: Hirschfeld rejects Marda = Kh. el-Mird and identifies Marda with Masada. He links the Moschus garden, six miles (c. 8.9 km) away on the shore, to ʿEin ʿAneva.
  - EVIDENCE: Fig. 63 (0-100 m) shows the core, 13 cells, caves, cisterns, a lime kiln and paths.
- **24 Scholarius (Kh. el-Muntar), (1818/1268).**
  - EVIDENCE: 30 stadia (5.5 km) S of Euthymius, on the hilltop where Eudocia built a tower.
  - EVIDENCE: van Kasteren recorded an enclosure wall, a water reservoir and a massive corner tower. Patrich gives c. 50 x 60 m.
  - EVIDENCE: Fig. 45 (0-20 m) shows an oval cistern and "Tower?".
- **23 Spelaion (Bir el-Qattar), pp. 38-40.**
  - EVIDENCE: 15 stadia (c. 2.8 km) from the Great Laura, W of Castellion. Sabas' cave is on the N slope.
  - EVIDENCE: van Kasteren: a large reservoir "constructed at the foot of the cliff" and several caves. In one cave groundwater trickled from a crack ("Well of Drops"); it is now dry, with travertine.
  - EVIDENCE: above the cliff are a mosaic-paved structure, cells and a second reservoir. Fig. 44 (after Patrich, 0-20 m) shows "The cave" inside the enclosure.
- **14 Theodosius (Deir Dosi), pp. 26-28, (1759/1255).**
  - EVIDENCE: six miles (8.9 km) from Jerusalem. Schick recorded a burial cave under the central church, "a crypt", cisterns and a gate in the E wall; his remains were destroyed in the rebuilding.
  - EVIDENCE: Talpiyot survey: a wall c. 1.3 m thick c. 40 m NW of the monastery.
  - EVIDENCE: "a huge open cistern was discovered about 800 m. southeast", local name ʿEl-Birkeh ("the Pool"), photo Fig. 32. The author's opinion is that it was the monastery's reservoir.
- **Buqeiʿa / Qumran.**
  - EVIDENCE: no listed site is in the Buqeiʿa plateau proper.
  - EVIDENCE: 31 Jeremias (Kh. ez-Zaraniq, 1845/1279), pp. 47-49, was founded in 531, 0.9 km N of Spelaion. Its name means "Ruin of the Runlets", from a former small spring. Schick recorded "caves, water reservoirs, rock-cut stairs". Farmer exposed reservoir sections and a chapel against the cliff. Fig. 58 (after Patrich, 0-250 m) shows the core and cells along a path.
  - EVIDENCE: **G ʿEin Fashkha (1928/1247), p. 75:** "Most of these remains, dating the Second Temple period, were built by the Essenes" of Qumran, 3 km N. It was reused as a hermitage with a square chamber, and sherds date that use to the 5th-6th c. The Marda-garden proposal "seems to be unfounded".

### Other not-held entries (EVIDENCE, condensed)
