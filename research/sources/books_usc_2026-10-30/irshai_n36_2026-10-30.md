# Irshai n. 36 in *The Aqueducts of Israel* (2002): screened check (30 Oct 2026)

**Source.** O. Irshai, "Terms and characteristic features of water installations and aqueducts in Rabbinical sources", in Amit, Patrich & Hirschfeld (eds), *The Aqueducts of Israel* (JRA Suppl. 46, 2002), pp. 68–81. The footnote is n. 36, on p. 74.

**Method.** A screened reader was told to stop if the note cited columns XI–XII. It cites column V, so it was read.

**What n. 36 says (EVIDENCE, paraphrased).**
- **Copper Scroll link.** The water channel under discussion also appears in the Copper Scroll. Irshai cites 3Q15 V 1–9 (DJD III, pp. 263–264, items 8–11).
- **Date.** Mazar and Billig, later in the volume, and Patrich (n. 37) treat the conduit as the lower aqueduct to the Temple. They date it Hasmonean.
- **Rabbinic tradition.** Lam. 4:4 and Lamentations Rabba 4:4 blame the sicarii of the revolt for destroying a conduit. In the Lamentations Rabba passage, R. Abba b. Kahana speaks of a conduit from Etam.
- **Other points.** It may be the channel repaired with surplus Temple funds (nn. 23–24). Avigad's Upper City finds suggest its water was not for private use.

**Relevance (INFERENCE).**
- Irshai ties the column V entries to Jerusalem's Low-level (Etam) aqueduct, a Hasmonean conduit. That is one more published view to log under V 1–9 alongside the editions.
