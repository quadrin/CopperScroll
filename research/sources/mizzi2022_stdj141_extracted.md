## Findings

### Terminology

- **EVIDENCE (p. 365 n. 1):** Mizzi does not use de Vaux's Period Ia/Ib/II labels, because scholars, including himself, have redefined them. He speaks of a "first" and a "second architectural phase".
- **INFERENCE:** his phases cannot be swapped for de Vaux's periods or for Magen and Peleg's A/B without a feature-level check, the same caution the construction-phase review already applies to A/B.

### Absolute dates

- **EVIDENCE (pp. 367–375):** the reoccupation dates to "the first half of the first century BCE" (p. 375).
  - The basis is the pottery: no vessel type is exclusive to the 2nd century BCE (pp. 368–372).
  - The Ptolemaic, Seleucid and John Hyrcanus I coins are all unstratified, surface finds, or from contexts with later coins (pp. 372–375). The one possible exception, L10A, is discussed in n. 47.
  - He compares the coins with Jericho pool A(C)94, whose fill held coins of Antiochus IV and Alexander Zebina with ten of Jannaeus (p. 374, citing Meshorer 2004).
- **EVIDENCE (p. 392 and n. 88):** the modest first building was transformed "probably around the mid-first century" BCE into the dense settlement with "an intricate water system". The date is cited to Mizzi and Magness, *RevQ* 33 (2021), not argued in this chapter.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 393 n. 89):** the second phase runs from about the mid-1st century BCE to about 68 CE, with no gap in occupation, only localized destructions and renovations (citing Mizzi and Magness, *JBL* 135, 2016).

### The early water system and the aqueduct (relative sequence)

- **EVIDENCE (pp. 377–378):** there was "an early water system" connected with cisterns L110, L117 and L118. It preceded the main aqueduct, and stratigraphically belongs to the first phase. He cites de Vaux's field entries for L119 and L119bis (14 Feb, 30 Mar and 2–4 Apr 1955).
- **EVIDENCE (p. 378):** the outflow channel of L117 was "built when the latter's walls were raised", together with the later aqueduct, which "replaced this early water system". That channel cuts L133, L140 and L141, so those belong to the first phase.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 378):** the walls of L125–L127 sit above the drain and are later. The east–west walls of L114–L116 were built against the aqueduct, so they assume it.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 378 n. 56):**
  - L110 "probably goes back to the Iron Age".
  - The stepped pools L117 and L118 are late Hellenistic, because that is when such pools first appear (citing Reich 2013).
  - He rejects Taylor and Gibson's Iron Age date for L117 and L118 (2020, pp. 198–200), and Magen and Peleg's assumption that L110, L117 and L118 were built together.
- **EVIDENCE (p. 379, Fig. 2 p. 380):**
  - An inlet and a small plastered basin in the north wall of L116, now buried, belonged to the early water system.
  - In the first phase, rainwater was collected in a basin in L130 and channelled into that inlet (p. 416 n. 181).
  - The L130 basin was later buried by ash containing pots and animal bones.
- **EVIDENCE (pp. 379–381, Figs. 3a–b):** a vertical seam shows that the north wall of L120, L122 and L123 is later than L116. L111 and L120–L123 belong to the second phase. Whether that extension came with the aqueduct or before it is undetermined, "though the former makes more sense" (p. 379).
- **EVIDENCE (p. 380):** beaten-earth floors in L100, L101, L105, L106 and L109 predate the aqueduct, according to de Vaux's field entries.
- **EVIDENCE (pp. 385, 388–390 and n. 85):**
  - The two kilns in L66 were decommissioned "by the construction of the aqueduct and the stepped pool L48/49", which was partly built over them.
  - Pools L48/49, L50 and L52 belong to the phase when the aqueduct was installed.
  - New kilns in L64 and L84 replaced the L66 kilns (p. 416 n. 184).
- **EVIDENCE (p. 417 n. 185):**
  - The walls of L110 were raised in conjunction with building the aqueduct.
  - Its maximum capacity is about 110 m³ (citing Galor), slightly less at the foundation.
  - With L110 as the only storage cistern, he estimates the first-phase population at no more than 15–30 people (pp. 416–417).

### Other positions he discusses

- **EVIDENCE (pp. 376–378 and n. 51, 54):**
  - He treats Stacey's pre-aqueduct reconstruction as close to de Vaux's Period Ia, but says Stacey's additions (the tower, L111 and L120–L123) "lack stratigraphic support" (p. 377).
  - He cites the Magness–Stacey exchange in *DSD* 14 (2007) and calls Stacey's model of the site's development "mired by several difficulties".
- **EVIDENCE (pp. 385–388, 395):** the tower is a later addition; Floor 2 in L38/41 was in use until the late 1st century BCE (a coin of Herod, KhQ2667, p. 387). He concludes the first-phase building had no defences.

## What this means for R01 (INFERENCE)

1. **A stratigraphic anchor that the construction-phase review lacked:** the main aqueduct is later than an earlier, smaller water system feeding L110, L117 and L118. The aqueduct, the raising of L117 and L110 and its outflow channel, and pool L48/49 all belong to one construction horizon. Mizzi dates that horizon to about the mid-1st century BCE, by citation.
2. **The L117 overflow test (the review's next step):** Mizzi assigns L117's outflow channel to the aqueduct phase, by its cuts through L133, L140 and L141. Stacey (2017) uses the overflow above the northern dump to argue a later date. So the two disagree on the channel's date, not on its place in the sequence. The test still needs the dump contact itself; Mizzi gives no dump section.
3. **Competing absolute dates for the main aqueduct now on record:**
   - Mizzi and Magness: about the mid-1st century BCE.
   - Magen and Peleg: a Hasmonean comparison.
   - Stacey: a dam of about 20–15 BCE.
   - Humbert 2016: final form after 68, with a lower earlier form before ([check](humbert2016_pp425-432_check.md)).
   - Taylor and Gibson's citation of Humbert for "after about 31 BCE" remains unsupported by Humbert's pages.
4. **Nothing here dates the external aqueduct line, the dam or any upper-gorge feature.** Mizzi's evidence is all inside the settlement.
