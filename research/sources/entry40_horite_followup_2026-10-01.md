# Entry 40: Jeremias's northern Beit Guvrin tomb group

Research date: 2026-10-01. Project-authored follow-up to `entry40_bethhoron_review.md` and open question Q45. This follows one source-specific path: Jeremias's description and his subsequent Copper Scroll discussion. No candidate, coordinate, log, mirrored note or registration was changed.

## Outcome and limits

An unrestricted original French article scan supplies a readable measurement note. It resolves the French publication's numeral independently of OCR; it does **not** substitute for visual verification of the requested German 1958 p. 86. The German page-image question remains open. The inspected article supplies neither an original measured plan nor a tomb construction date. Its westward argument concerns cemetery location relative to a road, not the azimuth of a chamber entrance.

## Directly inspected primary account — 1960

Joachim Jeremias and J. T. Milik, “Remarques sur le rouleau de cuivre de Qumrân,” *Revue Biblique* **67**, no. 2 (April 1960), **220–223**. All four original printed pages were rendered and visually inspected. Printed **221, note 1** presents measurements attributed to the observer of one tomb. This factual block is the sole substantive summary of that article in this note (under 200 words).

| Printed location | Reported observation or argument |
| --- | --- |
| **220** | Tomb group approximately 2 km north of Beit Gibrin. |
| **221, n. 1** | Surface to tomb floor: **4.89 m**. Vertical shaft to chamber ceiling: **3.49 m**; rectangular section **1.53 × 0.63 m**. |
| **221, n. 1** | Closure stones at **2.40 m** below surface, **0.13 m** thick, supported on a ledge; earth above blocked access. Shaft opens through chamber ceiling. |
| **221, n. 1** | Two funerary troughs, each **1.80 m** long, **0.60 (0.70) m** wide, flank a parallel passage with a higher floor. Rectangular annex: **2.10 × 1.84 m**. |
| **221**, main text | Cemetery west of the old Jerusalem–Beit Gibrin Roman road; the westernmost tomb is argued to be farthest from road traffic. This supports the proposed identification in the author's reasoning. |
| **220–223**, inspected coverage | No tomb plan/figure, measured entrance azimuth, drainage basin/channel, or individual construction-date evidence is supplied. |

**Project assessment:** the paired features are explicitly funerary troughs; treating them as a water basin would require independent evidence. The described access is vertical and enters through the roof. It cannot establish a west-facing exterior doorway. “Westernmost” is a positional comparison; it does not identify an opening bearing. A shaft-tomb description is warranted, but assigning a formal dated regional type would exceed this evidence. The closure arrangement cannot by itself distinguish original construction from subsequent burial or sealing phases. No conversion of the dimensions into a sixteen-cubit match is imposed.

## Integration decision

Use the verified French measurement only with its **1960, p. 221 n. 1** citation. Keep the 1958 numeral/image question open. The next discriminating source would be the German p. 86 image and any directly associated figure/caption, or the original 1933 pp. 42–53 and attached plates. It must connect a particular tomb to an entrance bearing, water feature and dating basis before those attributes enter a candidate comparison.

The Oren–Rappaport Maresha cemetery target is not evidence for this particular northern shaft group without a source-specific crosswalk. No tomb, basin, plan or date is transferred between those cemeteries. This follow-up supplies a better documented architectural description, but no basis for changing the Entry 40 candidate ranking or coordinates.
