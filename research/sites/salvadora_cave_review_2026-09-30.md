# Salvadora Cave: the crack, layers and disturbed finds

Reviewed 30 September 2026. **Result:** the reported rock fissure is now a specific physical feature: the arrowhead location in Fig. 4. Its context and dating do not establish a sealed cache or a Copper Scroll identification.

## Source actually read

Porat, Eshel and Frumkin, “השרידים הארכיאולוגיים מתקופת מרד בר-כוכבא ממערת הסלוודורה,” *Refuge Caves of the Bar Kokhba Revolt*, second volume (2009), **pp. 137-143**, was read visually in full. [Cave Research Center PDF](https://www.malham.info/_files/ugd/2121fb_1dd5ef95cf9e4204babd56420c2e856f.pdf): two covers, then seven chapter pages; printed page = PDF page + 134. The chapter uses Bar-Adon's archived diaries, but those original diary pages and associated object cards were not directly inspected.

## Geography and physical features

The published cave point is `18651/10299`, p. 137. Interpreting the five-digit components in 10-m units gives old-grid `186510/102990`; EPSG:28191 conversion yields latitude **31.51895**, longitude **35.38399**. This is a provisional notation interpretation and a **cave-level anchor**, with unknown source accuracy; it is not the arrowhead's geographic position.

The east-facing entrance is about 6 by 2 m. The elongated principal chamber is approximately 20 by 4 m and 2 m high, with branching rooms. The source describes natural karst development along bedding and north-south fissures (p. 137). Fig. 4, p. 140, gives the plan, sections, north arrow, looting trenches and arrowhead label. No geographic footprint was fitted to that plan.

## Dating and relevance to the scroll

The authors interpret the Roman assemblage and environmental setting as evidence of Bar Kokhba refuge use. The worn coin is not a diagnostic Bar Kokhba coin. The arrowhead's form does not date its precise deposition, and the recovered spoil does not link every Roman object to one episode. Conversely, the reported excavation layers should not all be described as surface finds merely because Christmas Cave's scientific samples were collected on the surface.

No entry association is established. “Cave” or “fissure” is too general to identify a place without its named geography and relational constraints. The crack is useful as a specific observed feature whose original find record can be sought; it has not become a deposit candidate through resemblance alone.

## Missing primary records

The 2009 bibliography, p. 143, cites **Bar-Adon 1971, “Excavations in the Judean Desert,” Hadashot Arkheologiyot 40, pp. 25-27**. The chapter also credits access to IAA archival diaries. The original short notice, diary sheets, niche-3 records and complete object catalog were not recovered in this pass. A current search did not locate a later full publication; that does not establish its absence. The statement that the excavation was unpublished describes the authors' position in 2009.

The useful next test is whether an original card or diary gives the arrowhead's discovery circumstances, the crack's contents and an association with a dated layer. That could improve deposition confidence; it would still need an independently justified scroll-place association.
