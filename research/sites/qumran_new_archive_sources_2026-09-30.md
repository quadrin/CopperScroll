This is a source search, **not** a new identification. Source names and catalog dates do not establish the date of the hydraulic features.

## The unresolved 1994 dam reference

Galor, “Plastered Pools, A New Perspective,” in *Khirbet Qumrân et ‘Aïn Feshkha II* (2003), printed p. 318 n. 7, says Humbert first described the dam in detail and cites **Humbert–Chambon 1994, pp. 192 and 342**. This lead was already recorded in [`qumranII2003_extracted.md`](../../atlas/research/registration/qumranII2003_extracted.md). The 1994 volume is not among the research copies found in this workspace, and no accessible scan of those exact pages was verified in this search. A later chapter reproduces its p. 192 aqueduct map as Fig. 8.2, but that reproduction alone does not establish which wall Humbert meant. Therefore the identity of Humbert's dam relative to **Ilan–Amit point 4**, **Ilan–Amit point 19**, and **Reeder–Jol's wall above the pothole** remains unresolved. Acquire/read pp. 192 and 342 and the associated figure captions before citing Humbert as support for any one of those three.

The already inspected [Magen–Peleg 2007 report](https://www.antiquities.org.il/images/shop/jsp/JSP6_Qumran_color.pdf), especially printed pp. 32–35 and Fig. 35, remains the baseline aerial and basin account. It is **not** counted here as independent new evidence. The [existing Qumran photo comparison](qumran_photo_correspondence.md) likewise already records a match at the long-tunnel mouth, but not the missing upstream sequence.

## Next discriminating acquisitions

1. **Humbert–Chambon 1994, printed pp. 192, 342, and the dam figure/plan**: determine the text's actual location and whether the drawing places a surviving wall at point 4, point 19, or elsewhere. Retain the French feature labels before mapping them onto later numbering.
2. **IAA 1993 survey `223-391-0008` and 2004 excavation `368-791-2715`**: ask for original gorge photographs, measured plan, station/benchmark list, feature labels, and notes on the small northern ravine versus the main-wadi basin. Distinguish a surviving wall from a reconstructed barrier and record observation dates.
3. **Historical aerials**: first identify actual Qumran frames and coverage. The Azaria Alon slide is known by catalog number but its upstream coverage is unknown; the Survey of Israel order service has not yet yielded a flight ID. A useful frame must show the intake sector at resolution adequate to trace channel and wall fabric.
4. **ASOR 1952-20**: register lower-channel fixed points against a dated plan before using the photo to infer any construction phase. Its published display is adequate for broad route context, not the upper-gorge feature test.

No candidate ranking, coordinates or chronology were changed by this source audit.

The IAA [archive access statement and terms](https://archives.iaa.org.il/Search.aspx) say that all 7,683 ATQ/SRF files are digitized, though only some are online; they ask researchers to inform the archive in writing for research use and require advance written permission for publication. This report links the official scans and reproduces none. An eventual request should identify **SRF 154 pp. `90424`–`90425` and `90428`**, **SRF 154A folder `17653`**, and the separate **1993 survey `223-391-0008`**, asking specifically for any omitted measured upper-wadi plan, intake photographs, feature register, station/benchmark data, and survey dates. No archive request was sent during this audit.

**Discriminating result:** the 1946 note strengthens the historical documentation of runoff collection on the settlement's west, but no newly inspected plan or photograph fixes which upstream wall caught the flow, when the tunnel was built, or whether Humbert's dam is Ilan–Amit point 4, point 19, or Reeder–Jol's wall.
