## Outcome

The published coin row requires a narrower interpretation than the existing notes give it. The catalogue distinguishes an individual specimen's findspot from the database total for its type. It does not by itself establish a three-coin deposit at 5178. The architectural identification of that locus remains unrecovered: no checked original plan or locus description places it in a staircase, courtyard cistern, occupation floor or later fill.

This prevents upgrading the mansion to demonstrated use in 58/59 CE. Even a confirmed cistern findspot would require a stratigraphic assessment: deposition in accumulated fill, disturbance or later clearance is different from use of the installation when the coin was struck.

Even a sealed deposit containing the Festus specimen would ordinarily supply a terminus after its minting, subject to the security of the context. It would not automatically date original construction, nor establish water storage or regular stair traffic in 58/59 CE. An upper-pit identification additionally requires its relative position and the intended step to be recorded together. Locus numbers are excavation identifiers, not map coordinates or a construction sequence.

**Disposition:** stop this focused lead at the missing architectural records and inaccessible per-coin appendix. Q48 remains unresolved. The new note narrows the alleged coin cluster and records more precise retrieval keys; it does not promote stray coins to period use. Logs, mirrored files and map data remain untouched; no commit.
