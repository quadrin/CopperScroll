# Humbert–Chambon 1994: dam and aqueduct reference check

30 September 2026. This is a source-access and attribution check for Copper Scroll entries 20–23, **not** a feature identification.

## Result

The printed **pp. 192 and 342 and their figure captions were not accessible for inspection**. The presently defensible conclusion is that later authors associate those pages with Qumran's aqueduct and a dam in the gorge. The evidence does **not** establish that Humbert's dam is the reconstructed main-wadi dam at Ilan–Amit point 4, their downstream wall at point 19, or the wall Reeder–Jol report above the pothole. No coordinate or geometry can be transferred from the unseen 1994 pages.

## Feature distinction

- **Ilan–Amit point 4:** A proposed dam across basin 2 in the main wadi, for which their survey reports no surviving wall. Eshel's aqueduct-head account is broadly compatible with this proposal, but cannot prove Humbert mapped a wall there.
- **Ilan–Amit point 19:** A downstream wall at the final turn toward the settlement. Humbert's later phrase “dam in the gorge” makes a settlement inlet interpretation less natural, but is too broad to exclude this wall without the 1994 plan.
- **Reeder–Jol wall above the pothole:** A reported observed wall upstream of the pothole. The available later references do not establish whether Humbert described or photographed it, nor whether it is the same feature as any Ilan–Amit point.

## Discriminating next source

Inspect a lawful copy of printed **pp. 192, 195–99 and 342**, including facing pages, figure numbers/captions and plan keys. Transcribe the French feature terms and identify whether the text presents an observed masonry wall, a field-note description or a reconstructed barrier. Then register the figure against Ilan–Amit's numbered plan and independent 1952 field photographs. Until that comparison, cite “Humbert's gorge dam” only as an author's retrospective description, with its physical referent unresolved.
