# Detailed findings guide

This guide points to the authoritative notes preserved in ``. It summarizes the working conclusions and disagreements rather than resolving every place-name. Later dated corrections supersede an earlier incompatible claim. Printed pages below refer to the cited source, not the page index of an uploaded ZIP.

## Text and research foundation

The project compares the Copper Scroll's entry divisions, readings, numerals and landmark vocabulary across Puech, Lefkovits and Milik, with later studies and image checks where available. Puech and Lefkovits share the 60-entry boundary scheme; Milik's finer divisions require the concordance. Restorations, ambiguous readings and competing place-name interpretations remain in `text/readings.json`, the concordance and phase summaries.

The Greek-letter groups, lexical searches and sequence models have their own records. Homographs can produce false lexical attestations; modern translations can differ from an edition's Hebrew. Letter photographs, radiographs and repeated copies must be tracked by provenance. A repeated image is not another independent witness. Sequence adjacency is a research hypothesis, not a measured walking route.

The entry involves a cave associated with a pillar, two openings, an eastward aspect, a northern opening and digging three cubits. Attaching eastward aspect to the cave versus requiring two east-facing exterior openings changes the model. The project scene currently overstates the paired-opening orientation. An exploratory 0.40–0.60 m cubit range gives 1.20–1.80 m; the original surface and excavation direction remain unlocated.

Maresha Tomb 515 provides a useful access comparison but no positive entry identification. An eastward interior photograph supplies no entrance bearing; an inter-tomb breach supplies no demonstrated pair of original exterior openings.

The latest check recovered the historical Twin Cave proposal near and south of 11Q. Taylor's printed p. 17 / Fig. 1.4 and Eshel p. 22 connect it to Bar-Adon's excavations. This is a separate lead from Abu Saraj IV/11 and 3Q. Its rock partition and paired caves need an original plan and phase test. Bar-Adon ʿAtiqot HS 9 (1989), pp. 15–17 is the precise next target; the original pages remain unread.

Milik 1960 p. 140, item 27, was visually rechecked. It gives 42 talents and a book in a jar; later duplicate-Copper-Scroll interpretations require a separate argument. No new manuscript transcription was made.

## Qumran / Sekakah: entries 20–23

The aqueduct district remains a leading regional comparison, with several competing feature anchors. Modern Nahal Sekakah's name is dependent on a biblical-site proposal, so it cannot independently confirm ancient Sekakah at Qumran.

The main tunnel crosswalk is stronger than the cache-feature identification. Masterman's breaks at about 3.35 and 7.01 m from his entrance nearly coincide with Magen–Peleg's 3.10 and 7.10 m from the western entrance, conditional on equivalent entrances and paths. This is not proof of the Scroll's eastern fissure. Intake/head alternatives include the upper system and a later bend/run-off interpretation; point-number correspondences and phasing remain consequential.

The latest primary reading adds de Vaux's 1953 settlement plan, Fig. 1 p. 311, and pool/channel alteration observations p. 312. Those observations do not locate the upstream wall/boulder/fissure in a common measured frame. Later earthquake interpretation must be separated from the initial damage observation and not transferred upstream without evidence.

Original illustration indexes locate Schulz's water-channel drawing at p. 53 and Strobel's network at Fig. 1 p. 56. Strobel Fig. 2 p. 71 concerns Masada. The drawings themselves remain unread. Their extent, scale and measured/reconstructed conventions require checking. No validated gorge registration or deposit coordinate resulted.

## Supplied books and ZIPs

Nine ZIPs represent eight distinct books. The archive survey documents contents and selected-page coverage, not complete readings. Printed-page mappings were checked only in stated sections and must be verified at a new page. Archive 09's 174 scans duplicate archive 02 byte-for-byte.

Useful cross-cutting controls include: ossuaries can continue beyond the Second Temple period in some regions; steps can serve local cisterns, caves, springs or fields; road antiquity differs from paving date; aqueduct path length differs from direct separation; ceramics or soil dates require a feature/context relationship. Later pilgrimage traditions and urban territory cannot automatically describe first-century installations.
