FIND CARD
Title (one line): The Mt Gerizim destruction date (111–110 BCE) did not change between 2008 and 2021, but the coins it rests on did. Both hoards close 9–10 years before that date.
Lane: B4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): In JSP 8 (2008) the 111–110 BCE date rests on "121 [coins] of Antiochus VIII and Cleopatra Thea, dated from 125–110 BCE" and on Akko-Ptolemais coins "132–110 BCE". The joint Antiochus VIII–Cleopatra Thea coinage ends in 121/120 BCE, so that range cannot be right, and in JSP 19 the group is 43 coins dated 125–121 BCE. The 2021 terminus rests on other coins: 240 of Antiochus VIII down to 113/112 BCE, and four of Antiochus IX, only one of them dated to 110/109 BCE. The silver hoard's latest coin also moves: "125 BCE" in 2008 (79 coins after Antiochus VII), 120/119 BCE in 2021 (104 coins after Antiochus VII), and the change rests on a single Akko coin of Antiochus VIII. The bronze hoard closes in 121/120 BCE. Further slips: in JSP 8 the "412 coins postdating his reign" are made up of parts that add to 163 + 79 + 121 = 363. On the same printed page of JSP 19 Magen says the 1,217 Akko coins are "dated from 169–164 BCE" and that 411 of them run "up to 110 BCE" (p.[45]). And on p.[59] he says "the latest coins … related to the destruction … are the coins of Alexander Jannaeus".
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): JSP 8 p.[170] (store p93, photo IMG_1538): the Seleucid counts, "412 coins postdating his reign", the silver hoard "latest date to 125 BCE", "only in 111–110 BCE". JSP 8 p.[171] (same spread): 652 Hasmonean coins = 76 John Hyrcanus I, 519 Jannaeus, 1 Aristobulus I, 57 undefined. JSP 8 p.[60] (store p38, IMG_1592): "a hoard of silver coins was found in a bronze vessel, the latest dating to 125 BCE (Fig. 121)"; plan Fig. 118 of P-II. JSP 19 Magen p.[37]–[39] (PDF 49–51): 43 coins of Antiochus VIII and Cleopatra Thea (125–121 BCE), 240 of Antiochus VIII, Antiochus IX "just four coins", capture "in 110/109 BCE". JSP 19 p.[44] (PDF 56): silver hoard of 129 = 25 Antiochus VII + 103 Demetrius II + 1 Antiochus VIII of Akko 120/119 BCE, "proof that the destruction … took place after 119 BCE, and … some nine years later". JSP 19 p.[45] (PDF 57): bronze hoard of 61, latest 121/120 BCE; the Akko sentence; Hasmonean 68 / 3 / 528. JSP 19 p.[59] (PDF 71): the Jannaeus sentence. JSP 19 Bijovsky p.[92] (PDF 104): hoards Cat. 701–761 (bronze) and 762–890 (silver), "both deposited … during the reign of Antiochus VIII"; Akko 1,217 coins = Cat. 351–389. Minor: the silver hoard is in a "copper juglet" (JSP 8 p.[170]) or a "bronze vessel" (p.[60]); JSP 19 places it at L4749 B47518 (printed 114, per `registration/magen2021_gerizim_coins_extracted.md` l.28), and that locus is not on the JSP 8 Fig. 118 P-II plan, which shows loci 4831–4886. The full table is in `gerizim_jsp8_vs_jsp19.csv`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): web searches for "Mount Gerizim destruction date coins Antiochus IX 110/109 silver hoard 120/119", for "Gerizim 'Antiochus VIII and Cleopatra' 125–110 destruction Hyrcanus critique", for reviews of JSP 19, and for "112/111 vs 111/110 numismatic debate". The only coverage found is general: Wikipedia, citing the NEAEHL figure of 546 Hasmonean coins, a third total; it notes no dispute of the coin basis. No review of JSP 19 turned up. Project: `registration/magen2021_gerizim_coins_extracted.md` and `registration/gerizim_magen_neaehl_extracted.md` already quote the Jannaeus sentence on p.59 and Bijovsky's "not before 110/109". Neither compares JSP 8 with JSP 19, or the hoard termini with the destruction date. That the 128 vs c. 110 BCE date is disputed is well known; the 2008→2021 drift of its coin basis and the arithmetic slip were not found anywhere. Tag: **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** (for the drift and the slips), within a known debate.
Why it might matter: The best-dated destruction of a sanctuary in the region is anchored on single coins: one Antiochus IX coin of 110/109, and one Akko coin that moves the silver hoard 6 years. In the 2008 version the anchor was a range that is impossible for that coinage. Both hoards, the only "event" deposits, close in 121–119 BCE. That fits a deposition about 10 years before the stated destruction, or a destruction near 120 BCE with later strays (the 528 Jannaeus coins show the site was not empty afterwards).
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Read the JSP 19 catalogue entries for the single Antiochus IX coin of 110/109 and for silver-hoard Cat. 762–890. Check whether the Akko Antiochus VIII coin's date is read or attributed, and look for its findspot and K number. Check the dated Akko autonomous issues among Cat. 351–389 that are later than 113/112. The Drive connector stops at PDF p.107 (see Blocked inputs). If the 110/109 coin comes from a sealed destruction layer, the date stands and only the 2008 basis was wrong. If it is a stray from topsoil or a post-destruction context, the terminus falls back to about 113/112–120 BCE.
Confidence: high that the printed discrepancies exist; medium for what they mean.
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. Gerizim is entry 57 (Q47), and the date of the sanctuary's end bears on any Gerizim reading.

Title (one line): In the Martyrius monastery volume the excavator shortens a coin's published date to fit 614 CE. The catalogue prints the same K number for two coins and holds no coin of Justinian, whose reign the excavator gives for phase IIB.
Lane: B4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The coin catalogue dates Cat. 16, a hexanummium of Heraclius struck at Alexandria (L227, B2185, K 28964), to 613–618 CE. The excavator writes that "we should accept the earlier dating (613 CE), one year before the Persian conquest". He does so to support an abandonment in 614, yet his own phase chapter runs phase IIB "to the Arab conquest in 638 CE". The catalogue gives K 28972 to both Cat. 5 (cast minimus, L230 B2312) and Cat. 19 (Justin II follis from the hoard in L419 B4105). The run 28960–28972 has no 28971. Magen dates the building of phase IIB to 552/3 under Justinian and rejects Di Segni's paleographic 567/8 or 582/3. Yet the 23 catalogued coins include no coin of Justinian (527–565): after Justin I (518–522) the next datable coin is Justin II, 567/568 (Cat. 19). The only possible exception is Cat. 9, an uncertain follis of 512–538.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Bijovsky & Berman 2015, "Coin Catalogue", pp.[383]–[386], Plate 63 (Drive `1Fl-86qvtf5LCr7lsl3rW9RPakdldg3VH`): Cat. 5 and Cat. 19 both K 28972 (pp.[383], [385]); Cat. 16 "613–618, Alexandria, DOC 2/1:340, No. 198" (p.[385]); the hoard Cat. 19–23 "found together within a jar" (p.[385]). Magen 2015, ch. 1, p.[8] (IIB built 552/3, Di Segni rejected) and p.[10] ("we should accept the earlier dating (613 CE)"; "a pottery jug … contained five coins") (Drive `1HF7tzO6f83jy3XtPKPY0UdobPaxeUyMW`). Magen 2015, ch. 2, p.[13]: IIB "to the Arab conquest in 638 CE" (Drive `1brFM894xQyvfNDuwfe-yEcsfNNPZdxsn`). Magen 2015, Architectural Remains, p.[97]: cell L419, a rounded niche in W79, "A jug with coins was discovered in the niche (see p. 385, nos. 19–23)" (Drive `16F9EnKBc7OgZ0iKDG4uJ5rCArCND9He3`). K-number run: `martyrius_k_numbers.csv`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): web search "Monastery of Martyrius … coins hoard jug Heraclius 613 Persian conquest Genesius 567/8 582/3 Di Segni Magen 2015 review"; Jewish Virtual Library summary read (it mentions damage in 614 and abandonment in the mid-7th century, and no coins). No publication found on the date override, the duplicate K number or the missing Justinian coins. Project: "Martyrius" occurs only as a comparandum (water systems, the SBF 36 sweep); no note reads its coins. Tag: **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** (none found).
Why it might matter: The one coin that could show occupation after 614 is shortened by hand to fit 614. The coins give no support to the excavator's Justinianic date for the main building phase, and they fit Di Segni's later inscription dates better. The duplicate K number means one of the two coins cannot be traced in the IAA database as printed.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Look up K 28971 and K 28972 in the IAA/ADCA coin records (not reachable here) to see which coin carries which number. Check DOC 2/1 No. 198 for the official date range of the Alexandria issue. The sample is small (23 coins, about 16 stray Byzantine), so the Justinian gap is weak evidence on its own. The site excavations of 1979–2006 (Magen p.[1]) may have produced coins that are not catalogued; ask whether the catalogue is complete.
Confidence: high for the printed facts; low–medium for the chronological reading.
Touches the Copper Scroll? no.
