FIND CARD
Title (one line): The 1990 and 1996 geophysical "cave" targets on the Qumran southern plateau were dug by hand to 17 m in 2002 and turned out to be natural stone lines. The project has no record of these surveys or of the test.
Lane: C4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): HA 117 (2005) reports that R. Price's Aug–Sep 2002 excavation "was based on GPR and seismic surveys conducted by the Tel-Aviv Department of Geophysics in 1990 and by J. Strange in 1996". A drilled probe in 1996 had shown "a drop in pressure at the seismic target depths" at about 16 m, read as "a subsurface paleo-chamber". The Western Square was dug by hand to 16 m and below, with two side tunnels of about 3 m at 17 m. It found nothing below 1.5 m, and the anomalies were traced to "concentrated lines of natural stones that intruded between the marl layers". The GPR targets in the Eastern Square were also "entirely geologic" below a 1.2 m floor. Magen & Peleg 2018 (print p. 70) describe the same class of work differently: they say it used "heavy earthmoving machinery to reach tremendous depths" and "added nothing", citing only Strange 2006. HA 117 says the 2002 shaft was dug by hand.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): bucket `gs://copper-scroll-cog-1030/hadashot/pdf/en/vol-117/00126_qumran-plateau.pdf` (HA 117, 22/03/2005, R. Price, "Qumran Plateau", map ref. NIG 24359/62845), paragraphs 2–3 (Western Square) and 4–5 (Eastern Square). Magen & Peleg 2018, bucket doc `Magen-2018-…-927bd2d8`, PDF p. 61 = print p. [70], "Southern Plateau", 2nd–3rd sentences. Eshel (Brill chapter) PDF pp. 16–17 for the Price, Gutfeld and Kalman seasons of 2004–06 and 2008. Orion list post (Goranson, 1998, https://orion.huji.ac.il/orion/archives/1998a/msg00612.html) says the 1996 team was "looking for an underground opening which technical readings had suggested".
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Repo main and branch_only grep for "Trinity Southwest", "Randall Price", "paleo-chamber", "Tel-Aviv Department of Geophysics", "seismic survey", "Strange 2006" and "seismic" in `research/history/salvage_records/searches.csv`: no hits. The project screened report id 126 as "held: cave report in held area ... first lines only" (`research/regional/haesi_features/phase2_2026-10-10/online_screen.csv`), and the salvage records stop at S34–S36 (Bauman, Baigent and Eisenman). Web searches for "Price 2002 Qumran seismic 16 m", "Strange 1996 Qumran radar" and "1990 Tel Aviv geophysical survey Qumran" found only HA 117 itself, worldofthebible.com and Orion posts. The test is published, but no one ties it to the later S34 anomalies. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT**.
Why it might matter: (1) The salvage-records ledger is missing two geophysical looks (TAU 1990 seismic+GPR; Strange 1996 drilled probe) and one deep test excavation (Price 2002, 17 m) at Kh. Qumran. (2) This is the only published ground-truth of a deep geophysical "cavity" in the Qumran Lisan marl, and it was a false positive caused by stone and pebble lines in the marl. That sets the prior for FIND 2. (3) It corrects Magen & Peleg's account: the 2002 shaft was dug by hand, not by machine.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Read Strange 2006 (Galor, Humbert & Zangenberg, *Qumran: The Site of the Dead Sea Scrolls*, pp. 41–54), which is not in the corpus, for the 1996 probe and any machinery. Look in the IAA archive for the 1990 TAU survey report (no publication found). Check the HA 117 Hebrew version (`hadashot/pdf/he/vol-117/00126_qumran-plateau.pdf`) for wording on "manual". To kill: find a source showing the 2002 shaft was dug by machine, or that the 1996 probe was at a different spot.
Confidence: high (for the facts as reported in HA 117)
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. HA 117 itself cites Allegro's "vessels of blood" reading for the buried cooking pots; the deep target was a scroll-cave search, now closed as negative.

Title (one line): The 2002 Qumran ERT section "west of ruins over dump" shows two resistive bodies, not one. Neither was ever excavated, and the second is not mentioned in any text.
Lane: C4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The published section (80 m, S–N, Bauman/Komex, file tag `J:\51660100\QWEST-ERT.cdr`) labels one "POSSIBLE CAVE". Its core is above 240 Ω·m at about 30–38 m along the line, about 4–7 m deep, with a halo at about 26–42 m and 2–9.5 m deep. Freund (2009) says this feature "still remains to be excavated". The same section has a second, unlabelled resistive body: a core of about 170–200 Ω·m at about 58–60 m along the line and 6.4–8.7 m deep, with a halo at about 54–65 m and 4–13 m deep that is open at the base of the section. It sits under the "WALL" marker at about 59 m. None of the later reports in the corpus excavated anywhere near either body at those depths, and the line is not drawn on Reeder's 2002 site map, so neither body can be placed on a plan.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Drive `Qumran_Freund2012_fig3.2_ERT_west_of_ruins_Bauman.png` (`1iT4sAYZ-iGWpebUW2Dq7CZlwcjOMxCZZ`, 976×730 px). Positions measured by pixel calibration: x 8.93 px/m (0 m at px 148, 80 m at px 862); depth 10.2 px/m (0 at py 355). Orange core of body 2: px x 668–686, y 420–444. Site map without the ERT line: `Qumran_Freund2012_fig3.1_site_map_2001-2002_Reeder.png` (`1YK3CbohS4uu4GREvVv1mkGm-sFlFymnn`). Freund 2009 wording: S34 extraction notes doc `1b77R4NN3JVk2vxA1YF3xqxpwm7IFZdioxgpn92ZutUk`, Addendum 2. Later excavations checked: Magen & Peleg 2018 print pp. 68–70 (N and NW refuse dumps dug in 1996–97, before the survey; eight 1×1 m squares L9000–L9007 in the NW of the plateau in 2002, "nothing came to light"); HA 117 (Price 2002, a different target, see FIND 1); HA 132 id 25871 (Cohen & Porat 2018, marl caves about 150 m N); HA 135 id 26421 (Traubman 2020, Room L101 inside the ruins); Eshel chapter (review of the work to about 2010).
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): The S34 extraction notes and Witten 2006 record body 1 only ("possible cave", "likely a cave", "about 2 m" in Witten against about 4.5 m in Freund 2009). Web search for "Qumran electrical resistivity tomography possible cave west of ruins dump excavated" found no excavation report. Repo grep for "QWEST", "possible cave" and "dump ERT" turned up only the S34 coding note in `research/history/salvage_records/README.md`, which says that S34 "give[s] no footprint, a depth reached or a result". Tags: body 1 untested, **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED** (the test-status check is new); body 2 **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** (no text found that mentions it).
Why it might matter: These are the only published deep (4–9 m) void-type anomalies at Kh. Qumran that are still untested, and they lie "directly next to the main buildings" (Freund 2009). The depth error is large: the 4–7 m core matches Freund's 15 ft, while Witten gives "about 2 m". There is also a strong alternative explanation. FIND 1 shows that stone and pebble lines in the Lisan marl produced false cavity signals 100–200 m away. A resistive body in conductive marl may be a gravel lens, a buried wadi channel or dry spoil, not a void.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Missing: the line's ground position (Jol et al. 2002 SPIE 4758 full text; Bauman's Komex project file 51660100; Freund 2009 fig. 6.2). With a position, overlay it on the Magen & Peleg 2018 plan (Fig. 9, PDF p. 3) to see which walls the 5 "WALL" markers are (at about 8, 24, 41, 59 and 74 m). To kill: the inversion's sensitivity at 6–9 m near the line centre is probably adequate, but the halo of body 2 is open at the section base, so it is poorly resolved. A 2-D inversion artefact under a wall foundation is also possible. No field test is allowed in this swarm.
Confidence: medium (body 1 untested); low (body 2 being a real feature)
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. The surveys were motivated by the search for scroll and treasure caves (Freund 2009 ch. 6). Not linked to any entry.

Title (one line): The Cave of Letters metal-detector null covers only the top 3–3.5 m. Bauman's own ERT puts the base of the resistive debris in central Hall B at about 7–8 m below the floor, and nothing there has been dug since 2001.
Lane: C4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The project's S34 note codes Bauman's 1999–2001 metal survey (EM38, Overhauser gradiometer, EM61; detection limit about 3–3.5 m for a "substantially sized hoard"; no anomalies) as counting "against any second, unrecovered metal deposit" in the cave. But Bauman's ERT line 2 (July 2001) shows a resistive (above 24,000 Ω·m) "Ancient Rockfall" body. It runs about 18–48 m along the line through central Hall B, with its base at about −12.8 to −13.2 m relative depth. That is about 7–8 m below the present surface at line positions of about 30–36 m, under an interpreted "Bar Kokhba floor" about 1–3 m down. So roughly the lower 4–5 m of the debris column there lies below the metal survey's stated reach. No excavation in the cave since 2001 has gone below about 1.5–2 m. Hadashot vols 116–137 contain no Cave of Letters report: the IAA 2014 and 2018 digs in Naḥal Ḥever were in Cave B and Cave C1-001.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Drive `CoL_Bauman2018_fig6.6_ERT_line2.png` (`1Enf7quLuDTi9W9HCqiOdmsCB9KNQvQPl`, 1200×540 px). Depth scale 14 px/m, 0 at py about 72. Column px x 430: surface py about 155, base of pink py about 258. Column px x 500: surface py about 138, pink py 191–252. Line location: `CoL_Reeder2018_fig4.9_map_ERT_lines_loci.png` (`1Cc5TzpYlqxJWME3xIcaeClnK3JjlrNrJ`), where the chapter's line 2 is labelled "ERT Line #1". Metal-survey limit and excavation depths: S34 extraction notes doc (Bauman ch. 6; Savage ch. 10, Loci 6, 23, 27 to about 1–1.5 m). Literature check (queries run, result, tag): The thickness of the roof-fall debris and the metal-survey null are both published (Bauman 2018; CSEG Recorder 2002: "a significant thickness of roof fall debris"). Web search for "Cave of Letters excavation after 2001" found nothing later (Wikipedia lists no post-2001 work). Repo grep for "Cave of Letters" and "Bauman" found no note comparing the null's depth with the ERT debris depth. Tag: **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED**. The new element is the depth comparison, which limits how much the null can be used as evidence.
Why it might matter: It corrects a coding implication. The metal null rules out a large metal deposit only in the top about 3.5 m of Hall B. Shroder's 1st–2nd-century oven under 2–3 m of boulders in SE Hall B shows that roof fall continued after the Roman period, so deeper debris is not necessarily all pre-Roman. The deeper floor-like GPR reflectors (Jol ch. 5) are in the same untested zone.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Read Bauman 2018 ch. 6 text (Perlego) for the stated EM61 and gradiometer depth of investigation in boulder rubble, which may be less than 3.5 m, and for whether the "ancient" rockfall is dated. If Bauman states a detection limit near 8 m, or if Hall B rests on bedrock at about 3 m along line 2's actual position, the card dies. Also check that relative depth on fig. 6.6 is measured from a datum and not from the surface, since my thickness figures assume it is a datum (Reeder's cross-section uses "metres above or below entrance #1").
Confidence: medium
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. Freund (2004, ch. 7) identifies the Cave of Letters with entry 25. This card limits only the negative evidence; it is not an identification. Registration is for the owner under AGENTS.md.
