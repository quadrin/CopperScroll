# Side-quest D2: reusing the tools of failed runs, with the inventories as datasets (6 Nov 2026)

Lane D2. I chose one reuse: inventory records as data, tested against terrain. I did not use the project's tomb and cave inventories as the base. Their oriented records are few (tombs Amendment 1: 97 checked ASI units, all in Region A; caves: 46) and they carry known duplicate and grid errors. I went back to their common source instead: the **IAA Archaeological Survey of Israel (ASI) online database, all 153 maps, 20,593 site points, English texts** (fetched 6 Nov from `https://survey.iaa.org.il/aspxService/Service_Eng.aspx/GetPolygonsSites?MapId=<id>&sitesId=null`, at 1 request per 1.2 s). Terrain comes from **Copernicus GLO-30** (9 public tiles N29–N33 / E034–E035, `https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/`). Run F's 547-point denominator is not used. Nothing in the repository was changed.

Method in one paragraph. A regex extractor (`private/D2/scripts/extract.py`) reads explicit entrance statements ("W-facing entrance", "the entrance (0.6 m wide) faces NE", "burial cave facing E"; 642 statements) and surveyors' slope words ("on N slope"; 1,523). Each statement is classed as **rock-cut** (cave, tomb, burial, kokh, arcosolium, shelter) or **built** (building, structure, tower, booth, enclosure, room, kiln, church …) by the last subject word before the match. I checked recall against the tombs lane's hand-checked Amendment 1 orientations: 92 of 97 units are recovered. The 5 misses use positional phrasing ("one in the S wall"). Precision: 25 random built units were read and 24 are true entrances; the exception is a mosque niche. Units are **ASI record × subject class**, with the distinct 8-wind facings of each unit. **Deduplication:** (1) a record that states the same facing twice counts once; (2) two units of the same class with the same facing set within 100 m count once (4 dropped; this catches sites repeated on overlapping sheets); (3) units with more than one facing (43) are left out of the primary tests. Terrain is measured at the published point by a plane fit to GLO-30 over 3×3, 7×7 and 13×13 cells. The primary scale is 7×7 (about 200 m), because ASI points are given to about 50–100 m. Points on slopes under 3° are dropped. Two checks come before any test. **DEM control:** for 899 records, the surveyor's own slope word agrees with the GLO-30 downslope aspect within ±45° in 69% of cases (median error 26°). **Test against terrain:** for each class, the deviations (facing minus aspect) are shuffled across sites to make pseudo-facings. This keeps the terrain and the coupling strength, and removes any absolute compass preference. I then compare the counts in 4 sectors (N/E/S/W; a NE-type facing counts half in each neighbouring sector) and the mean resultant vector (20,000 permutations, seed 20261106). I tested 8 sectors first and dropped them: surveyors round to cardinals, so that test gives false significance (see Dead ends).

FIND CARD
Title (one line): In ASI survey records, the doorways of built structures face E and S and avoid W regardless of slope. The pattern is present in Byzantine, undated and Negev structures, not only in Iron Age houses.
Lane: D2
What is odd (2–4 sentences): In 132 deduplicated ASI built-structure units with one stated entrance direction, the entrance has no link to the slope: 23.5% face within ±45° of downslope, against 21.6% under the shuffle (p = 0.33). The entrances do have a strong compass preference after terrain is held fixed. Sector counts, observed against the terrain-null expectation: **N 29.5/34.4, E 48.5/34.1 (p = 0.002), S 39/31.2, W 15/32.3 (p = 0.0001)**. The residual resultant points to 113° (ESE), p < 5×10⁻⁵. The pattern holds in the hill country north of 31° N (n = 99; W 13.5 vs 24.3, p = 0.004), in the Negev (n = 33; W 1.5 vs 8.1, p = 0.001), at records whose only period is Byzantine (n = 17; E 9 vs 5.0, p = 0.02; resultant p = 0.0006) and at undated records (n = 65; W 6.5 vs 16.6, p = 0.001). It also holds map by map (18 sheets E > W, 2 sheets W > E; sign test p = 0.0004). On west-facing slopes (n = 46) the doors face E 10, S 13 and W only 6, so they face across or up the slope. Rock-cut entrances from the same texts, under the same test, behave in the opposite way (next card). So this is not an artefact of the extractor or the DEM.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `out/D2/facing_units.csv` (one row per unit, with ASI URL, class, facing, aspect at 3 scales, slope, period flags, matched phrase); `out/D2/orientation_results.json` (keys `built_asp3`, `built_asp6`, `built_asp1`, `built_asp3_slope8`, `built_statedslope`, `subgroups.built_*`). Example records: ASI Map 49 site 291 ("field tower … on a hill slope descending to the N … The entrance to the structure faces E"); Map 225 site 156 ("All entrances … face southeast"); Map 104 site 30 (one of the few W-facing doors). Full statements with coordinates: `private/D2/data/statements_dem.csv`. Scripts: `private/D2/scripts/` (`fetch_all.py`, `extract.py`, `dem_sample.py`, `analyse.py`, `subgroups.py`).
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Web: "Faust doorway orientation settlement planning cosmology ancient Israel Iron Age II east-facing" found Faust 2001, *Oxford Journal of Archaeology* 20(2): 129–155 (https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/doorway-orientation-settlement-planning-and-cosmology-in-ancient-/), which reports that Iron Age II buildings face E and rarely W, says climatic and functional reasons do not explain this, and reads it as Israelite cosmology. Popular summary: https://thetorah.com/article/houses-oriented-towards-god-in-the-east (over 80% of isolated buildings face E/NE/SE; no other periods compared). Eastward orientation of Negev desert installations is also published (Avner and others; e.g. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0959774323000276). I found no study that holds slope aspect fixed or that compares periods on a national survey database. Repo main and branch_only: "doorway orientation", "Faust", "entrance faces" + "aspect" give no analysis. Tag: **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED**. The preference is published. The new parts are (a) the terrain control (doors ignore slope) and (b) the same E-not-W preference in Byzantine and undated field towers, booths and enclosures, and in the Negev. A cosmology specific to Iron Age Israel cannot explain those. A shared climatic or practical reason, or a long-lived regional habit, fits better.
Why it might matter: It is a direct, checkable test of a well-known interpretive claim, with a public database and public terrain. For this project: a scroll clause or survey description that has a built entrance facing east carries little information, because that is the regional default for built doors.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Read all 132 units by hand: the extractor can mistake a niche or a window for a door (1 of 25 in the audit). Add Faust's Iron Age II house list and test it with the same terrain shuffle. Code periods from the ASI finds field per structure, not per site. Kill it if the hand-read set loses the W deficit, or if a reporting bias can be shown (surveyors noting doors more often when they face east).
Confidence: medium (statistics high; extraction and per-site period coding are automatic)
Touches the Copper Scroll? no. It gives a base rate for "facing" clauses about built features.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): Rock-cut tomb and cave entrances follow the slope and have no compass preference. "West-facing" adds nothing beyond "on a west-facing slope", and Jerusalem tombs do not face the Temple Mount beyond what terrain explains.
Lane: D2
What is odd (2–4 sentences): For 183 deduplicated rock-cut units (all but one north of 31° N; most of them kokhim tombs), 52.5% of entrances lie within ±45° of GLO-30 downslope, against 25% when facings are shuffled across sites (p < 2×10⁻⁴). On the surveyors' own slope words (n = 52), the figure is 61.5% against 31.8%. With terrain held fixed, no direction is preferred: sector counts N 50.5/50.0, E 50/44.0, S 41/44.0, W 41.5/45.1; resultant p = 0.57; quadrant p = 0.68. By slope sector the facings sit on the diagonal: W-facing slopes give 22 of 45 W-facing tombs, and N, E and S slopes behave the same way. Kokhim tombs only: n = 100, W 31 vs 25.0 (p = 0.057), S 18 vs 25.2 (p = 0.045). That is not significant after 8 sector-by-subset looks. Jerusalem: of 71 rock-cut units within 3 km of the Temple Mount, 35% face within ±45° of the bearing to it, against 27% under the terrain shuffle (p = 0.07). At 5 km the figures are 31% against 26% (p = 0.13); at 8 km, p = 0.40.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `out/D2/orientation_results.json` keys `rock_asp1/3/6`, `rock_asp3_slope8`, `rock_statedslope`, `rock_kokhim`, `subgroups.rock_*`, `temple_test` (Temple Mount point 31.7781 N 35.2354 E); `out/D2/facing_units.csv` (subj = rock). Cross-check with the project: the tombs lane's Amendment 1 orientations (`research/regional/tombs/amendment1_codes.csv`, `data/manual_check_amendment1.csv`) are reproduced for 92 of 97 checked ASI units.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Web: "Jerusalem Second Temple period rock-cut tombs entrance orientation topography no fixed direction Kloner Zissu necropolis" found no quantitative test; Kloner & Zissu 2007 itself is blocked in the project. The project's own Hachlili–Killebrew note (tombs README: "no particular orientation of the tomb entrances", p. 51; 27 Jericho tombs) is the nearest statement. Repo: the tombs README Amendment 1 calls west-facing kokhim tombs "common: 24 of 56 oriented strict units (43%)", with no terrain comparison. Tag: **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED** (topographic control is widely assumed; this gives the measured coupling and a null result on any residual compass or Temple preference).
Why it might matter: For the tomb clauses that use a western entrance (tombs-lane branches 52-M and 56-P, and "west-facing rock"), the 43% west share is a slope effect. A west-facing entrance is as common as a west-facing slope, so the clause narrows nothing unless the slope is named too. The contrast with the previous card (built doors ignore slope and avoid W) is also the cleanest validation of the extractor and the DEM.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Add the 46 oriented cave records of `caves_entry25` (Judean Desert cliffs) once they are deduplicated against ASI. Read Kloner & Zissu 2007 for a stated rule. Repeat with the 1940s 1:20,000 contours in Jerusalem, where GLO-30 is a surface model that includes buildings. Kill it if hand-read facings in the Jerusalem ring show a preference toward the Temple at p < 0.01.
Confidence: medium
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. It sets a base rate for the "western entrance" tomb clauses used in the tomb lane's scoring of entries 52 and 56.

## Dead ends

- Facing counts in 8 sectors (chi-square against the terrain shuffle): surveyors write N/E/S/W far more often than NE/SE/SW/NW, while pseudo-facings spread evenly. This gives false significance (rock-cut "SW 0 of 101"). Replaced by 4 sectors with intercardinals split, and by the resultant vector.
- Orientation from the project inventories alone: tombs.csv F7 has 29 values, Amendment 1 has 97 checked ASI units in Region A only, and caves.csv has 46 facings. Too few, and they duplicate ASI. I used the ASI source instead.
- Temple-Mount orientation of Jerusalem tombs: p = 0.07 / 0.13 / 0.40 at 3 / 5 / 8 km; not pursued.
- Period subsets of rock-cut tombs (Iron-only, Byzantine-only, bench tombs): n = 2–19, too small. Roman-only, n = 70: no preference (p = 0.51).
- Map references in ASI texts against the online points (1,172 records; 43 over 1 km): almost all point to another place named in the text (e.g. 106/103 and 106/123 give Theodosius Monastery's reference; 109/7/35 gives Cross and Milik's tower NE of Kh. Abu Ṭabaq), or they are OIG/NIG or E/N swaps. Clear typos: 106/3 Ramat Talpiot (E and N swapped); 77/37 and 77/111 carry a Judean Desert reference (1773/1488, 1722/1488) on a sheet far away. Low value, not carded (`private/D2/data/mapref_audit.csv`).
- I did not pursue H's character model on CIIP or ostraca. The model is trained on DSS Hebrew plus Mishnah and Tosefta, with no Aramaic, so it does not fit the Idumean ostraca. CIIP Hebrew items with published rival restorations are few and mostly names, where H is weak (22 of 40 names). Too few for a calibrated test in one pass.
- I did not pursue A2's frame registration on other photo pairs; the lane note asks for one reuse.
