# Side-quest A4: the Holy Sepulchre underground 3D model (6 Nov 2026)

**Input:** `gs://copper-scroll-cog-1030/models/grabeskirche-untergrund/GrabeskircheUntergrund.glb` (517,017,896 B; MD5 `380113c821a21e6992be2879e904fab3`, the same as the repository checksum) and `GrabeskircheUntergrund_C4D.zip` (276,979,487 B). Drive copies: 06 Imagery, file IDs `1H5mroRNR7WGQ0-ztxXhd5UJHX3rtSonq` (GLB) and `1o1s8vHwvYFWyQCCvtsAqD7iduZUV0NRS` (zip).

**What the model is:** Holger Siegel (DEI Jerusalem; ORCID 0009-0002-1265-4629), *A three-dimensional computer-generated reconstruction of the bedrock beneath the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem* / *Eine dreidimensionale computergenerierte Rekonstruktion des Untergrunds der Grabeskirche in Jerusalem*. University of Wuppertal research-data repository, issued 2026-05-26, DOI https://doi.org/10.57899/bkf2-pv55 (item page https://publicdata.fdm.uni-wuppertal.de/items/28aad4c9-a273-42ba-90f6-94ef7f08c085; metadata API on `publicdata-bak.fdm.uni-wuppertal.de/server/api/core/items/28aad4c9-...`). It was made in MAXON Cinema 4D Studio and exported to GLB (the `asset.generator` string reads "13.061"). It has one bedrock mesh, "Untergrundmodell" (5,966,136 vertices, 1,988,712 triangles, a HyperNURBS subdivision). It also has 99 textured planes that carry the source plans and sections in their registered positions: PEF/SCHICK/197, /203 and /227; Schick in ZDPV 1885 and 1889, PEQ 1888, 1889 and 1897, and CSB 1889; Corbo 1981, 23 plates; and Gibson & Taylor 1994, 12 figures.

**Licence:** the model, the modelling work and the metadata are **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. The source drawings are excluded: the `tex/` folder and the images embedded in the GLB carry "No reuse, reproduction, modification, extraction, republication, redistribution" (`tex/README_LICENSE_NOTICE.txt` in the zip, dated 2026-05-28, and the repository page). For this run that means numbers measured from the mesh may be published with attribution. **No texture was extracted or viewed.** Mesh-only renders are in `private/A4/` and are not for the repository.

**Data files here (all derived from the mesh or the glTF JSON; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, attribution: Siegel 2026, DOI above):**
- `glb_nodes_world.csv`: all 132 nodes with world yaw, tilt and centre. Elevations are m asl as the model encodes them; x/z are model-local metres, with +x roughly east and +z roughly south.
- `mesh_enclosed_voids.csv`: 38 enclosed downward-facing (ceiling) surfaces of 1 m² or more, with ceiling and floor elevations and the nearest small source plane.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The model registers its bedrock to Schick's PEF/SCHICK/203 frame at 8.2°, and every Corbo 1981 and Gibson & Taylor 1994 plan sits 1.3–2.0° away from it. Model datum: 2,420 ft (737.616 m), with Corbo's ±0.00 at 753.53 m asl.
Lane: A4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The root node is literally named `---NULL_8.2°_2420ft_73761.6cm_üMM---`. It sits at 737.616 m, which is exactly 2,420 ft. The bedrock mesh and the Schick 203 general plan both have world yaw 8.20°. The Corbo 1981 general plates have world yaw 6.15° (Pl. 1), 6.74° (Pl. 6), 6.82° (Pl. 67) and 6.94° (Pl. 3). The Gibson & Taylor figures have 6.18–6.71° (Fig. 2 6.57°, Fig. 36 6.71°, Fig. 45 6.18°). So the 20th-century plans need a 1.3–2.0° different rotation from Schick's to fit the same rock. That gives a 1.1–1.8 m tangential offset 50 m from the pivot. There is also a 0.8° spread among Corbo's own general plates. All Corbo and Gibson & Taylor plans sit at the elevation 737.616 + 15.914 = 753.53 m asl (Corbo's ±0.00, ≈ 2,472.2 ft). On that datum the mesh puts the top of the Calvary rock at 758.31 m (+4.78 m), which matches the published "4.8 m high" (Hesemann 1999: 170, via the Calvary article). It also gives a St Helena chapel floor at about 748.8 m (−4.7), the quarry floor at 746.6–747.8 m (−5.8 to −6.9), the lowest point under St Vartan at 743.4 m (−10.2), and the cistern floor near Abraham's convent at 737.3 m (−16.2).
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `glb_nodes_world.csv` (node 0 root; node 12 mesh; node 83 PEF_Schick_203-13_Plan; nodes 33–35 and 58 Corbo general plates; nodes 59, 63, 65 and 66 G&T). Levels came from 0.5 m max/min grids of the mesh. Method: memory-mapped accessor 1, world transform of node 12 = translation (0, 753.53, 0) with yaw 8.2°.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): web searches on the Calvary height and St Helena depth, and on Wilson's 1865 levels for the Holy Sepulchre, found no published absolute (m asl) datum for Corbo's ±0.00 to check against. The corpus has no Corbo 1981 or Gibson & Taylor 1994 PDF: `pdf-search` "Sepulchre", "Calvary", "Helena" and "Golgotha" in SBF 36 and NEAEHL returned only bibliography hits. Tag: **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** (that this registration exists and its numbers). The underlying point, that the Schick, Corbo and Gibson & Taylor surveys differ in orientation, is unverified.
Why it might matter: the rotation mismatch either shows a real north or axis difference between the 19th- and 20th-century surveys, or comes from the modeller's fitting. Either way, features modelled from Schick and checked against Corbo can be off by over a metre at the edges, for example at the Khan ez-Zeit and the Russian compound. The 753.53 m datum is the model's claim, not a measured value. If it is wrong, every absolute level in the model shifts with it.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): compare the north arrows on Corbo 1981 pl. 1–3 and on PEF/SCHICK/203-13 (we may not extract them from the textures, so use the original publications). Get one published m-asl level for the Rotunda or Calvary floor, for example from Wilson's OS 1:2,500 or the Albright 1:2,000 sheets in Drive 07, which have spot heights, and test the 753.53 m datum against it. Kill it if both plan families share one north and the offset comes from the modeller.
Confidence: medium (numbers high; interpretation low)
Touches the Copper Scroll? no.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The mesh contains about 38 enclosed rock-cut voids. The third largest (18 × 12 m, about 12 m deep, floor about 740.6 m) has no dedicated source plate, and no kokhim tomb (Tomb of Joseph of Arimathea) is modelled at all.
Lane: A4
What is odd (2–4 sentences): Clustering the downward-facing (ceiling) faces gives 38 enclosed cavities. The two largest are the cavity under Schick's shaft east of the church (211 m², floor about 739.5 m; next to PEF/SCHICK/197 and ZDPV 1889 pl. IV) and the St Vartan / quarry chamber (200 m², floor about 744.7 m; next to G&T figs 6–10). The third, at model x −37, z −71.5 (north of the St Helena chapel), has a ceiling plan of 18.0 × 12.4 m, a ceiling at about 752.6 m and a floor at about 740.6 m. Its nearest dedicated source plane is 26 m away, so only the general Schick 203-13 plan can stand behind it. It may be the "Great Cistern" or the Coptic St Helena cistern; the C4D file has cameras named `GreatCistern.S/SO`. Two bottle-shaped cisterns with round mouths lie north-west of the Rotunda (floors about 744.9 and 745.0 m; at Corbo pl. 60 and 64). But nothing resembling kokhim appears west of the Rotunda, where the kokhim Tomb of Joseph of Arimathea lies. There, the model shows only a flat rectangular cut at about 752 m.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `mesh_enclosed_voids.csv`, rows 1–7. Private mesh-only renders: `private/A4/top_surface_hillshade.png`, `vertical_span.png`, `west_of_rotunda_detail.png`. Context for new data the model lacks: HA 136 (2024), "Jerusalem, Church of the Holy Sepulchre" (Stasolla, Sapienza; permit G-20/2022; Drive `1ud4Ex_16Ahc0gZeerdvDY3AupnBUiBoT`). It reports bedrock with quarry marks, cultivation soils, dismantled tomb structures and water channels linked to a cistern in the northern Rotunda and the Arches of the Virgin. None of this is among the model's sources.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): web searches "Coptic Patriarchate cistern of St Helena dimensions", plus the dannythedigger.com and jerusalemexperience.com cistern pages. They describe the cistern but give no measurements. Corbo 1981 and Gibson & Taylor 1994 are not in the corpus. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT**. That the model omits the kokhim tomb is **NEW TO THE LITERATURE**, since nothing has been published on the model.
Why it might matter: it shows which parts of the underground are documented by a source and which are interpolated or taken from Schick's general plan alone. It also shows that the most diagnostic Second Temple feature on the site, the kokhim tomb, is missing from the "bedrock" model. Any use of the model to test where Golgotha was, or the ancient ground surface, should take this gap into account.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): check PEF/SCHICK/203-13 (an original or PEF online copy, not the embedded texture) for a large cistern at that position. Check Corbo 1981 pl. 64–65 and Kloner & Zissu 2003 (Drive 03; no text layer) for the kokhim tomb's plan and level. Kill the "undocumented" part if 203-13 draws the cavity with these dimensions.
Confidence: medium
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. The model is a well-registered set of levels for rock-cut cisterns and tombs in Jerusalem. No scroll entry is implied.
