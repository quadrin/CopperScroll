## B5, card 2: Kuhn's ThLZ reports date his study to "Herbst 1953", mention no mirrors, and record the Berlin Theologentag of 3–6 Jan 1954

- **Verdict: SURVIVES, TAG CHANGED** (sub-tag dropped). Final tag: KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED for the date; NEW TO THE PROJECT for the Berlin presentation. The "THLZ 74 miscitation" is **not** a NEW TO THE LITERATURE item. It is a footnote typo: Lefkovits's own bibliography (PDF p. 592, "THLZ 79 (1954), 303-304") and his p. 308 note ("THLZ 79 (1954)") give the volume correctly.
- **Confidence:** high. **Cost:** cheap.

**Evidence checked.** I downloaded the OpenDigi OCR of both volumes myself.
- ThLZ 79: "bei meinem Aufenthalt in Jerusalem im Herbst 1953 einiges entziffern". Confirmed.
- The Theologentag heading reads "Deutscher Evangelischer Theologentag zu Berlin 3.–6. Januar 1954". The editorial note says "der Kurzbericht über das Referat von K. G. Kuhn/Göttingen über die Kupferrollen … eigentlich in die Neutestamentliche Sektion … gehörte". Confirmed: the résumé reports a Theologentag paper.
- ThLZ 81: "So habe ich sie damals immer nur unter Glas gesehen" and "im Jahre 1953". Confirmed. The 1956 text has no mirror apparatus.

**Reviewer addition (alternative source of the error).** Both German texts contain the words that Lefkovits's sentence may have come from.
- ThLZ 81 says the letters showed through "gewissermaßen in Spiegelschrift" (as mirror writing).
- ThLZ 79 has "Als im Frühjahr 1952 Beduinen …" next to "Herbst 1953".
- So "spring" and "mirrors" may be misreadings of Kuhn's own German, not facts from another source such as Yadin 1957. This makes the card's conclusion (an error, not a second visit) more likely. It does not change the tag.

**Round-1 link.** This runs the kill test that R1 asked for ("needs new data only to exclude a second, spring visit (Kuhn, THLZ 79)"). The result and the Berlin date are new, so it is not a restatement.

**Note.** "First made public at Berlin" means the earliest public statement found. A press mention before January 1954 was not searched for.

## C1b, card 2: SWP's 21-kokhim two-tier tomb "near" ʿAin ed Duk has no later record in the project

- **Verdict: WEAKENED.** Final tag: NEW TO THE PROJECT (provisional).
- **Confidence:** medium (absent from project records); low (absent from the literature). **Cost:** moderate.

**Evidence checked.** SWP Memoirs III p. 190 (workspace OCR `scratch/C2/dl/memoirs3.txt` ll. 9957–9964): "a chamber with 21 kokim in two tiers … The lower tier contains three kokim at the back, and 4 each side. The upper tier has only three on the left hand side". Confirmed. `tombs.csv` has no Duk / Naʿaran tomb row.

**Why weakened.**
- (1) **SWP's own count does not add up.** 3 + 4 + 4 + 3 = **14** loculi, not 21. The card repeats "21-loculus" and gives the 3 + 4 + 4 / 3 breakdown without noticing. Either the total or the description is wrong in SWP, and the title should not rest on "21".
- (2) The literature check missed the obvious place: the Jericho Second Temple necropolis publications (Hachlili & Killebrew 1999, IAA Reports 7; Bennett and Kenyon's Jericho tombs). Their surveys of the hills west and north-west of Jericho may cite or re-find SWP's tomb. I could not open the one web copy; the fetch needed approval that went unanswered. Until that is checked, "no later record" holds only for the project's own data.
- (3) **ARGUMENT (Doq, entry 31) overstates its value.** Second Temple activity at the Duk/Nuʿeima springs is well known (the aqueducts from ʿEin Duk and Nuʿeima; Naʿaran). A kokhim tomb near the spring adds nothing to the Doq search area. The card says the tomb is not a candidate, which is right.
