FIND CARD
Title (one line): The last block of the EBAF repertory (EBAF 15747–15778, all "1953, de Vaux") includes views of loci that the same volume's field notes say were first dug in 1954 and 1955.
Lane: A2
Round-1 link: A2-3 (same register). This is a new contradiction: date against content, not number against date.
What is odd (2–4 sentences): Rows 1172–1204 (pp.390–391) date EBAF 15747–15778 to 1953. Three frames contradict that date. EBAF 15755 is "Qm., loc. 123", but L123 was created on 23-2-1955 ("Nouveau locus…", p.330), when L122 was split. EBAF 15753 (album 98) has the caption "Le locus 99 aux périodes Ib et II … la façade extérieure occidentale … Les portes aux montants appareillés … communiquent avec les loci 12 et 13" (p.277). Yet L99 was opened on 10-4-1954, and before that there was only "une tranchée … faite en 1951 jusqu'à un pavement" (p.322). Its lower plastered floor was reached on 21-3-1955. The repertory labels EBAF 15753 "loc. 148", and L148 belongs to the "voie" trench, which the notes say was "fouillé en 1955" (from 3-2-1955, p.322). The block's numbers also come after all the 1956 frames (15004–15051).
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): IMG_3025 p.390, rows 1172–1190 (EBAF 15747–15765, catalogue pp.310–319); p.391 rows 1191–1204 (15766–15778). IMG_2967 p.277, caption 98. IMG_2990 p.322, L99 entry ("Photographies dans l'album : 98, 272"; 10-4-1954; 11–12-4-1954; 21-3-1955) and "Loci 99, 128, 148 : la tranchée « voie »" (3-2-1955, 4-2-1955). IMG_2994 p.330, L122 and L123 entries. Table: `late_block_15747_15778_vs_fieldnotes.csv`. Private crops: `private2/A2/H94_p390_rows1155-1190_EBAF15035-15765.jpg`, `…p391_rows1191-1204…`, `…p322_L99_and_voie_entries.jpg`, `…p330_L122_L123_entries.jpg`, `…p277_caption98.jpg`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Corpus: `hexview.py pdf-search` for "15753", "15 753", "15755", "15 755", "15756" across the store (HG2003, HCM2016, HF2019, Magen 2018, Wagemakers 2014): 0 hits. Repo main and `branch_only/`: no hit for these numbers. Web: no discussion of EBAF numbering or misdated frames found (queries listed under card 2; see also round-1 card 3). The 2003 English edition and the EBAF photothèque register are not in the corpus. Tag: **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** (provisional, because the 2003 edition was not checked).
Why it might matter: Anyone who dates a Qumran photo by its repertory year would put 1955 states of L99, L122/123 and the west trench in 1953. The other frames in the block (tower, NW quadrant, L30, L34, L43, L48–49, L71, caves 4Q–5Q) carry no date of their own. If the block is one film, all 32 frames are probably 1955 or later. Two captions in the block also disagree with the repertory's locus (see card 4: 98 = L99, not L148; 15 = the tower, not L99). That suggests the block's entries were compiled from loose prints.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Kill it if 15753/15755 can be shown to be pre-excavation views labelled with later locus numbers. The editors do this elsewhere: L71 for 1949/1951 frames, and L134 "(?)" for a 1951 trench frame. That reading does not fit caption 98, which describes Ib/II levels and door jambs. Confirm it from the EBAF register or the original sleeves (Open Jerusalem `FR-EBAF/Ph`), or by matching the album-98 photograph with a dated 1954/1955 view of the west façade (PAM 41572 = album 272, "Les loci 148 et 99"). The catalogue page (310–319) does not settle the question: EBAF catalogue pages are partly topographic. For example, 1951 9×12 cemetery frames sit on pp.264–268 among the 1953 tomb pages.
Confidence: medium-high that the volume contradicts itself; medium that the printed year (rather than the locus label) is the error.
Touches the Copper Scroll? no.

Title (one line): The 1994 repertory lists four more negatives of the copper rolls *in situ* in Cave 3Q besides Reed's ASOR 14/15: EBAF 13781–13783 (de Vaux, 1952) and PAM 41958 (printed "1955").
Lane: A2
Round-1 link: new. It extends the 3 Nov reading note, which knew only Lot 9, ASOR 14/15.
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The project's photo records (DQCAAS README, the non-Allegro captions file, the 3 Nov reading note) know the in-situ copper rolls only through Reed's ASOR 1952-14/15. Lot 2, p.387, rows 978–980, lists EBAF 13781, 13782 and 13783: "1952, de Vaux, 24 × 36, page catalogue 105, Rouleau de cuivre *in situ*". They sit in a 1952 run with Cave 2Q, the cliff panoramas, the aqueduct (13779–13780) and "Hajjar el-Asbaʿ, dominant les grottes" (13784). None of them has an album number. Lot 3, p.393, row 1320, lists PAM 41958, "1955, 9 × 12, Grotte 3, rouleaux de cuivre in situ", with no catalogue page. The rolls were found on 20 March 1952, lifted, and taken to the Palestine Museum in 1952 (de Vaux, RB 1953, as extracted in `research/history/devaux1953_cave_list_extracted.md`), so a 1955 exposure of them *in situ* is impossible. PAM 41958 must be a copy negative, or its year is wrong.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): IMG_3023 p.387, block of rows 968–981 = EBAF 13771–13784. In the definition column, "Rouleau de cuivre in situ" appears three times, followed by "Hajjar el-Asbaʿ". In the catalogue column the run reads 105 ×5, then 089. IMG_3026 p.393, row 1320, PAM 41958 (block 1319–1323: PAM 41578a aerial; 41958 rolls; 41959 Grotte 4Qa; 41960 "Ouvriers, Grotte 3Q"; 41961 panorama; all printed 1955). Lot 9 (p.406): ASOR 14 and 15, "EBAF Qumran catalogue page 105" (3 Nov reading note, `00_Reading_and_index/Copper_Scroll_Humbert_I_reading.md`; OCR line "1952 Reed ASOR 14 105 Rouleau de cuivre dans la grotte 3Q"). DJD III vol. I, Table des planches (store PDF p.19): Pl. III.2 "Les rouleaux de cuivre en place dans la grotte 3Q", with no photographer named. Private crops: `private2/A2/H94_p387_rows968-981_EBAF13771-13784_rouleau_in_situ.jpg`, `…p393_PAM41958_rouleaux_in_situ_1955.jpg`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Repo main and `branch_only/`: "13781", "13 781", "13783", "Rouleau de cuivre in situ", "catalogue page 105": no relevant hit; the numeric hits are coordinates. Store `pdf-search` for "13781", "13 781", "13782", "13783": 0 hits. Web: `"Copper Scroll" "in situ" Cave 3 1952 photograph de Vaux École biblique negative photo credit` and `"41.958" OR "41958" PAM copper scroll cave 3 photograph`. Both found only general pages (Wikipedia, BAS image pages, EBAF news); none gives an EBAF or PAM number for the in-situ views. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT** (the numbers are printed in 1994, so not new to the literature).
Why it might matter: The in-situ views are the only record of how the two rolls lay in 3Q ("l'un sur l'autre", against the back wall; RB 1953). EBAF catalogue page 105 seems to hold at least five images of the deposit: 3 EBAF and 2 ASOR. The planned frame concordance (reading note, "next work", second item) should include EBAF 13781–83 and PAM 41958, and record PAM 41958 as a copy with no exposure date.
ARGUMENT (scroll consequence): this concerns the find context of 3Q15 as an object, not any entry. More views of the deposit could show which roll lay on top and the state of the outer turns before lifting. That bears on B5/A1b questions about damage before 1955, not on any identification. It neither helps nor hurts any candidate.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Kill it (as "more views") if EBAF 13781–83 turn out to be EBAF copy negatives of Reed's ASOR 14/15. The shared catalogue page 105 allows this, and Lot 2 names "de Vaux" as operator throughout. Telling the cases apart needs the images. The IAA Leon Levy DSS Digital Library indexes PAM numbers, so an online lookup of PAM 41.958 (AI-only, web) is the next check. The EBAF frames need the EBAF photothèque. No request was made.
Confidence: high (that the repertory lists these rows); medium (that they are distinct exposures).
Touches the Copper Scroll? yes. These are photographs of 3Q15 in situ; they bear on the object, not on its places.

Title (one line): The whole Lot 2 table read: EBAF numbers are out of year order in at least eight places (1958 frames numbered below 1955 and 1956 frames), and three 9×12 blocks contradict the header's "11 430 à 15 778 = 24 × 36".
Lane: A2
Round-1 link: A2-3. This runs its kill test: "read the full Lot 2 table (pp.370–394) and test whether number order matches date order elsewhere".
What is odd (2–4 sentences): In number order, the dated blocks run 1909 → 1951 (glass) → 1953 → 1949 → 1951 → 1953 → 1954 (ends 12265) → **1958 (12916–12986, Feshkha)** → 1955 (13539–13752) → 1952 (13753–13798) → 1951 (13801–13821) → 1958 (14137–14195) → 1955 (14357–14389) → 1956 (15004–15051) → 1953 (15747–15778). The 1958 Feshkha block has lower numbers than every 1955 and 1956 frame. If the numbers are accession numbers, the 1955–56 film was therefore registered after the 1958 film. R1's explanation ("1949 Leica film entered late") then becomes a general rule: the numbers were assigned in batches, out of order, after 1958. Three of these blocks are 9 × 12 (13801–13821, 1951; 14137–14195, 1958; 14357–14389, 1955). The p.370 header says the whole 11 430–15 778 range is 24 × 36.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Checked by eye: IMG_3020 p.381 (1954 run ends at 12265; the next row is 1958, EBAF 12916, "Feshkha, loc. 6"); IMG_3023 p.387 (1952 run to 13798; 9 × 12 frames 13801–13821 "1951", tombs 3–10 and loci 1–4; 14137–14144 "1958, 9 × 12, Feshkha"); IMG_3025 p.390 (1956 frames 15015–15051, then 15747 "1953"); IMG_3015 p.370 (header). The other blocks come from Tesseract OCR of the 13 spreads (pp.370–395) with spot checks. Table: `lot2_ebaf_number_blocks.csv`. Private crops: `private2/A2/H94_p381_1954_to_1958_transition.jpg`, `…p387_9x12_blocks_13801-14144.jpg`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): As for round-1 card 3 (R1 reran it); no discussion of EBAF numbering found. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT** (the 1958-before-1955 inversion may be new to the literature; not decidable without the EBAF register).
Why it might matter: It closes the question round 1 left open. An EBAF number dates nothing, not even relative to other EBAF numbers. The header's format ranges cannot be used to sort originals from copies, since 9 × 12 frames occur inside the "24 × 36" range. Any concordance must take year and format from each row, never from the number range.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Kill it if the 1958 dates on rows 672–~740 are misprints for 1956 (the first Feshkha season). The subjects rule this out: they are Feshkha loci 14–35, which the notes date to 1958 (pp.357–364). The EBAF register (`FR-EBAF/Ph`) is the external control.
Confidence: high (printed facts); low (cause).
Touches the Copper Scroll? no.

## Dead ends

- OCR "Qm., loc. 104" for 1953 rows 325–331 (EBAF 11914–11920) is an OCR misreading. The print reads "10A" (p.375), and caption 22 agrees. Not an error.
- 1951 frames labelled "loc. 134" (11520 = album 385, "(?)"; 13821 = album 386): the caption itself says the 1951 trench was "peut-être à l'emplacement du locus 134". Not an error.
- "Loc. 71" in 1949, 1951 and 1953 frames: the field notes say the cistern was "déjà visible" when its clearance began on 7-3-1954. The label is retrospective, not an error.
- Flag "Feshkha loc. 6, 1954" (row 670): OCR line drift. On the page the 1954 run ends at L100 and Feshkha loc. 6 is 1958.
- The automatic join of captions × repertory by EBAF number joined 105 of 218 caption numbers and gave 13 flags. Nine were OCR drift (e.g. 12199 really is L66, as the caption says). The four real ones are in card 4.
- EBAF catalogue page as a date proxy: not usable. Pages are partly topographic (1951 9×12 cemetery frames on pp.264–268, among the 1953 tomb pages).
- Round-1 card 1 (Boer P17 = KhQ 1627?): album 302 is PAM 41005/41006 (a general view of L91, 83, 82, 81 and 88; Lot 3, p.392), and 304 shows L91 still incompletely excavated in 1992. Neither shows the vessel, so the album comparison R1 proposed cannot be made. The kill test (exposure–registration lag) is still open.
