# A1c — EDF revers plates for segments 2–9: the remaining open 1955 labels (6 Nov 2026, orchestrator, bounded pass)

Follow-up to A1-1 and A1b-1 (see `../A1/FINDS.md`, `../A1b/FINDS.md`, `../review/R7.md`). Source: Brizemeure, Lacoudre & Puech 2006 (STDJ 55), store copy B (`Brizemeure-Lacoudre-Puech-2006-…-39ffb801`), relevé plates CXII–CXXVI (PDF pp. 404–418) and verso photographs Pl. XXXII–XXXIV (PDF pp. 319–321). Renders were made at 110–300 dpi in the workspace and are not kept (publisher's images). Data: `labels_seg2-9_from_EDF_plates.csv`.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): EDF's own revers plates resolve the "?" on segments 6–7 and show E1 split by the 3|4 cut and E4 drawn on both sides of the 4|5 cut
Lane: A1c (follow-up of A1 / A1b)
What is odd (2–4 sentences): EDF's text (PDF pp. 67–79) leaves three anomalies in the 1955 white-ink cut-mark ranges: E1 never appears, E4 is listed on two segments, and segments 6 and 7 end in "?". On EDF's own revers drawings the "?" on segments 6 and 7 are simply the last labels (D3, D8) drawn truncated at the cut edge; E1 appears only as fragments on both edges of the 3|4 cut (a partial glyph on segment 3, a broken "E" on segment 4), i.e. it was split by the saw cut; and a full "E4" is drawn at the right end of segment 4 and again at the left end of segment 5. Segment 3 also carries one tick with two numbers (11 and 12), visible on the 1994 photograph as well as on the drawing.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): EDF 2006 copy B, Pl. CXII (PDF p. 404), CXIV (406), CXVI (408), CXVIII (410), CXX (412), CXXII (414), CXXIV (416), CXXVI (418), foot of the "revers" drawing; verso photos Pl. XXXII–XXXIV (PDF pp. 319–321), lower third of the strip. Row-by-row readings in `labels_seg2-9_from_EDF_plates.csv`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Wright Baker describes the F–A × 1–12 scheme (BJRL 39, 1956, p. 52; DJD III pp. 205, 207; see B5b) but lists no per-segment ranges; EDF gives the ranges and draws the labels; no publication found that reconciles EDF's text anomalies with its plates (Google Scholar/web: "rouleau de cuivre" "repère" "découpe de 1955"; "Copper Scroll" "Wright Baker" marks segments; project repo + branch_only: "E4", "D3 ?", "repère"). Tag: NEW TO THE PROJECT (the plate readings); KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED for the scheme itself.
Why it might matter: with these readings every letter of the large roll has its 12 positions accounted for except the first position of two turns (F1 not on segment 2, E1 split by the 3|4 cut), so the marks can serve as a turn-by-turn length scale for the large roll (cm per mark per turn), which A1/R1 used to fix the orientation (F inner, A outer).
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): (1) checked after the owner's prompt: the 1988 USC verso previews ARE in the repository (`images/previews/cuts-01-10/`, ~1000 px tall): cut 4 colour UC15246035 and b&w UC15246026 show "E4" painted near the right end (and an "E" whose number is hidden, = E3); cut 5 colour UC15245686 and b&w UC15246312 show, along the foot, a lone "5" exactly at the left edge, then E6, E7, E8. The strip curves away at that edge, so an E4 further left (where EDF draws it, beside E5) would be out of view: the 1988 photographs are consistent with EDF but cannot confirm the second E4; (2) at book resolution the left end of segment 5 is also unreadable on EDF Pl. XXXIV; (3) the Manchester 1955–56 photographs with Baker's paper index strips (DJD III p. 207) would settle E1 and E4.
Confidence: high (what the plates draw); low (that E4 is painted twice on the copper rather than drawn twice).
Touches the Copper Scroll? yes — physical record of the 1955 opening and the large roll's winding geometry.
