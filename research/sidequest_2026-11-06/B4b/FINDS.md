# Side-quest swarm, lane B4b (follow-up to B4-1: the JSP 19 catalogue): finds, 6 Nov 2026

Source: Magen, Bijovsky & Tzionit 2021, *Mount Gerizim Excavations III: The Coins* (JSP 19). Bucket store doc `Magen-Bijovsky-Tzionit-2021-Mount-Gerizim-Excavations-III-Th-1b6843b6` (284 pp.). It is cited by printed page [n]; PDF page = printed + 12 (`hexview.py pdf-text`/`pdf-page`). The catalogue is typological. Each row is one registered coin (area, locus, basket, K no.). "Total by type" is a merged cell that covers the rows of one type, so a ruler's isolated-coin count is the sum of those cells. Each total was read from the page images (PDF 157–168), because the text layer loses the merged cells. Data file: `jsp19_catalogue_counts.csv` (20 rows).

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The Gerizim silver-hoard basket held 130 registered coins, not 129. The 130th is a bronze of Antiochus IV catalogued as an isolated find, so the basket mixes hoard and non-hoard coins, and the only coin that dates the hoard to 120/119 is in question.
Lane: B4b
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The silver hoard is published as 129 coins, all silver, Cat. 762–890, from "Area P, L4749, B47518". Their K numbers form one continuous run, K 29807–29936, with a single gap at K 29935. K 29935 is Cat. 233: a 12.5 mm, 1.67 g bronze of Laodicea ad Mare (quasi-municipal, Antiochus IV, ca. 168–164 BCE), "P | 4749 | 47518 | total 1". That is the same locus and the same basket as the hoard, yet it is listed among the isolated coins. So basket B47518 was not the vessel's contents alone, or the hoard was not all silver. This matters because the hoard's closing date of 120/119 rests on one coin. Cat. 890 is the only non-Tyrian coin and the only coin later than 126/125 (an Akko tetradrachm of Antiochus VIII, year 193). In 2008 JSP 8 gave the hoard's latest coin as 125 BCE. Cat. 890's own number, K 29922, sits inside the Antiochus VII block, between Cat. 786 (K 29921) and Cat. 785 (K 29923), the two S.E. 183 tetradrachms.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): JSP 19 printed [146] (PDF 158), Cat. 233 row: "Laodicea ad Mare (Syria) | ca. 168–164 | … SC 2: 76, No. 1431 | P | 4749 | 47518 | 1 | 29935" (checked on the page image). Printed [184]–[187] (PDF 196–199): Cat. 762–890 with their K numbers. Recomputed from the catalogue: the 129 K numbers fill 29807–29936 except 29935, with no duplicates. The K blocks follow date groups, e.g. DII S.E. 185 = K 29807–29833 and S.E. 186 = K 29834–29868. Cat. 890 (PDF 199): "ʿAkko-Ptolemais | 120/119 | … Eagle stg. l. on thunderbolt. To l. field: M; to r. field, date: [glyph lost in text layer] (= year 193) | SC 2: 515, No. 2337.2 | 29922". Bijovsky p.[114]–[116] (PDF 126–128): "A hoard of 129 Seleucid silver coins … (Area P, L4749, B47518"; "All coins in the hoard are silver issues"; Cat. 890 "provides the terminus post quem". Plate 58 (PDF 276): the photo of Cat. 890 is a 173×165 px embedded image, too coarse to read the date. JSP 8 p.[170] (store `01-JSP8-…` p93): "the latest date to 125 BCE". For comparison, the bronze hoard (Cat. 701–761, K 11768–11828) is a clean run of 61 numbers with no gaps and nothing outside it.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Corpus: `grep` for "Laodicea", "29935", "47518", "Cat. 890" over the repo (main) and `branch_only/`. "47518" occurs only in `registration/magen2021_gerizim_coins_extracted.md` (as the hoard locus); the other hits are unrelated numeric matches. Web: "Mount Gerizim Seleucid silver hoard 129 tetradrachms Tyre Antiochus VIII Ptolemais 120/119 Bijovsky" found only IES product pages and unrelated hoard news. No review of JSP 19 found. Tag: **NEW TO THE LITERATURE**.
Why it might matter: This hoard is the corpus's closest analogue to a temple treasury hidden in a wall (B4 card 3). Its closing date is the basis of Magen's statement that the destruction came "some nine years later" (p.[44]). If B47518 also took coins from the collapse fill, as the bronze coin shows it did, then Cat. 890 may be a fill coin as well. In that case the vessel closes at 126/125, which is the 2008 figure and fits Bijovsky's alternative of a deposit c. 125 tied to John Hyrcanus (p.[117]). The inference does not run the other way: a bronze in the basket does not prove that Cat. 890 was outside the vessel.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): (1) The IAA coin database records for K 29922 and K 29935 (registration date, basket, identification history) and the digital appendix the volume cites (printed 82). (2) The excavation diary or basket list for B47518 (unpublished; JSP vol. IV/VI). (3) A better photo of Cat. 890 to confirm year 193 and the Akko type. Kill: if the IAA record shows that K 29935 was registered from a sub-basket outside the vessel and that K 29922 came from inside it, the 120/119 close stands and only the "129, all silver" wording needs a note.
Confidence: high for the registration facts; low–medium that Cat. 890 is intrusive.
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe: it decides when the one sanctuary "treasury" analogue in the corpus was hidden.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The 110/109 BCE destruction terminus and the "latest coins 112–110 BCE" rest on three coins whose dates the catalogue itself queries. All three come from Area P and none has a published stratum; the queries drop out as the text passes from Bijovsky to Magen.
Lane: B4b
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The catalogue's only coin of 110/109 is Cat. 346, an Antiochus IX bronze from Antioch. It is printed as "110/109(?)", with the date read "ΓΣ(?) (= S.E. 203)"; the variant is not in SC, which lists only S.E. 199–200 for the type. It is the only coin of its type and comes from Area P, L5079, B51884, K 35244. The "112–110" Akko coins are Cat. 383 ("113/112(?)", "Date unpublished", P L4494 B46980) and Cat. 384 ("112/111(?)", P L5072 B50685). Bijovsky calls their dates "not completely clear". Magen's "411 … up to 110 BCE" has no catalogue coin dated 110; the "110" is the end of the range given to coins with illegible dates (Cat. 385, "126–110"). The hedging fades from page to page: Bijovsky p.[100] "If the reading of the apparent date on Cat. No. 346 is correct", p.[104] "most likely", p.[126] "according to the last coin of Antiochus IX, Cat. No. 346", p.[129] "not before 110/109"; then Magen p.[39] "captured, in 110/109", p.[42] "one coin to 110/109". Without the three queried readings, the latest secure dates are 113/112 (Antiochus IX Cat. 345, S.E. 200; Antiochus VIII Cat. 340, but its "[L]Σ" is partly restored) and, for Akko, 116/115 (Cat. 381–382).
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Catalogue printed [153]–[154] (PDF 165–166): Cat. 340, 344, 345, 346 (page image checked). Printed [156] (PDF 168): Cat. 381–385. Bijovsky p.[100] (PDF 112): the conditional and Fig. 6. The Fig. 6 photo (PDF 112, embedded 225/239 px) shows a heavily corroded coin and no date can be read from it. p.[102] (PDF 114): the Akko dated series runs "almost without gaps to 116/115"; Seyrig ended the series at 118/117 and thought a private 110/109 Akko coin "a fake". p.[104] (PDF 116): the ratio argument (68:3:528); the stratigraphic level of the late coins is left as a condition ("unless these coins (Cat. Nos. 344, 383 and 384) derived from a stratigraphic level…"). Cat. 344 (114/113) is named there where Cat. 346 is clearly meant, since the next paragraph lists "344–346, 383 and 384". Magen p.[31], [39], [42], [45] (PDF 43, 51, 54, 57). The extraction note `registration/magen2021_gerizim_coins_extracted.md` §0 records that the volume has no locus register.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Web: "Antiochus IX winged thunderbolt bronze Antioch S.E. 203 110/109 Gerizim destruction coin date reading" found coin-dealer and type pages only (numisview SC 2364, wildwinds). B4 already ran critiques of the date debate (Barag, Shatzman, as summarised by Bijovsky p.[103]) with no hit. Project: no note cites Cat. 346, 383, 384 or L5079 (grep). Bijovsky herself publishes the conditional, so the tag is **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED**. The count of the three coins, the hedges lost in Magen's chapter, and the "411 up to 110" having no supporting entry are not found anywhere.
Why it might matter: The coin dates allow a destruction any time after 113/112, which Barag and Shatzman (both before 2021) had already argued. The precise "110/109" is one corroded coin. The terminus is therefore 113/112 secure, with 110/109 resting on one reading.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Get the IAA records and photos of K 35244 (Cat. 346), K 34834 (Cat. 384) and K 32147 (Cat. 383). Get the locus descriptions for P L5079, L5072 and L4494 from the forthcoming architecture volumes (not in the corpus). The reading of SNG Israel 1 No. 2697 (cited for Cat. 346) would show whether S.E. 203 is attested for this type elsewhere. Kill: a clear S.E. 203 on another specimen of the type, plus a sealed destruction context for L5079.
Confidence: high for the printed facts; medium that the precision is overstated.
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe: it sets the date of the end of the Gerizim sanctuary (entry 57).

FIND CARD
Title (one line): In JSP 19 the ruler totals, and Magen's text counts, add the hoard coins to the isolated coins for some rulers but not for others. Several of the totals do not add up.
Lane: B4b
What is odd (2–4 sentences): For Antiochus III, Antiochus IV, Antiochus VII, Cleopatra Thea & Antiochus VIII, and Antiochus VIII, the heading count equals the isolated coins plus that ruler's coins in the hoards: 3,267+1, 2,067+2, 242+25+1, 39+4 and 236+3+1. Magen's text uses these headings: 43 (p.[37]), 240 (p.[37], [45]), 268 (p.[37]). Other rulers break the rule. Demetrius II first reign: 37 against 36 isolated plus 2 in the bronze hoard. Demetrius II second reign: 190 = 87 + 103 silver, with bronze-hoard Cat. 707 left out. Zabinas: 455 = 412 + 43, but the bronze hoard has 46. Further slips: Magen p.[45] dates "455 of Alexander II Zabinas … from 125–122 BCE", while the catalogue's own Zabinas types are dated 129/128 (Cat. 308, 81 coins), 128/127, 127/126 and ca. 126/125. For the bronze hoard, Bijovsky p.[114] says "the latest four coins … struck … by Antiochus VIII … bear the same date, 121/120", while the catalogue gives Cat. 760 as "date … illegible" and Cat. 761 as "Seleucid uncertain" (Fig. 11 counts it "Illegible"). The hoards themselves check exactly: bronze 61 = Fig. 11, and silver 129 = Figs 12–14 year by year.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Ruler headings and Total-by-type cells: printed [145]–[156] (PDF 157–168, page images read; strips in scratch). Hoards: printed [181]–[187] (PDF 193–199). Bijovsky Figs 11–14, p.[113]–[116] (PDF 125–128). Magen p.[37] "227 coins … 268", p.[45] (PDF 49, 57). The full table is in `jsp19_catalogue_counts.csv`. Checked as consistent: John Hyrcanus I 68 (Cat. 391, one type), Aristobulus I 3, Jannaeus 528 = 38 + 490 (Cat. 409), Akko 1,217 = 641 undated + 572 dated + 4, Demetrius I 152.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): B4 and R6 web searches (no review of JSP 19) plus the two searches above. Project: `registration/magen2021_gerizim_coins_extracted.md` covers printed 157–171 and does not sum the type totals. Tag: **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** (small).
Why it might matter: Every 2008→2021 comparison in B4-1 used these headings. They are database totals that mix hoard and isolated coins. So "Antiochus VIII 240" includes the hoard coins, and "Cleopatra 43" includes 4 coins from the bronze hoard, all dated 122/121. The isolated-coin baseline is smaller: 39 and 236. Anyone using the volume for circulation statistics needs the isolated figures (CSV column E).
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): The digital appendix (coin by coin, cited at printed 82) would settle which coins the headings include. It is not in the corpus. A single-coin misattribution in the database (e.g. Cat. 707 entered as "uncertain") would explain the off-by-one cases and kill the "inconsistent" part.
Confidence: high for the sums (read from page images); medium for the explanation.
Touches the Copper Scroll? no.

## Status of B4-1 after the catalogue

B4-1 **survives, sharpened**. (1) The JSP 8 basis cannot be reproduced. The 2021 catalogue has 43 joint Cleopatra–Antiochus VIII coins (39 isolated + 4 from the bronze hoard), all dated 123/122–122/121; JSP 8's "121 … 125–110" matches nothing. (2) R6's correction 2 ("the terminus also rests on 411 Akko coins up to 110") is answered by the catalogue. No Akko coin is dated later than 112/111(?), and the two latest (Cat. 383, 384) are queried readings. "110" is the range given to illegible coins. B4's original wording, "anchored on single coins", is correct for the precise 110/109: that rests on Cat. 346 alone, and Bijovsky calls its reading conditional. The secure terminus is 113/112. (3) The silver hoard's move from 125 (2008) to 120/119 (2021) rests on one coin, Cat. 890, as B4 said. The catalogue now adds that the hoard's basket also held a bronze catalogued elsewhere (card 1). (4) John Hyrcanus I 68 is internally consistent in 2021; the fall from 76 (2008) is unexplained but not an error inside JSP 19. (5) The conflict over the Jannaeus coins stands as B4 stated it. Magen p.[59] calls them "the latest coins … related to the destruction". Bijovsky p.[105] says "most coins reached the site after it was destroyed", and p.[104] puts 93% of them (Cat. 409, 490 coins) possibly after 76 BCE. Magen p.[39] and p.[45] also speak of a Hasmonean garrison, which contradicts his own "abandoned for around 400 years" (p.[59]). R6 correction 1 (the Akko colon sentence) is accepted.

## Dead ends
- Reading the date of Cat. 890 from Plate 58: the embedded image is 173×165 px and the date letters are a few pixels high. A reading of "ΓΠ" (183) rather than year 193 was considered and dropped. The plate also shows no Tyrian club in the left field, which fits SC 2337 (Akko, eagle on thunderbolt; see silver.kbr.be SC 2337 page).
- Reading the date of Cat. 346 from Fig. 6 (PDF 112): too corroded at the published resolution.
- Per-year check of Magen's "411 Akko after 126": not possible, because type totals are not broken down by year (Cat. 356 = 572 coins for the whole dated series).
- Stratigraphy of the late coins (P L5079, L5072, L4494) and of L4749: JSP 19 has no locus register; JSP 8's P-II plan (Fig. 118) shows loci 4831–4886 only. Searching JSP 8 for "5079" and "4749" gave nothing usable.
- JSP 8 "79 coins after Antiochus VII" in the silver hoard: no 2021 subgroup equals 79 (DII by year: 10/13/27/35/18). Left unexplained.
