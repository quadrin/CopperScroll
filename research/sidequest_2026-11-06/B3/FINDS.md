FIND CARD
Title (one line): The "Heliodorus stele" complex is SC 126, not SC 57. The final report says so, but CIIP IV.2 (3504, 3511, 3733), IIP and Wikipedia still give SC 57.
Lane: B3
What is odd (2–4 sentences): CIIP IV.2 gives "Subterranean Complex 57" as the findspot of three inscriptions: the Seleucus IV ordinance (the "Heliodorus stele", 3511, fragments c–e "in Room 1"), the inscribed altar naming Sohemus and Ilaeus (3504, "SC 57, Room 1"), and the wall graffito of Abselamus (3733). *Maresha IV* states three times that the complex dug in 2000–2010 "was misidentified and published as SC 57" and is in fact SC 126. Real SC 57 is a different place: a small round columbarium that Macalister recorded in 1900, with its own plan (Plan 2.57). The excavators had already headed a chapter "Subterranean Complex 57 (126)" in 2014, four years before CIIP. Even so, CIIP IV.2, the BAR volume title, the IIP database (e.g. MARE0018B, which takes SC 57 from the BAR volume title) and the Wikipedia article on the stele all still say SC 57. Anyone who uses SC 57 to put the stele on the Maresha plan will land on the wrong cave.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates):
- *Maresha IV*, Preface, pdf p. 7 (printed vi): "SC 126 was mislabeled as SC 57, and SC 2 was mislabeled as SC 75".
- *Maresha IV*, Ch. 2, pdf p. 120 (printed 109), entry "Subterranean Complex 57", Plan 2.57: Macalister's "small columbarium"; "SC 126 was misidentified and published as SC 57 during its excavation from 2000 to 2010".
- *Maresha IV*, Ch. 2, pdf p. 195 (printed 184), entry "Subterranean Complex 126", Plan 2.126 (map reference given there): "incorrectly published as SC 57". Room 1 is where "Three pieces of the 'Heliodorus stele' were discovered". Room 2 has the Abselamus graffito on the southern wall ("Gera 2018:1196; No. 3733").
- *Maresha IV*, Ch. 6, no. 9, pdf p. 333 (printed 322), and Table 6.1, pdf p. 344: "SC 57/126" and "57 (126)". References, pdf p. 372: "Stern I. and Alpert B. 2014b. Subterranean Complex 57 (126): The Site", in Stern ed. 2014, BAR S2652, pp. 5–8.
- CIIP IV.2 3504: pdf p. 203 (printed 949), "Findspot: Subterranean Complex 57, Room 1", exc. reg. no. 57-41-305-S2. CIIP IV.2 3511: pdf pp. 210–211 (printed 956–957), "excavated in Room 1 of Subterranean Complex 57". CIIP IV.2 3733: pdf p. 450 (printed 1196), "Findspot: Subterranean Complex 57".
- Web: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliodorus_stele ("Room 1 of subterranean complex no. 57"; no mention of 126). https://search.inscriptionsisraelpalestine.org/inscriptions/mare0018b (SC 57 from the BAR volume title).
- Checked for knock-on effects: the preface's second relabelling (SC 2 / SC 75) leaves CIIP unaffected. *Maresha IV* p. 137 says the label SC 75 was kept, and CIIP 3505 cites SC 75 only as a possible findspot.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Web searches for `"Heliodorus" stele Maresha "Subterranean Complex 126" OR "SC 126"` and `Maresha "Subterranean Complex 57" misidentified renumbered 126 Stern 2025` found nothing outside *Maresha IV* that flags the renumbering or how it affects the CIIP lemmata. Wikipedia and IIP still use SC 57. Project search (main plus 1,876 branch-only files) for "SC 126", "Complex 126" and "Heliodorus": the only hit is the CIIP reading note on `research/full-coverage-2026-10-14` (`atlas/research/sources/new_batch_2026-10-13/full_coverage_caves.md` l.133–134), which repeats "SC57 Room1". Tag: **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED**. The renumbering appears in the 2014 chapter title and in *Maresha IV*. The evidence here adds that CIIP 3504, 3511 and 3733, IIP and Wikipedia were never corrected. It is NEW TO THE PROJECT.
Why it might matter: The stele is one of the best-known Seleucid documents from Judaea/Idumaea (178 BCE; the Heliodorus of 2 Maccabees 3). Its findspot is used to argue that the looted fragments (a)+(b) came from the same room (CIIP 3511 comm.). A concordance key SC 57 → SC 126 (with Room 1 and Room 2) puts the stele, the altar and the graffito in the right cave on Plan 2.126. It also stops readers from confusing them with Macalister's columbarium SC 57.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Data in hand: compare Plan 2.57 with Plan 2.126 in *Maresha IV* (`hexview.py pdf-page Stern-2025-... 120` and `... 195`). Also compare the BAR S2652 site plan (not in the corpus) with Plan 2.126: same four rooms, Room 1 with the southern burrow towards SC 169. If the BAR plan instead matches Plan 2.57, the "misidentification" claim fails. Missing: BAR S2652 (Stern ed. 2014) is not in the corpus.
Confidence: high (that the published sources disagree); medium (that SC 126 is the right number, which rests on *Maresha IV* alone).
Touches the Copper Scroll? no (Idumaean Shephelah; it is a lesson in how a complex-numbering error spreads through corpora).

Title (one line): *Maresha IV* ch. 6 fixes the place of CIIP IV.2 3738, which CIIP leaves unplaced (SC 89, Room 40, the olive-press wall), and changes two CIIP readings
Lane: B3
What is odd (2–4 sentences): CIIP IV.2 3738 ("μνεία τοῦ" graffiti) has the findspot "Subterranean complex in Maresha; the inscriptions are known only from the photographs". *Maresha IV* no. 12 publishes the same two graffiti, explicitly "as in CIIP IV.2 3738.1". It places them on the wall of the olive press in SC 89, Room 40, "above the service area between the pressing stations", and reads (b) as Δημητρίου instead of CIIP's Μ++ΙΑΤΟΥ = μνεία τοῦ. *Maresha IV* also places CIIP 3734 (Hermes) in SC 89, Room 17, where CIIP gives only "SC 89". It completes CIIP's ἐπὶ ὠφελί++ | +++ as ἐπὶ ὠφελίας | σύ. It cites 3734 as by "Werner Eck and Dirk Kosmann", but CIIP signs it WA/DK (Ameling/Kossmann). It also adds SC 30 to CIIP 3732 (Sime the beauty), which CIIP places only as "es-Suk, ca. 525 m W of the tell".
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): CIIP IV.2 3738: pdf pp. 454–456 (printed 1200–1202), figs. 3738.1–3. *Maresha IV* ch. 6 no. 12: pdf pp. 334–336 (printed 323–325), Figs. 6.10:1–2. CIIP 3734: pdf pp. 450–451 (printed 1196–1197), signed "WA/DK". *Maresha IV* no. 13: pdf pp. 336–337 (printed 325–326), Fig. 6.11 ("published by Werner Eck and Dirk Kosmann ( CIIP IV.2 3734)"). CIIP 3732: pdf pp. 448–449. *Maresha IV* no. 2: pdf p. 327. Full row-by-row join: `out/B3/ciip_iv2_x_maresha_iv_concordance.csv`. The join is complete for wall inscriptions: every CIIP Marisa wall text from a subterranean complex (3510, 3732, 3733, 3734, 3737, 3738) has a *Maresha IV* ch. 6 number. FIND CARD
Title (one line): One lead weight, two dates: CIIP IV.2 3679 (Agathocles, year 205 = 108/7 BC) is *Maresha IV*'s SC 97 weight (year 200 = 113/112 BC, "the last series"), but CIIP 3675 has a later year-201 weight
Lane: B3
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The fragmentary lead weight of the agoranomos Agathocles, exc. reg. 97-01-101-M1 from SC 97, appears in CIIP IV.2 as 3679, dated "Year 205 (=108/7 BC)". The same object appears in *Maresha IV* (ch. 3, Amphora Stamps and Scale Weight no. 3, reg. "9/98 01.101 1. M1"). There it is called "a lead weight of the last series used in Maresha", a series "recently redated to the year 200 SE, i.e. 113/112 BCE" (Finkielsztejn, INR 17, 2022, Note C). *Maresha IV* reads its deliberate cutting as damnatio memoriae after John Hyrcanus' conquest. But CIIP IV.2 3675, a weight of the agoranomos Sosibius from SC 169, is dated "Year 201 (=112/1 BC)". If Agathocles is year 200, then Sosibius, not Agathocles, is the latest dated series, unless Sosibius' date has also been re-read. CIIP's own commentary on 3676 gives the physical basis for the change: on every Agathocles mould the E (5) was cut "larger and thicker … than the Σ (200)", so the date was altered.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): CIIP IV.2 3675: pdf pp. 378–379 (printed 1124–1125), LΑΣ = year 201, exc. reg. 169-02-16-M1. CIIP 3676 commentary on the recut E: pdf pp. 379–380 (printed 1125–1126). CIIP 3679: pdf p. 382 (printed 1128), wt 83.7 g, "SC 97, courtyard east of Room 3", exc. reg. 97-01-101-M1. *Maresha IV*: pdf p. 259 (printed 248), Fig. 3.14:3, with dating sources on pdf p. 260. Finkielsztejn 2022, INR 17: 79–94 (abstract: https://lockwoodonlinejournals.com/index.php/inr/article/view/2838; full text not read). Registration numbers joined: CIIP 97-01-101 (M1) = *Maresha IV* 97–1–101 (the same locus/basket also gives a figurine, *Maresha IV* pdf p. 255).
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Web search `Finkielsztejn 2022 Maresha lead weights Agathocles Sosibius agoranomos year 200 redating 113/112 BCE` returned INR 17 (2022), "Four Notes …", whose Note C gives "a new reading of the date on the last series of weights from Maresha". The abstract does not mention Sosibius. Project: Agathocles appears only in the CIIP reading note (`research/full-coverage-2026-10-14` branch, `full_coverage_caves.md` l.142), which takes 108/7 BCE. Nothing in the project joins it to *Maresha IV*. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT**. The redating is published; the tension with CIIP 3675 may already be dealt with in the INR article, which I could not read.
Why it might matter: This weight series is one of the main supports for dating Hyrcanus' conquest of Maresha (112/111 vs after 108/7 BCE). The same object carries a 5-year difference between the two publications. Whether Agathocles or Sosibius was the "last series" changes which weight dates the end of Seleucid civic administration there.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Missing: the full text of Finkielsztejn, INR 17 (2022) Note C. If it re-reads Sosibius' LΑΣ, or puts Sosibius before Agathocles, the tension disappears. Data in hand: CIIP figs. 3675.1–2 and 3676–3679 (`hexview.py pdf-page Ameling-... 378` to `383`) let anyone check the ΣΕ / ΑΣ numerals by eye.
Confidence: medium (that the two publications differ: certain; that the conflict with 3675 is real: low–medium until INR 17 is read)
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe (Hasmonean-era chronology only; no link to any scroll entry)
