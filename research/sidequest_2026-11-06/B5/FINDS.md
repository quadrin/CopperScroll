FIND CARD
Title (one line): A college minute of 6 Sept 1955 says Wright Baker had already tested his method "on an actual piece of the scroll" before the first cut, and that the scrolls themselves had been "fully examined in the U.S.A."
Lane: B5
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The published accounts describe the first cut, about 1 Oct 1955, as an untested gamble. Baker cut alone that evening "in case the worst had happened" (Allegro 1964 pp. 7–8; repeated in Shanks 2007 p. 18). They also say the American work was done on *replicas* (de Vaux 1953 p. 557, "réplique oxydée"; Allegro 1964 p. 3, "replicas of the rolls oxidized artificially"; Shanks 2007 p. 13). Baker's own minute to the Registrar says something different. At a meeting with Bowden, Harding, Prof. Fish and Allegro, Baker "was able to demonstrate them [his methods and treatments] on an actual piece of the scroll, to the complete satisfaction of everyone", and the scrolls "have been fully examined in the U.S.A. from a metallurgical point of view". Either a real fragment of 3Q15 was used for a cutting trial in Manchester, and possibly a sample went to Baltimore, or the minute is loosely worded. No published account I read mentions either.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Drive `Manchester_TPA-1-132_Selected_Cutting_Correspondence_1955-1956.pdf` (1QYCHkEFl7bzCdXFGrgkE7ZRG8Rk97TTv), PDF p. 2, minute no. 13048/55 signed "H. WRIGHT BAKER 6/9/55", stamped FILED 10 OCT 1955, paragraphs 1–2. Compare Allegro 1964 pp. 3, 7–8 (Drive 1JTa1JlpTCO0j3mKRBAuQg1iH7FEu3lR2); de Vaux RB 60 p. 557 (repo `research/history/devaux1953_cave_list_extracted.md` l.115); Johnson 2002 p. 39 (Johns Hopkins "studied the problem"); Shanks 2007 pp. 13, 18.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Text search of Brooke & Davies 2002 (Johnson, Davies, Lundberg & Zuckerman), Shanks 2007, Allegro 1964, and the repo extractions of Allegro 1959/1960/1966 for "trial", "test", "experiment", "piece of the scroll", "demonstrat", "U.S.A.", "America", "Corwin", "Baltimore". The only hits are the replica experiments and Baker's lone first cut. Puech 2006 pp. 171–173 says nothing of a trial. Repo `git grep` on main: TPA/1/132 appears only as an archive request (atlas-workbench.json), and its contents have never been used. Baker's own reports (BJRL 39, 1956, pp. 45–56; DJD III pp. 203–210) were not read and may mention a trial fragment. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT** (could be NEW TO THE LITERATURE if Baker's two reports are silent).
Why it might matter: If a detached piece of 3Q15 was sawn or coated in a trial in summer 1955, there is a fragment whose place in the scroll and whose present location are unrecorded. Its loss or damage would be one cause of a lacuna that nobody has explained. Segment 1, the uninscribed "page de garde", is one candidate for the trial piece. A sample sent to Johns Hopkins would be a further unrecorded fragment.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Read Baker's BJRL 1956 pp. 45–47 and DJD III pp. 203–207, looking for "trial", "fragment" or "test piece". Look in the Allegro photo collection (Manchester; WSRP microfiche) for any photograph of a loose fragment before Oct 1955. The EDF condition reports (Puech 2006 pp. 67–90) would show whether segment 1 or any loose piece carries Araldite or saw marks older than the main cutting. The idea is killed if Baker's report says the trial was on a replica or on scrap copper.
Confidence: medium that the minute says this (the text is clear); low on what it means.
Touches the Copper Scroll? yes: it concerns the physical history of 3Q15 (a possible lost or treated fragment).

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The Manchester letters contradict the published chronology and witness list for the opening of the first roll (Oct 1955 – Jan 1956).
Lane: B5
What is odd (2–4 sentences): (1) On 19 Oct 1955 Baker wrote that the first roll was "completely opened and rough cleaned in my spare time on 4 days". Allegro 1964 p. 19 says "In little more than a week the roll was open", and Shanks 2007 p. 19 repeats it. Allegro's own letters, as Davies cites them, fit Baker: first cut by 1 Oct, roll done by 5 Oct. (2) Davies 2002 p. 29 says the roll was finished "to the satisfaction of all, including the Jordanian embassy official who had been delegated to keep an eye" on it. Baker's letter of 19 Oct says "Mr. Homoud was not able to visit us as expected", and Harding's letter of 12 Oct mentions "some slight misunderstanding with the Embassy in London". Harding's 12 Oct conditions also limit access to Allegro "and anyone he brings with him". That is the background to Dajani's unannounced visit of 15 Nov 1955 at the Ambassador's request (Davies p. 30). (3) Allegro 1960 (Foreword p. 6) has Dajani attending the cutting "in 1956". The only documented Dajani visit is 15 Nov 1955, after the first roll was finished. (4) The second roll arrived on "2 January" (Allegro 1964 p. 20) or "3 January 1956" (Davies p. 31).
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): TPA/1/132 PDF p. 3 (Harding to Bowden, 12 Oct 1955, conditions 1–4 and the Embassy sentence) and PDF p. 4 (Baker to Harding, 19 Oct 1955, ref. 15879/55, paras 1–2). Davies 2002 pp. 28–31 with nn. 4–6 (letters 1.10.55, 5.10.55, 19.10.55). Allegro 1964 pp. 19–20. Allegro 1960 Foreword p. 6 (repo `research/history/allegro1960_treasure_extracted.md` l.224). Shanks 2007 p. 19. All rows are in `timeline.csv`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Davies 2002 used Allegro's private letters (Joan Allegro), not the College file. Puech 2006 p. 171 gives only "entre juillet 1955 et janvier 1956". Lefkovits 2000 p. 5 says "By mid-January 1956". Searches for "Homoud" and "embassy" in the corpus texts and the repo returned nothing apart from Davies' unnamed "embassy official". Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT** for the contradictions. Each source on its own is published or archived.
Why it might matter: Who was in the room matters for questions about the custody of the scroll. Examples are who handled loose fragments (see the first card), who held photographs, and why the College file, according to the 12 Oct reply from the Manchester archivist, has no cutting photographs. The "4 days" figure is also the best measure of how fast the small roll was sawn, which bears on how much care was possible at the saw lines.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): The full TPA/1/132 file goes beyond the 6 selected pages. It, or the Allegro letters in the Manchester Museum, would show whether Homoud is Davies' "embassy official" and whether he ever came. If Homoud did visit before 5 Oct, point (2) is killed. Dajani's attendance in January 1956 would be shown by any photograph from the second-roll cutting with him in it. Allegro 1964 Plate 7 shows Dajani with Kuhn, but no place or date is given.
Confidence: medium.
Touches the Copper Scroll? yes: it concerns the custody and opening history of 3Q15.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): Kuhn dates his own study of the unopened rolls to September–October 1953, not to "the spring of 1953" as Lefkovits 2000 and Shanks 2007 say; his four columns are cols VII–X of the opened scroll.
Lane: B5
What is odd (2–4 sentences): Kuhn writes that he studied the rolls in the museum showcase "en septembre-octobre 1953". He saw A1 and B1 on the originals and A2 and B2 only on Harding's photographs. He says nothing of mirrors. Lefkovits 2000 p. 4 has "In the spring of 1953 ... with help of mirrors and photographs", and Shanks 2007 p. 12 repeats both points nearly word for word. Kuhn's other dates fit the autumn: de Vaux's letter to him of 1 Dec 1953 (p. 201 n. 1), and Allegro's "I was working in the museum at the time" (1964 p. 15). Allegro joined the team only late in 1953 (Johnson 2002 p. 39: December 1953). Checked line by line against the opened text, Kuhn's A1, A2, B2 and B1 are cols VII, VIII, IX and X. His line counts were 16/16/17–18/16; the opened scroll has 16/16/17/17. Two of his readings come from what was hidden. His "isolated title" זהב at B1 11 is the first word of X 11, with the rest of the line covered by the next turn. His B2 numbering runs one line too high after line 9, so his lines 13, 14 and 18 are lines 12, 13 and 17 of the opened scroll.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): Kuhn RB 61 (1954) pp. 193 (date), 194–195 (method, A/B, photographs), 195–196 (line counts, columns 6–9 of 10–12), 199 (transcription), 201 n. 1 (de Vaux letter 1 Dec 1953), Drive 1Q4urT-ZFgOLEck3ty_ZDx8b62rClVuN-. Lefkovits 2000 printed p. 4 (bucket doc `Lefkovits-2000-...-db4f91b2` PDF p. 25). Shanks 2007 p. 12. Concordance: `out/B5/kuhn1954_vs_opened_scroll.csv`, using the project's line text `research/agent_review_2026-10-07/wave1/T08_real_or_legend/data/scroll_lines_with_numerals.txt`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Lefkovits 2000 p. 5 n. 10 cites a "detailed discussion of Kuhn's readings" in his 1993 dissertation (vol. 2 pp. 1190–1204, unread), and his "90 percent correct" figure implies he made a mapping. Puech 2006 uses Kuhn's transcription twice: p. 216 n. 268, a letter lost in the cutting, and p. 224 n. 412. Puech p. 171 n. 2 also rejects Kuhn's deliberate separation of the two rolls. Two web searches turned up nothing about Kuhn's dates (the BAS Library article is behind a paywall). Kuhn's THLZ 79 (1954) résumé, which may be the source of "spring" and "mirrors", was not read. Tag: **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED** for the column mapping. **NEW TO THE PROJECT** for the date conflict and how it spread (Lefkovits → Shanks).
Why it might matter: Kuhn is the only witness to the outer turns of both rolls before the saw went in. His readings at B2 18 / IX 17 (b-ṣ-d … '-b-n against the edition's מצד נאב) and at the other outer-face lines are evidence of the letters before cutting. Puech has used this for one letter. A full pass on the eight saw lines that cross cols VII–X has not been done. It is a physical test only, not a new reading.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Read Kuhn's THLZ 79 (1954) 303–304 and THLZ 81 (1956) 541–546. If either dates a visit to spring 1953 or mentions mirrors, the conflict becomes two visits, not an error. Read Lefkovits's dissertation vol. 2 pp. 1190–1204 to see whether his mapping matches the CSV. Comparing the line numbers with Lefkovits's cut list (2000 pp. 446–447: cuts 13–17 cross cols VII–X) would show which of Kuhn's line ends lie on saw lines.
Confidence: high for the date conflict; high for the VII–X mapping; medium for the two artefact explanations.
Touches the Copper Scroll? yes: it concerns the physical state of the outer turns before cutting.

## Smaller contradictions logged in timeline.csv (not carded)
- Find date of the rolls. Allegro 1960 p. 19 and Johnson 2002 p. 39 give 14 March 1952. DJD III p. 201, Puech 2006 p. 171 and Allegro 1964 p. 2 give 20 March. 14–25 March is the span of the work in cave 8 (DJD III p. 7). Puech already notes Johnson's error (p. 171 n. 1).
- Allegro present at both cuttings ("the only Dead Sea Scroll scholar present": Lefkovits 2000 p. 5), or kept away from the second roll (Davies 2002 p. 31; Puech p. 171 n. 6)?
- Claims about loss to the saw. The College press statement (TPA PDF p. 1) says "no single character ... rendered unrecognisable", and Baker in the NYT, via Shanks p. 33, said "not a letter" was lost. Against this: Baker 1956 says 2% doubtful (Lefkovits p. 447), and Puech n. 268 shows a letter lost in the cutting from Kuhn's transcription. Already criticised by Lefkovits.
- How the band became two rolls. De Vaux 1953 p. 558 says it was cut in two. Kuhn 1954 p. 194 says it was separated on purpose before rolling. Allegro 1959, Johnson 2002 and Puech 2006 say the rivets failed while it was being rolled. Kuhn's own evidence is that the rivets stayed on A's outer end, the holes are about 2 cm from B's start, and A was rolled from col I while B was rolled from col XII. So the broken joint lies on the *outside* of both rolls, and the two rolls were rolled from opposite ends of the band toward the joint.
- Strips. Baker (6 Mar 1956) says each strip ran "around about half the circumference". The published count is 23 segments (cuts 1–14 on the large roll, 15 onward on the small one: Lefkovits pp. 446–447). The letters give no count, so there is nothing to contradict.
- The assistant. Baker's minute names a "girl assistant", his niece with a B.A. from Bristol, and a margin note reads "Rosalind Baker". The 1 June 1956 release thanks "his assistant" (Shanks p. 35). Rosalind Baker as cleaner is already published (Lundberg & Zuckerman 2002 p. 48). The pay figures (about £7/week; £7 4s guaranteed by Harding) appear only in the letters.
