Lane: A1b
What is odd (2–4 sentences): A1 card 1 and R1 treated the labels as published only in EDF's text, with "A5 à A4" and "A8 à A12 ?" unresolved and DJD III unread. EDF's revers plates draw every label. Segment 13 (Pl. CXXXVI) has B12, A1, A2, A3, A4; segment 14 (Pl. CXXXVIII) has A5, A6, A7; segment 15 (Pl. CXLI) has A8, A9, A10, A11 and a fifth "A" whose number is illegible. So the text on PDF p. 73 contradicts the book's own plate, and the "?" on p. 74 is the illegible last label. On segment 15, A8's number is not painted: it is the blue "étiquette papier" bearing "8" (the text, p. 74, mentions "une étiquette de papier, portant le chiffre huit" but does not say what it is). This matches Wright Baker, DJD III p. 207: after the Araldite, "the angular positions were marked upon the edge of the scroll in white paint, along with a letter corresponding to the convolution", and paper strips fixed with cellotape carried "the index letters and numbers". The numbers come from the 5-in. guard disk "bearing numbered graduations" (p. 205). The Feb 1994 verso photos agree at book resolution. Pl. XLI (seg 12) shows B8, B9, B10. Pl. XLII (seg 13) shows A1, A2. Pl. XLIII (seg 14) shows A6 at the left and a second "A" with tick near the right edge. Pl. XLIV (seg 15) shows A and probably 10. B12 → A1 inside segment 13 confirms 12 graduations per turn. Labels increase left to right on the verso, so right to left on the recto.

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The "parallel engraved lines" that EDF dates to the scroll's period of use on segments 13–14 are not ruling. In the 1988 frames they are fine straight scratches about 7–13° oblique to the text, crossing letter relief without deflection, which is evidence against the ancient date.
Lane: A1b
What is odd (2–4 sentences): EDF text (PDF pp. 73, 78, 88) reports "lignes parallèles gravées datant de l'époque fonctionnelle" on the avers of segments 13 and 14 (and segment 18). The plate legend is more cautious: "Traces d'utilisation (?) de la période fonctionnelle". Wright Baker (DJD III p. 209) says the lines "have not been carefully laid out" and mentions no ruling. In CT13_RLT the lines are fine straight light scratches rising to the right at about 7–13°, and they run unbroken over the hills and valleys of punched letters. A line scratched *before* punching would be deformed by the punch, so these postdate the inscription, and they look fresh against the corrosion. A plausible modern source is the 1955 tools: broken slitting-saw pieces used "as tiny hand saws" (DJD III p. 207) would leave parallel straight striations.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `usc:DSS_3Q15_CT13_RLT_T` scratches from about (1130, 3157) to (1663, 3037), from (1090, 3447) to (1383, 3380), and from (1357, 3660) to (1690, 3620). Full-res window x 1100–1700 / y 2800–3400 shows 3–4 parallel lines crossing letters. EDF Pl. CXXXVI (p. 428, seg 13 avers: about 7 oblique lines in the lower-middle, fractions 0.48–0.79) and Pl. CXXXVIII (p. 430, seg 14 avers: 5–7 lines reaching the right edge). CT14_RLT window x 1700–2700 / y 3350–4350 at 0.6 scale was inconclusive.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Repo/branches grep "lignes parallèles", "Traces d'utilisation", "ruling" (Copper Scroll context): no hit. Web "Copper Scroll 3Q15 segment 13 14 parallel incised lines ruling OR scratches Brizemeure Lacoudre": nothing. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT** (the lines are published in EDF). The challenge to their dating is **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** as far as checked.
Why it might matter: Ruling lines on the scroll would be a manufacturing find. These are not ruling, and they may not be ancient at all. No study should treat them as evidence of layout or use.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): The recto was rolled inward and inaccessible before the 1955 sawing, and inner faces were cleaned only after each segment was removed (DJD III pp. 227–228). So if a line runs continuously across the 13|14 saw cut (CT13 left edge against CT14 right edge, matched heights), it predates the opening and EDF's date may stand. If the lines stop at the cut, they are 1955 or later. Data are in hand: register CT13_RLT and CT14_RRT edges (not done; budget).
Confidence: medium (oblique, not ruling); low (post-inscription and modern origin)
Touches the Copper Scroll? yes (surface marks on segments 13–14, cols VII–VIII)

## Correction to A1 card 2 (RE frames)
The catalogue titles say RET/REB are "Recto edge top/bot light", a fourth light position. In both CT15 RE frames the strip is turned (projected width about 1340 px against 1700 in RLT), and the raised, Araldite-glossy edge they show is cut 15's **right** edge (the 14|15 side), not the riveted left edge. The left edge looks the same as in RLT. For cut 16 the raised edge is the rivet edge. So the RE views do not single out the join on cut 15. The USC catalogue already notes the raised right edge in CT15 REB. A1's "RE because of the rivet join" inference is weakened.
