## B2, card 1: WBADB grid errors (Patrich 1994a site 95; Bar-Adon 1972 site 20) carried into `water.csv`, one cistern counted twice

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** Tag kept: NEW TO THE LITERATURE. Confidence: high. Cost to confirm: cheap (data in hand).

**Evidence checked**
- WBADB `wbadb_sites.csv`, row WBS:4146 ("Patrich 1994a, Site 95"). It gives easting 188140 with code `18-12/13/104`. The code puts the site in the 1-km square E 181 / N 123.
- IAA online, `asi_jerusalem/sites.csv` row 392 (record 12783). It reads "Poorly preserved complex (the 'Great Laura', Complex 25)", at ITM 231389, which is OIG E ≈ 181.4. The code agrees with the IAA point, so WBADB is about 6.75 km too far east.
- Note: the site is the Great Laura (Mar Saba), not simply "a cistern". `water.csv` types it as a cistern.
- `water.csv` lines 881 (`ASI-109/7/95-cistern`) and 1780 (`WBADB-S4146-cistern`). I read both with csv.DictReader: `dup_of`, `superseded_by` and `group_id` are all empty. The same holds for `ASI-109/7/52-*` and `WBADB-S4050-*`. The site-52 offset is about 594 m (0.00627° of longitude).
- Bar-Adon 20, ASI 1 p. 107 (store page 55, IMG_1728), read on the image. Entry "20. 19-15/94/1 … Kh. Umm esh-Shughut" prints a single number, "1542". The neighbouring entries print two numbers each (19: "1923 1565"; 21: "1948 1531"), and their codes match their grids. So WBADB's easting of 190000 is a fill. The code points to E 199.x.
- Small count difference: the card says "54 other Patrich rows linked". I count 73 of 81 WBADB Patrich rows in `water.csv` with `dup_of` or `superseded_by` set. The card probably counted sites, not rows. This does not change the finding.

**Literature check**
- Web: "West Bank and East Jerusalem archaeological database Greenberg Keinan coordinate errors corrections". It found only the TAU project page, the sourcebook and BL repository entries. No errata.
- Repo and `branch_only`: "Shughut" appears only in data tables (WBADB, `imagery_gaps` `site_points.json`, `ba72_headers.csv`) and in a page-block list. No note records the error.

**Alternative explanations tried**
- The site-20 code itself could be a misprint. Against this: the northing in the code (154) agrees with the printed 1542, and the sequence 19 → 21 runs E 192–195. That does not prove E 199. The size of the error (about 9 km) therefore rests on the code, but the point that 190000 is a placeholder holds either way.
- Datum or rounding for site 95: ruled out, because the code and the IAA record agree.

## B2, card 2: Bar-Adon's 1968 open-air sites 116–139 never re-surveyed (only the cave survey went back)

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** Tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT. Confidence: medium. Cost to confirm: moderate (a post-1972 open-air survey of the strip may exist outside the corpus).

**Evidence checked**
- `private/B2/survey_join_full_with_coords.csv`. Bar-Adon: 185 rows, 92 re-recorded.
  - Band 116–139: 42 rows. Of these, 38 are `not_rerecorded_*` (25 "outside later coverage"), 2 `rerecorded` (118 Cave of the Coin, 130 Rujm esh-Shajra) and 2 `possible`.
  - The card's "35 of 63 non-cave" is consistent once cave rows are removed.
  - The nearest later records in the band are all ʿAtiqot 41 cave rows (Cave XIV/12, XIV/17, "Caves in Naḥal Qane XIV/48–50", "Cave, Niches and Rock Shelters").
- Independent test: IAA online records (`asi_jerusalem/sites.csv`, 1,041 rows in the B2 box). Exactly one falls in E 180–191 / N 113–121, and it belongs to Map 109/7, at the edge. No IAA ASI map covers the band online.
- ASI 1 pp. 126–131 (store pages 65 and 67), read on the images. The entries are open-air structures: horseshoe and round structures, a row of paved circles about 350 m long, enclosures, the "fortress".

**Literature check**
- Web: "survey Dead Sea western shore Qumran to Ein Gedi open-air structures enclosures after Bar-Adon 1968 resurvey Hirschfeld Ras Feshkha Kidron Mazin". No later open-air survey of this strip found.
- Davidovich et al. 2014 (*Archaeometry* 56) abstract: "several dozen enclosures" in the Judean Desert. It names no sites, so it is not checked against these site numbers.

**Alternative explanations and residual risks**
- WBADB stops at about 2007, so later IAA work (the post-2017 cave and looting operations, sinkhole-zone salvage) is not in the join.
- Hirschfeld's shore work (*Qumran in Context*, 2004) was not opened.
- The 500 m rule is generous in places where Bar-Adon's grids are 100 m-rounded.

None of these kills the card. It remains a project-level gap statement.

## B2, card 3: Bar-Adon site 138 ("fortress" 30 × 30 m; robbed cemetery of nine tombs) unlinked to ʿEin el-Ghuweir

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** Tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT. Confidence: low on identity; high that the record exists and is unlinked. Cost to confirm: moderate (Bar-Adon 1977 BASOR fig. 1 or the cemetery plan).

**Evidence checked**
- ASI 1 p. 130, site 138 (store page 67), read on the image: "מצודה בנויה אבנים גדולות, מידותיה 30×30 מ'. סמוך לה מבנה בעל צורת טרפז" ("a fortress built of large stones, 30 × 30 m; beside it a trapezoid-shaped building"). For B: "בית קברות בנוי בקפידה יחסית לקברות הבדואים … תשעה קברים שדודים שעומקם 1.5 מ'" ("a cemetery built with more care than Bedouin graves … nine robbed tombs 1.5 m deep").
- The phrase "built with more care than Bedouin graves" is not in the card. It strengthens the reading that this is a non-Bedouin, possibly Qumran-type, cemetery.
- Code 18-11/95/1. A and B are about 280 m apart, matching the card's "about 300 m".
- Bar-Adon 1989 (ʿAtiqot Heb. 9, store `…31489edd`), page index 34, chapter 5 "עין אל-ע'ויר", read on the image. It gives the excavated building as "(נ"צ 1890/1147)", 8 × 11 m. This matches WBS:4521. 138B lies about 1.19 km NNE of this point, as the card says.
- The published "cemetery about 800 m north of the building" would fall in the same ASI 1-km square (…/95) as 138B, about 0.7 km from it. With 1968 grids and a vague "800 m north", **the same-cemetery reading is the likelier one**. Further alternatives:
  - The survey's count of nine graves against the roughly 20 excavated graves fits a pre-excavation surface count.
  - Hiam el-Sagha (Eshel & Greenhut 1993: 20 shaft graves) is the other known Qumran-type cemetery. I did not establish its position. Card 3 should list it as an alternative to check.

**Literature check**
- Web: "Hiam el-Sagha cemetery Eshel Greenhut location Ein el-Ghuweir distance north". It found the BAR endnote (20 graves, no location) and the cavac Ain el-Ghuweir summary (cemetery 800 m N; Blake 1964–66; no mention of a fortress or robbing).
- Repo: no note reads site 138, which agrees with the card.

## C1a, card 1: earthwork cluster under the Mishor Adummim industrial zone (HEXAGON June 1974, gone by May 1978)

**Verdict: WEAKENED.** Tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT, but the "no corpus record" part fails. Confidence: high that something visible in 1974 had been graded by 1978; low that it is archaeological. Cost to confirm: moderate.

**Evidence checked**
- Private crops `mishor_full_A.png` and `mish_74_78.png`. The ring, strips, bars and compound are clear on F014. The 1978 F007 frame shows grading, sheds and the first housing estate.
- `ring_raf_esri.png`: the dark parallel strips are visible on the 1940s layer. The ring cannot be resolved there.

**What fails**
- The card says "the only record I found for the area is the Euthymius monastery excavation reports". But WBADB lists 18 records within 1.5 km of the ring's estimated position (I computed this from the private file and give distances only).
- Almost all of them come from **Sion 1994**, a survey of the Mishor Adummim / Khan el-Ahmar area. Examples: WBS:3165 "Site." about 250–425 m away (affine and visual-tie positions); WBS:3152, WBS:3147 and WBS:3133 ("Three enclosures", Site J) at 0.5–0.9 km; Kh. Handoma (Site F) at about 1.2–1.4 km.
- So the ground was surveyed after 1978. The description of WBS:3165 is the bare "Site.", which may or may not be a remnant of the cluster. The lane never ran the WBADB proximity check.

**Alternative explanations**
- The ring has a closed, raised, irregular rim, 80–100 m across, beside tracks. It fits a bulldozed berm: a military laager, an earth reservoir or site preparation for the zone, which began in the mid-1970s.
- The strips go back to before 1948. Dark parallel plots about 8 × 40 m in a wadi-side setting fit cultivated plots or check-dam fields better than structures.
- The card itself already says that a CORONA 1967 check is the best kill.

**Literature check**
- WBADB proximity (above). Repo grep for "Sion 1994" gives WBADB rows only, with pages 225–228 cited. The full Sion 1994 title was not found in the corpus.

**What it needs:** read Sion 1994 for WBS:3165 / 3152 / 3147 / 3133, and do the CORONA 1967 check.

## C1c, card 2: June 1974 west-shore waterline on 30 transects; GLO-30 about 5 m above the expected 1974 lake level

**Verdict: SURVIVES.** Tags kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT (table) and KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED at most (DEM). Confidence: medium for the waterline table; low for the DEM-bias reading. Cost to confirm: moderate (needs an IHS gauge value for June 1974).

**Evidence checked**
- `shorelines.csv`. GLO-30 at the 1974 line runs from −381 to −396.8 off the cliff. Three cliff transects (N 3508.5–3509.5) read −315 to −351 and are correctly excluded.
- The spread is large: an IQR of 5 m and a single transect 17 m high (N 3502.0, −381.0). The values nearest the expected level are at ʿEin Feshkha (N 3510.5–3513, −394 to −396.8).

**Alternative explanations**
- (a) Landward picking bias on the plain (wet beach, closing filter). The card names it.
- (b) GLO-30 is a surface model (TanDEM-X 2011–15), so vegetation on the exposed floor raises it. But the vegetated Feshkha transects read *lower*, so this does not explain the bias.
- (c) A vertical-datum difference (EGM2008 against the Israeli datum) is well under 1 m. Not enough.
- (d) The "expected" −398 to −399 m is itself an inference from USGS OF 78-176, which gives "near −400 m in 1977". A web search found no printed 1974 value.

**Note:** this card is coupled to C1c card 3. If the picks are 100–150 m landward (explanation (a)), the DEM bias shrinks, and so does card 3's anomaly.

**Literature check:** web search "Dead Sea level 1974 −397 / −398 / −396 metres" found the USGS report, the EGU 2008 abstract and Britannica, with no 1974 tabulated value.
