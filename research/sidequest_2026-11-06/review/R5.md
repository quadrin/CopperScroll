## A4 card 1: the QC report describes another build (Vincent & Abel 1913 plates removed; PEF/SCHICK/154 not in the model)

- **Verdict: SURVIVES** (tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT). The wording of the 154 half should be softened (see notes).
- **Confidence:** high. **Cost:** cheap.
- **Evidence checked:**
  - **Repository record.** Repository API (`publicdata-bak.fdm.uni-wuppertal.de/server/api/core/items/28aad4c9-…`), bundle ORIGINAL:
    - GLB 517,017,896 B, MD5 `380113c8…` (matches the card);
    - `QualityControlReport.txt` 12,247 B;
    - C4D zip 276,979,487 B.
  - **QC report** (downloaded in full). It says "74 image files individually opened". Rows 68–69 are `VincentAbel1913_PlancheXII.png` and `…PlancheXIII.png`. It gives "Embedded buffer: 541,594,012 bytes", "72 PNG images", "Materials: 70", vertices 5,967,069, triangles 1,989,023 and 104 draw calls. All as the card says.
  - **GLB JSON chunk** (range-read `-r 0-140000`; chunk length 137,116 B). Buffer 516,880,752 B; 70 images; 68 materials; 100 primitives; summed POSITION count 5,966,985; triangles 1,988,995. No "Vincent" string, no "154" string. Generator string "MAXON CINEMA 4D Studio 13.061".
  - **Zip central directory** (range-read of the last 60 kB). 76 entries, 72 PNGs, `.c4d` = 8,441,485 B (QC: 8,497,631 B), no Vincent or 154 file.
  - **Abstract.** It names "the documents PEF/SCHICK/154 and PEF/SCHICK/203" as "the starting point".
  - The arithmetic is consistent: 74 − 72 = 2 PNGs, 72 − 70 = 2 embedded images and 70 − 68 = 2 materials. Two plates were removed.
- **Literature check:**
  - Repository: the discover search for "Siegel" finds only this item (handle 123456789/72), with no second version. The metadata has no `relation`, `provenance` or `version` fields.
  - Repo main and branch_only: grep for "Grabeskirche", "bkf2-pv55", "Gibson and Taylor" and "Siegel". The only hits are unrelated (Dalman figure captions; Siegelmann).
  - Web: `"PEF/SCHICK/154"` and "Conrad Schick PEF archive SCHICK 154 Holy Sepulchre". There is no publication on the model.
- **Alternative explanation (partly accepted):** the abstract calls 154 and 203 "documents … in which Schick recorded his findings". Open Jerusalem lists a Schick text, "Notes to the Plan and Sections of the ground of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre (Part II), [1898]" (https://www.openjerusalem.org/items/participate/18080). I could not confirm its PEF number. So 154 may be a written source that was never meant to be a textured plane. The fact holds: nothing in the GLB, the zip or the `.c4d` names 154. But "missing from the model" should read "not represented by any plane or texture". The Vincent & Abel removal is certain.

## A4 card 2: Schick frame at 8.2°; Corbo and G&T plans 1.3–2.0° off; datum 2,420 ft (737.616 m) and Corbo ±0.00 at 753.53 m

- **Verdict: WEAKENED** (tag kept: NEW TO THE LITERATURE, only in the trivial sense that the model is unpublished).
- **Confidence:** high on the numbers, low on the interpretation. **Cost:** cheap.
- **Evidence checked.** I recomputed world transforms from the GLB JSON:
  - Root node `---NULL_8.2°_2420ft_73761.6cm_üMM---` translation 737.616. 2,420 × 0.3048 = 737.616 exactly.
  - Node 1 `UntergrundHyperNURBS` yaw 8.2°. Mesh (node 12) world yaw 8.20° at y 753.530. `PEF_Schick_203-13_Plan` 8.20°.
  - Corbo general plates: Pl. 1 6.15°, Pl. 3 6.94°, Pl. 6 6.74°, Pl. 67 6.82°.
  - G&T: Fig. 2 6.57°, Fig. 36 6.71°, Fig. 45 6.18°.
  - Almost all plates sit at 753.53 m. Every number in the card reproduces.
- **What fails:**
  - **(a) The title's "every Corbo 1981 and Gibson & Taylor 1994 plan" is false.** Corbo detail plates run from 3.99° (Pl. 52–54 cistern) through 5.40° and 5.52° (Pl. 24 TR-XI, Pl. 46) to 8.29° (Pl. 24 TR-V, only 0.09° from Schick) and 11.46° (Pl. 23). The quarter-turned plates 16, 19 and 61–64 run 95.2–100.9°, i.e. 5.2–10.9° after the 90° turn.
  - **(b) The plane yaws mostly record how each drawing is set on its printed page or scan.** Plates from one survey (Corbo) spread over 7.5°, and the general plates alone over 0.8°. So the 1.3–2.0° difference between the general plates and Schick does not show a north or survey difference between Schick and Corbo.
  - **(c) The "1.1–1.8 m tangential offset 50 m from the pivot" does not follow.** Each plane was rotated until it fit, so no residual misfit is implied.
  - **(d) The 753.53 m datum is still the model's own claim.** A web search for a published m-asl level of the Rotunda or Calvary ("feet above the Mediterranean", Wilson/OS) found nothing.
- **What survives:** the model's datum and registration constants are documented exactly, and nobody has published them.
- **Literature check:** same repo greps as card 1, plus the web query above.

## A4 card 3: 38 enclosed voids; void 3 (18 × 12 m) has no dedicated source; no kokhim tomb

- **Verdict: WEAKENED.** The "undocumented cavity" part is effectively killed; the "no kokhim tomb modelled" part stays as a fact but is a scope limit (tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT).
- **Confidence:** medium-high. **Cost:** cheap for the geometry; needs the original PEF sheet to close.
- **Evidence checked:**
  - **The voids CSV.** `mesh_enclosed_voids.csv` row 3: centre (−37.1, −71.5), ceiling median 752.63, floor 740.63, "nearest small source plane" G&T Fig. 10 at 26.5 m.
  - **Footprint test.** I transformed each source plane's accessor min/max into world space and asked which planes actually cover the void. `PEF_Schick_203-6_I-K` is a vertical E–W section, elevation span 730.9–776.8 m. It passes **1.6 m** from the void centre, inside its along-section extent. `PEF_Schick_203-4_9-10` (a N–S section) passes 10.2 m away. The worker's "nearest *small* source plane" metric left out the long 203 sections.
  - So a dedicated Schick section runs through the cavity. The cameras `GreatCistern.S/SO` also show that the modeller treated it as a known feature (probably the Great Cistern). Whether the I–K sheet draws the cavity at 18 × 12 m can only be checked on the PEF original; the licence bars using the texture.
- **Kokhim:**
  - No node name refers to the Joseph of Arimathea tomb (I checked all 132). The `Schick1885_ZDPV_Felsgraeber` planes sit at about x −68 to −72, z −67 to −75, north of Calvary, not west of the Rotunda.
  - The model has no source plane for the tomb.
  - A HyperNURBS-smoothed bedrock surface cannot resolve niches about 0.5 m wide.
  - So "the kokhim tomb is not modelled" is true but expected. It is a limit of the model's sources and scale, not a gap in the documented record.
- **Literature check:** repo grep "Arimathea" and "kokhim" finds only Allegro extracts and tomb rows, nothing on this model. I did not repeat the worker's web search for the cistern's dimensions.

## C4 card 1: the 1990 and 1996 Qumran plateau "cave" targets were dug by hand to 17 m in 2002 and were natural stone lines

- **Verdict: SURVIVES** (tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT). The card's point (3), the "correction" of Magen & Peleg, is **struck**.
- **Confidence:** high. **Cost:** cheap.
- **Evidence checked:**
  - **HA 117.** `hadashot/pdf/en/vol-117/00126_qumran-plateau.pdf` (Price, 22/03/2005) reads: "based on GPR and seismic surveys conducted by the Tel-Aviv Department of Geophysics in 1990 and by J. Strange in 1996"; "manual excavation continued down to the 16 m level and below … side tunnels … at the 17 m level"; "Concentrated lines of natural stones … source of the seismic anomalies"; and, for the Eastern Square, "proved to be entirely geologic". All as the card says.
  - **The project record.** In `online_screen.csv`, id 126 is "held … first lines only". `salvage_records/searches.csv` mentions Price only in S27 (Cave 12, 2017). There are no hits for "Trinity Southwest", "paleo-chamber" or "Strange 2006".
- **What fails (point 3):**
  - Magen & Peleg p. [70] (PDF p. 61) say: "Excavations by others … (Strange 2006). Some of those excavators used heavy earthmoving machinery". They do not name 2002 or Price.
  - Strange's own Orion post of 1997 (https://orion.huji.ac.il/orion/archives/1997b/msg00352.html) speaks of "the heavy equipment" that dug a trench on the plateau in the 1996 work.
  - So Magen & Peleg are most likely describing 1996, not the 2002 hand-dug shaft. There is nothing to correct.
- **Literature check:** web search on Strange 1996, backhoe and trench found the Orion posts and worldofthebible.com; there is still no publication linking this test to the later S34 anomalies.

## C4 card 2: the 2002 Qumran ERT "west of ruins over dump" shows a second, unlabelled resistive body

- **Verdict: SURVIVES** (tags kept: body 1 KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED; body 2 NEW TO THE LITERATURE).
- **Confidence:** low that body 2 is a real void-type feature; high that it is in the figure. **Cost:** needs new data (the line's position, SPIE 4758 full text).
- **Evidence checked:**
  - **The figure** (Drive `1iT4sAYZ-…`, 976×730). The labelled "POSSIBLE CAVE" is at about 30–37 m along the line. A second orange-cored body sits at about 57–62 m and about 5–9 m deep, under the WALL marker at about 59 m, with a halo open towards the base. The markers read WALL at about 8, 24, 41, 59 and 74 m, plus FENCE at about 33 m.
  - **The S34 extraction notes doc.** It has body 1 only (it puts it at "about 25–35 m", against the card's 30–38 m; a minor difference). It quotes Freund 2009: "It still remains to be excavated."
- **Literature check:**
  - **Missed by the worker.** The same section is published as Bauman et al. 2002, *CSEG Recorder* 27(10), Fig. 4. The project already logs it in `registration/qumran_online_followup.json` ("Possible cave is an interpretation, not verified cavity").
  - I fetched the CSEG article. Its text gives only the caption and the general sentence "GPR, 2-D resistivity, and EM were used to search for subsurface voids". Body 2 is mentioned nowhere, so the tags stand.
- **Alternative explanation:** the line runs "over dump", and body 2 sits right under a WALL marker. Loose dry spoil, wall footings or a gravel lens in marl are all resistive. C4 card 1 shows that stone lines in this marl gave false "cavities". An inversion artefact under a resistive foundation is also possible.

## C4 card 3: the Cave of Letters metal null covers only the top 3–3.5 m; ERT puts the base of the debris in Hall B at about 7–8 m

- **Verdict: WEAKENED** (tag kept: KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED).
- **Confidence:** medium. **Cost:** moderate (Bauman ch. 6 text).
- **Evidence checked:**
  - **The figure** (Drive `1Enf7quLuDTi9W9HCqiOdmsCB9KNQvQPl`, Fig. 6.6). The caption reads "corrected for relief as surveyed in the cave", so the relative depth is a datum, which supports the worker's assumption. Near line position 30 m the pink body runs from about −5.5 to the rubble/bedrock contact at about −13 m. That is about 7.5 m thick, so the measurement reproduces.
  - **Conflicting statements in the same notes doc the card cites.** The S34 notes say "Rubble is 0 to about 6 m thick" (Bauman ch. 6). For Jol's GPR they say "Bedrock is at the surface to about … 4–5 m in the halls". The card does not report either statement.
  - **ERT resolution.** ERT resolves the lower boundary of a very resistive body (above 24,000 Ω·m) poorly.
- **What follows:** "the lower 4–5 m of the debris lies below the metal survey's reach" is overstated. On the authors' text figures it is about 0.5–2.5 m.
- **What survives, and an extra point in the card's favour:**
  - The core point holds: the metal null limits only the top about 3.5 m, and debris continues below that depth.
  - The 3.5 m figure is for a 55-gallon drum (EM61). For a smaller hoard the depth reached would be less.
- **Literature check:** the CSEG Recorder 2002 text gives no thickness ("a significant thickness of roof fall debris"). Repo grep "Cave of Letters" and "Bauman" finds no note comparing the depths.

## D1 card 1: ROSAPAT's 21.5 m depth for Birket Musa is an error

- **Verdict: SURVIVES** (tag kept: NEW TO THE LITERATURE). Confidence lowered from high to **medium**.
- **Cost:** moderate (Augustinović 1951 and Robinson–Smith 1970 would trace the source of the number).
- **Evidence checked:**
  - **The catalogue.** `hexview pdf-text` on PDF p. 132: "38) Birket Musa [PADIS code 0025] … Site extension: 220 × 160 m, 21.5 m deep", with the bibliography as the card gives it.
  - **The project record.** Entry-29 README lines 232 ("remain unreconciled … no typo correction") and 646 (Netzer: c. 180 × 150 m, at least 3 m, at least 80,000 m³).
  - **The arithmetic** (756,800 m³; 2.96 m) is correct.
- **Caveat:** Netzer's figures are **minima**, so they do not logically exclude 21.5 m. The SWP 5–6 ft is surviving wall height, which the project itself notes is not depth. The case is one of strong plausibility: a 21.5 m deep, 0.75 million m³ basin in the Jericho plain would be unique and noticed. It is not a contradiction. A catalogue-wide `pdf-search "m deep"` gives this as the only depth in an entry (the other two hits are a 7 m fosse and a 6 m trench).
- **Literature check:** web search `"Birket Musa" Jericho reservoir "21.5" OR "2.15" deep` found nothing.
