## B4, card 1: Mt Gerizim destruction date unchanged 2008→2021, but its coin basis changed

**Verdict: SURVIVES** (NEW TO THE LITERATURE kept). Two sub-points need correcting (below). **Confidence:** high for the printed discrepancies; medium for what they mean. **Cost:** moderate (the JSP 19 catalogue, Cat. 701–890 and the single Antiochus IX coin are beyond what the connector returns).

**Evidence checked.**
- JSP 8, store `01-JSP8-Mount-Gerizim-II-indexed-843e1fce` p93 (`hexview.py pdf-text`): "121 of Antiochus VIII and Cleopatra Thea (125-110 BCE)"; "A total of 412 coins postdating his reign …: 163 … 79 … and 121". 163 + 79 + 121 = 363, so the slip is confirmed. Also "A copper juglet from Building P-II … the latest date to 125 BCE", and "only in 111-110 BCE". Store p38 has the silver hoard in a "vessel, the latest dating" (OCR fragmentary).
- JSP 19, Drive `1afKbqio94MQ1KaqChqBNw47IwfHejARy` (`read_file_content`, 396,786 characters, text stops at PDF p.107, as the worker said):
  - p.[37]: "Forty-three coins … Antiochus VIII … and … Cleopatra Thea (125–121 BCE), and another 240".
  - p.[39]: "in 110/109 BCE, just four coins were found".
  - p.[44]: silver hoard of 25 + 103 + 1, the Akko coin "dated to 120/119 BCE … some nine years later".
  - p.[45]: bronze hoard, latest "three of Antiochus VIII, dated to 121/120 BCE".
  - p.[59]: "the latest coins … related to the destruction … are the coins of Alexander Jannaeus".
  - Bijovsky p.[92]: "Cat. Nos. 701‒761 … 762‒890 … during the reign of Antiochus VIII".
  - All quotations on the card are accurate.

**Corrections.**
1. The "self-contradictory" Akko sentence (p.[45]) is probably a list punctuated with a colon, not a contradiction. It reads "1,217 coins from autonomous ʿAkko-Ptolemais … dated from 169–164 BCE: 411 coins … up to 110 BCE; 240 of Antiochus VIII …; four of Antiochus IX …; 455 of Alexander II Zabinas". The items after the colon are mostly not Akko coins at all, so 1,217 is a separate item, not a total that 411 belongs to. Drop this slip.
2. The 2021 terminus does not rest on one Antiochus IX coin alone. The same paragraph cites the 411 coins "up to 110 BCE" and says "The latest coins are dated, as mentioned, to 112–110 BCE". "Anchored on single coins" overstates the case. Whether those 411 dated issues really reach 110 is the catalogue question the card already names.

**Literature check.**
- Web: "Mount Gerizim destruction John Hyrcanus coins Antiochus IX 110 BCE silver hoard Tyrian tetradrachms Bijovsky review" and "\"Gerizim\" destruction date 112/111 OR 111/110 OR 110/109 Hyrcanus numismatic evidence critique Magen coins". Hits: Wikipedia, a Pepperdine coin collection, IES product pages, and INR "Hasmonean Coins: Update and Observations" (title only). No review or critique compares JSP 8 with JSP 19.
- Project: `registration/magen2021_gerizim_coins_extracted.md` (l.66, Bijovsky "not before 110/109") and `gerizim_magen_neaehl_extracted.md` (l.77). Neither compares the two volumes.

**Alternative tried.** JSP 8 may have lumped the joint coinage with Antiochus VIII's sole reign. Even so, 121 is not 43 + 240, and the range is still wrong. Counts that rose between volumes are expected from further identification (the worker notes this too).

## B4, card 2: Martyrius, the 613 override, the duplicate K number and no Justinian coin

**Verdict: WEAKENED.** The printed facts hold. Two of the supporting arguments do not. Tag NEW TO THE LITERATURE kept for the duplicate K number and the override (small points). **Confidence:** high for the printed facts; low for the chronology. **Cost:** cheap for the facts; the IAA coin database is needed for the K numbers.

**Evidence checked.** I downloaded Drive `1Fl-86qvtf5LCr7lsl3rW9RPakdldg3VH` (its text layer is empty) and rendered pp.[383]–[385].
- Cat. 5 (L230 B2312, cast, 450–550) **K 28972** (p.[383]); Cat. 19 (L419 B4105, Justin II, 567/568) **K 28972** (p.[385]). The duplicate is confirmed.
- Cat. 16: "613–618, Alexandria, DOC 2/1:340, No. 198", K 28964. The heading "The following coins were found together within a jar" is above Cat. 19–23.
- Magen ch.1 (Drive `1HF7tzO6f83jy3XtPKPY0UdobPaxeUyMW`) p.[10]: "but we should accept the earlier dating (613 CE), one year before the Persian conquest". p.[8]: "ascribe its construction to 552/3 CE", with Di Segni's 567/8 or 582/3 rejected. Both confirmed.
- No Justinian coin is catalogued. Cat. 9 (512–538, uncertain) is the only coin that could be one.

**What fails.**
1. The card sets the 613 override against "his own phase chapter runs IIB … to … 638". This is not a contradiction. The same ch.1 p.[10] gives a two-step model: "initially damaged and abandoned, during the Persian conquest in 614 CE … and was finally abandoned during the Arab conquest in 638 CE". A coin of 613–618 fits that model whichever date is taken. The override is unjustified as numismatics, but its only effect is to keep the coin out of 614–638.
2. The "missing K 28971" means nothing. The catalogue's K numbers are not one run: 4467–4471, 20986–20987, 28958, 28960–28972, 37352 and 37562 all occur. A gap at 28971 is expected (an unidentifiable coin, or one not catalogued).
3. The Justinian gap rests on 16 Byzantine coins, and Cat. 9 might be Justinianic. The card says this itself.

**Literature check.** Web: "Magen 2015 \"Monastery of Martyrius\" review Genesius inscription 552/3 Di Segni 567/8 dating coins Heraclius". Hits: Wikipedia, CMHL corpus entry, library records. Nothing on the coins. Project: no note reads these coins.

## D2, card 1: ASI built doorways face E and S and avoid W "regardless of slope"

**Verdict: WEAKENED.** The compass preference is robust. "Regardless of slope" is not. Tag KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED kept. **Confidence:** medium. **Cost:** cheap (data in hand).

**Re-run.** `python3 -I private/D2/scripts/analyse.py private/D2/data/statements_dem.csv scratch/R6/d2res` reproduces the card exactly:
- built_asp3: n = 132; within ±45° of downslope 0.235 against a null of 0.216 (p = 0.33).
- Sector counts: N 29/34.4, E 48/34.1, S 39/31.2, W 15/32.3 (p = 0.0001).
- Residual resultant 112.7°, p ≈ 0.

**What fails.** Coupling to slope rises steadily with slope:

| slope | n | within ±45° of downslope | facings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–5° | 41 | 9.8% | E 13, W 1 |
| 5–8° | 47 | 21% | |
| ≥ 8° | 44 | 38.6% | |

The ≥ 8° figure is in the worker's own key `built_asp3_slope8`: 38.6% against a null of 22.6%, p = 0.007. The card cites that key but does not report it. On slopes of 8° or more, the compass residual is **not** significant (p_resultant 0.14, p_chi4 0.39; W 8 against 10.9 expected). So the doors ignore slope mainly on gentle ground, where aspect at the 200 m scale says little. On real slopes they partly follow it, and the E-not-W preference is not shown there.

**Precision audit.** In 20 random built units I found:
- 3 lime-kiln openings (5/136, 5/140, 5/197): openings, not doors;
- 1 rock-hewn Herodian burial cave classed as built (102/432 Bethphage), because "(near the Franciscan Church)" is the last subject word;
- 16 true built entrances or pen openings.

Over the whole set, 11 kiln units and 6 units with cave or tomb words in the context are mixed in. With both groups removed, n = 116 and E 33 against W 10, so the W deficit holds and grows stronger.

**Literature check.** I agree with the worker. Faust 2001 (*OJA* 20(2)) is the published E-facing claim for Iron II. A prevailing-westerly-wind explanation is a well-known alternative, and the result fits it as well as any cosmology. Repo: no analysis found.

Road bias of the null: of the 4,341 non-hoard HA excavations, 50.4% lie within 1 km of an Itiner-e road (median 0.989 km). The 42 HA hoards: median 0.673 km, 54.8% within 1 km. The road geometry passes a check: excavated milestone sites lie 3–24 m from the lines.

**Literature check.**

## E2, card 2: no Itiner-e road through the Tabor corridor, yet HA-ESI records a 7.5 m paved road at Ḥorbat Devora

**Verdict: SURVIVES** (NEW TO THE PROJECT kept). The card misreads its key source, and the correction makes the card stronger. **Confidence:** medium–high that Itiner-e has a gap here. **Cost:** cheap to moderate (Tepper's Legio paper; TIR).

**Evidence checked.**
- `scratch/B1b/txt/hadtext/en/vol-122/01606_h-orbat-devora.txt`, l.28–61: "total width 7.5 m", curbstones, an upper level 4.7 m wide, E–W. All confirmed.
- The card says the report "does not date it" and gives "sherds only 'worn' and no road date". **That is wrong.** The report says the probe trench "yielded potsherds from the road and the roadbed that dated only to the Late Roman period and therefore, the entire road can be dated to the second century CE onward". It calls it "the ancient Roman road".
- It also says: "This road was unknown to the research on Roman roads in the Galilee". It proposes that the road led east from Legio, or linked Sepphoris, Gat Hefer and Kafr Kana with Mount Tabor.
- So the card's kill condition (a medieval caravan route) is answered by the report itself, and its "low" confidence that the road is Roman should be raised.

**Distances recomputed** from `scratch/E2/roads_itm.npy`:
- Devora: 8,266 m to Sepphoris–Tiberias (Certain);
- Kafr Kama: 7,488 m;
- Tabor summit: 10.1 km.

**Kafr Kama** (`vol-126/08521`): the curbs stand 2.1 m apart, and the road is dated Umayyad by coins of 697–750 found beneath it. It is a village road, not part of a Roman network. It does not belong in the headline.

**Literature check.**
- Web: "Roman road Dabburiya Mount Tabor Legio Tiberias … Cinamon" found only news of the 2022 Acre–Tiberias section and Tepper's Legio *capita viarum* paper (title).
- The excavator's own sentence (HA 122) already publishes that the road was outside the known Roman network. That Itiner-e lacks it has not been stated anywhere I found, so NEW TO THE PROJECT stays.
