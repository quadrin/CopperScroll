## A1 card 1: the 1955 cut-mark labels (F→A × 1–12) in EDF 2006, the impossible "A5 à A4", and the missing verso frames

- **Verdict: SURVIVES** (tag kept). One interpretive point in the card is **inverted**, and correcting it makes the card stronger (see notes).
- **Confidence:** high that the transcription errors exist; medium-high that the labels count 12 marks per turn (upgraded from medium).
- **Cost to confirm:** cheap. The data are in hand (see "new route" below). A1 thought they were blocked.

**Evidence checked.** EDF 2006 `pdf-text`, PDF pp.68–74. Every range in the card matches the print: seg 2 "(F2 à F7)", seg 3 "(F8 à F12)", seg 4 "(E2 à E4)", seg 5 "(E4 à E8)", seg 6 "(E9 à D3 ?)", seg 7 "(D4 à D8 ?)", seg 8 "(D9 à D12)", seg 9 "(C1 à C7)", seg 10 "(C8 à C12)", seg 11 "(B1 à B6)", seg 12 "(B7 à B11)", seg 13 "(B12 à A4)", seg 14 "(A5 à A4) ont été écrits à l'encre blanche en 1955" (PDF p.73), seg 15 "(A8 à A12 ?)". §6.2.1 and §6.2.9 (PDF p.58) confirm that EDF traced the marks on Mylar and repainted them. I recomputed the USC catalogue rows (`index.csv`, 294 cut-segment rows) and they agree with the card's reading of the verso marks.

**Literature check.**
- Repo main and branch_only, `grep` for "repères de (la) découp", "encre blanche", "cut mark": no Copper Scroll note. The hits are unrelated ("cut marks" on bone and similar).
- Web search: "Copper Scroll Wright Baker cutting segments marked letters numbers each strip ink labelled convolution 1955" returned only Wikipedia-level pages.
- DJD III (Baker, pp.203–210) is still unread (blocked, 26.7 MB), so the tag stays provisional, as A1 says.

**Alternative explanation tested, and a correction.** A1 reads the labels as "F (outer, column I) to A (inner, column VIII)". Kuhn 1954 p.195 says the opposite about roll A: "Le début du texte du rouleau A se trouve donc à l'intérieur ; … il a été enroulé à partir du commencement." Kuhn's outer columns A1 and A2 are cols VII and VIII (B5 card 3, checked). So **col I is at the centre**: F is the innermost turn and A the outermost.
- This fits cutting order. The saw starts on the outside, so the first turn cut would be labelled "A".
- It also **resolves A1's "unexplained point"**. Width per mark rises from about 1.1 cm (F, segs 2–3) to about 2.4 cm (A, seg 15). That is what 12 equal angular marks per turn predict as the radius grows outward. Twelve marks of 1.1 cm give a turn about 13 cm round (d ≈ 4.2 cm); twelve of 2.4 cm give about 29 cm (d ≈ 9.2 cm). Kuhn gives roll A an outer diameter of 11 cm.
- Kuhn also gives roll A "6 ½ enroulements" (OCR "6 21/*"). Six letters F–E–D–C–B–A means six labelled turns. That is independent support for the scheme.
- The large roll was cut second (Allegro 1960 p.27: "in January 1956"; the first roll gave cols IX–XII). This matches A1's reading of Baker's 19 Oct letter.
- So the "Why it might matter" list of turn boundaries is still right, but read from the inside out.

**New route to confirm (cheap).** EDF 2006 vol. II has **pre-restoration verso photographs of every segment**, made in Feb 1994 before EDF repainted the marks: Pl. XXX–LII, PDF p.(316+segment no.), so seg 6 = p.322, seg 14 = p.330, seg 15 = p.331. On Pl. XLIII (seg 14, PDF p.330) the big white "14" is clear. At the foot, left of centre, there is a small white numeral that reads as **"6"** with a tick mark, and there is a faint tick at lower right. A "6" fits A5–A7 (A6), not "A4". This is a low-confidence reading at book resolution. Read the plates for segs 4, 6, 7, 14 and 15 at 400–600 dpi before going to WSRP.

**Side note on A1's side observation.** It is **killed**. "grotte n° 8" is not an EDF error: DJD III p.201 has "la grotte 8 = 3Q", and Puech (EDF 2006 PDF p.195) has "grotte 8 désignée maintenant par le sigle 3Q". This is the 1952 survey numbering.

## A1 card 2: the 115-TIFF set is the colour recto set minus cuts 1 and 9; no verso frames; RE frames only for cuts 15–16

- **Verdict: SURVIVES** (tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT).
- **Confidence:** high for the arithmetic; low-medium for the claim that the RE frames were made because of the rivet join.
- **Cost to confirm:** cheap for the inventory. Moderate for whether WSRP verso TIFFs exist (needs `unzip -l` of the 4.5 GB zip, or WSRP).

**Evidence checked.** I recomputed from `out/A1/frame_inventory_1988.csv` and the project `index.csv`:
- The catalogue has 122 colour recto, 122 b&w recto, 25 colour verso and 25 b&w verso rows (the 25 include the cut 16 verso-fragments view).
- The inventory totals are recto colour 120, recto fragments 2, verso colour 24, and 115 delivered: 120 + 2 − 1 (cut 1) − 6 (cut 9) = 115.
- Bucket `index.json` holds 93 `usc:` items and none of their names matches verso.
- RE codes (RET/REB) appear only for cuts 15 and 16.
- A Drive name search did not turn up any verso TIFF. That search was noisy and not exhaustive.

**Literature check.**
- Repo grep for verso combined with TIFF, "missing" or "not delivered": nothing that says the delivery lacks versos.
- The project does know that verso *masters* exist. `research/preregistration/arrivals/items/usc-wsrp-masters-cuts-21-22.md` asked USC on 8 Oct for "colour versos UC15246578, UC15246893" (cuts 21–22). `phase4_summary.md` l.341 counts 22 verso *previews* for cuts 1–10.
- No note states that the 115-frame high-res set is recto-only.

**Alternative explanation.** The verso masters may simply never have been scanned at TIFF resolution. That would not change the practical point. RE frames could also mark edge-on views of the broken join for some reason other than the rivets. A1 already labels that part an inference.

**Notes.** The remedy is partly in hand: EDF Pl. XXX–LII (pre-restoration verso, 1994) and Pl. CLXXXIX ff. (post-restoration verso) cover all segments at book resolution. These are not the 1988 state, but they show the cut labels and the rivets.

## B5 card 1: Baker's minute of 6 Sept 1955 says he tested his method "on an actual piece of the scroll", and the scrolls were "fully examined in the U.S.A."

- **Verdict: WEAKENED.**
- **Final tag:** NEW TO THE PROJECT (the minute's wording only).
- **Confidence:** high that the minute says this; low that it implies an unrecorded trial cut or sample.
- **Cost to confirm:** needs new data (Baker, BJRL 39 and DJD III pp.203–210).

**Evidence checked.** TPA/1/132 (Drive `1QYCHkEFl7bzCdXFGrgkE7ZRG8Rk97TTv`), via `read_file_content`: "was able to demonstrate them on an actual piece of the … scroll, to the complete satisfaction of everyone", "… have been fully examined in the U.S.A. from a metallurgical point of view", signed "H. WRIGHT BAKER. 6/9/55". Confirmed. Shanks 2007 (Drive PDF) has "first test cut … that could hardly have been known in advance" and "Experiments were conducted in the United States on replicas". Confirmed.

**Alternative explanation (main reason for the weakening).** Detached original fragments were known from the start. DJD III p.201 (repo `research/history/djd3_text_cave_list_extracted.md` l.171) says "the upper roll had shed many small fragments" in the cave in 1952.
- A demonstration of "methods and treatments" (adhesive coating and sawing) on one loose fragment is therefore the mundane reading. It does not conflict with "the first cut of the roll was a gamble".
- It also does not create a new unrecorded fragment: the shed fragments are already a known and unlocated class.
- "Fully examined in the U.S.A." fits Corwin's Johns Hopkins study (de Vaux 1953 p.557; Johnson 2002 in *Copper Scroll Studies*). It needs no sample of 3Q15, although one cannot be excluded.

**Literature check.** Copper Scroll Studies text (Drive `1w-Y2yZjeAUSwnB7sF09Yfyc1uxFN2l0j`): there are no "trial" or "experiment" hits about the scroll. The Corwin passage is present. Web query (Baker, detached fragment, adhesive): nothing.

- **Verdict: WEAKENED.**
- **Final tag:** NEW TO THE PROJECT, for point (2) only.

- **Cost to confirm:** needs new data (the full TPA/1/132 file).

- (4) 2 vs 3 January is trivial and already in `timeline.csv`.
- (2) survives as a genuine tension. Baker says by 19 Oct that the official had not come; Davies has him satisfied by 5 Oct. Davies' phrase could still mean satisfaction expressed at a distance.

**Literature check.** "Homoud" gives no hits in the repo or corpus, and nothing on the web in one pass.
