## A1b card 1: EDF revers plates settle the labels of segments 12–15
- **Verdict:** SURVIVES. Tag kept: KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED (scheme), plus a NEW TO THE LITERATURE erratum note (the text and plate contradict each other). **Confidence:** high (labels); medium ("8" = Baker strip). **Cost:** cheap.
- **Evidence checked:**
  - I rendered EDF PDF pp. 428, 430 and 433 at 200 dpi.
  - Pl. CXXXVI (seg 13) revers foot reads **B12, A1, A2, A3, A4**.
  - Pl. CXXXVIII (seg 14) reads **A5, A6, A7**, all clear.
  - Pl. CXLI (seg 15) reads A + blue paper "8", then A9, A10, A11, and an A with an illegible number.
  - The text on PDF p. 73 does print "(A5 à A4)", and p. 74 prints "(A8 à A12 ?)" and "étiquette de papier, portant le chiffre huit".
  - Extra check: the post-treatment Pl. CXLIII (p. 435) repaints "A 8, A 9, A 10, A 11" and a bare "**A**". So EDF itself could not read the fifth number, which supports the card's reading of the "?".
  - DJD III p. 207 (PDF 227): "marked upon the edge of the scroll in white paint, along with a letter corresponding to the convolution"; paper strips with "index letters and numbers".
- **Alternative explanations:** relabelling by EDF is ruled out by its own Mylar-before-repainting sequence, and Pl. CXLIII keeps the same labels. On the paper "8": seg 13 also carries an "étiquette papier", but in its central zone with no number (Pl. CXXXVI). So paper labels are not all index strips. The seg 15 label sits in the label row, at the A8 position, which supports the card but does not prove it.
- **Notes:** this card overlaps B5b card 1 (Baker's scheme) and should be merged with it.

## A1b card 2: rivet heads of cut 15 against EDF; "at least 6" positions
- **Verdict:** WEAKENED. Tag kept: NEW TO THE LITERATURE (provisional). **Confidence:** medium (r2 is a genuine discrepancy); low (r1). **Cost:** moderate (EDF radiographs, registration of RLB/RRT).
- **Evidence checked:**
  - I ran the card's own kill test (a). EDF Pl. CXL (avers, before treatment), **Pl. CXLII (avers, after treatment, drawn after the old adhesive was removed)** and Pl. CXLI/CXLIII (revers) all draw exactly **4** left-edge rivets, at the same heights, with nothing in the upper third.
  - Text p. 74: "quatre rivets"; seg 16: "cinq trous".
  - The card's 3-rivet registration is sound. Frame gaps r4→r7→r5 are 0.26 and 0.16, against EDF 1→3→4 at 0.26 and 0.16.
- **What holds:** a head at fraction ≈0.39 (r2), seen in two lightings, is absent from all EDF plates. EDF rivet 2 shows no head in 1988. The union is 5 positions, which equals seg 16's five holes in EDF's text.
- **What fails:** r1, the hole in the adhesive at ≈0.13. EDF's cleaned plate still shows no rivet there, so "at least 6" is not supported. The defensible claim is "5 positions, of which EDF plots 4 on seg 15".

## A1b card 3: segment 13–14 "lignes parallèles" are not ruling and are against an ancient date; cross-cut kill test
- **Verdict:** WEAKENED. Tag changed to **NEW TO THE PROJECT** (the observation); the dating challenge loses its basis. **Confidence:** medium (lines cross letters, so they postdate the punching); low (modern origin, now disfavoured). **Cost:** moderate (registering CT14_RRT).
- **Kill test run (cheap version):**
  - I set seg 14's avers right edge (Pl. CXXXVIII) against seg 13's avers left edge (Pl. CXXXVI), the two sides of the 13|14 cut, aligned on the bottom edge at the same plate scale.
  - Both sides draw oblique lines rising to the right at the same slope, running up to the cut.
  - Two of the three bands meet at matching heights (≈345 vs ≈355 px; ≈450–500 vs ≈450 px at 200 dpi). The lowest seg 14 band (≈665–695) has no partner at seg 13's edge.
  - 1988 frames: in CT13_RLT the scratches cross letters and run to within a few mm of the left edge. In CT14_RLT the right edge (x≈1750–2550, y≈2900–3900) is too corroded to trace them, though faint oblique lines are present.
- **Meaning:**
  - On EDF's own record, at least some lines continue across a 1955 saw cut. They therefore predate the opening. The recto was inaccessible once the roll was rolled, so they were made between the engraving and the rolling.
  - That is EDF's "époque fonctionnelle" (with its "?"). It is not a modern date, and not the 1955 hand-saw explanation.
  - "Not ruling" stands, but EDF never calls the lines ruling: the legend says "Traces d'utilisation (?)".
  - EDF's tracings are hand-drawn, so this is consistent with continuity across the cut but does not prove it.

## B5b card 1: Baker explains the F–A × 1–12 marks
- **Verdict:** SURVIVES. Tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT. **Confidence:** high. **Cost:** cheap.
- **Evidence checked:**
  - DJD III p. 205: "guard disk 5 in. … bearing numbered graduations"; p. 207: as in A1b-1.
  - BJRL p. 52 (djvu ll. 2810–2815): "corresponding to the convolution. The positions were also marked on paper strips attached to the segments".
  - A small extra 1956/1962 difference: in BJRL the strips are attached "to the segments", in DJD III "to the scroll" after a segment was removed.
- **Literature check:** web and store searches turned up no later account that explains EDF's marks.
- **Notes:** it is a duplicate of A1b-1's scheme point, so merge the two. The card is right that the direction (A = outer) is not stated by Baker.

## B5b card 2: Muhammad (Mohamed) Saleh, Jordan's representative
- **Verdict:** SURVIVES. Tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT. **Confidence:** high (name and role); low (both identifications). **Cost:** needs new data.
- **Evidence checked:**
  - BJRL p. 56, in the Department of Antiquities' appended statement: "bursary to our Technical Assistant, Mr. Mohamed Saleh". DJD III p. 210 (PDF 230): "their representative, Mr. Muhammad Saleh".
  - Store-wide "Saleh": the only hits relevant to the Copper Scroll are DJD III and Kenyon's "M. Saleh 19[54-5]". The web found nothing.
- **Alternative explanation:** the status table says the representative is "not an embassy official". That overreaches. Harding's 12 Oct 1955 letter mentions a "slight misunderstanding with the Embassy", so Allegro's "Jordanian embassy official" of 5 Oct may be a different man. Saleh is a second named witness, not a replacement.

## B5b card 3: DJD III (1962) is a revision of BJRL (1956)
- **Verdict:** SURVIVES. Tag kept: NEW TO THE PROJECT / probably NEW TO THE LITERATURE. **Confidence:** high (text). **Cost:** cheap.
- **Evidence checked (my own IA download):**
  - BJRL: "at the edge opposite the centre of the first column", "may have been used to hold the metal onto the scribe's table"; "the damaged area proved to be unlettered"; "the first turn of the larger is sadly elliptical"; "only a trifling loss of letters".
  - DJD III p. 205 swaps in "This may have been damaged during the drilling process", with the "unlettered" clause gone. Pp. 209 give "centre of the leading edge" and "first turns in both scrolls are nearly flat".
- **Alternative explanation:** OCR can garble fractions, but it cannot add or drop whole clauses.
