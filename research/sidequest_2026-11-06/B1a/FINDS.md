Title (one line): HA-ESI 117 records a Second Temple Hebrew inscription incised before firing on a sherd from Thiede & Lass's Moẓa dig (2001). The report gives no reading, and no later source located here publishes one.
Lane: B1a
What is odd (2–4 sentences): The Moẓa report (Thiede† and Lass, HA-ESI 117, 4 Apr 2005, id 147) says "An enigmatic ostracon, bearing a Second Temple Hebrew inscription, incised before firing, was also found (Fig. 5)". It gives no reading, no dimensions and no specialist. The sherd came from the loose fill (L7) between two collapse layers of a Crusader vaulted building. The same fill held a Hospitaller-cross tile. The Hebrew caption has the same wording (שבר אוסטרקון הנושא כתובת בעברית מימי הבית השני). The published photo (IAA image 3472-5.jpg, scale bar to 5 cm) shows a sherd about 4.5 cm across with several deep incised strokes and a curved stroke. An inscription incised before firing is a potter's or owner's mark made in the workshop. It is not an ostracon in the strict sense. The second Moẓa report (HA-ESI 118, id 403) does not mention it. Neither does any of the 4,667 online HA-ESI reports or any article in ʿAtiqot 41–121.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): HA-ESI 117 report id 147, `hadtext/en/vol-117/00147_mo-z-a.txt` and `hadtext/he/vol-117/00147_*.txt` (paragraph on L7; caption 5). Permit A-3472 (2001) and G-87/02. Map ref as printed NIG 21550–70/63320–30 (a published site). Photo: https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/images//3472-5.jpg (IAA copyright; private copy at `/home/claude/swarm/private/B1a/moza_HAESI117_id147_fig5_IAA.jpg`, not for the repo). ʿAtiqot citations of the dig without the inscription: ʿAtiqot 108 art. 13 (2022; cites "Thiede and Lass 2005" only for settlement remains near Tel Moẓa) and ʿAtiqot 58 (Greenhut, Crusader reservoirs at Moẓa; cites Thiede 2005, *The Emmaus Mystery*).
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Corpus: grep of all 4,667 EN HA-ESI texts for Thiede/Lass/A-3472 found only ids 147 and 403 plus bibliographic citations. Grep of the ʿAtiqot 41–121 text corpus (`gs://…/atiqot/text/`, 1,222 files) found no ostracon or inscription. Repo main and branch_only have Thiede only in the haesi_features coding (an aqueduct row) and a Qumran pottery note. Web: "Thiede Lass Moza ostracon Hebrew inscription", "Excavation of a Crusader Building at Motza", "CIIP Motza Qalunya inscription incised before firing". The only fuller publication found is Thiede, Lass and Lewis, "The Excavation of a Crusader Building at Motza", *BAIAS* 23 (2005), title only (https://aias.org.uk/volume-23/). Its content could not be read. IIP (search.inscriptionsisraelpalestine.org) shows no Moẓa or Qalunya site. CIIP IV.2 in the bucket could not be searched (hexview `pdf-search --doc Ameling-…-75db1ea8` crashed). CIIP IV.1, which would cover the Emmaus–Moẓa area, is not in the store. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT**. It may be **NEW TO THE LITERATURE** as an unread inscription, if BAIAS 23 and CIIP IV.1 are silent.
Why it might matter: Second Temple Hebrew inscriptions on pots made in the hills west of Jerusalem are rare. This one was found redeposited in a Crusader building at a site that one excavator promoted as Emmaus. The report appeared after Thiede's death in Dec 2004, which may be why the inscription was never followed up. It is the lane's "mentioned once and never published" type, with a published photo that would allow a reading.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Kill it if BAIAS 23 (2005) or CIIP IV.1 (Iudaea/Idumaea, 2018, the Emmaus/Qalunya section) gives a reading. Neither is in the store. Otherwise an epigrapher (or a separate model instance, flagged as a non-expert reading) can read IAA photo 3472-5 and the original in the IAA stores (excavation basket of L7). Do not take the strokes as letters without the original: the photo is lit from one side and several strokes could be tool marks.
Confidence: medium (that it was mentioned once and is unpublished online); low (that it is unpublished anywhere)
Touches the Copper Scroll? no (Moẓa is no scroll place, and the object is a potter's inscription)

FIND CARD
Title (one line): HA-ESI 118 reports a colourless glass cylinder seal in Neo-Assyrian style (worshipper before the moon god Sin of Ḥarran) from the Garden Tomb, Jerusalem. Neither later Garden Tomb report nor ʿAtiqot nor any web source found here publishes it.
Lane: B1a
What is odd (2–4 sentences): Zelinger's 2005 trial excavation at the Garden Tomb (HA-ESI 118, 20 Mar 2006, id 327) found the seal in the disturbed fill of the underground complex first dug by R. Wyatt. Its size is given as diam. 0.75 cm, length 1.7 cm, dated 8th–7th c. BCE, with T. Ornan named for the seal. Katsnelson's glass section calls it "unique", made of "transparent, colorless glass, which is quite rare in such an object", wheel-cut, and with a wide perforation that suggests reuse as a bead. Transparent colourless glass with wheel-cut decoration is rare before the Hellenistic period, so the object is either an important Iron Age II glass piece from Jerusalem or a later object misdated. Neither Zelinger's later Garden Tomb reports (HA-ESI 120 id 885, 2006 season; HA-ESI 122 id 1376, 2007 season) nor any other text in the corpus mentions it. The only later citation (HA-ESI 131 id 25554, Bet Guvrin) cites Katsnelson 2006 for the Roman beaker bases, not the seal.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `hadtext/en/vol-118/00327_jerusalem-the-garden-tomb.txt` (the paragraph on the circular building L102, and "The Glass Finds" by N. Katsnelson). Permit A-4549. Funders named in the report: WAR (Wyatt Archaeological Research) and a Texas foundation. Later seasons: `en/vol-120/00885_…`, `en/vol-122/01376_…`. Citation: `en/vol-131/25554_bet-guvrin.txt`.
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Corpus: the seal appears in no other HA-ESI text and in no ʿAtiqot 41–121 text (grep "Garden Tomb", "Gordon's Calvary"). Repo: the site is coded in `research/regional/haesi_features/phase2_2026-10-10/features.csv` (rows 459–461: shaft, cistern, quarry) and in `research/text/entry46_alternatives_2026-10-12/pit_followup.csv`. The seal is not coded anywhere. Web: "Garden Tomb Jerusalem glass cylinder seal moon god Sin Haran Zelinger Ornan", "\"Garden Tomb\" \"cylinder seal\" glass Neo-Assyrian Jerusalem". No publication was found. The 2023–24 coverage of the Tel Hadid moon-god shell seal cites Gezer as the regional parallel, not this seal. Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT**. It may be **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED** (Ornan may have published it in a study of moon-god glyptic that this pass did not find).
Why it might matter: The Garden Tomb's date is contested: Barkay argued for an Iron Age tomb; the Protestant tradition and the funders treat it as the tomb of Jesus. An Iron Age II Assyrian-style seal from the complex north of the Damascus Gate is datable evidence about the area's Iron Age use, and it was announced only in a short note. It is the lane's "find mentioned once, never published in full" type. The rarity claim (colourless glass, wheel-cut, Iron Age) is the oddity to check.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): Search Ornan's bibliography (glyptic of the 8th–7th c.; moon-god imagery in Israel) and the IAA seal catalogue for "Garden Tomb". If a full publication exists, re-tag as KNOWN. Then check whether that publication confirms the material. A colourless glass identification of an Iron Age seal would itself merit a materials analysis, so ask whether XRF or LA-ICP-MS was done. It would be killed if the object was re-identified as rock crystal or as a Roman or later bead. The seal itself is in IAA hands (excavation A-4549).
Confidence: medium (unpublished in the corpus and in the web searches run); low (unpublished anywhere)
Touches the Copper Scroll? no

FIND CARD
Title (one line): The online HA-ESI corpus holds almost no Judea & Samaria Staff Officer (SOA) excavations from 2005 to 2017: 9 SOA excavation reports in vol. 116 (2004), 4 in the 3,266 reports of vols 117–129, then a return from vol. 133 (2021). So HA-ESI "silence" for West Bank places in those years is structural.
Lane: B1a
What is odd (2–4 sentences): Vol. 116 (2004) has 9 reports out of 79 (11%) excavated under SOA "L-" permits: Peleg and Feller at Shimʿa, Sansanna, Rosh Ẓurim, Karme Ẓur, Sheqef–Negohot, Betar ʿIllit, Har Gillo, En-Nabi Yaqin. From vol. 117 (2005) to vol. 129 (2017) the corpus has 3,266 EN reports. Only 4 of them are SOA excavations (Emanuel Cave 2010, Nahal Tirza 2013, Makabim 2014, Tel Ḥevron 2016). The other West Bank appearances are passing mentions or SOA-coordinated surveys inside Israel. The judgement is per report, by text marker: an L- permit, "on behalf of the Staff Officer", or SOA as director. From vol. 132 (2020) West Bank reports return: Qumran (vols 132, 135), Nahal Ḥazazon, Khirbat Mazin, Khirbat es-Samra, Noqedim, Karme Ẓur, Peẓaʾel. A separate test agrees: of the 4,263 EN reports with a parsable map reference (D3's `refs_raw.json`), only 15 lie in the box E 232–260 km / N 600–700 km (NIG, Judean Desert and Jordan Valley). Only one of them is a genuine West Bank report in vols 118–131 (Nahal Tirza, id 2278). The other three in those volumes are misplaced references: Ramla id 980 and Eshtaʾol id 993 are D3 error rows. Silwan id 1510, "ʿEin el-Lauza, map ref. 23220/62960" in both EN and HE, is about 10 km east of Silwan. It is probably a further map-reference error that D3 did not list. I have not verified it, and it belongs to D3's lane.
Evidence (exact sources: file path / page / figure / frame / coordinates): `/home/claude/swarm/out/B1a/b1a_soa_reports_by_volume.csv` (per volume: total EN reports, SOA excavations with report ids, mention-only counts). Vol. 116 ids 37, 39, 44, 45, 46, 47, 66, 68, 69. Vols 117–129 SOA excavations: 1589, 2278, 8514, 24926. Returning reports: 25932, 26421, 26422, 26503, 26580 (SOA excavator), plus 25868, 25871, 25780, 26389, 26493, 26504 (West Bank sites, SOA named in the text). The marker regexes are in `scratch/B1a/py` (not committed).
Literature check (queries run, result, tag): Repo main and branch_only: "Staff Officer"/SOA appear only in the arrivals register and the IV/17 and Ibziq notes, as archive routes. No note records the publication gap. `research/regional/haesi_features/README.md` calls Qumran/Feshkha silence "silence of the readable corpus" but gives no structural reason. The 24 Oct hoard follow-up (`gs://…/hadashot/derived/hoard_followup_2026-10-24.md`) reads all 4,667 online reports and concludes "Hadashot adds no silver hoard from the window". Web: "Greenberg Keinan … Hadashot … not published since 2004", "Emek Shaveh Hadashot … Staff Officer reports". These searches found nothing that states when HA-ESI dropped and resumed SOA reports. Greenberg & Keinan's 2009 sourcebook (Drive 03; not in the bucket text store) is the likely place it is discussed. SOA results of those years appear in the *Judea and Samaria Publications* series (JSP 8–10 page photos are in Drive 02). Tag: **NEW TO THE PROJECT**. Specialists probably know the policy, so it may be **KNOWN BUT UNDER-DISCUSSED**.
Why it might matter: Any project inference that treats the online HA-ESI corpus as uniform coverage is biased for West Bank places in 2005–2019. That includes "silent" looks at Qumran, Feshkha, Jericho, the Buqeiʿa, Nebi Musa and Ibziq, and the hoard follow-up's negative result. Those years are structurally empty, not searched and found empty. The other venues are JSP volumes, *Qadmoniot*, the SOA archive and Greenberg & Keinan's WBADB. Most Copper Scroll candidate places lie east of the Green Line.
How to confirm or kill it (with data in hand, or what is missing): (1) Read Greenberg & Keinan 2009 (Drive 03) for their statement on where SOA work was published, and check JSP 8–10 contents for 2005–2017 excavations. (2) Repeat the count with a real Green Line polygon over D3's coordinates, not keyword markers. The marker method misses SOA reports whose opening omits the permit. If a polygon count finds many 2005–2017 West Bank reports, the finding dies. (3) For the project, flag every HA-ESI "silent" look at a West Bank place in 2005–2019 as `coverage: structural gap`.
Confidence: medium (the pattern is robust in both tests; the cause is not documented here)
Touches the Copper Scroll? maybe. It does not touch the text, but it bears on how much the project's HA-ESI negative evidence at West Bank candidate places is worth.

- Inscriptions: Qiryat Shemuel ossuary of "Alexa bar Shalom" (id 1497) is published as a monograph (*The Tomb and Ossuary of Alexa Son of Shalom*). The Tiberias Muslim-cemetery Hebrew amulet, the ʿEn Tut seal, the Western Wall Plaza reused gravestones and the City of David Givʿati Hebrew graffito (id 250) were not pursued for lack of budget; they are probable CIIP I material.
- Same permit, different site names (32 pairs with a report in my slice; `b1a_shared_permits.csv`): these are typos in permit numbers, one development permit covering several sites, or regional and local names for one place (Modiʿin and Kh. Umm el-ʿUmdan). Nothing substantive.
- Same place, different names (67 pairs ≤150 m apart; `b1a_same_place_pairs.csv`): mostly Arabic and Hebrew name pairs or 100 m-precision generic references. Kh. Murt es-Seil (id 564) against Kh. er-Raʿi (Garfinkel's "Ziklag", id 25174) is a false match: Murt es-Seil's areas lie about 1.5 km away. - Excavation year against publication year: no report in vols 116–137 dates its excavation after its publication. Mismatches between EN and HE excavation years are trivial (Ḥorbat Rimmon id 1019: EN 2004–2005, HE 2003–2005).
- Pre-2004 print material (92 HA issues and 19 ESI volumes, Drive 03): not processed in this pass. The bucket holds only the online 2004–2025 texts. The Drive copies are 70–137 MB scans over the connector limit, as `haesi_features/README.md` already records. The next worker would need the bucket `pdf/` page-split copies, if any exist.
