## New observation

- **The exact null is not a safe baseline for this model.** Strings that are not the truth, or that are scored on the wrong strips, come first above the exact-null rate: decoys 8 of 76 (P = 0.041), truth at a wrong column 9 of 76 (P = 0.015). The truth at its own line does no better (6 of 76).
- **Why, in part: the templates.** Ten letter forms (ג ז ט נ ס פ צ and final ך ם ף) use font skeletons; the other 17 use the scroll's traced strokes. On the new lines, when the true letter has a font template, its traced-letter variant scores lower in 79 % of pairs at the line and 67 % at the wrong column. Between two traced letters the truth wins 51 % at the line and 57 % at the wrong column (`confirm_result.json`, diagnostics). The letter's template, not the image, drives much of the ranking. INFERENCE: the font skeletons fit corrosion texture differently from the traced strokes.
- **Registration.** Round-1 registration can put two lines on the same row (X 2 and X 4: cut 17, orientation 1, row 124, exploratory).

## What it changes

- The 30 October B result stands: on the previews this model does not read. On unseen lines there is no excess over the exact null. On round 1's own lines the exact-null excess (P = 0.0045) recurs at a wrong column (exploratory), so it is not a reading signal.
- Analysis by synthesis with the round-1 model on the previews is closed. This is the third failed test of the approach, after round 1 and B2 (bucket frames at half resolution, B2's own model).
- **Method lesson for any later reading model.** The exact null and a decoy are not enough. A model can prefer the true string without the image, for example through template quality that tracks letter frequency. A wrong-image control (the same set scored where the line is not) is needed before any significance claim. On round 1's lines the decoy test alone would have passed (7–1, P = 0.035).

## Limits

- One model, one image source (public previews, about 27 px per letter), one candidate generator. The test says nothing about full-resolution frames with this model.
- The exact null assumes local additivity and exchangeable letters. The controls show that this model breaks the second assumption.
- The power check used a toy model. With weak real signal (pairwise win rate about 0.64) the frozen rule would often fail. The observed truth-first count (6, below the decoy and wrong-column counts) gives no sign of such a signal.
- Other workers viewed cuts 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10 by eye. B3 viewed no image. The scorer is automatic and had no free parameter.
