## Question and what could change

**Question.** On the full-resolution 1988 frames, can a forward model built from the scroll's own letter appearance pick the edition reading of a line out of edition-style one-letter confusions?

**What could change.** A pass would have produced computed rankings for the disputed words at XII 10 (R1–R7 of the frozen XII 10 protocol), IX 10, I 12 and X 15. These would be exposed-image model outputs, not readings.

## What was done

1. **Strips** (`prep2.py`, `prep2_frames.json`). For each cut, every raking-light frame (RCT RLT RRT RCB RLB RRB; RT RB on cuts 4, 17, 21) was read at half resolution, rectified to a vertical strip and cropped. The crops are private (`SP/products/B2/strips/`).
2. **No cross-frame registration.** SIFT on the strip texture found 0–108 inliers between lights. Silhouette ECC reached 0.82–0.86 for side lights. Each frame is therefore scored on its own, and scores are summed.
3. **Calibration lines** (`items2.py`, `calibration_items2.json`). Word-level "undisputed" rule: no ETCBC flag, no Greek, no `variants.csv` row on the word. 76 TRAIN lines (including the 14 still-eligible round-1 lines and all lines of columns I, IV, VI, which have a missing cut). 33 HELD-OUT lines, seed 20261031. Five one-letter competitors per line, drawn from the edition-confusion pairs.
4. **Model** (`model2.py`, `run2.py`). Letter templates learned in two Viterbi passes on TRAIN lines, per column and light class, shrunk toward a scroll-wide mean and a rendered V-groove prior. Letter score = z-score of the max-pooled normalised cross-correlation (NCC) against that letter's own pooled background. Registration: the letters shared by all candidates are aligned; the disputed slots are scored per candidate.
5. **Exploratory runs** (TRAIN only, §8 of the protocol; `explore/`). They exposed a bug (blank font glyphs) and a flaw in the round-1 style baseline. The one score-based choice: learned over rendered-only templates (TRAIN position-level p 0.24 against 0.87).
6. **Confirmatory run**, once, with the frozen code: `heldout_result2.json`, `calibration_rankings2.csv` (194 rows, one per held-out candidate), `target_rankings2.csv` (NOT RUN).

## Result

| Statistic | Observed | Chance (exact) | One-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lines with an observed competitor | 25 of 33 | — | — |
| T: truth beats every observed competitor | 2 | 4.89 | P(T ≥ 2) = 0.98 |
| W: true letter beats its alternatives, per position (secondary) | 22 of 62 | 28.67 | P(W ≥ 22) = 0.97 |

- Eight held-out lines had no observed competitor: the substituted letters fell outside the aligned run in every frame (II 1, II 5, II 8, IX 2, IX 17, X 9, XI 1, XI 2).
- Descriptive: the round-1 style rank (ties against the truth) put the truth first on 0 of 33 lines (MRR 0.22). This number uses the wrong baseline and is not a test.
- The position-level count is below chance, lower tail P(W ≤ 22) = 0.055. On a single position, the true letter won 20 of 51 contests (39 %). This is not significant. It hints that letter scores are not fully exchangeable between a common letter and its rarer confusion partner.
- On TRAIN (exploratory, same model): T = 4 against 3.6 expected, W = 38 against 34.5. Held-out and TRAIN agree: no line-level signal.

## New observations

1. **The learned letter forms collapse to one band pattern.** In every column the learned templates show the same horizontal light/dark edges at the top and bottom of the letter band. Letter shapes do not emerge, except where the rendered prior dominates. The edition text aligns the model to the text rows, not to the letters. Without letter-level positions on TRAIN lines, Viterbi learning cannot bootstrap letter appearance from these frames. (Private figure `B2_fig1`.)
2. **The 1/K chance baseline is too high for "truth plus one-letter neighbours" sets.** If scores add over letters, the truth beats each competitor with probability 1/2 at its position. With five competitors at different positions, the truth is first by chance with probability about (1/2)^5 = 0.03, not 1/6. Exact ties for unobserved competitors pushed this lower again. On TRAIN this produced below-chance ranks under the round-1 style statistic (E5: 0 of 30 first; E7: 1 of 38). Round 1 (30 October) used candidate sets of this shape and the baseline 1/K, so its test was conservative. Recomputing round 1 under this null would be a new exploratory analysis, not a rescue of the frozen round-1 result.
3. **The 1988 frames cannot be co-registered across lights from the image texture.** Under different raking lights the corrosion texture changes too much. A multi-light model of these frames needs a light-independent registration (for example fiducials in the frame, or a registered facsimile).

## What this changes

- It closes the mechanical-reading route on these images for this model class. Higher resolution alone did not help: the failure point is letter localisation, not pixel count.
- The disputed letters at XII 10, IX 10, I 12, X 15 and VII 11 stay as they are. The XII 10 protocol still needs two human readers.
- Any future candidate-ranking test with one-letter competitors must use an exact star-shaped null, not 1/K.

## Limits

- Half of full resolution was used for compute (4 shared CPUs). Letter height was fixed at 70 work px for all cuts; it was not measured per cut.
- Line rows come from a nominal-position window and the shared-letter alignment; they were not authenticated against neighbouring Hebrew by a reader.
- The groove cross-section (V, half-width 0.08 letter height) was set a priori. It was not fitted to measured stroke profiles. In this model it only enters as the prior of the learned templates.
- Frames were scored independently. Evidence across lights was summed, not fused.
