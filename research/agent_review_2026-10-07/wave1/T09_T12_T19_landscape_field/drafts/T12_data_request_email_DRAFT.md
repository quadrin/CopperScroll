**Subject:** Research request: Judean Desert cave-survey inventory (Qumran–Jericho cliffs, Wadi Qelt), for a non-invasive Copper Scroll (3Q15) landscape study

Dear colleagues of the Judean Desert Survey and Excavation Project,

I am writing on behalf of an independent, non-commercial desk-research project on the Copper Scroll (3Q15). The project compares the scroll's landmark descriptions with published archaeological and landscape records. Our working notes are public at github.com/quadrin/CopperScroll. We excavate nothing and use no metal detectors. Any fieldwork would only ever take place under the appropriate licence and through a licensed archaeologist.

We have read the published overviews of the project. These are Sion, Ganor, Klein, Hamer, Cohen and Amichay, *Qadmoniot* 164 (2022), pp. 41–51, and Klein et al. in Sion, Uziel, Ganor and Klein (eds.), *New Studies in the Archaeology of the Judean Desert* (IAA 2023), pp. 35–74. We would be grateful to know whether a version of the cave inventory could be shared for research for the following areas only:

1. The cliffs and marl terraces from Wadi Qumran south to Ras Feshkha, and north to Wadi Kuteif / Wadi el‑ʿAṣla.
2. The western escarpment of the Jericho oasis from Jebel Qarantal north to Wadi Nuweiʿimeh / ʿAin Duk.
3. The Wadi Qelt gorge between ʿAin Qelt and the exit to the Jericho plain, around Deir el‑Qelt (St George's).

For each recorded cave, the fields that would help us most are:
- the survey or cave identifier;
- the location, at whatever precision you think appropriate (a 100 m or 250 m grid cell is enough for us);
- the number of openings and their aspect (which way they face);
- any noted architectural features (pillars, steps, installations);
- approximate dimensions;
- periods represented, especially Hellenistic and Early Roman;
- whether the cave has been excavated, looted or disturbed.

We are particularly interested in caves with two openings facing east, and in caves near water installations. The scroll describes features of these kinds.

We would accept any conditions you set. These could include a signed confidentiality agreement, no publication of coordinates, aggregated or generalized data only, citation of the project and its staff in any output, and sharing our results with you before publication. If sharing is not possible, a pointer to the published survey reports (for example in *Hadashot Arkheologiyot* or *ʿAtiqot*) or to the archive files we should request would already help a great deal.

Thank you for considering this request, and for the project's work in protecting these sites.
