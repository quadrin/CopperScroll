# DRAFT ground-survey plan: non-invasive geophysics at the top-ranked Copper Scroll candidate spots

Status: planning draft only. No permits have been applied for, no one has been contacted and no fieldwork has happened. Sources are cited in REPORT.md §19. Each claim is marked EVIDENCE or INFERENCE.

## 0. Ground rules this plan assumes
- Every method listed is non-invasive. There is no excavation, coring, sampling or metal-detector sweep unless the licence explicitly allows it. In Israel, searching for antiquities, including with a metal detector, requires an IAA permit (EVIDENCE, per the Wikipedia summary of the Antiquities Law 1978, which cites the MFA text). The EAC guidelines note that the Valletta Convention requires prior authorization for detection equipment (EVIDENCE, Schmidt et al., EAC Guidelines 2, p. 39).
- The research question must be framed as landscape and water-system archaeology, not treasure hunting. INFERENCE, high confidence: a licensing authority would not accept a treasure-hunting proposal, and the scroll's quantities are disputed in any case.
- Under both Israeli and Palestinian law, any find belongs to the state. Israel: Antiquities Law 1978 s.2 (EVIDENCE, UNODC SHERLOC text). Palestine: Decree-Law 11/2018 (its text was not read here, so this is INFERENCE).

## 2. Method choice
Sources: EAC Guidelines 2 (Schmidt et al.), US EPA environmental-geophysics pages, Martínez-López et al. 2013, Geometrics.

| Method | Detects | Strengths at 1–4 m | Limits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| ERT (resistivity imaging; Wenner–Schlumberger or dipole–dipole, 0.5–1 m electrodes) | Air-filled voids (high resistivity); fill/bedrock contrasts; walls | DOI ≈20–30% of the current-electrode spread (EPA). Cavities found down to ≈3× their radius; best when electrode spacing ≤ cavity diameter (Martínez-López et al. 2013) | Slow. Needs electrode contact, which is hard on dry rock and rubble. Resolution falls with depth. Metal objects are not identified as such |
| GPR (200–500 MHz) | Interfaces, voids, metal (strong reflectors) | High resolution in dry, low-loss ground such as limestone and dry gravel (EPA) | Depth of investigation can drop to centimetres in saline or clay layers (EPA). EAC p. 15 warns of strong attenuation in salty soils, which matters on the Qumran marl and near the Dead Sea. Rough terrain on summits |
| Magnetometry (fluxgate gradiometer) | Ferrous objects, fired features, cut features with susceptibility contrast | Fast area coverage; 0.25 m × 0.25 m for characterization (EAC p. 13) | Does **not** detect gold, silver or copper. Geometrics: "non-ferrous materials do not cause anomalies (gold, silver, copper…)". Heavy iron litter at visited sites (railings, cable car, fences) |
| EM induction (conductivity / in-phase, e.g. EM38-type) and metal detectors | Conductive metal, ferrous and non-ferrous (EPA) | The only methods here that respond directly to silver or copper | Shallow. Response falls steeply with depth and depends on target surface area, not mass (EPA). Deep large conductors ≤10–15 m at best. Urban and metallic noise. Needs explicit licence |
| Microgravity | Voids (negative anomalies) | Independent of salinity. 1–10 m targets at 1–10 m station spacing (EPA) | Slow. Needs ±1–2 cm elevation control and terrain corrections. Results are ambiguous without other data (EPA) |

Recommended combination (INFERENCE, medium confidence):
- **Spot 1:** ERT and GPR where the ground is dry limestone or colluvium; ERT and microgravity on the marl; EMI on short lines only. Collect GPR at 0.25 m line spacing and 0.05 m along-line (EAC p. 14).
- **Spot 2:** ERT on terraces, with lines parallel and perpendicular to each channel head. GPR on dry fills. EMI. Avoid the wet channel bed.
- **Spot 3:** GPR at 200–250 MHz for 3–4 m depth in dry rubble, ERT with 1 m electrodes for a deeper DOI, and microgravity across the eastern corner. Magnetometry only as a ferrous-litter map.

Precedent (EVIDENCE): GPR, 2-D resistivity, EM and metal-detection surveys were run at Qumran and its cemetery around 1999–2001 (Bauman, CSEG Recorder). Earlier GPR claims at Qumran appear in *Qumran Chronicle* 9 (2000), pp. 123–137.

## 3. Team and equipment (INFERENCE)
- **Licence holder.** A research-licence PI must be a faculty member of an academic institution, hold at least an MA in archaeology and have Near Eastern field experience (EVIDENCE for the IAA licence: Matskevich & Weinblum 2021). The requirements of the Staff Officer and of DACH were not checked.
- **People.** A geophysicist, two field assistants, a surveyor with RTK GNSS, a local partner or liaison, and a rope-access/safety lead for cliffs and gorges (cliff work is standard in the IAA desert project, which uses rappelling: EVIDENCE, IAA release via the Israeli embassy).
- **Equipment.** Multi-channel ERT system (48–96 electrodes), GPR with 250 and 500 MHz antennas, fluxgate gradiometer, EMI conductivity meter, gravimeter (optional; hire), RTK GNSS, and an approved drone for photogrammetry if permits allow.
