## Headline: wave 1's Koḥlit "convergence" is withdrawn

Wave 1 said three independent lines pointed Koḥlit away from Tell es-Sultan. On closer reading, none of the three holds up as new or independent:

| Wave-1 line | What wave 2 found |
|---|---|
| b. Qiddushin 66a, "Koḥalit in the desert" | Not new. Allegro's provisional notes (c. 1956, per Lefkovits p. 73), Milik (DJD III p. 274, citing it only to dismiss it), Lurie (1963), Goranson, Lefkovits (p. 75 n. 10) and Zissu (2001) all know it; Zissu already used it against Puech (p. 149). Every Talmud manuscript and early print checked spells the name with a vav (כוחלית); Elitzur documents the regular -at → -īt shift. |
| List order pulls entry 60 away from Tell es-Sultan | Mostly a modelling artifact. The T06 kernel was normalised over a place list crowded around Jericho; with density-neutral kernels P(entry 60 = Tell es-Sultan) stays at 0.28 against a prior of 0.30. The order cannot separate the Koḥlit proposals (all within ~3×); it only says entry 60, if not at a proposed site, probably lies north near entries 57–59. |
| The ground north of the tell is now a refugee camp | Affects access, not identity. |

Where Koḥlit stands now: open between Tell es-Sultan (Puech) and the ʿEin Samiya valley / Kh. el-Marjama (Zissu), with a slight archaeological lean to Tell es-Sultan.
- Zissu's ʿEin Samiya loses its main textual support: no one who has examined the original reads "Janoaḥ" at XII 10. Milik weighed and rejected it (DJD III p. 298), Wolters saw the original in 1991 ("certainly not a ṣade", Copper Scroll Studies p. 319), and Puech finds no inserted yod (2006 p. 206). Zissu worked from facsimiles. And on Lefkovits's own reading, the pit is at Janoaḥ itself (p. 425), so the ʿEin Samiya entry-60 target contradicted itself; the consistent target is Kh. Yanun.
- Tell es-Sultan is still only a circumstantial hypothesis: its "great cistern to the north" depends on Puech's own restoration at IV 1, and it has no name argument.
- Archaeology (feature matrix): Tell es-Sultan 6.75 vs ʿEin Samiya 5.95 out of 12.5. The order flips if desert setting and name count more. Both have a Bronze Age shaft-tomb field directly north of the tell. The best physical match to entry 11 ("the pool east of Koḥlit") is the hewn-stone reservoir at the east foot of Tell es-Sultan (SWP III pp. 222–223), which is undated.
- Corrections: Milik's own Koḥlit was the Carmel (Wadi ʿEin es-Siaḥ), not Beit Kahil or Kh. Kuḥlah (Zissu misread DJD III). Jericho's Second Temple Jewish cemetery lies west–southwest of the tell, not north. Zissu's "Sifre Deuteronomy" is Sifre Numbers §124.

## Other results

- **Primary-text checks (W2-E).**
  - 1QapGen XXII 13–14 does equate Shaveh with the valley of Beth-hakkerem (Fitzmyer p. 108; Machiela p. 82), but Milik, Yadin and Puech already used it, and the Milik/Puech placements are a few km apart.
  - The Michmash-gorge idea for Beth Tamar was raised and rejected by Milik (DJD III p. 267) and asked by Puech.
  - Elitzur's criteria eliminate wave 1's ha-Melaḥ ↔ Mâlḥah and "hurubbeh = cistern" leads and strengthen the finding that name likeness alone is chance-level.
- **Greek letters (W2-D).** CIIP adds parallels only for ΔΙ (a 1st-c. quarry counting mark at Hippos; a Jerusalem theatre-seat label) and a disputed ΤΡ weight. ΚΕΝ, ΧΑΓ, ΗΝ and ΣΚ appear nowhere, and CIIP's name index has no Κεν-, Χαγ- or Ην- names. A pre-registered test on Ilan's Lexicon undercuts "initials of ordinary Jewish persons". Still no system above 2 of 7.
- **Plates and letter model (W2-F).** Puech 2006's plates (42–74 px per letter, heavy JPEG) cannot settle any disputed letter. Only ו/ר passed the pre-registered gate, marginally and unstably; at X 15 (Siloam) the model gives no usable evidence. Proper RTI/3D imaging of the originals is the real bottleneck.
- **Browser (W2-G).**
  - DQCAAS has 296 Allegro photos of the cut strips, all low-res (720 px; high-res via Manchester), plus 12 drawn column copies showing XII 10 and all the Greek groups.
  - The ISAC record is the same 1967 CORONA frame (26 Sep 1967, 2 m). Frames DF040–042 are already scanned and free to download with the user's EarthExplorer login.
  - HUJI GeoHub has 1945 RAF verticals at 1:15,000 for every target (online previews too small; 600-dpi scans for a fee; place labels unreliable).
  - Bavarian frame 1031 shows no pits or tombs north of the tell; frame 1032 must be ordered from the Kriegsarchiv.

## What would actually move Koḥlit
1. **XII 10 at the segment 21/22 cut**, imaged at high resolution (Amman originals, the EDF copies, Manchester's master photo C.21–23, or RTI). Three narrow questions: is there an extra engraved stroke, is the second letter ב or כ, and is the last letter ח or ה.
2. **Date the ʿAin es-Sultan reservoir** (Dorrell, PEQ 1993; Warren 1869). If it is Early Roman, entry 11 strongly favours Jericho; if Ottoman, Jericho's best match disappears.
3. **Locate and date Kallai's rock-cut pool at Kh. el-Marjama** (Kallai 1972 sites 65–66; Mazar, IEJ 1995; Zohar, IEJ 1980). If it is east of the tell and Roman, the pool criterion swings to ʿEin Samiya.
4. **Kenyon, Jericho II–III**: are there Roman tombs at the mouth of a northern shaft?
5. Cheap imagery: download CORONA DF040–042 (free, already scanned), and order 600-dpi RAF scans PS30 5107/5137 (north of the tell) and PS32 5038 / PS30 6054 (ʿEin Samiya).
