=====================================================================
BOTTOM LINE
=====================================================================
1. No identification moves decisively. Koḥlit is still unidentified. Puech, its main advocate, writes that the name "reste assez difficilement identifiable" (2006 p. 175 n. 49). EVIDENCE.

2. Zissu's ʿEin Samiya loses its main textual support. INFERENCE, medium.
   - Milik weighed and rejected the Janoaḥ letter-string on the original (DJD III p. 298).
   - Puech (2002, 2006, 2015), working from the EDF imaging, denies any inserted yod.
   - Wolters examined the original in 1991. He allows yod/waw + nun, but reads a different word.
   - So no one who has examined the original reads ינח; Zissu worked from facsimiles.

3. Puech's Tell es-Sultan remains a hypothesis built on circumstantial features. INFERENCE, medium.
   - One of its features (the "great cistern to the north", entry 15) depends on Puech's own restoration of the direction word.
   - It has no name argument.
   - It answers b. Qid. 66a only weakly.

4. Wave 1's "three lines of evidence" against Tell es-Sultan mostly dissolve (section 4).
   - The b. Qid. 66a argument is decades old, and Zissu used it against Puech specifically (2001 p. 149).
   - The order model restates an old debate in numbers, and its result is driven by entries 57–59.
   - The refugee-camp point concerns access, not identity.

MILIK
- ADAJ 4–5 (1960), translation pp. 139–142 (OCR). EVIDENCE.
  - Koḥlit appears 4 times:
    "On the hill of Kohlit" (p. 139)
    "In the pool, situated to the east of Kohlit" (p. 139)
    "In the East Pit, situated to the north of Kohlit" (p. 140)
    "At the tunnel in the Smooth Rock to the north of Kohlit, which opens towards the north and which has tombs at its entrance" (p. 142)
  - At IV 1: "which is at [....]qeh" (p. 140). No Koḥlit there.
  - I found no Koḥlit note in the commentary (pp. 143–155, OCR).
  - p. 138 warns that "beth, kaph, sometimes mem, very much alike; he and heth of almost identical forms", and that yod/waw can be "a simple vertical stroke".

- DJD III (1962). EVIDENCE.
  - Count: 4. "ce site très important revient quatre fois dans le document: ii 13, iv 11, xii 10" (p. 285). These are Milik's items 4, 12, 21 and 64.
  - תל = "colline naturelle" (p. 240, C 20). כחלת = the adjective "kohlit", standing for the full name עין כוחל (p. 234, B 19g).
  - The identification (pp. 274–275, D 71–75):
    - The Beirut marble plaque prefacing Massekhet Kelim names a spring "ʿEn Koḥel".
    - Milik places it at Wadi ʿEin es-Siah, "Fons Eliae", on the WEST slope of Mount Carmel.
    - "Caverne de l'immersion" = ʿAin Umm el-Farag.
    - The pool is east of the spring (II 13). The pit is north-east of it (IV 11).
    - The narrow cave north of Koḥlit is near el-Farag (XII 10).
  - The treasure is legendary; the catalogue lies "à Kohlit, source d'Elie" (p. 281).
  - ON b. QID. 66a (p. 274, verified on the page image fig_DJD_p274_D71.png):
    "Après les essais d'identification peu satisfaisants (כוחלית שבמדבר de bQiddušin 66a; Beit Kâḥil à l'ouest-nord-ouest d'Hébron; Ḥ. Kuḥlah au nord-est de Bersabée) la solution est venue en partant du folklore religieux juif."
    -> Milik cites b. Qid. 66a, Beit Kahil and Kh. Kuḥlah only to set them aside.
    -> Zissu (p. 148) says Milik "proposed" Beit Kaḥil and Kh. Quḥlet. That does not match DJD.
  - XII 10 reading note (p. 298; fig_DJD_p298_note.png):
    "צח, malgré la tête très petite du ṣadé, semble préférable à ינח où les deux premières lettres seraient en ligature, comparable à celle de la l. 9."
    -> Milik considered the Janoaḥ letters in 1962 and rejected them.
  - Also p. 240, C 22: צחיח "si la lecture est bonne".

- Milik's original reading (1956–57; via Lefkovits pp. 73, 425–426). EVIDENCE as reported.
  - XII 10: "In the pit nearby … in a hole opening toward the north".
  - The second word is שכנה; the fourth is בחלה. No Koḥlit at XII 10.
  - Wave 1's T03 table puts בחלה on the second word. That is an error.

ALLEGRO
- Provisional MS (c. 1956; Lefkovits's siglum "Allegro-pr"; reported p. 73). EVIDENCE as reported.
  - "In the tell of Kḥlt"; כחלת "may be the same as" כוחלית of b. Kid. 66a.
  - This is the earliest link to b. Qid. 66a found in this search.
- 1960 book (read directly). EVIDENCE. There is no Koḥlit at all.
  - I 9 reads bḥl šl bḥlh, "In the trough (?) of the Place of the Basin (?)", which he puts in the Temple (bathhouse below the Môqēd chamber) (p. 33; nn. 17–18, pp. 136–137).
  - II 13, IV 1 and IV 11–12 read bḥlh, "in a hole" (pp. 35, 39).
  - XII 10 reads "bšyt škynh bṣpwn bḥlh ptḥh ṣpwn" = "In the Pit (Shîth) adjoining on the north, in a hole opening northwards" (p. 55). n. 314 glosses škynh as šekēnah "neighbouring" (f.); the Šîth is "under the Altar itself" (n. 315, p. 170).
- 1964 revised edition (via Lefkovits p. 73 and Zissu p. 148). EVIDENCE as reported. KHLT at I 9, II 13 and IV 11; XII 10 is still "in a hole".

LEFKOVITS 2000. EVIDENCE.
- Count: 4, plus a restored 5th at IV 1, שב[כ]חלת (pp. 73, 154–156).
  - The restoration was "originally proposed by Lefkovits diss 1, 364-65, 367".
  - Wise follows it; Goranson "apparently agrees".
  - Puech offered the same reading independently at the 1996 Manchester symposium (p. 155 n. 10).
- Name: Kaḥelet "may well be the same as" Koḥalit of b. Qid. 66a (p. 74).
  - Rashi: "a district in the Wilderness". Jastrow: = Nophah, Num 21:30 (Transjordan). Goranson: Transjordan.
  - Root כחל, "azure".
  - The Koḥalit hyssop was probably named after the place (p. 75). Sources: m. Par. 11:7; m. Neg. 14:6; Sifre on Num 19:6; b. Suk. 13a; b. Ḥul. 62b (p. 75 n. 13).
- Entry 11 (p. 135): "In the pond which is in the east of Kaḥelet".
- Entry 19 (pp. 179–180): "Eastern deep pit suggests that Kaḥelet had more than one pit."
- Entry 60 (pp. 425–432): "In the deep pit which is in Janoah, in the north of Kaḥelet, its entrance is hidden".
  - The sign after the bet "is either a ligature of yod plus nun or a ṣade, followed by a he/ḥet/taw" (p. 427).
  - "Although rejected by Milik, the second word may well be שבינח" (p. 427).
  - Why Ephraim, not Galilee: "Joshua lists Janoah, Upper Beth-horon, and Jericho together. The Scroll mentions Horon (9:7) and Jericho (5:13), also indicates that Kaḥelet was near Jericho" (p. 428).
  - Alternatives he lists: Jonah/dove, Noah, marsh (reading ṣade), Ṣehab, Ṣiaḥ, Ṣaḥ, "dry land", Shebna (pp. 428–432).
- Drawings (p. 451).
  - Allegro's drawing: "a yod-nun-waw ligature followed by a cut … the two waws could be two legs of a he/ḥet/taw".
  - Milik's drawing: "a yod-nun ligature that looks like a ṣade".
- His own view: no site. "Near Jericho", with Janoaḥ (Ephraim) to its north (p. 428). He also entertains Transjordan (p. 75).

PUECH. EVIDENCE.
- 1997 (RevQ 18: 184–85 n. 62): first proposal. Not read; known via Zissu p. 149.
- 2002 (CSS pp. 78, 82–83 n. 62): XII 10 "the copyist engraved šbnh … (neither ṣade nor yod can be inserted)".
- 2006, p. 175 n. 49 (main statement). Tell es-Sultan "à titre d'hypothèse". His features:
  - a tell/butte (4);
  - a pool to the east (11), with the "source très abondante qui sourd au pied du tell" (2 Kgs 2:19–22);
  - a great cistern to the north (15);
  - an underground chamber to the north (19) and (60), with tombs hiding the entrance;
  - Qumran-type graves on the north side of the tell (Kenyon 1981 pp. 173–174; not verified here);
  - the sequence "Vallée d'Achor au nord de Jéricho";
  - Elisha -> Elijah -> the Carmel legend.
  - On b. Qid. 66a: "Jean Hyrkan a en effet conquis des 'villes' en Idumée … Bien que situé dans l'oasis de Jéricho, Tell es-Sultan est encore dans le désert, mais le toponyme peut ne pas être unique." (The 2002 version says Samaria and Idumaea.)
  - Rejects a district reading ("rien ne prouve").
  - Excludes ʿAin Feshkha ("pas de butte").
  - Zissu's site "dépend d'abord de la lecture Yanoaḥ de Lefkovits (2000) en XII 10, lecture qui n'est pas à retenir".
  - Answers Zissu's two-names objection with Dhibon/Qarḥoh and Jerusalem/Ophel/Zion.
  - Rejects TAVO B V 18 (between ʿAin el-Ghuweir and ʿAin et-Turabe): "pas de tell ni surabondance d'eau douce".
- 2006, p. 181 (entry 4).
  - "Ce toponyme … reste toutefois non identifié." The name is "pas … unique".
  - Not Carmel, not a district, not Pixner's centre.
  - n. 103: Zissu's Janoaḥ reading "est impossible", and his site "ne convainc pas davantage dans la séquence des toponymes". (Puech cites it as "XII 4", a slip for XII 10.)
  - n. 105: "blue" might mean a place where indigo was worked.
- 2006, pp. 186–187 (entry 15).
  - Restores שב[צפון כ]חלת: "Comme le canal est au nord du tell en (4) ainsi que le souterrain en (19) et (60) et que la piscine est à l'est en (11), il est probable que šb[ṣpwn soit la solution".
  - Also places entry (16) at Koḥlit.
- 2006, p. 206 (entry 60).
  - Reads שכנה ("qui se trouve", a rare qal of kwn) or שבנה. Takes the second צפון as "cachée".
  - The right-hand stroke "ne doit être considéré que comme une double frappe du burin … De yod inséré il n'y a point, encore moins de ṣade".
  - Counts Koḥlit 5 times against 4 for Sokokah.
- 2015 (English translation of 2006). Same content: pp. 13–15 n. 49, 31, 39, 52–53, 111–113. One translation slip: 2006 and DJD say Milik's spring is on the west slope of Carmel ("versant ouest"; CSS 2002: "western side"), but 2015 p. 13 n. 49 says "south slope".

ZISSU 2001 (PEQ 133: 145–158). Read in full. EVIDENCE.
- Count: 4, "or five if we accept Lefkovits'" (p. 146).
- Order: "no continuity or pattern can be found that would aid in identifying the place" (p. 146).
- b. Qid. 66a: dated to John Hyrcanus, following M. Stern. So he looks for Koḥalit on the desert margins of Hyrcanus's conquests in Samaria or Idumaea (pp. 146–147).
- Hyssop: m. Neg. 14:6, m. Par. 11:7 and "Sifre, Deuteronomy 19, 6" make Koḥalit a region, "the opinion of Goranson (1992: 287)" (p. 147).
- Reviews earlier proposals (pp. 147–149): Milik (Carmel) and his critics; Lurie (Tell Muḥalḥil, where Bar-Adon 1972 site 83 found only a 3.5 × 2.5 m structure); Pixner; Puech.
- XII 10: Janoaḥ is "obligatory when one examines carefully the copies of Wright-Baker and Milik; Allegro's copy … does not allow" it (p. 149). Janoaḥ = Yanun or Kh. Yanun (Onom. 108:20).
- Four required features (p. 150): mound, pool (spring), cistern/reservoir, pits (caves).
- Site: the ʿEin Samiya valley, "map ref. 181/155, Israel grid", about 17 km south of Yanun.
- Name: preserved in Wadi Kuḥeila (Mandate sheet 15-18, 1941).
- Features he reports:
  - Kh. el-Marjama, a tell of over 30 dunams;
  - a spring of about 300 m³/h;
  - a rock-cut pool and a reservoir;
  - many caves;
  - kokhim tombs at Kh. Samiya, including an Early Roman lamp and ossuary fragments (Baramki 1942).

COPPER SCROLL STUDIES (2002). EVIDENCE.
- Goranson (pp. 226–228).
  - Koḥlit is a district, "most plausibly identified as an area east of the Jordan River".
  - Accepts Lefkovits's IV 1 instance. Rejects Pixner and Beyer.
- Eshel (p. 106 and n. 35).
  - "Little is known about Kohlit other than it was located in the desert."
  - Adds: "there is an aqueduct at ʿEin Samiya".
- Bar-Ilan (pp. 204–205).
  - A specific place, "not necessarily the place mentioned in b. Qid. 66a".
  - Rejects Pixner and Goranson.
- Thiering (pp. 285–287): "the Blue", a room at Kh. Qumran (loc. 101/102).
- Wolters (pp. 317, 319–320).
  - The second letter of כחלת "is almost certainly a heth".
  - On XII 10 see section 3.
- Puech (pp. 78, 82–83): as above.

OTHER SOURCES CHECKED

RABBINIC TEXTS, checked on Sefaria (kohalit_rabbinic_passages.csv). EVIDENCE.

PLATES LOCATED AND RENDERED (Puech 2006 scan)
- Pl. CCCLV–CCCLVI: radiographs of col. XII, PDF 676–677.
- Pl. CCCLXXXI: galvanoplastic copy, PDF 704.
- Pl. CCCLXXXII: Puech's facsimile (Fig. 13), PDF 705.

- Composite figure: fig_XII10_word2_plates.png.

WHAT THE PLATES SHOW (as an observer, not an epigrapher)
(a) Facsimile. Draws word 2 as ש, ב, a short thick stroke touching a narrow nun-like letter, then ḥ/h. The dashed saw-cut line runs through that last letter. No ṣade is drawn. This is Puech's reading made visible, not independent evidence.
(b) Galvanoplasty. ש and ב are clear, then a narrow tall letter. The last letter sits on a deep vertical crease: the cut between segments 21 and 22. At this resolution I cannot tell whether a separate short stroke stands to the right of the narrow letter.
(c) Radiograph. Confirms that the end of word 2 falls at the 21/22 cut. Far too coarse to judge the disputed stroke.
(d) Puech's col. IV facsimile (fig_IV1_puech_facsimile_top.png). Shows the IV 1 Koḥlit restored in dashed outline across a lost piece; only ]חלת survives.
LIMIT: the decisive strokes sit at a saw-cut edge. Settling them needs the original or high-resolution RTI.

INFERENCE (medium)
- All the candidate readings turn on three judgements at the cut:
  - whether a small stroke is engraved at all;
  - whether the second letter is ב or כ;
  - whether the last letter is ḥet or he.
- ינח is not impossible on Wolters's description. But all three people who examined the original read something else.
- Janoaḥ does not by itself favour ʿEin Samiya:
  - Yanun is about 31 km NNW of Tell es-Sultan, and about 18 km N of ʿEin Samiya (distances.txt).
  - Josh 16:7 runs the border "from Janoah … to Naarah … and touched Jericho".
  - It favours a district only if בצפון means "in the northern part of", as in Zissu's "the pit at Janoaḥ in northern Kaḥelet" (p. 149).
