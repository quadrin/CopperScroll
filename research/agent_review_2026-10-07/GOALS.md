## The observations that would actually move Koḥlit (R07)

1. **XII 10 at the segment 21/22 saw cut**, imaged at high resolution. Sources: the Amman original, the EDF galvanoplastic copies, Manchester's master of Allegro strip group C.21–23, or new RTI. Three questions: is there an extra engraved stroke after the bet/kaf; is the second letter ב or כ; is the last letter ח or ה. This decides whether "Janoaḥ" is possible at all. *Status:* request for C.21–23 sent to Manchester Museum, 7 Oct. The [image-reading freeze `fd3f334`](https://github.com/quadrin/CopperScroll/commit/fd3f334ea2a40c0f8e99921ef663f8b126112fe1) fixes finite alternatives and decision gates before future inspection; no new scan opened or unused status certified.
2. **Date the ʿAin es-Sultan reservoir** (24 × 40 ft, hewn stone, cemented west wall with niche; SWP III pp. 222–223). Sources: Dorrell, PEQ 125 (1993); Warren 1869; Sellin–Watzinger 1913. If Herodian or Early Roman, entry 11 strongly favours Tell es-Sultan; if Ottoman, its best match disappears. *Status (follow-up A):* the present reservoir dates from 1898. The only ancient element is a curved spring-house wall with a niche, judged late Roman/Byzantine on opinion only. No Herodian pool is shown. The settling observation is sealed finds under the lowest course, ~1 m behind the 1898 back wall (Dorrell p. 112).
3. **Bearing and date of Kallai's rock-cut pool at Kh. el-Marjama.** Sources: Kallai 1972 sites 65–66; Finkelstein et al. 1997 pp. 732–733; Mazar, IEJ 45 (1995); Zohar, IEJ 30 (1980). If it lies east of the tell and is Roman or earlier, the pool criterion swings to ʿEin Samiya. *Status (follow-up B, E):* still not located. The only documented reservoir is a basin under a Byzantine church crypt at the spring, SW of the tell (Zohar p. 219). Roman–Byzantine dams and aqueducts are reported at Kh. Samiya, S/SE. Kallai is the chapter in Kochavi’s edited 1972 volume; pp.172–173 remain unavailable after the latest115-file folder/catalogue audit. The supplied HA76 p.19 has now been read: no pool, bearing, date or Roman water phase. [B](followup/B_kh_el_marjama.md) records the original inspection and access routes.
4. **Kenyon, *Jericho* II–III** (1965 pp. 516–545; 1981 pp. 173–174): are there Roman tombs at the mouth of a northern shaft? *Status (follow-up C):* Vol. II yes in kind: D9 is a Roman burial cut into a Bronze Age shaft; N.S.1 is a Roman cistern to the north; the strip just north of the tell was never searched. III pp.173–174 is now inspected: about18 north-slope shaft graves, dated by type to the first century AD; later quarry pits cut graves and lack an absolute date. No early accessible pit-mouth/tomb-mouth relation is established. The separate E section and Plate111b remain needed ([C](followup/C_kenyon_jericho_II_and_kh_yanun.md)).
5. **Finkelstein et al. 1997 pp. 822–829**, for caves, pits or tombs at Kh. Yanun / Yanun (the target under the Janoaḥ reading). *Status (follow-up C):* none recorded at Kh. Yanun (Roman 3.3%); burial caves only near Yanun village.
6. **Imagery base:** CORONA DF040–042 done ([D](followup/D_corona_1967.md)). Still needed: 600-dpi RAF PS30 5107/5137 and PS32 5038 / PS30 6054 (HUJI, fee) and BS Pal. 1032 (BayHStA Kriegsarchiv).
