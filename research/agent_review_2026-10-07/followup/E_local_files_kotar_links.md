# E — Owner's local files, the Kotar check, and access links

Coordinator report, 7 October 2026. Labels: EVIDENCE / INFERENCE.

## 1. Three local files not read before

**ESI 7–8 (1990), "El-Qasr, Monastery", p. 149 (Figs 124–126 on pp. 149–150; communicated by Y. Hirschfeld)**
- EVIDENCE: The site lies "at the edge of the ʿEin Samiya basin", map ref. 1823/1544. It was surveyed in February 1987.
- EVIDENCE: The complex has three parts:
  - A small community-type monastery (11.4 × 13.7 m), built of ashlars from a small quarry ~20 m to the east. It has a crypt (4 × 4 m) and a plastered cistern (3.1 × 4.0 m) whose ceiling of stone beams rests on two arches.
  - A farmhouse ~250 m east, with a central cistern, oil-press stones and two winepresses.
  - An animal pen ~100 m south of the farm.
- EVIDENCE: "Most of the ceramic material at both places belongs to the Byzantine period."
- INFERENCE: The arched, plastered cisterns in the ʿEin Samiya basin may be Byzantine. Any "great cistern" match there needs its own date. The dark square in [D](D_corona_1967.md) is not el-Qasr; el-Qasr lies ~0.9 km farther SE.

**Amit, Hirschfeld and Patrich (eds), *The Aqueducts of Ancient Palestine* (Hebrew, 1989)**
- The owner's PDF is a scan (354 pp.); its HTML copy (Ben-Yehuda project) holds only part of the volume. The Hebrew pages were OCR'd with tesseract.
- EVIDENCE: The Jericho chapter is E. Netzer, "The aqueducts and royal estate of the Hasmonean period in the western Jericho plain" (PDF pp. 286–297). It covers the Wadi Qelt (Naḥal Perat) aqueduct, built under John Hyrcanus. It also covers the Naʿaran aqueduct (ʿAin Duk/Nuweiʿima, possibly also ʿAin el-ʿAuja water), built early under Alexander Jannaeus.
- EVIDENCE: The plaster typology chapter (Porat) gives these date markers:
  - white single-layer plaster (type I,1): Hasmonean;
  - grey recipe-2 plaster: from Herod onward;
  - uniform red plaster: from the 3rd–4th centuries;
  - mottled red plaster: Early Arab.
- EVIDENCE: Nothing on the ʿAin es-Sultan reservoir or on ʿEin Samiya.
- INFERENCE: A plaster sample from the ʿAin es-Sultan basin or from Kallai's Marjama pool could be typed against this scheme. That is a cheap test if anyone reaches either site.

**Magen and Peleg, *Back to Qumran* (2018), "Site description" chapter**
- It covers the Qumran water system (aqueduct, tunnels, pools L48–L71) and is already used by the project.
- Nothing bears on Koḥlit.

## 2. Kotar (kotar.cet.ac.il) check

- EVIDENCE: Neither Kochavi (ed.), *Judaea, Samaria and the Golan: Archaeological Survey 1967–1968* (1972), nor Uffenheimer (ed.), *Bible and Jewish History* (1971, which holds Kallai's "Baal Shalisha and Ephraim"), is on Kotar as a book. A full-text search finds them only as citations in other books, and "כלאי סקר ארץ בנימין והר אפרים" returns nothing.
- EVIDENCE: Kotar does hold the gazetteer *Kol Makom ve-Atar*, entry "ʿEin Samiya 182155" ([link](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/kotarapp/index/Chapter.aspx?nBookID=18224547&nTocEntryID=18533393)). It says that Roman and Byzantine remains lie across the spring at Kh. Samiya: dam walls, aqueduct remains, church remains and a cemetery.
- INFERENCE: Kallai's "rock-cut pool next to aqueducts" may be on the Kh. Samiya side, S/SE of the tell. That is consistent with [B](B_kh_el_marjama.md). It is not yet located.
- The *Encyclopaedia Biblica* (Hebrew) vol. II entry "Baal Shalisha" is on Kotar, but it was not opened.
