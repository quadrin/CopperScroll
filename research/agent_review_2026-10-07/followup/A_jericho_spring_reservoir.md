# A — The ʿAin es-Sultan spring and reservoir (GOALS R07 item 2)

Agent report, 7 October 2026, saved by the coordinator. Labels: EVIDENCE = what the source says or shows; INFERENCE = the agent's reading.

**Sources**
- P. G. Dorrell, "The Spring at Jericho from Early Photographs", *PEQ* 125 (1993) pp. 95–114. PDF page = journal page − 93.
- C. Warren, *Underground Jerusalem* (1876) (UJ).
- C. Wilson and C. Warren, *The Recovery of Jerusalem* (1871) (RJ).
- The SWP text is not in these files, so it is cited only through Dorrell's quotations.

## 1. What stood at the spring

- EVIDENCE: The spring rises about mid-way along the tell's east side, "within a reservoir which is now enclosed" (Dorrell 95). Warren says it issues from the "eastern foot" of his mounds 4–6 (UJ 194).
- EVIDENCE: Travellers before 1890 describe a round or semicircular enclosure of hewn stone.
  - Pococke (1745) saw "six niches", two still whole (97).
  - Robinson (1838) found it "mostly broken away" (96).
  - The SWP gives "24 feet by 40 feet", with water coming out "beneath piles of broken stones" (96).
- EVIDENCE: Dorrell thinks an original round basin was breached, leaving "a larger, shallower, and more irregular pool" (96). Recorded depths are 4–15 inches (96).
- EVIDENCE (photos): In 1856 the pool was "fairly straight-sided" and edged with large blocks (97; Fig. 1, p. 98).
  - Behind it, a semicircular wall was "cut into the tell" (97).
  - Its upper courses are flat-faced and closely jointed, and its recesses keep smooth stucco with painted bands.
  - Its lower courses are patched with unsquared stones (110; Figs 2–4, pp. 98–99).
- EVIDENCE: Other features: an enclosed staircase (110), a thick enclosure wall (110), and outflow-channel walls "probably built of re-used blocks" (111).
- EVIDENCE: A round-headed niche was exposed when rubble was cleared before 1892 (111; Fig. 13, p. 104). The basin rim below it looks "built rather than rock-cut", but Dorrell is not certain (111).
- EVIDENCE: A mill-pool and mill-race were built c. 1891–93 (111; Figs 15, 17, pp. 105–106). In 1898 the mill-pool was "extended to form the present reservoir" (111, citing Sellin and Watzinger 1913).
- EVIDENCE: The January 1908 plan shows a long north–south basin.
  - Its curved north end follows the old spring-wall, and the mill lies to the SE (Figs 20–21, p. 107; text 111).
  - A dividing wall with a sluice appears after 1911 (111; Fig. 23, p. 108).
  - The mill was out of use by 1954 (111).
- INFERENCE: In Fig. 24 (1954, p. 109) the back wall is regular squared blocks with a low arched outlet, which looks modern.

## 2. Dating

- EVIDENCE: The present reservoir dates from 1898 (111). Its back wall stands about 1 m east of the spring-house wall and follows the same curve (112).
- EVIDENCE: Opinions on the spring-house wall:
  - C. Wilson (1885): a small "Roman temple", with Byzantine capitals lying about (97).
  - Clermont-Ganneau (1874): an apse and niche of a pagan temple (97).
  - Harper (1894): "probably … Roman" (97).
  - Dorrell: "very probably of late Roman or Byzantine date" (97). He calls it a Roman spring-house and compares a 3rd-century AD fountain-house on coins of Pella (112).
  - Warren: a "temple or portico, probably of the Roman period, built of small stones" (UJ 194–195).
- EVIDENCE: The spring-wall was "partly rendered" c. 1892–94 (111). None of the three sources mentions any British Mandate or Jordanian works.
- INFERENCE: The SWP's "cemented west wall" is older than the 1890s rendering. It is probably the ancient painted stucco (110).

## 3. Antiquity and destruction

- EVIDENCE: Dorrell cites Josephus only to identify the spring (95). None of the three sources mentions a Hasmonean or Herodian pool.
- EVIDENCE: The wall was "extensively plundered" c. 1890, and the staircase was robbed out (111). Villagers stopped Clermont-Ganneau's clearance in 1873 (95).
- EVIDENCE: Dorrell thinks the spring's level and position have barely moved "since classical times" (112).
- EVIDENCE: Kufic-inscribed sherds and a blue-and-white dish fragment came from ʿAin es-Sultan (RJ 381).

## 4. Warren at the spring

- EVIDENCE: He camped under a mound near the spring (UJ 168) and dug "the mound above the spring-head" (181). The work resumed "the following spring" (192).
- INFERENCE: His visits were 1867 and 1868.
- EVIDENCE: He calls the aqueducts from the spring "comparatively modern", built on older lines (182). He dates the sugar mills after the Muslim conquest (186). He suggests Roman Jericho may lie at Birket Mousa (197).
- EVIDENCE: He describes no reservoir or tank at the spring itself (UJ 162–197).

## 5. Bottom line

- INFERENCE: The evidence makes an Early Roman date for "the reservoir" less likely.
  - The reservoir that stands today was built in 1898.
  - The SWP's 24 × 40 ft pool was a broken, irregular successor basin edged with reused blocks.
  - The only ancient part is the curved wall with the niche, and its date rests on opinions that lean late Roman or Byzantine.
  - Warren's "small stones" and the absence of any reported drafted-margin masonry argue weakly against Herodian work.
  - So Jericho keeps a Roman-period spring-house, but not a demonstrated Herodian pool.
- **Settling observation:** find sealed coins or pottery under the lowest course of the curved spring-house wall or the basin rim. These should survive about 1 m behind the 1898 back wall (112). Recording whether the basin is rock-cut or built would also help.
