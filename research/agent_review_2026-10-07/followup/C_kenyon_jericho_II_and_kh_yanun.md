## A. Kenyon, *Excavations at Jericho* II (1965)

### (i) Qumran-type graves

- EVIDENCE, p. 2: "Roman tombs and graves are scattered over a wide area in the northern cemetery". They "are also found on the Tell", where they are of the grave-arcosolium type, "so characteristic of the cemetery at Qumran". Most date to the 1st century AD.
- EVIDENCE, ch. 6 (C. Bennett, pp. 516–545), in the tomb field N and NW of the tell:
  - 7 Roman tombs: G2, G3, G5, G81, H23, K23 and J41.
  - 14 graves: G66–68, G80, G84, J5, J6, J16, J22–25, P4 and Q1.
- EVIDENCE: G66–68, G84 and Q1 are "grave–arcosolium": a shaft with a side recess, sealed with mudbricks (pp. 536–537).
  - G68, an adult, is a single north–south burial with "the head on a mud brick facing north".
  - G84, a child, is a single north–south burial with "skull to the north".
  - Q1 lies east–west.
  - The pottery is matched to Qumran Period II (pp. 517–518).
- INFERENCE: Puech's "north side" graves are probably the on-tell graves of p. 2, published in detail in Vol. III. Unverified.

### (ii) Hellenistic / Early Roman remains

- EVIDENCE, p. 2: "In the Roman period, there was no occupation on the Tell." Stray finds point to occupation in the oasis and in the cemetery area.
- EVIDENCE: N.S.2 is a Roman occupation site with a mudbrick wall (p. 544).
- EVIDENCE: Single sherds: a Late Hellenistic-type casserole from K23 (p. 527) and a pre-Herodian cooking pot from C1 (p. 539).
- This volume has no trench or square data for the tell itself.

### (iii) Spring, pool or water installations east of the tell

- None in this volume. The only remark is that a cemetery east of the tell is "unlikely", because that land was farmed (p. 169).

### (iv) Shafts, pits and cisterns north of the tell

- **D9** (Area D, nearest the tell; pp. 276–277; fig. 137.2): a Roman grave with a "rectangular shaft, at the base of which was a recessed ledge for the burial". It was "cut into the side of the M.B. shaft", which is 3.90 m deep. This is a Roman burial set into the shaft of a Bronze Age tomb.
- **G2, G81 and J41** (p. 516): Roman reuse of Bronze Age tombs. G2 held ossuaries; G81 held six coffined burials (p. 532).
- **N.S.1** (pp. 539–544): a **Roman cistern** on the "outskirts of the Ain es Sultan camp towards Khirbet Mefjar", which lies north of the tell.
  - Finds: 14 "Herodian" lamps, painted bowls matched at Qumran, amphorae, a drain pipe and a stone bowl.
  - It was rescue-dug, and no plan was made.
- **P29** (p. 544): "a roughly-hewn pit" with a flexed skeleton at 2.10 m and Roman sherds. It was dug to 4.80 m, but "the floor was not reached and excavation was discontinued". Area P is the farthest area from the tell.
- **Survey gap** (p. 169): the search skipped the ground "just north of the Tell, for here there was a considerable superficial deposit". It also skipped the line of the NE road.

### (v) Maps

- Fig. 14 (p. 35) and fig. 91 (p. 168) are distribution maps of tomb areas A, A+, B, D, G, H, J, K, L, M, N, O, P and P2. The tell is at the bottom, and the refugee-camp blocks are drawn in.
- Area plans are on Pls XXXIV–XLI. The Area P plan (Pl. XXXVIII, p. 770) shows P4 but not P29.
- The plateau ends at the Wadi Nawaʿimeh, 1150 m north of the tell (p. 165).
- INFERENCE (measured off fig. 91): Area D is ~100–200 m N of the tell's edge, J ~250 m NNW, G ~400 m NW and P ~900 m NNE.

### Native observations and published interpretations

- **Burials: III 173–174, Trench II/Site O, Stage XXIV, phase lxxvi.** Kenyon reports about eighteen burials in the excavated area. Most occur in the northern part of the trench, reaching the stated local ordinate 42.50 m N over the Middle Bronze revetment; some occur throughout the trench, including its southern end. Graves have a shaft with a lateral undercut recess for the body, normally closed by mudbricks. A described eastern-section example lies at local 37.50–38.00 m N, 8.17 m H. The maximum **surviving** shaft depth is 1.25 m below the present surface; Kenyon's approximation to the original surface/depth is an inference qualified by erosion and later truncation, not an observed intact original datum.
- **Orientation and dating: III 173–174.** The described on-tell graves run east–west, with heads to the west. Kenyon compares their grave-arcosolium form with the northern cemetery and Qumran, while explicitly distinguishing Qumran's north–south orientation. The on-tell graves have **no offerings**. Their first-century AD attribution follows typology/comparisons, citing **II 516 and 536–537** and de Vaux, *L'Archéologie et les Manuscrits de la Mer Morte* 37–39; it is not a direct date from grave-associated offerings in these pages. The oasis inhabitants' use of this burial area is Kenyon's attribution. Shared type does not establish the same burial community or the Scroll's named tombs.
- **Later pits: III 174, Stage XXV, phase lxxvii, under the Roman-to-modern period heading.** Numerous pits occur over much of the northern end. They truncate many grave shafts and contain backfilled rubble. Kenyon interprets them as quarries for the mound's bricky material, to spread on fields or make bricks. The relative observation is that they postdate the graves. **Their absolute date is unknown** beyond that relation. The summary on **121** gives a possible range from the first century AD to the author's present; that broad range supplies no secure first-century pit-opening date. Earlier excavation trenches removed the evidence at the southern end, so report silence there is a coverage gap.
- **Phase separation in context: III 119–121 and 172–173.** A cistern mentioned in the Trench II summary belongs to **Early Bronze Stage XX**, not the Roman burial phase. The preceding Iron Age house phases and their **plan 256a** are not a plan of the Roman grave/pit relation.
