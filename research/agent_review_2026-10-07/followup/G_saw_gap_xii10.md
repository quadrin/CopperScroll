# G — Saw-gap check at XII 10 (the 21/22 cut), 8 October 2026

This is an exploratory geometric check. No image of XII 10 was opened and no reading is claimed; the [frozen XII 10 protocol](../wave1/T03_T04_registry/XII10_IMAGE_READING_PROTOCOL.md) still governs any future image. Each statement is marked EVIDENCE (with its source) or INFERENCE.

**Question.** Could the metal removed by the 1955–56 saw cut between segments 21 and 22 hide a whole letter, such as the yod or waw needed by readings R4–R6 (שבינח, שכינה, שכונה)? Could it hide part of the final letter (ה, ח or ת)?

## The saw

EVIDENCE: H. Wright Baker, "Notes on the Opening of the 'Bronze' Scrolls from Qumran", *BJRL* 39.1 (1956) pp. 45–56. It is open on the Internet Archive as item `bulletin-of-the-john-rylands-library_1956-09_39_1`, and its p. 50 numerals were checked against the page image and the OCR.

| Item | Value | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Saw | standard commercial high-speed-steel "slitting saws", 1¾ in diameter | 50 |
| Blade thickness | "0.006 in. thick" = 0.152 mm | 50 |
| Effect | "an extremely clean cut, removing a negligible amount of material" | 50 |
| Where the cuts went | between the letters if possible; otherwise across the strokes near their centre | 50–51 |
| Damage claimed | "no letter has been made unrecognizable by the sawing" | 51 |
| Sheet | 0.03–0.04 in thick; plaque about 8 ft × 11 in | 45 |

INFERENCE: a slitting saw has no tooth set, so the slot is about the blade's width, roughly 0.15–0.25 mm allowing for wobble. No gap has been measured at cut 21/22.

## Letter sizes in column XII

INFERENCE. The figures come from the 143 hand-verified facsimile letter boxes in [W2F](../wave2/W2F_plates_model/), excluding XII 10. They assume the plates are printed at about 1:1: the images are embedded at 150 ppi, and Puech gives the radiograph plates as 1:1. The scale is good to about ±10–15%. As a cross-check, the copy image's 1760 px height equals 298 mm, against Baker's 279 mm for the plaque width.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Letter height | about 6.7 mm |
| Yod width | median 2.4 mm; minimum 0.8 mm |
| Waw width | median 2.2 mm; narrowest drawn stroke about 0.5 mm |
| Gap between letters in a word | median 1.2 mm |
| ה / ח / ת width | median 6.1 / 4.7 / 5.8 mm |

These are boxes drawn on the facsimile. They do not measure the grooves in the metal.

## Where the cut falls in the word

EVIDENCE, as recorded in [W2A](../wave2/W2A_kohlit_dossier/REPORT.txt), lines 95–101 and 362–374:
- Puech 2006 facsimile, pl. CCCLXXXII: the dashed saw-cut line runs through the **last letter** of word 2.
- Galvanoplastic copy, pl. CCCLXXXI: the last letter sits on the 21/22 crease.
- Radiographs, pls. CCCLV–CCCLVI: word 2 ends at the 21/22 cut.
- Lefkovits p. 451, describing Allegro's drawing: a yod-nun-waw ligature "followed by a cut", where the two strokes could be the legs of a he, ḥet or taw.

Milik (DJD III p. 298), Wolters (CSS pp. 319–320) and Puech (2006 p. 206) discuss the stroke *before the nun*. None of them treats saw loss at that spot.

## Result

1. **A whole letter cannot have been lost in the cut itself** (INFERENCE, high).
   - Size: the slot (0.15–0.25 mm) is 3–20 times narrower than any yod or waw, at any plausible plate scale.
   - Position: the editions place the cut at the final letter, about one letter-width away from where R4–R6 need their yod or waw (between ב/כ and נ). The saw neither removed nor created the disputed stroke.
   - This depends on the editions placing the cut correctly. The protocol still treats the 21/22 location as a hypothesis drawn from the plates.
2. **Loss in a damaged zone along the cut: unknown.** Lundberg and Zuckerman (CSS p. 52) report the cut edges deteriorating over time, but no width has been published. For a whole letter to be lost, that damage would have to reach about 1–3.5 mm.
3. **The final letter: cut through, effect unknown.** About 0.15 mm of it is gone. INFERENCE (low–medium): if the saw crossed the roof near its centre, as Baker describes and Allegro's drawing suggests, the joint that separates he from ḥet would lie about 2–3 mm from the cut. Edge damage or the crease in the copy could still hide it.

So the saw cut can be set aside as an explanation for a missing yod. The he/ḥet question remains for the images.
